5

The first thing Harry saw from the Minister's box was open air.

Their seats occupied the highest point of the stadium. Nothing stood between the front row and a drop of several hundred feet except a gold rail polished thin by nervous hands. Far below, the pitch spread beneath its floating lights, and the players warming above it appeared no larger than insects.

Winky sat in the front corner with both eyes closed. Her fingers were locked around the arms of her chair, and her bare feet did not touch the floor. The seat beside her appeared empty, though its velvet cushion remained pressed down as if someone occupied it. Harry noticed. Across Hermione, Draconia met his eyes and gave the smallest shake of her head.

Kingsley knows.

Harry touched the mirror in his pocket and continued towards their seats.

The box filled slowly around them. Cornelius Fudge arrived in robes covered with tiny Union Jacks and greeted Lucius with the warmth of a man who remembered donations more readily than disagreements. Ludo Bagman wore the old yellow-and-black robes of the Wimbourne Wasps, stretched across a body broader than during his playing years. His round face shone with excitement. Barty Crouch entered without looking at Winky, followed by Percy carrying folders.

The Bulgarian Minister wore red velvet and appeared unable to speak English until Fudge attempted to explain the sport using hand gestures. He interrupted in perfect English and laughed for nearly a minute. Mr Granger liked him immediately.

Harry sat in the front row between Hermione and Draconia. Sirius took the seat behind him, with Remus beside him, still wearing the dancing shamrock hat. Its leaves moved whenever Ireland was mentioned.

"You kept it," Harry said.

Remus removed the hat and placed it on Sirius.

"Temporary error."

Sirius's expression disappeared beneath green felt. Harry turned forwards before laughing became dangerous.

The stadium lights dimmed, and a hundred thousand voices lowered with them. Bagman lifted his wand to his throat.

"Ladies and gentlemen—welcome!"

His voice exploded from every corner, and the crowd answered. Sound struck Harry physically, travelling through the floor, the seat and the gold rail beneath his hands. Flags snapped around the stadium. Green and red fireworks burst above the pitch, and Harry's heartbeat changed to match the noise.

"And now," Bagman announced, "presenting the mascots of the Bulgarian national team!"

The eastern stands fell silent. Music began, soft enough at first to seem incapable of surviving the crowd. A hundred women walked onto the pitch, moonlight gathering around pale skin and dark hair. They moved in time with something more powerful than music, and Harry forgot the match. He forgot the stadium, the box and why standing on the rail would be a poor decision.

The nearest Veela lifted both arms. Harry rose.

Hermione seized the back of his jacket. Draconia caught his belt.

"Sit," she ordered.

"I can fly."

"Not without a broom."

"Details."

Harry put one foot on the lower bar. Sirius pulled him backwards by the shoulders, dropping him hard into his seat. The music stopped, and Harry blinked. Around the stadium, men stood in places they ought not to have reached. One Irish supporter hung from the upper railing by both hands; a Ministry official was attempting to climb a flagpole.

Harry looked from Hermione to Draconia. Neither had released him.

"That was strange," he said.

Hermione stared. "You tried to jump."

"It seemed reasonable at the time."

"You said you could fly."

"I usually can."

Draconia let go of his belt. "I have never been less impressed."

Harry turned towards Sirius. His godfather had removed the shamrock hat.

"Not one word."

Remus's mouth had disappeared behind one hand. Beside Narcissa, Lucius sat perfectly still, with two of his wife's fingers resting against his sleeve.

"Not one word," Lucius repeated.

Mr Granger looked at his wife. "Did I—"

"You offered to purchase Bulgaria."

"Ah."

Draconia settled back in her chair.

"The match has not begun, and every man in this box has failed."

Hermione's face remained pink from effort. "That is unfair to Remus."

They looked at him. Remus cleared his throat.

"Experience with magical compulsion."

Sirius groaned. "Now I feel worse."

The Bulgarian supporters roared their approval, and Bagman introduced Ireland's mascots. A vast green-and-gold comet flew into the stadium and divided into thousands of leprechauns, which formed a giant shamrock above the pitch. Gold coins rained from it, bouncing across seats and filling laps. Harry caught several. Hermione examined one between her fingers.

"Leprechaun gold vanishes."

"How quickly?"

"Several hours."

Draconia filled a pocket. "Then I have several hours to spend it."

The leprechauns formed an enormous smiling face, blew a kiss towards the Veela and scattered.

"Better mascots," Harry said.

"You did not attempt to die for them," Draconia replied.

"Exactly."

The players entered. Bagman's amplified voice named Ireland first.

"Connolly! Ryan! Troy! Mullet! Moran! Quigley! And—Lynch!"

Seven green blurs shot onto the pitch. Harry raised his Omnioculars, bringing the players into sharp focus. Troy circled the hoops, while Mullet and Moran passed the Quaffle between them without looking. Above their formation, Lynch searched the air before the Snitch had even been released.

Bulgaria emerged in red. Volkov and Vulchanov carried Beaters' bats scarred from years of play. Dimitrov, Ivanova and Levski formed a line behind them, and Zograf swept before the hoops. Krum entered last.

He looked older on the pitch than in photographs, compact and almost narrow, his shoulders curved over the broom. Nothing about him suggested grace until he moved. Then every other Seeker appeared stationary. He crossed the stadium once, and Draconia's miniature version stood on her shoulder and folded its arms.

"One Krum," she said.

Harry watched Lynch take position opposite him.

"Three Chasers."

The referee released the balls, and the match began.

Quidditch at Hogwarts had taught Harry speed. The World Cup taught him scale. Troy seized the Quaffle from the first toss and accelerated. Mullet crossed beneath her, Moran rose, and the formation opened, folded and changed before Bulgaria's Chasers recognised the attack. Troy passed backwards; Moran caught. Mullet was already waiting beside the right hoop.

Ten points. The Irish stands erupted, and Harry rose with them.

"Did you see that?"

Hermione still held the Omnioculars backwards.

"No."

He corrected them without lowering his own.

"Replay dial. Left side."

"Which left?"

"Your left."

"Objects do not possess my left."

Draconia reached across Harry and turned the dial. Hermione watched the goal again, her brows drawing together.

"They passed before the receiver was in position."

"Because she knew where Mullet would be."

"That seems risky."

"That is why it worked."

Bulgaria answered through Dimitrov, the Quaffle striking the left hoop before Ryan completed his dive. Ten-all. The pace increased, and Harry tried to follow the entire pitch at once, only to lose an attack whenever he glanced towards another. Chasers crossed in patterns too fast to become plans. Bludgers moved like cannon fire. Volkov struck one towards Troy; Quigley intercepted it with a crack audible across the stadium and sent it into Ivanova's path.

Lynch and Krum held their positions above the play. Krum barely appeared to search. He watched, and Harry understood the difference. A Seeker who chased every flash spent the match following light. Krum studied movement around absence: a player's glance towards something small, the referee shifting, floodlight catching something where no broom passed.

Harry lowered the Omnioculars. To the naked eye, Krum almost disappeared.

"He's good," he admitted.

Draconia looked smug.

"Ireland has scored four times," Harry added.

"Krum has not needed to act."

"That isn't the same as winning."

"Not yet."

Moran scored again. Fifty-ten. Hermione began shouting, apparently unaware of it until Draconia turned towards her.

"What?"

"Neutrality?"

"That was an excellent pass."

"Cowardice wearing respectability."

"Watch the match."

Harry smiled into his Omnioculars.

At seventy-ten, Bulgaria became physical. Vulchanov sent a Bludger through Ireland's formation, breaking a three-player attack. Ivanova seized the loose Quaffle and drove towards the hoops, but Ryan blocked her shot with the tail of his broom. Levski recovered it. Connolly drove a Bludger across his path, and the return pass went wide.

Above them, Krum dropped towards the pitch. Every voice in the stadium rose as Lynch dived after him. Harry leaned forwards, recognising the line of the descent before either Seeker reached the grass.

"Wronski Feint."

"What?" Hermione asked.

Krum pulled out ten feet above the ground. Lynch did not. He struck the pitch, and the sound silenced the Irish stands. Krum climbed without looking back. Draconia's smile vanished.

"That was deliberate," Hermione said.

"It is a legal move."

"He knew Lynch would follow."

"That is the move."

"He could have killed him."

Harry watched mediwizards race onto the pitch.

"Lynch chose to follow," he said.

The defence sounded weaker aloud. Draconia studied his face.

"Would you?"

He pictured the dive: the speed, the ground rushing near, the certainty that the other Seeker would either follow or surrender.

"Yes."

Hermione's face tightened. "Then perhaps the rule is wrong."

The match resumed with Lynch bleeding from the nose. Ireland scored twice while Bulgaria's attention remained divided, taking the score to ninety-ten. Harry's excitement returned, edged now with unease. Krum's choice had been legal; it had also used another player's courage as a weapon. Harry knew too much about people who treated survival as consent, but he kept watching.

At one hundred and thirty to ten, the Veela began dancing. The leprechauns responded with rude hand gestures. The referee attempted to intervene and was struck in the face by a fireball, while the Bulgarian Minister shouted and Fudge demanded rules nobody could hear. The crowd divided between outrage and delight.

Harry stayed in his seat. He remained aware of the women on the pitch in the same way one watched fire: beautiful, dangerous and best viewed beside two people prepared to restrain him. Draconia noticed his hands gripping the chair.

"Learning?"

"Surviving."

"A familiar strength."

Her knee pressed briefly against his, grounding him more effectively than embarrassment. Ireland scored, taking the lead to one hundred and forty to ten.

Krum moved. No feint announced it this time. He accelerated from near the Bulgarian hoops towards the centre, his body flat against the broom. Lynch followed a second later. Both descended through Chasers and Bludgers while the Snitch remained invisible to almost everyone else.

Harry found it near the grass: a flicker of gold, barely separate from the floodlit pitch. Ireland scored again. One hundred and fifty to ten. Krum dropped beneath Mullet, and Lynch crossed the line too wide.

Below them, Ireland did not slow. Troy intercepted Ivanova's desperate pass and sent the Quaffle to Mullet. One hundred and sixty to ten. Moran stole the next possession before the scoreboard finished changing. One hundred and seventy to ten.

The Snitch turned. Krum's hand closed.

The stadium split open, half becoming sound and half stunned silence. Bagman shouted above both.

"KRUM HAS THE SNITCH—but Ireland wins!"

The score appeared in green fire.

IRELAND — 170

BULGARIA — 160

For one breath, nobody understood. Then Ireland celebrated.

Harry was lifted by the people around him. Sirius seized his shoulders from behind, Hermione shouted into his ear, and Draconia stared at the score as though arithmetic had betrayed her personally. The row behind surged towards the rail. Hermione lost her footing and caught Harry around the middle; he grabbed her before momentum could carry them both into the row ahead, locking one arm across her back while Sirius held his jacket.

For several seconds, they remained tangled inside the celebration. Hermione's face was bright with victory, loose curls escaping around her temples. The quiet shamrock pin Harry had given her pulsed green between them.

"You were right," he said, the roar forcing him close enough for his mouth almost to touch her ear.

"I know."

"You could pretend to be surprised."

"That would weaken the moment."

Draconia caught his forearm and pulled them both upright. Her gaze dropped to Hermione's arm still around his middle before returning to his face. The irritation in it seemed too precise to belong entirely to Bulgaria's defeat.

"If you have finished celebrating arithmetic," she said, "some of us are enduring a tragedy."

"Ten points," Hermione replied with dangerous satisfaction.

Draconia turned. "Do not."

"Ireland by ten after Krum catches the Snitch."

"Your boat was unsporting."

"My boat was correct."

Harry laughed. Draconia's stare snapped towards him.

"You predicted Ireland by thirty."

"I was closer in spirit."

"That is not how numbers work," Hermione said.

Ireland circled the pitch beneath falling shamrocks. Krum landed alone near the Bulgarian bench, blood running from a broken nose. His face remained unreadable while officials carried the Cup towards the winners. Draconia watched him.

"He knew."

Harry lowered his Omnioculars. "What?"

"The score. He knew catching it would lose."

Hermione's triumph faded. "Then why end the match?"

Harry replayed the final dive. Krum had watched Ireland dominate every formation, Bulgaria gaining points only when physical play broke the rhythm. Another ten minutes could have widened the lead beyond any respectable finish.

"He chose the loss," Harry said. "Before it became worse."

Draconia nodded.

"That is not victory," Hermione said.

"No." Harry looked at Krum standing beneath the green celebration. "But it was control."

The word stayed with him after Ireland lifted the Cup.

They left the box with the crowd. Gold passages became rivers of people, Ireland supporters singing from every staircase while Bulgarian drums answered from lower levels, slower now but no less proud. Sirius kept a hand on Harry's shoulder. Lucius walked behind Draconia, Narcissa kept Hermione and the Grangers within reach, and Remus scanned faces at the edge of the group beneath his ridiculous hat.

Winky was gone. Her chair stood empty, and the indentation in the seat beside it had vanished. Harry told Sirius.

"Kingsley is checking the enclosure," he said.

"Crouch left before the Cup presentation."

Lucius heard. "He often does. Ceremony interests him less than administration."

"The invisible person?"

"Unknown."

That answer had multiplied throughout the day. Harry touched his mirror as the crowd carried them outside. Night had fallen fully, and lanterns formed bright paths through the woods. Earlier rain had softened the earth; thousands of feet had turned the slopes to mud.

They found Cedric near the first gate. Amos wore three Irish scarves and carried enough leprechaun gold to retire until morning.

"Knew they'd do it!" he shouted.

Cedric's Bulgarian rosette had disappeared beneath his coat. Cho stood beside him with an expression of patient defeat. Hermione held up one finger.

"Ireland by ten."

Cedric stared. "You predicted that?"

Draconia walked past. "We do not encourage her."

Hermione followed, smiling. Harry lingered to arrange a meeting at the tents, then hurried after them.

The campsite celebrated. Fires burned brighter than Ministry rules allowed, music came from every direction, and leprechauns flew between tents throwing gold into cups and cooking pots. A wizard wearing nothing but an Irish flag danced on Mr Roberts's registration table while two officials attempted to convince him that the surface was not a stage.

Harry had never seen adults behave so much like students after a Quidditch final. Sirius joined a chorus despite knowing only six words. Remus knew all of them and denied it. Mr Granger attempted Irish dancing with a family from Cork, while Mrs Granger photographed the result for legal protection.

Even Lucius relaxed. He removed his travelling cloak and sat outside the tents with his sleeves rolled to the elbows. A thin glamour concealed the mark on his left forearm, but Harry saw him check it twice. Narcissa saw too. Neither spoke.

Cedric and Cho arrived with several Hufflepuffs. Hagrid brought half the Hogwarts staff assigned to Cup duties, including Professor Flitwick and Professor Sprout. The combined campsites shared food until every table disappeared beneath plates. Harry sat on the grass between Hermione and Draconia, his throat sore from shouting and his hair smelling of fireworks. An Ireland rosette had attached itself to his sleeve and sang whenever conversation became quiet enough.

He felt happy. The recognition frightened him precisely because the feeling was so simple, arriving without a rescue to be grateful for or a danger he had just survived. He pressed the key beneath his shirt.

Where are you?

With them.

What do you know?

He could find them.

Draconia leaned into his shoulder while explaining Krum's decision to Cedric. Hermione interrupted every third sentence with arithmetic. Cedric listened to both and directed occasional questions towards Harry, apparently believing him capable of mediation.

"I support Ireland," Harry said.

"Coward," Draconia replied.

"Winner."

She elbowed him, and he laughed.

Near midnight, the celebrations thinned. Hagrid returned to the staff campsite, and Cedric went back to his father. The Grangers retired after Mr Granger discovered that the gold in his pocket had vanished. Hermione went with them only after confirming that Harry's emergency mirror was beneath his pillow.

Draconia paused outside the Malfoy tent.

"You chose the correct team for the wrong reasons."

"Winning?"

"Temporary luck."

"One hundred and seventy points."

"One hundred and sixty plus Krum."

Harry smiled. "Sleep well."

She started through the flap.

"Draco."

She looked back.

"The clasp suits you."

Her fingers touched the silver broom at her shoulder.

"Good night, Harry."

She disappeared inside. Harry watched the canvas fall closed, his attention remaining there until he realised that his fingers had risen to his own shoulder, mirroring the place where the clasp rested on hers.

Sirius waited beside the Black tent wearing the shamrock hat again.

"Remus?" Harry guessed.

"Violent man."

"It suits you."

"Betrayal everywhere."

They entered the tent. Harry put his wand holster on the bedside table, reconsidered, and fastened it around his forearm again. Sirius noticed from the doorway.

"Nott?"

"Habit."

"All right."

"You don't believe me."

"Not remotely."

Harry sat on the bed. "I thought I saw him."

"I know."

"If he was here, why didn't anything happen?"

Sirius leaned against the frame.

"Perhaps you frightened him away with the jacket."

"Draconia likes it."

"Then I withdraw the joke."

Harry threw a pillow. Sirius caught it.

"Sleep."

"You too."

"I have first watch."

"Kingsley has Aurors outside."

"They are welcome to watch me watching."

Harry lay down. The ugly lamp cast warm light across the canvas wall.

"Sirius?"

"Mm?"

"Next Cup."

Sirius's expression softened.

"Next Cup."

He closed the flap. Harry fell asleep to distant singing.

The singing became screaming.

Harry woke with his wand in his hand. Orange light flashed across the tent walls, broken by shadows of running people, crossing spells and something large passing close enough to pull the canvas inwards. For one disoriented instant, he was beneath Hogwarts again. Stone scraped, water moved across the floor, and a voice travelled through the dark.

Then the brass key struck his chest as he sat up.

Where are you?

The World Cup.

What can you feel?

Wand. Holster. Key.

What do you know?

The doorway stood empty beyond the thin canvas partition. Sirius was not there, and the knowledge failed.

"Sirius?"

No answer came. Harry left the bed, but Remus entered before he reached the flap, wearing trousers and an open shirt. His wand was raised, his scars stark in the flashing light.

"Shoes."

"Where's Sirius?"

"Outside."

"What's happening?"

"People in masks."

Harry froze with one foot inside his trainer. Remus saw.

"Harry."

"Death Eaters?"

"We don't know."

They both knew. Another spell exploded near the entrance, shaking the tent. Remus crossed the room and fastened Harry's shoe himself.

"Your mirror."

Harry checked his pocket.

"Wand?"

He held it up.

"Stay beside me."

They went outside, into a campsite that had become fire.

Tents burned in scattered rows, their extension charms collapsing interiors into impossible shapes. Smoke moved low beneath the trees. People fled in every direction carrying children, bags or nothing at all. Above them, four bodies floated through the night.

Mr Roberts hung upside down in his nightshirt. His wife spun beside him, their two children drifting between them with arms reaching and mouths open in screams that disappeared beneath the crowd. Masked wizards marched below, white faces reflecting the fires above black robes. Their raised wands turned the family with lazy movements while others followed, laughing.

The sound reached Harry, and everything else stopped. Fire lost its heat. The crowd moved without noise, and Remus's hand on his shoulder seemed to belong to someone at the far end of a corridor. The masks became one face, white and empty and inhuman.

The diary's eyes opened behind Draconia's. Quirrell turned beneath Hogwarts, Voldemort's ruined face emerging where the turban should have been. Dementors lowered their hoods.

"Harry."

Remus spoke beside him, but the name did not reach. A masked wizard struck Mr Roberts with a spell, twisting his body to renewed laughter. Harry could not breathe.

A hand closed around his wrist. Draconia stood in front of him, wearing a dark cloak over her nightdress, her hair loose down her back. Several paces behind, Narcissa held a silver shield over Hermione and the Grangers.

"Where are you?" Draconia asked.

Harry stared at the nearest mask.

"Potter."

"The campsite," Hermione answered from beneath the shield.

Draconia did not look away from him.

"He has to say it."

The procession moved closer. Harry felt the key against his chest.

"The campsite."

"What can you feel?"

"Your hand."

"What else?"

He swallowed. "My wand. The ground."

"What do you know?"

The answer would not come. Someone shouted behind him, and Sirius emerged from the smoke beside Lucius, both with their wands drawn. Lucius's left sleeve had been ripped open from elbow to wrist. The glamour was gone, exposing a faded black skull. Several masks turned towards it, and their laughter changed.

"I know how to find them," Harry said.

Sound crashed back. Draconia released his wrist, and Sirius reached them, gripping the back of Harry's neck for one second as though confirming flesh and bone.

"Forest. Now."

"The Roberts—"

"We'll get them."

"They have children."

"Harry."

Sirius's voice broke through every argument.

"Take Hermione and Draconia. Follow Remus. Do not turn back."

It was the correct order. Harry hated him for giving it.

Remus directed the Grangers towards the tree line. Narcissa took Draconia's hand, and Hermione moved with her parents between them. The six started towards the forest while Sirius and Lucius stayed behind. Harry followed for three steps, then five.

A child screamed overhead. He turned back.

The masked group had raised the Roberts family higher. Mrs Roberts's nightdress hung towards her shoulders. One child, a boy no older than eight, slipped sideways from the spell holding him, rotating until only an ankle remained caught in invisible force. The wizard beneath him laughed.

Lucius walked towards the man. For one terrible second, Harry understood the movement incorrectly. The Dark Mark lay exposed on his arm; the attackers had recognised him. These were people Lucius had once chosen, whose objects he had stored and secrets protected. Perhaps every kindness since the Chamber had been borrowed. Perhaps fear had called him back into his proper shape.

Draconia saw her father crossing the field and stopped. Narcissa pulled her forwards.

"Do not look."

"Mother—"

"Keep moving."

Lucius lifted his wand. The masked wizard opened both arms in welcome.

"Malfoy!"

Lucius fired. The spell caught the man in the chest and threw him backwards through a burning tent. Silence passed through the attackers. Lucius turned his wand towards the sky.

"Arresto Momentum!"

The child stopped falling. Sirius summoned him across the field and caught him against his chest, then lowered him to the ground and wrapped his own jacket around his shoulders. He tried to guide the boy towards a fleeing Ministry witch, but small fingers caught his shirt instead.

The masked line erupted.

"Traitor!"

"Blood coward!"

"Malfoy!"

Curses converged on Lucius. Silver serpents rose before him, green, red and purple light breaking against their bodies. The shield rippled, but held. Narcissa stopped pulling Draconia and looked at her husband, pride and terror changing her face at once.

"Go," she told Remus. "I am staying."

"Cissa—" Draconia began.

Narcissa took her daughter's face between her hands.

"Your father made his choice. Honour it by surviving."

She kissed Draconia's forehead and ran towards the field. Draconia did not move. Harry knew that stillness, and he caught her hand.

"Where are you?"

Her eyes remained on Lucius.

"Draco."

She looked at him. "The campsite."

"What can you feel?"

"You."

Harry tightened his grip.

"What do you know?"

Draconia's breath shook.

"He chose us."

Hermione took her other hand.

"Come on."

They ran.

The forest path had almost disappeared beneath the fleeing crowd. Families stumbled between the trees, children crying against their parents' shoulders, while Ministry officials shouted contradictory instructions from opposite sides. Spells lit the branches in violent bursts of colour. Remus kept the Grangers ahead of him, with Harry, Hermione and Draconia close behind, but the narrowing path pressed everyone together until Harry could scarcely see past the people in front.

A curse struck the trees. Wood exploded overhead, and the crowd scattered as a trunk crashed across the path between Remus and the trio. Harry caught a glimpse of Mr Granger turning towards them. Hermione reached for her father, but bodies surged into the gap before their hands could meet.

"Dad!"

Smoke swallowed him. Hermione scrambled towards the fallen trunk, trying to climb over it, and Draconia caught her before she could put herself beneath the feet of the crowd.

"You will be crushed."

"My parents are there."

"With Lupin."

"We don't know that."

Harry drew the emergency mirror. Sparks crawled across its surface where a face should have appeared.

"Sirius?"

Nothing answered.

"Remus?"

Static clouded the glass. The curse that had felled the tree had disrupted more than wood, and smoke and struggling bodies had swallowed every familiar landmark. Beyond the fallen trunk, the main path continued left into a crush of people. A smaller route curved right towards the stream; for the moment, it remained almost empty.

"The third meeting point," Hermione said, breathing hard. "Eastern water station."

"Right path," Harry agreed.

They slipped away from the crowd. Darkness deepened beneath the trees, and Harry lit his wand, with Hermione following suit. Draconia kept hers dark and watched the path behind them.

"Your father is with Remus," Harry told Hermione.

"You don't know."

"No. But he was on the other side of the tree with him."

"The curse—"

"Remus would shield them before himself."

Hermione's mouth tightened.

"I know."

She did not sound convinced. Harry could offer nothing that would make them certain, so he reached for her hand instead. She took it, her fingers cold despite the heat drifting through the trees.

The stream appeared ahead, catching scattered reflections from the burning campsite. Enchanted boats still floated along it, but the written predictions had washed away, leaving blank leaves to collect against the bank. Beyond them stood three barrels beneath a sign that glowed EAST. The water station was deserted.

"We're first," Harry said.

Draconia examined the clearing's edge.

"Or they cannot reach it."

Hermione released his hand to try the mirror again. The same restless static filled the glass.

A branch snapped beyond the clearing. All three raised their wands, and Cedric emerged from the trees with a girl held against his chest. Blood covered one side of his face.

"Harry?"

He faltered at the sight of them, as though reaching someone familiar had undone the effort keeping him upright. The girl wore Hufflepuff colours and looked about eleven; her eyes were closed, and one leg lay at an awkward angle. Cedric lowered her carefully beside the nearest barrel while Hermione knelt at her side.

"What happened?"

"Stampede. She fell." He wiped blood from his eyebrow. "I think her leg's broken."

Hermione bent to check the girl's breathing.

"She needs a healer."

"The main path is blocked."

Green flashes flickered between the trees in the direction of the camp. Harry looked from them to the motionless girl.

"We get everyone here," he said. "Then signal."

He sent red sparks upwards, followed by gold. They rose barely twenty feet before striking an invisible barrier and scattering across it. Cedric swore beneath his breath.

"Anti-signal charm."

Draconia's gaze moved through the trees.

"Someone does not want Aurors finding this clearing."

The words had scarcely left her when masked figures entered from two sides. There were three: a wizard in blue-trimmed robes, another who limped, and a third who kept his left hand behind his back.

"Children," the limping man said, with unmistakable amusement.

Cedric stepped in front of the injured girl, and Harry moved beside him. Hermione and Draconia spread apart, forcing the newcomers to divide their attention.

"No adults?" the wizard in blue asked.

"We sent them away," Harry replied. "They were slowing us down."

The man laughed.

"Potter."

Recognition passed between the three masks. Harry's scar remained quiet, and its silence frightened him more than he had expected.

The wizard on the right fired first.

"Protego!"

Blue light shattered across Harry's shield. Beside him, Cedric sent a Stunning Spell towards the limping attacker, and Draconia altered it halfway across the clearing. Red became silver; the spell struck the man's mask and tore it from his face.

Harry recognised him at once. He had been standing in the campsite registration line, wearing Ministry robes and checking people through. Hermione recognised him too.

"You checked the wards."

The man touched his exposed face.

"Those wards checked what I allowed them to see."

He aimed at her, but Draconia struck his wrist before he could cast. Then the third wizard entered the fight, and the clearing became a far more dangerous place.

He shouted no incantations and wasted no movement. His wand traced small, precise gestures, sending curses low over the ground and high through the branches, forcing Harry to defend in two directions at once. Harry ducked the first. The second cut his cheek, leaving a line of heat beneath his eye.

The wizard raised his left hand. His glove had torn, and wandlight caught the black stone of a ring: a serpent laid across an eight-pointed star.

"Nott."

The masked head tilted. Draconia went still, and Nott turned his wand towards her.

"Your father should have accepted his warning."

Harry stepped between them.

"He didn't understand the warning."

"He understood enough."

Harry drew a shield as Nott's wand moved. The impact drove him backwards, his shoes carving through the mud, until Cedric caught his shoulder and steadied him.

"He's mine," Nott said.

"Awful manners," Harry replied. "You haven't introduced yourself."

Nott attacked. Harry had duelled teachers, a memory, and friends who stopped before a spell could do lasting harm. Nott held nothing back. His first curse became black flame against Harry's shield, creeping over its surface and feeding on the magic that sustained it. Harry released the shield before the fire could reach his wand.

"Expelliarmus!"

Nott turned the spell aside.

"Confringo!"

Harry flung himself out of the way as the barrel behind him exploded. Water swept across the clearing in a silver wall. Cedric shielded the injured girl, while Hermione caught the flood and froze it around one attacker's feet. Draconia bound his wrists with ropes that tightened each time he struggled.

The unmasked Ministry wizard Disapparated. The wards should have stopped him; the sharp crack of his departure proved they had not.

"Compromised!" Hermione shouted.

Nott heard her.

"Very good."

"Minister, we have a positive identification of Nott from three witnesses and a possible sighting from Harry before the attack."

"Possible," Fudge repeated at once.

"Lucius can identify the spellwork," Narcissa said.

Fudge kept his attention away from Lucius's exposed arm.

"Spellwork is not a face."

"The signet was visible," Draconia replied.

"A ring can be copied."

"He referenced information only Nott and the diary possessed."

Impatience sharpened Fudge's voice.

"Children under attack are not reliable witnesses to exact phrasing."

Harry stepped forward. Sirius moved with him, and the key beneath his shirt warmed.

"He asked Draconia whether she still woke with Riddle's voice inside her."

Fudge's eyes flicked towards her. She held his gaze.

"That could be common knowledge after the Chamber."

"It was not published," Lucius said.

"Then someone heard."

"You have an answer for everything," Hermione said.

"That is my responsibility."

"No. Your responsibility is finding answers. You're choosing them before you look."

Mr Granger placed a hand on her shoulder and kept it there, standing beside her as Fudge noticed how many people had begun listening.

"Nott will be sought for questioning," the Minister said. "The masked individuals will be prosecuted. The public will be informed that no evidence suggests the return of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named."

Harry's scar pulsed. For an instant, he remembered red eyes taking pleasure in the Mark across all that distance.

"He's alive."

He said it quietly, but Fudge went still.

"Harry," Sirius warned.

"He felt the Mark."

"How could you know?"

"Because I felt him."

Whispers spread through the spectators. Fudge's face lost its colour, and Harry watched him collect himself before answering.

"You were struck during an attack. Confusion is understandable."

"I know the difference between pain in my ribs and pain in my scar."

"Dumbledore examined him," Sirius said. "There is a connection."

"Dumbledore has theories."

"And you have a hat. Only one of those has been useful."

Fudge turned to Kingsley.

"Remove civilians from the site. Obtain statements privately. No speculation is to appear in official reports."

"Witness experiences are not speculation."

"You have your instructions."

Kingsley's face revealed nothing.

"Yes, Minister."

Fudge walked away. His assistants followed, their quills moving again before they reached the path. Harry watched them go.

"He believes me."

"Yes," Sirius said.

"Then why—"

"Because believing you requires action, and action requires admitting the world became dangerous while he was responsible for it."

Lucius drew one blanket higher despite himself.

"Fudge will choose a smaller danger."

"Nott?" Harry asked.

"Nott. Masked vandals. Ministry failures corrected by Ministry review."

"Winky," Draconia said.

Narcissa held the sleeping elf on the opposite chair. Winky had cried until exhaustion overcame her.

"If necessary," Lucius agreed.

Hermione's expression hardened. The war had not begun; Harry understood that before anyone used the word. The argument over whether it existed already had.

Bartemius Crouch returned to his tent alone at three minutes past four in the morning.

He knew the time because he had begun counting shortly after the Dark Mark appeared. Ninety-seven minutes had passed since he found Winky unconscious beneath the trees; ninety-three since Kingsley Shacklebolt sealed his wand inside an evidence case. Eighty-one since Winky ceased belonging to his household, although that number resisted the clean arithmetic of the others.

He had not counted how long his son had been free. That calculation required a beginning he could no longer identify.

The Ministry encampment stood apart from the public tents, separated by a low rope and wards that had once been unobtrusive. Most had collapsed during the attack. Canvas offices leaned in the wind; one had burned down to its enchanted pegs, while another continued producing cups of tea despite having neither a roof nor anyone inside it.

Two Aurors straightened as Crouch approached the entrance.

"Sir."

He inclined his head.

"Do you require an escort?" the older one asked.

"No."

One word would have begun the truth. Yes. Escort me to Shacklebolt. Summon Amelia Bones. Close every international Portkey and Floo connection. Search for a man the Ministry believes died in Azkaban more than a decade ago.

Ask me how I know him. Ask me what I did.

Crouch walked past them.

His tent stood at the end of the row, outwardly identical to the other Ministry shelters: brown canvas, a brass number, mud gathered beneath the entrance. Inside were a narrow office, a washroom and two beds for staff expected to remain overnight. Nothing there could explain the empty seat in the Top Box. He had arranged that deliberately.

He closed the flap, sealed it with his borrowed wand and drew the curtains across both windows. Then he placed his palm against the centre pole.

"Recognise."

The wood warmed. A seam appeared in the floor, passing between the beds and turning at precise right angles to outline a square no wider than a travelling trunk. Crouch knelt, lifted the concealed panel and took out a black leather case.

The combination required fourteen numbers: his wife's birthday, their wedding anniversary, their son's birthday. He entered dates from a life that had once seemed to possess an orderly future, and the locks opened.

Inside lay three empty bottles, folded clothes and a silver instrument resembling a watch without hands. Fine black cracks divided its glass. Crouch lifted it, turning the face towards the light.

It had been made to track a single enchantment. For nearly eleven years, a pale blue point had brightened whenever the Imperius Curse holding his son began to weaken, allowing Crouch to renew it before it could fail. At first, the point remained dim for months at a time. Later, the intervals shortened to weeks. During the past year, it had flared almost every night.

Now the glass was broken, and the face beneath it lay empty. His son had gone beyond more than the instrument's reach. The enchantment itself had been severed.

Crouch sat on the edge of the nearest bed. Percy Weasley's wand remained in his right hand. The young man had surrendered it with such immediate obedience that Crouch had been forced to look away.

His own wand had produced the Dark Mark. Yet a wand recorded magic, never the hand that held it; every hearing he had led reminded him of that distinction. Nott could have taken it. Winky might have lost it during the attack. A masked wizard could have found it beneath the seats.

Harry Potter had seen two sets of footprints leaving the stream.

Crouch closed his eyes. He remembered his son at nineteen, white-faced in the centre of the courtroom as chains fastened him to the chair. The boy had screamed his innocence towards the gallery, the judges, and finally the father who refused to look at him. Those cries had followed Crouch home, where his wife heard them too.

She was already dying. Illness had thinned her hair and hollowed the hands that caught his sleeve when she asked for the impossible. She did not argue that their son was innocent. In the privacy of their bedroom, they had abandoned lies too small to shelter either of them. She asked only that he should not die alone.

Crouch arranged the exchange during a final visit to Azkaban. Polyjuice Potion gave his wife their son's face and his son hers. The Dementors sensed despair rather than identity. A dying woman remained behind the bars, and a convicted Death Eater left wearing his mother's appearance.

She had lived long enough to touch their son's face again. She died believing love had saved him, and Crouch spent the following years discovering how far survival could fall short of salvation.

The Imperius Curse kept the boy indoors. Winky fed him, washed him and spoke to him when Crouch could not endure entering the room. Newspapers were forbidden, visitors remained downstairs, and every window carried wards. To the neighbours, the house was simply silent. Crouch imposed an order that made the prison his own, and told himself it was mercy.

The broken instrument cut his palm. Blood gathered along the edge of the glass just as a soft knock sounded against the tent pole. He stood so quickly that the bedframe struck the wall.

"Who is it?"

"Percy Weasley, sir."

Of course.

He closed the case and returned it beneath the floor. The panel sealed without leaving a line. He straightened the blanket, cleared the blood from his hand and adjusted his cuffs before removing the wards.

Percy entered with a stack of damp parchment pressed against his chest. The night appeared to have aged him several years, yet he apologised for the inconvenience.

"The preliminary incident reports, sir. Mr Fudge requests your assessment before the international delegations depart."

Crouch accepted them. The first page described an outbreak of disorder caused by unidentified masked individuals. The second called the Mark an imitative display intended to provoke alarm. Harry Potter's testimony occupied an appendix marked UNVERIFIED — POSSIBLE SCAR-RELATED DISTRESS. None of the reports mentioned a second invisible person.

The Ministry had produced a lie before Crouch finished deciding whether to tell one.

"Shall I inform Mr Shacklebolt that you are ready to make a statement regarding your wand?" Percy asked.

Crouch looked down at the report and imagined the hearing. Public opinion would open his wife's grave and turn her final act into conspiracy. Every conviction he had secured would be examined for corruption, his name stripped from doors and legislation. Aurors would hunt his son across Britain, approaching him as a murderer and creating the very battle they expected.

He could find the boy first. He knew his habits, the shape of his anger, and the weakness left by years without proper exercise. He knew the old circle's safe houses, the names Nott might still trust, the routes a fugitive would take out of the country.

One day. Give him one day to correct what belonged to him.

"No," he said.

Percy waited as Crouch signed the first report.

"Mr Shacklebolt has an active crime scene. He does not require speculation from a man whose property was stolen."

"Of course, sir."

"You will make no reference to the invisible footprints until the evidence has been reviewed."

Percy hesitated, drawing a breath half a second later than he should have. Crouch looked up, and the young man's expression settled into obedience.

"Yes, sir."

He left. The tent flap fell closed behind him, and Crouch remained beside the desk while celebration smoke mingled with the remnants of the Dark Mark beyond the window.

He had built his career on the certainty that private loyalties became public dangers when men placed them above the law. Then his wife had asked him to save their son.

There had been no certainty since.

Three miles beyond the final Ministry ward, a church stood without a village.

Its roof had fallen inward years ago. Ivy covered the eastern wall, leaving the stone faces of two weathered saints exposed to the rain. Water dripped through broken rafters and gathered in the aisle where pews had once stood. The altar remained, split through its centre and white with bird droppings.

Apparition released Barty Crouch Junior in front of it, Nott's hand still locked around his arm. His knees struck stone. He caught the altar's broken edge with one hand and pressed the other to his stomach, retching until the effort became a dry, shaking cough.

Nott waited.

"That," Barty managed, "was unpleasant."

His voice had been used too little; every word scraped.

"It was Apparition."

"I remember."

"Then your surprise is theatrical."

Barty laughed. The sound travelled through the church and disturbed something winged in the rafters, sending it out into the rain. Nott released his arm.

He stayed bent over the altar until the room stopped pitching around him. His father's darkened windows had shown him enough of himself to know what captivity had left: a thirty-one-year-old body starved into uncertain age, wrists too narrow for his hands, hair knotted beyond his shoulders, and skin grey from years without daylight.

His attention had survived intact. He mapped the broken windows, both exits and the angle of Nott's wand before allowing himself to notice the bundle beneath the altar.

"Clothes," Nott said. "Food. Strengthening potion. Nothing marked by either family."

Barty reached towards the folded robes, but his hand shook. He withdrew it, disguising the movement by touching the crack in the altar.

"My father?"

"Alive."

"Winky?"

"Also alive. Dismissed, unless Crouch reconsidered after we left."

Pain moved through Barty before he could prevent it.

"He dismissed her?"

"She was found with his wand beneath the Mark."

"She did not cast it."

"No."

Rain clicked against the loose fragments of stained glass in the western window. Barty examined his empty hand.

"I wanted to see whether I still could."

"You nearly announced yourself to half the magical population of Europe."

"I announced him."

"Your master does not yet possess a body capable of enjoying the tribute."

Barty turned. The frailty left his posture so completely that Nott raised his wand.

"Do not speak of him as though he is less than he was."

"Do not mistake worship for usefulness."

They regarded each other across the ruined altar. Barty saw a man who had needed thirteen years of fear to remember his loyalties. He could imagine Nott's judgement in return: a fanatic who could barely stand, too hungry to trust and too useful to discard. Neither assessment was inaccurate.

"Where is he?" Barty asked.

"Safe."

"I asked where."

"And I answered as much as your condition earns."

Barty smiled without warmth.

"You came into my father's house twice before tonight."

Nott's wand remained steady.

"Did I?"

"The curse did not make me deaf. He believed it did. Winky hoped it did." Barty's tongue touched the corner of his mouth, a nervous movement adulthood had never altered. "You stood outside the room in February. You asked whether I could be controlled. In May, you asked how much I remembered."

"And how much do you remember?"

"Everything."

He meant it without boasting. The courtroom, Azkaban, his mother becoming him and dying with his face: each memory remained clear. His father's curse had entered his mind night after night while Winky pleaded with him to eat, sleep, remain quiet, and be grateful for a life made of locked rooms and commands.

He remembered the World Cup through gauze and invisibility, the roar when Ireland scored, Winky's fingers around his wrist. His father's wand had protruded from a pocket, and Barty remembered taking it. For one minute beneath the trees, no order occupied his head. The Dark Mark rose when he called, beautiful enough to hurt.

"Potter saw us leave," Nott said.

Barty kept smiling.

"Potter saw footprints."

"He saw you move towards the east path."

"Then he turned away."

"To save a child."

"Yes." Fascination softened Barty's voice. "He does that."

Nott's mouth tightened. Barty could not tell whether the answer troubled him or whether he disliked the reverence Barty made no effort to hide.

"Potter is not to be approached until preparations are complete."

"You believe I need that instruction?"

"You conjured the Dark Mark above his head."

"I was celebrating."

"Try to contain your happiness."

Barty laughed again and took the strengthening potion from the bundle. The cork resisted his fingers. Nott opened it with a flick of his wand and sent it across the space between them; Barty caught it, barely. Humiliation burned hotter than the effort as he swallowed the potion in one draught.

Colour did not return immediately, but the tremor eased. He lowered himself onto the altar step with more control.

"What preparations?"

Nott conjured a small blue flame inside the hollow base of a fallen statue. It gave neither smoke nor much comfort.

"Hogwarts requires a new Defence professor."

"It usually does."

"Dumbledore has approached a man he trusts."

"Dumbledore trusts very few men."

"This one mistakes being difficult to like for being difficult to deceive."

Barty tilted his head.

"You intend to kill him?"

"No. A corpse cannot provide hair."

Understanding came slowly, then all at once. Barty looked down at his starved hands.

"You need me to wear his face."

"I need someone inside the castle who can deceive the Goblet, guide Potter through three tasks and deliver him alive to the final one. Your master believes that someone should be you."

The ruined church seemed to become still around him. For thirteen years, Barty had inhabited rooms chosen by others: the cell in Azkaban, his mother's dying body, the locked bedroom in his father's house. Even the Top Box had been another prison, filled with people unable to see him and an elf ordered to keep him in his seat.

Now a door stood before him. It led to Hogwarts, to Harry Potter, and to the master who had called him faithful when his own father called him dead.

Barty set the empty bottle on the stone.

"When do we take him?"

"After you can cross a room without falling."

"Then I suggest you find me another bottle."

Nott reached inside his robes and laid a wand on the altar. Barty's old wand remained sealed in an evidence vault under a dead man's name. This one was dark, unpolished and a little too long for his hand.

He touched it. The nearest blue flame bent towards him, and Nott went very still. Perhaps he understood at last why their master valued a ruined man above servants who had preserved their fortunes, families and freedom.

Captivity had taught Barty Crouch Junior hunger.

The survivors left the World Cup grounds by emergency Portkey before dawn. With the campsite too badly damaged for ordinary departures, Ministry workers gathered families into assigned circles and gave each group an object connected to a secure destination.

The Malfoys could have returned to the Manor, but Narcissa refused.

"Nott knows our wards," she said. "He entered once."

Harry heard what the admission cost her. Sirius offered the cottage before Lucius could ask.

"One night," Lucius said.

"Until the security is checked."

"One night."

"You can sleep outside after that."

"Preferable."

The Grangers came too. Their house had no magical protection capable of stopping people who had compromised Ministry wards, so Mr Granger telephoned a neighbour and explained that a gas leak had delayed their return.

Their Portkey was a broken broom. At 4:17, everyone reached for it, and the familiar hook caught Harry behind the navel. The campsite disappeared in a wash of green light. He landed in the cottage orchard hard enough to reopen the cut beneath his eye, but this time no one laughed.

The wards answered their arrival in a silver wave across the grass, counting, examining and admitting each person. Winky woke in Narcissa's arms and looked towards the cottage.

"Where is—"

Crouch's old binding tightened around her throat despite her dismissal. Draconia caught her hands before she could turn them against herself.

"You do not have to speak."

Winky wept without sound.

Inside, the house expanded to receive them. Rooms opened where walls had stood, beds unfolded, and the kitchen table lengthened. The cottage made room for fear, blood and too many people with the same quiet readiness with which it had welcomed Harry.

Sirius directed everyone with the fierce concentration of someone delaying his own collapse. Healers arrived from St Mungo's through the gate. They reopened Lucius's wound, cleaned it and sealed it properly, then treated the Grangers' bruises. Hermione's palms had been cut by the bark when she tried to climb the fallen tree. Draconia's shoulder required a sling, which she removed the moment Narcissa turned away.

Harry received three healing charms and a potion for his ribs. It tasted of metal, and he had barely swallowed when the Healer held out another dose.

"Again."

"I drank it."

"Second dose."

"Why?"

"The first one repaired damage. The second prevents inflammation."

Harry looked to Sirius in silent appeal.

"Excess," his godfather said.

Harry drank.

Cedric's Patronus arrived shortly after sunrise. A silver Labrador entered the sitting room and announced in his voice that Eleanor had reached St Mungo's, was breathing independently and was expected to recover. Then the dog turned towards Harry.

"The Healer said the airway charm saved her life."

It dissolved, leaving everyone looking at him. Harry examined his empty potion cup.

"I guessed."

Hermione sat beside him.

"Correctly."

"Madam Pomfrey is still going to kill me."

"Probably."

Draconia lowered herself onto his other side.

"We will provide character evidence."

"That sounds threatening."

"It is."

Harry leaned back, his shoulders touching theirs, and for a while none of them spoke. Silence between the three of them rarely lasted long. Hermione usually filled it with a fact she had been waiting to share; Draconia supplied an observation sharp enough to demand an answer. Harry specialised in saying the wrong thing at exactly the right moment and pretending the timing was deliberate.

This silence needed no repair.

Eventually, Hermione took the World Cup programme from her coat. Rain had blurred half the gold lettering. Mud covered Krum's left boot, a corner had burned away, and a dark handprint marked the page where she had recorded the final score. She smoothed it across her knees.

"One hundred and seventy to one hundred and sixty," Harry said.

"Yes."

"You intend to be unbearable about that for the rest of the year."

"I made a sound prediction based upon each team's scoring pattern."

"Unbearable," Draconia confirmed.

"You both mocked the chart."

"The chart required its own seat," Harry said.

"And it was correct."

Her indignation was tired but familiar. Harry felt something inside him loosen at the sound of it.

Draconia reached into her pocket and brought out the remains of the Ireland rosette that had attached itself to his sleeve during the celebration. Most of the green ribbon had torn away, leaving a bent silver shamrock and its clasp.

"I found this beside the water station."

Harry accepted it.

"You stopped to collect my rosette?"

"I stepped on it while avoiding a curse."

"Less touching."

"Accuracy often is."

He turned the clasp between his fingers. Blood had dried in one groove; he cleaned it against his sleeve and closed his hand around it.

"The match still happened," Hermione said quietly.

Harry looked towards the birthday photograph above the mantel. In its garden, Hermione reached for the paper bird caught in his hair while Draconia leaned towards him, laughing. Sirius hovered behind them, Remus smiled, and their families gathered around Hagrid. The small accident kept happening, bringing fresh laughter each time.

"I know."

He did know, and the certainty surprised him. The attack had come afterwards. It had frightened them, hurt people and stained the memories that followed, but it could not travel backwards and take the earlier hours. The stadium had opened beneath the night sky. Sirius's hand had gripped his when the players flew out. Hermione's impossible prediction had come true, filling her face with astonished delight.

Voldemort had taken enough from people without being handed those good hours as well.

Harry pinned the bent shamrock to the front of his jumper. Draconia studied it.

"That is crooked."

"I've had a difficult evening."

She reached across to straighten it, and pain flickered over her face before she hid it. Harry caught her wrist. Her pulse beat fast and uneven beneath his fingers; her eyes lowered to his hand, then lifted to his face. He loosened his grip, though neither of them moved apart.

"Sling."

"No."

"That sounded like an opinion. The Healer gave an instruction."

"You disregarded at least four tonight."

"I'm trying to grow."

Hermione retrieved the abandoned sling from beneath Draconia's chair.

"Traitor," Draconia told her.

"Obviously."

Between them, Harry and Hermione settled her arm back into it. Draconia complained throughout and permitted every second. When they finished, Hermione stayed close enough to rest her head briefly against Harry's uninjured shoulder. Draconia's knee remained pressed to his. Exhaustion had worn away their usual efforts to disguise either choice.

Draconia's free hand lay on the sofa between them. Harry's smallest finger brushed hers. She turned her hand a fraction, making the contact deliberate, and continued arguing with Hermione as though nothing had happened.

Harry sat between them with his scar quiet, his ribs aching and a ridiculous silver shamrock pinned above his heart. He could not quite feel safe yet.

He felt found.

The room remained crowded. Narcissa sat with Winky near the fire. Lucius slept beneath the influence of a pain potion, one hand still closed around his wand. The Grangers spoke quietly at the table, and Remus reinforced the windows.

At the front door, Sirius checked the locks. Harry watched him work through all six, pausing at each to make certain it had caught. Then he began again.

"Sirius."

His godfather did not hear. Harry stood, wincing as his ribs tightened, and crossed the room.

"Where are you?"

Sirius's hand stayed on the final lock.

"Home."

"What can you feel?"

He glanced down at the brass key hanging from Harry's neck.

"The door."

Harry laid his hand over Sirius's.

"What do you know?"

His godfather turned. His eyes were red.

"They got inside the Ministry wards."

"Not these."

"Nott escaped."

"We came back."

"I lost you."

"You found me."

Sirius's face twisted, and Harry stepped into him before embarrassment could keep either of them still. His godfather held him carefully around the ribs.

"Next Cup," Harry said.

After everything that had happened, the promise sounded foolish. He needed to say it anyway, and Sirius understood.

"Next Cup."

Behind them, the photograph on the mantel continued moving. No masks entered its garden, and no Mark crossed its sky.

The people inside kept laughing.
