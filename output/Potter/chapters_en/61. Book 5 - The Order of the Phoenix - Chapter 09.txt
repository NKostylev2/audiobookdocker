Dust fell from the doorframe as a spell struck the ward outside. Umbridge turned sharply towards the sound. Harry stayed back from the desk, his left hand closed around the mirror and his wand lowered in his right. The gold light still clung to the glass. It had thrown Umbridge's hand back, but it no longer spread through the room. He could feel the charm tugging against his palm, holding the glass where he had chosen to keep it. He kept the mirror low so he would not strain his healing shoulder, tightened his grip and took one more step from the desk.

The alarm continued somewhere beyond the office. It was not a bell so much as a sustained, furious note which seemed to pass through the stone rather than the air. Every charm in the room trembled in answer. The lace covering Umbridge's desk lifted at the edges. One of the broken kitten plates gave a last miserable crack and shed a porcelain ear upon the carpet.

"Stop that right now," Umbridge said.

Harry looked at the silver lines sealing the door.

"You first."

Another blow hit the ward. Blue light flared around the frame. For a moment the silver containment lines held. Then Professor McGonagall's voice cut through the wood, sharpened by a Sonorous Charm and anger Harry had never heard her attempt to disguise.

"Dolores, remove this ward immediately."

Umbridge's hand tightened around her wand.

"The student is distressed and armed."

"The student is bleeding," Remus said from the mirror.

Umbridge glanced down. The words on Harry's hand had darkened. Blood gathered at the centre of the letter m, a bright bead swelling until it slipped over his knuckle. Harry watched it fall onto the carpet between them. Some detached part of him wondered whether Umbridge would charge him for the stain.

"I asked him to complete a sentence," she said. "Mr Potter chose to interrupt the process before the quill could settle."

"That isn't how blood quills work," Remus replied.

"You possess no authority here, Mr Lupin."

"I possess enough education to recognise blood magic."

The office door shuddered a third time. This impact carried a different spell. The sound dropped away, swallowed so completely that Harry felt it in his teeth rather than heard it. Black lines spread across the silver ward like cracks through ice. Harry could not see who had cast it. He heard Snape's voice beyond the door a moment later, instructing the others to stand clear. Umbridge crossed the room quickly.

"Mr Potter, surrender your wand and sit down."

"No."

"Your guardians cannot protect you from the consequences of threatening a member of staff."

Sirius's face had gone very still inside the mirror.

"He hasn't threatened you," he said. "And I would be careful about testing the second half of that sentence."

"He drew his wand."

"After you locked the door," Harry said.

"Because you attempted to abandon an authorised detention."

"After you used dark magic without telling me what it was."

"An inflammatory characterisation from a child with a documented history of—"

The door came off its hinges. Snape's spell held it clear of Harry rather than sending it flying across the room. The wood separated from the frame in one clean movement, remained upright for half a second, and then fell flat across the carpet with a tremendous crash. McGonagall stood beyond it, wand raised and a tartan shawl slipping from one shoulder of her teaching robes. Snape occupied the space beside her in black from throat to boots, his face the colour of old parchment. Professor Dumbledore stood one step behind them. Hermione and Draconia were there too. McGonagall took in the broken plates, Harry's wand, Umbridge's position and the dark feather lying beside the unfinished sentence. Then she saw his hand.

Her expression did not change. That was worse.

"Mr Potter," she said, each word precise enough to cut glass, "step into the corridor."

Umbridge raised her wand across the space between them.

"He will remain where he is."

McGonagall looked at her.

"I was not speaking to you."

Harry moved. The instant his shoe crossed the broken threshold, Hermione reached for his left wrist, then checked herself when she saw the mirror. She walked beside him instead, keeping close without pulling at his strapped shoulder. With McGonagall between him and Umbridge, Harry returned his wand to its holster. Draconia came around his other side. Her gaze dropped to the back of his right hand. She stopped breathing.

The change in her was so swift that Harry almost missed it. Concern vanished beneath something blank and cold. Her fingers closed around his forearm, well below the wound. They were cold, and she seemed unable to loosen them until Harry turned his hand slightly towards hers.

"I stopped after two words," Harry said. "I put it down as soon as I knew something was wrong."

Hermione stared at him.

"Harry, you're bleeding."

"I know. I stopped."

"I know you did. Just don't tell me it doesn't matter."

Draconia still had not spoken. Behind them, Dumbledore entered the office. He did not look at Umbridge first. His attention went to the quill.

"Nobody touch anything," he said.

The instruction carried no force beyond his voice. It did not need to. Snape stepped over the fallen door and swept his wand above the desk. A clear dome formed around the parchment, quill and pink blotter. Within it, red ink reappeared across the unfinished line although no hand held the feather.

I must.

The letters shone wetly.

"A common blood-reversal charm," Umbridge said. "Its presence proves nothing."

"On the contrary," Snape replied. "It proves that the writing medium contains material taken from the writer. The pattern matches the incisions on Potter's hand. We will preserve both for independent comparison."

"Keep the original sealed, Severus," Dumbledore said. "The examiners can use a duplicate for their first comparison."

Snape inclined his head and tightened the clear dome around the desk.

McGonagall's eyes remained on Umbridge. "You will explain why a student was confined with a blood instrument."

"I have explained. The quill is an approved corrective device issued under Ministry authority. Mr Potter refused a lawful instruction, attempted to leave detention, drew his wand and established an illicit communication outside the school. I activated a temporary safeguarding ward to prevent him from acting in distress."

"He asked what the quill did," Remus said from Harry's hand.

Everyone looked at the mirror. Harry had almost forgotten he still held it. Sirius and Remus remained visible beyond the gold threads, waiting beside the visitors' Floo within Hogwarts. Sirius was speaking to someone outside the frame, his voice clipped with the effort of remaining still. They had reached the school; the alarm had brought the staff to Harry before they could reach the office.

"We heard the entire detention," Remus continued. "Harry asked for the nature of the magic and its cost. Professor Umbridge refused to tell him. He wrote two words, experienced pain, put the quill down and said he was going to Professor McGonagall. The door locked after he attempted to leave."

"A private mirror can be enchanted to produce any voice its owner desires."

Hermione made an incredulous sound.

Umbridge smiled at her. "Miss Granger, you are not a participant in this inquiry."

"I checked the mirror before Harry went inside. The connection was live."

"Then you assisted him in breaching school communications policy."

"Professor McGonagall approved the open connection," Hermione said. "Harry checked with her after dinner. We checked it again before he went inside. Ask her."

Her voice shook on the last word. She appeared furious with it for doing so. Umbridge opened her mouth. Draconia spoke first.

"She knew."

Harry turned towards her. Draconia was looking at the desk. At the sentence. Her face remained frighteningly empty, but Harry felt the tremor in the hand holding him.

"She knew the mirror would raise the guardian alarm if she tried to confiscate it," Draconia said. "Harry warned her. She tried to take it anyway. Now she's saying the alarm proves he was out of control."

Umbridge's smile thinned.

"You have inherited your father's talent for inventing conspiracy where ordinary authority will do, Miss Malfoy."

Draconia released Harry and took a step towards the office. He touched the back of her sleeve with the tips of his right fingers, keeping the marked skin clear of the fabric. "Draco." She stopped at the threshold.

She looked over one shoulder.

"Not while she has a wand," he said quietly.

"I also have a wand."

"That is exactly what worries me."

Something moved beneath the blankness in her face. Not amusement. Not quite. Enough that she returned to Harry's side. He let go of her sleeve as soon as she moved. Dumbledore turned to Umbridge.

"The office will remain sealed until an independent examination is complete. You will surrender the quill, the parchment, the ward record and your wand for Prior Incantato."

"I will do no such thing."

"Then I must suspend you from contact with students pending investigation."

"You lack the authority."

"Within Hogwarts—"

"Within Hogwarts," Umbridge interrupted, "Educational Decree Number Twenty-Four confirms my appointment as High Inquisitor and grants that office final discretion in matters of student discipline where existing staff have failed to maintain order. The Minister anticipated your resistance."

She produced a folded square of parchment from her robes. It expanded in her hand, the Ministry seal already burning at its foot. Harry knew that seal. He had seen it upon every statement denying the name of the man who had held a wand against his throat. McGonagall read over Dumbledore's shoulder. Her lips compressed.

"This authorises disciplinary review," she said. "It does not authorise blood magic."

"The Commissioner's office classified the quill as a low-risk corrective instrument in nineteen eighty-six. Temporary dermal inscription does not meet the Ministry threshold for injury unless scarring persists beyond forty-eight hours."

Hermione's grip tightened around Harry's wrist. The wording was too polished to be improvised. Umbridge had known exactly what she could do before Harry entered the room. Perhaps before she had assigned the detention. Somewhere in the Ministry, someone had written a definition of injury narrow enough to place blood outside it.

Harry looked down at the words carved beneath his skin. He had blood on his hand, a recording of her instructions and adults who had seen the door refuse to open. He had thought those things would settle it. Umbridge spoke as if the only important question was how long the cut would last.

"How fortunate," Sirius said from the mirror, "that my solicitor has never been impressed by Ministry thresholds."

Dumbledore spoke towards the mirror. "Sirius, remain at the visitors' grate. I am opening a connection to this office." He touched his wand to Umbridge's fireplace. Green flame climbed nearly to the mantel as the school-side ward admitted the internal transfer. Umbridge wheeled towards it. Sirius Black stepped out of the fire before she could object.

He wore no cloak. Haste had disordered his hair, and one sleeve of his jumper was pushed to the elbow as though he had dressed while running. Remus emerged behind him carrying a narrow leather case and looking pale with fatigue. He had brought his stick for the long corridors; on better days Harry hardly saw him use it now. Umbridge backed away. It was small. Barely one step. Harry saw it. So did Sirius.

"Mr Black," she said. "This is an unauthorised intrusion into a staff office."

Sirius brushed soot from his shoulder.

"No. This is a lawful guardian answering a magical distress summons you activated with your own hand."

His voice was calm. That frightened Harry more than shouting would have done. Sirius crossed the room. He passed Umbridge without looking at her, stepped over the fallen door and stopped in front of Harry.

For a second he said nothing. His eyes travelled from Harry's face to the mirror, his closed wand holster and, last of all, the blood gathering along the letters. Harry expected anger. He expected the sharp questions Sirius used when terror had dressed itself in fury and needed somewhere to go. Instead Sirius took a handkerchief from his pocket and folded it around Harry's hand without touching the wound.

"You called," he said.

Harry swallowed. "I said I would."

"You did."

There was too much inside those two words. Sirius looked up. His eyes were bright, but his hand remained steady around Harry's wrist.

"Good," he said. "That was exactly right."

The cupboard door inside Harry's head loosened by one impossible inch. He had left. He had called. Someone had come. It had not prevented the first cut. It had stopped him being made to finish the sentence, or sit there writing another.

Harry had not realised until then how afraid he had been that it would not count. Remus reached them. He did not immediately examine the wound. He touched two fingers to Harry's uninjured shoulder first, light enough that Harry could move away if he wanted.

"May I see?"

Harry nodded. Only then did Remus lift the handkerchief.

"Two words," Harry said again.

"Two too many," Sirius replied.

"I stopped."

"I know."

"She locked—"

"We heard."

"I drew my wand, but I didn't cast anything."

"Harry." Sirius waited until he met his eyes. "You do not have to make yourself innocent enough to deserve being protected."

The corridor seemed to narrow around them. Harry looked away first. At the edge of his vision, Draconia closed her eyes.

Umbridge's office became a crime scene, although the Ministry refused to call it one. Dumbledore sealed the doorway in silver fire. Snape preserved the magical residue upon the desk, the carpet and Harry's hand. McGonagall sent Hermione running for Madam Pomfrey and Draconia for Professor Flitwick, then appeared surprised when neither girl moved.

"We are not leaving him," Hermione said.

"Mr Potter's guardian is beside him, Miss Granger. Professor Lupin and the rest of us are here as well."

"One professor did this."

McGonagall's face altered.

"Yes," she said after a moment. "She did."

She summoned a silver cat instead and sent it down the corridor with instructions for both professors. Its bright tail vanished around the corner while McGonagall remained between the children and the office. Professor Flitwick arrived before Madam Pomfrey, wearing soft indoor shoes and carrying a half-marked essay he tucked into his pocket without looking at it. The little professor examined the containment ward without crossing the threshold, moving his wand through the air in minute circles while blue symbols formed around its tip.

"Interesting," he said, which in Flitwick's voice usually meant something was about to lose an argument. "The ward records distress as its justification. Yet it activated before the guardian alarm, and before Mr Potter drew his wand. There is no record of a spell cast by him. Neither his wand nor the mirror triggered the containment."

Umbridge's smile became rigid. "The ward anticipated escalation."

"He hadn't done anything yet," Hermione said.

"It decided leaving meant danger," Harry said. "That was all I tried to do."

Flitwick glanced up at him, then returned to the blue symbols. "That is precisely what the record shows."

"Quite. A containment charm responds to conditions placed within it. This one was instructed to interpret movement towards the exit as danger. It did precisely what it was designed to do."

"The student was armed."

"Every student in this castle is armed," McGonagall said. "That is the school's principal method of education."

Madam Pomfrey came around the corner at such speed that her apron streamed behind her. She carried a medical case in one hand and a small camera in the other.

"Which child?"

Harry raised his hand. Her mouth disappeared into a line.

"Hospital wing. Now."

"Poppy," Dumbledore said, "we need the mark preserved before treatment."

"Then you may preserve it while walking."

"There is also a question of—"

"If the question is whether I intend to leave a bleeding child in a corridor while adults discuss jurisdiction, the answer is no."

She turned on Harry. "Move."

Harry moved. Sirius came with him. Remus collected the mirror and leather case. Hermione and Draconia fell into step without asking. McGonagall joined them after informing Umbridge that any attempt to leave the sealed corridor would be treated as interference with evidence. Umbridge called after Harry before they reached the stairs.

"Your detention remains incomplete, Mr Potter."

Everyone stopped. Harry turned. Dolores Umbridge stood behind the fallen door, surrounded by broken smiles painted upon porcelain.

"Then I suppose," Harry said, "your quill will have to learn to write by itself."

He continued walking before Sirius could laugh. Sirius failed anyway.

Madam Pomfrey did not take Harry into the main ward. She led them instead to the examination room beside her office, a narrow space with white curtains, two hard-backed chairs and a bed whose sheets had been tucked so tightly that sitting upon it felt like vandalism. Cabinets of pale wood covered one wall. The air smelled of dittany, clean linen and the bitter silver solution used to sterilise instruments touched by curses. Harry sat upon the edge of the bed.

Sirius took the chair beside him. Hermione and Draconia occupied the other together through a combination of determination and disregard for its width. Remus remained near the open door until Madam Pomfrey noticed how much of his weight rested upon his stick. She conjured a stool beside the cabinet and pointed at it.

"Sit down, Lupin. The case may hover. You may not."

McGonagall stayed outside to meet the Ministry representatives already travelling through the Floo network. They arrived very quickly when the accused person was one of their own. Madam Pomfrey set Harry's hand upon a square of black cloth. Under the examination lamps, the cut appeared worse. The letters had not merely marked the surface. Each narrow line sank through several layers of skin, edges swollen and red. Blood had dried in the creases between his knuckles. Draconia's knee pressed against his.

"This will sting," Madam Pomfrey said.

"Everything that heals seems to."

"That is because you only visit me after attempting something ridiculous."

"I attended a detention."

"Precisely."

She cleaned the blood away with a silver-soaked pad. Harry breathed through his nose and watched the ceiling. A faint crack ran across the plaster above the lamp. Harry followed it with his eyes until the sharpest part of the sting passed. Remus opened his leather case. Inside lay stoppered phials, evidence labels, a charmed measuring rule and a stack of parchment bearing the Black family solicitor's crest. Harry recognised none of it as belonging to Remus.

"When did you collect that?" he asked.

"Before we left Hawthorn," Remus said. "When we heard you asking about the pain. It was beside the fireplace."

"I asked our solicitor for it after the train," Sirius added. "It's been in the front hall since Saturday."

Harry stared at him.

"Why?"

"Because you are my godson."

"That isn't an explanation."

"It explains an alarming number of things."

Madam Pomfrey asked Harry to turn his hand beneath the lamp. He hesitated before letting her see the words again. In the corridor he had wanted everyone to notice them; here, with Sirius sitting close enough to see the tiny movements of his fingers, he wanted to put his hand away.

"I don't like looking at it," he admitted.

"Then look at me," Sirius said.

Harry did. Sirius had soot along his jaw and a small red scrape upon his thumb, probably from the motorbike. Ordinary marks. Harry kept his eyes there while Madam Pomfrey took three photographs of his hand. Each photograph developed at once, first grey, then colour. The letters showed clearly under the examination lamp. Pomfrey checked each image before placing it face down, sparing Harry another look. She measured the depth of every cut. Snape's preserved sample of blood arrived in a glass sphere carried by a paper bird. Remus labelled it without touching the magic. Hermione recorded every step. At first Harry assumed she did it because documentation made her feel useful. Then he noticed that she had written the date twice and misspelled dermal.

"Hermione."

Her quill stopped.

"What?"

"You don't have to do that."

"Someone does."

"Remus is doing it."

"I am checking."

"You wrote September twice."

Hermione looked down. The error appeared to offend her more than the observation. Her fingers tightened around the quill until ink gathered at its point and dropped onto the page.

"She planned it," Hermione said.

The room went quiet.

"She chose the sentence. She had the ward prepared. She had an answer ready for everything, even how long the mark was allowed to last before it counted." Hermione's breathing had become shallow. "We checked the mirror. We made rules. We stood outside the door, and she still—"

"The rules worked," Harry said.

She looked at him as if he had misunderstood something fundamental.

"Harry, she cut you."

"I stopped before I finished the sentence."

"That doesn't make what she did all right."

"No. But if I hadn't called, it would have been the whole sentence. Then another. She told me the detention wasn't finished even after everyone arrived. She would have kept going."

He looked at the dark feather of writing across his hand.

"The plan didn't make her safe. It got me out."

Hermione's face folded before she could stop it. She put the quill down and covered her mouth with one ink-stained hand. Harry reached for her automatically, forgot which hand he was using and hissed when the wound pulled. Draconia caught his wrist before Madam Pomfrey could.

"You have two hands," she said.

"I usually don't have to think about it."

"Try thinking about it for the next few minutes. You've already frightened us enough."

He gave Hermione the other one, keeping his elbow close so he would not pull at his shoulder. She held it in both of hers and pressed her forehead briefly against his knuckles. Harry felt Sirius look away to give them privacy within a room too small to contain any. Draconia did not look away.

Her gaze remained on the wounded hand she held suspended above the cloth. The cold composure had begun to crack around its edges. Harry could feel the tremor she kept forcing out of her fingers.

"Draco," he said.

"Not yet."

"Not yet what?"

"Give me a minute. If you're nice about it, I really will start crying."

Madam Pomfrey set the probe down. "You may cry, Miss Malfoy. It will not interfere with my examination."

"I am not crying."

"Of course not."

Draconia's eyes flashed. Tears made them brighter.

"Your welfare file," she said. "The reports mentioned the locked rooms at the Dursleys'. I saw a file with your name on her desk. What if she read them?"

"We don't know whether she read that part," Remus said gently.

"She locked him in anyway."

Nobody contradicted her. Harry looked at the examination-room door. It stood open behind Remus; beyond it, he could see the yellow edge of a lamp in Madam Pomfrey's office. He had not considered what Umbridge might have learned from a file written by people who said they meant to help him. The room altered around him. White walls narrowed into darkness; silver cabinet handles became a latch too high for small fingers; the folded cloth beneath his hand smelled suddenly of dust and spiders. He counted the windowpanes. Four across. Three down. Twelve. Draconia's grip changed at once, loosening without leaving.

"Harry."

"I'm here."

"Where?"

The question came in Remus's voice. Harry focused on him.

"Hospital wing. Hogwarts. Monday night."

"Who is touching you?"

"Draco. Hermione."

"May they continue?"

Harry checked rather than answering from habit. Hermione's hands were warm around his left. Draconia's thumb rested against the pulse inside his right wrist, below the cuts.

"Yes."

The cupboard receded. Madam Pomfrey waited until his breathing settled before applying dittany. Green vapour rose from the cuts. Pain flashed hot, then cooled to a steady throb.

"The redness should fade by morning," she said. "The tissue should close within two days, although you may see the letters for a while after that. Permanent magical scarring is unlikely after so little exposure."

"So the Ministry will say it did not count," Harry said.

"The Ministry may say whatever fiction allows it to sleep. My medical report will call it an injury caused by a blood-based curse."

Sirius looked at her. "May I have that in writing?"

"You may have a copy. With your agreement and Harry's, I will also send the clinical findings to the appropriate professional body. His private history will not be part of it."

"Poppy," Remus said warmly, "I have always admired you."

"Admire me from the corner."

A sharp knock struck the door. McGonagall entered without waiting. Her dressing gown had vanished beneath fully buttoned robes, though one side of her hair remained slightly less severe than the other.

"Amelia Bones has arrived," she said. "So have Senior Undersecretary Fawley, two Ministry examiners and a representative from the Commissioner's office."

Sirius stood. Harry did too. Madam Pomfrey held up a hand before he could get off the bed. "Stay seated, Potter. I have not finished."

"You are not attending anything until I have finished."

"They'll decide what happened without me."

"They have the mirror recording and enough witnesses to keep them occupied. You may give your account after I've treated the hand. You are not going back there bleeding because a civil servant finds it convenient."

"She has a point," Remus said.

"She usually does. She enjoys using it."

Madam Pomfrey gave him a look and reached for a fresh dressing. Harry sat very still. Sirius hesitated at the door.

The impulse beneath it was obvious. Take Harry. Put him on the motorbike. Fly to Hawthorn. Place walls, wards and distance between him and anyone carrying Ministry parchment. Harry knew the impulse because part of him wanted to accept it. The castle had been his escape once. Now the corridors held blood on a wall, a voice in the pipes, Dementors at the gates, a spy in a false professor's skin and a pink office where a closed door became a cupboard. He did not want to think of Hogwarts that way. He remembered the first feast, finding the library with the girls, and sleeping in a bed nobody could order him out of. Those memories were still his. They were becoming harder to reach tonight.

Hawthorn had Sirius. A kitchen that stayed warm after midnight. A bedroom whose lock belonged to Harry. Remus at breakfast. No one would call him unstable for sleeping badly. No one would punish his fear by testing it. He could leave. He could let the school keep Umbridge without him. Then he imagined a first-year sitting at that desk tomorrow. Someone without a mirror. Someone who had not been taught that leaving counted as courage.

"I'm not going home," Harry said.

Sirius turned back.

"I didn't ask."

"You were about to."

"I was about to tell you that you can."

"I know."

That was the difference. He had never known before. Harry looked down at the bandage Madam Pomfrey wound around his hand.

"I don't want her deciding where I belong. I want to stay—but I don't want another night like this."

"Her remaining here is not your responsibility."

"No. It's Dumbledore's. And yours. And Bones's. And every adult who gets paid to make this place safe." Harry met Sirius's eyes. "But whether I stay is mine."

Sirius flinched so slightly that nobody except Harry appeared to notice. The words could have become a fight. Once, they would have. Sirius's fear would have turned into command; Harry's fear would have turned into defiance; both of them would have mistaken winning for protection. Sirius crossed the room again. He crouched in front of Harry so they were level.

"Then we set conditions," he said. "No private detention with her. No closed door. The mirror remains on you, and if another member of staff tries to confiscate it, the entire bloody castle can fall down next time."

"Preferably after we've left the room," Harry said.

"I shall put structural restraint on the list."

"Sirius," McGonagall warned.

"All right. No falling masonry. I reserve the right to complain loudly."

Harry smiled despite himself. Sirius touched two fingers to the underside of Harry's chin, a gesture so brief it almost became a question.

"Staying is your choice," he said. "Surviving it is not a test you take alone."

"I know."

"Say it like you believe me."

Harry looked at Hermione, still holding his left hand. At Draconia beside her, pale and furious and present. At Remus, who had taught him to name the room. At McGonagall, whose anger had not once been directed at the child standing nearest the damage.

"I'm beginning to," he said.

Sirius nodded. Then he stood, turned towards the door and became Lord Black with such completeness that Harry almost felt sorry for the Ministry officials waiting downstairs. Almost.

The inquiry lasted until two in the morning. Harry attended the final hour after Madam Pomfrey had treated his hand and agreed to one short account, followed by a break if he needed it. Amelia Bones sent a written request bearing the words witness, not accused. He chose to go with Sirius rather than wait for a morning interview. The distinction mattered.

The meeting took place in the staff room. Someone had enlarged the long table and placed recording quills at both ends. Dumbledore sat at its centre with McGonagall and Snape to his right. Umbridge occupied the opposite side between Senior Undersecretary Fawley and a square-faced witch whose purple robes bore the silver knot of the Educational Standards Commission. Amelia Bones sat at the head.

Sirius took the chair immediately beside Harry. Remus sat on his other side, with Hermione and Draconia behind them as corroborating witnesses. Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy had arrived while Harry was being treated; they remained near the back wall, close enough for Draconia to reach without appearing to need them. Mr and Mrs Granger were present through the standing visitors' mirror McGonagall had arranged after the train attack, two worried faces framed together in a sitting room hundreds of miles away. They had been contacted with Hermione's agreement; the mirror stood near her chair, where she could see them without facing the whole table. There were more adults in the room than children.

For once, that made Harry feel safer rather than smaller. Harry's part of the account took less than ten minutes. He stopped twice to correct himself and once because he could not remember whether Umbridge had stood before or after he drew his wand. Bones told him to leave that part uncertain. The recording would establish the order. Umbridge talked for much longer.

She did not deny the quill. She did not deny the containment ward or her attempt to confiscate the mirror. Denial would have been too fragile. Instead she renamed everything. Blood became a self-supplied corrective medium. Pain became a temporary tactile reinforcement. The locked door became a protective boundary. Harry's refusal became escalating noncompliance. The guardian alarm became a disproportionate response initiated by an improperly modified personal object.

"It isn't improperly modified," Sirius said for the fourth time. "The guardian enchantment was registered with Hogwarts and the Department of Magical Family Affairs this summer. Your file should show the date."

The Commission witch glanced at her file. "It was registered under emergency welfare provisions intended for orphaned minors without reliable institutional access."

"Harry was an orphaned minor without reliable institutional access."

"He is presently resident at a boarding school."

"Where a teacher has just locked him inside an office and cut him."

"The phrase cut him is emotionally prejudicial."

"So was the quill."

Amelia Bones removed her monocle and rubbed the bridge of her nose.

"Enough. The Auror Office will examine the object under statutes governing blood magic. The school will retain its own evidence under seal, and all parties will receive copies of the examination record."

"The Commission objects to Auror involvement in an educational matter," Fawley said.

"The Auror Office does not require the Commission's permission to investigate an alleged curse."

"No curse has been alleged."

Every person on Harry's side of the table looked at his bandaged hand. Fawley adjusted his cuffs.

"No legally prohibited curse has been established."

There it was. The narrow space between wrong and forbidden. The Ministry had built an office inside it.

Bones turned to Madam Pomfrey. "Will the injury leave a permanent mark?"

"Not after two words and prompt treatment. Residual redness may remain while the tissue heals; that is different from permanent scar formation."

"Had he completed the assigned sentence?"

"Repeatedly? Almost certainly."

"But he did not," the Commission witch said. "The standard requires demonstrable lasting harm, not speculation regarding interrupted use."

"So stopping made it less provable," Harry said.

Silence gathered around the table. The woman looked at him as though surprised he could speak legal language without permission.

"Stopping limited the physical consequence."

"And protected Professor Umbridge from meeting your threshold."

Sirius's mouth twitched. The recording quills scratched furiously.

Umbridge leaned forward. "Mr Potter repeatedly frames reasonable safeguards as deliberate persecution. This is precisely why the Ministry must remain involved in his education."

Harry felt anger rise. It no longer arrived as heat alone. Now it carried the train with it: masked faces, Hermione falling, Draconia's blood across his sleeve, the terrible clean desire to make someone hurt because he had been made afraid. Something pressed faintly behind his scar. It was not a formed thought. It felt like attention, but Harry could not tell whether that feeling meant someone else was there. He placed both feet flat upon the floor.

Table beneath his fingers. Sirius at his left. Remus at his right. Monday becoming Tuesday. Twelve panes in the hospital window, none here. He counted the green buttons on McGonagall's sleeve instead. Five. The pressure withdrew. Harry leaned towards Remus and quietly named what he had felt. Remus nodded, made a note and asked whether it was happening now. Harry shook his head before turning back to the table.

"I asked what it was," Harry said. "She wouldn't tell me. Then I tried to leave and she locked the door. Call that whatever you like. It's still what happened."

Snape's gaze sharpened upon him. Harry knew the professor had seen what he had done. Umbridge had not.

"The question before us," Dumbledore said, "is not whether Dolores possessed parchment granting her disciplinary discretion. It is whether any parchment can convert concealed blood magic into safe instruction merely by naming it approved. Hogwarts will not permit the use of that quill upon another student."

"You cannot overrule a Ministry classification," Fawley said.

"I can prevent an object entering my classrooms."

"And the High Inquisitor can inspect your refusal."

"Then she may do so without the quill."

They argued for another half-hour. At last, Bones forced an interim agreement into existence through exhaustion, statute and the threat of publicly summoning the Wizengamot before breakfast. The Auror Office would retain the quill for independent testing. Umbridge would remain High Inquisitor while the review proceeded, protected by the Minister's decree. She could assign detentions, but none involving Harry could occur behind a closed door or without a second adult chosen from the permanent Hogwarts staff. No blood-based disciplinary instrument could be used upon any student until the inquiry concluded. Umbridge objected to every condition. Sirius objected to her breathing. Nobody left satisfied. That, Harry was beginning to learn, was what adults called compromise when one side had brought evidence and the other had brought authority.

"Before this meeting closes," Lucius said from the back wall, "record a formal parental objection to the Commission's classification."

Fawley glanced towards him. "Your recent enthusiasm for child welfare would carry greater weight had it begun before your political circumstances changed."

Draconia went rigid. Lucius did not.

"I am aware," he said. "Record it anyway."

The answer removed whatever pleasure Fawley had expected from the blow. One of the recording quills scratched across a fresh line. Narcissa moved to Draconia and laid a hand between her shoulders. She looked at Harry over her daughter's head.

"The door locked because an adult arranged for it to lock," she said. "Not because you failed to leave quickly enough."

Harry's throat tightened.

"I know."

Narcissa held his gaze with the patient severity of someone willing to wait for a truer answer.

"I'm trying to know," he amended.

"Good."

Before the chairs scraped back, McGonagall asked Harry what he was prepared to have released. He agreed to a summary of the detention and its findings. He did not want the welfare file, the cupboard or the private conversation in the examination room turned into another notice for students to read between lessons.

"None of that belongs in it," she said. "You will see what we release."

Harry checked the short extract while Sirius waited beside him. It said what Umbridge had done. It did not require him to show strangers every reason it had frightened him. He put his initials at the bottom and handed it back. As chairs scraped back, Umbridge collected her files.

"I trust," she said, "that Mr Potter understands this decision is not an exoneration. His conduct remains under review."

Harry looked at the white bandage around his hand.

"I'll try to survive the suspense."

McGonagall coughed into her fist. Snape did not bother.

By breakfast, everyone knew. Nobody knew the same version. According to one Ravenclaw, Harry had attempted to curse Umbridge and been subdued by an enchanted pen. According to a pair of second-year Hufflepuffs, Umbridge had carved I am a liar across his face. A Gryffindor fourth-year swore Sirius Black had arrived through the wall and challenged the entire Ministry to a duel. The most ambitious account involved Dumbledore transforming into a phoenix, Snape poisoning three officials and Professor McGonagall striking Umbridge with the fallen door. Harry preferred the last one. The truth waited for him outside the Great Hall. It had been copied onto plain parchment and fixed beside the doors beneath a heading written in Hermione's unmistakable hand.

WHAT WAS DONE

Below it appeared a short sequence of events, the names of the adult witnesses, two statements checked against the mirror recording, Madam Pomfrey's medical classification and Professor Flitwick's finding that the containment ward had treated escape as evidence of danger. The account kept to the agreed extract. It included neither Harry's private medical history nor the conversation in the examination room. At the bottom, clearly separate from the findings and in smaller writing, someone had added:

The Ministry calls this temporary. Ask whether that makes it acceptable.

Students crowded three deep around the page. A smaller note bore McGonagall's approval for this school-issued account. Umbridge might still object to it; the permission meant Hermione was not passing off an unauthorised notice as an official finding. Hermione stopped beside Harry.

"I didn't write the last line."

"I did," Draconia said.

She had come up silently on his other side. Her uniform was immaculate, but her eyes were shadowed and she blinked slowly in the light from the doors. Even a few hours' sleep had not made the night feel over. Harry looked at the copies.

"When did you do this?"

"Before breakfast. McGonagall released the extract you approved, and the others helped with the copies."

"So while I was asleep."

"You required it."

"So did both of you."

Hermione shifted her bag higher upon her shoulder. It contained only the morning's books; Harry could see her witness notebook in the box Padma was carrying towards the Ravenclaw table. At least that part of yesterday's promise had survived.

"We did sleep," Hermione said. "Madam Pomfrey sent us to bed after the inquiry."

"And then?"

"Ernie copied the medical statement. Padma checked the times against the recording Professor McGonagall released. We put the page together before breakfast."

Draconia rubbed at one eye before catching herself. "I don't think either of us slept well. But we didn't stay up all night writing it."

Harry looked from her to Hermione. Neither looked rested. Neither was pretending quite as hard as yesterday, either.

"Good," he said. "I'd like my complaints to have some effect."

"A dangerous ambition," Draconia replied.

"You are both impossible," Harry said.

"And yet," Draconia replied, "you seem attached."

Her gaze dropped to his bandaged hand. The lightness vanished. Harry flexed his fingers. The movement hurt less than it had during the night, though Madam Pomfrey had warned him the nerves would remember the quill after the skin stopped showing it.

"Still here," he said.

Draconia did not answer. Instead she took his hand, lifted it carefully and turned the bandage beneath the morning light. Students continued talking around them. Harry felt suddenly aware of every face and not one of them. Her thumb brushed the uninjured skin below his wrist.

"You left," she said.

"I tried."

"You called."

"Eventually."

"Do not reduce this too."

Harry looked at her. There were a thousand things Draconia could not say in a crowded entrance hall. He remembered the Chamber and the hospital wing where she had raised her wand against him with someone else's expression upon her face. He could not know which memory troubled her now. He only knew that she was holding his hand with the same care he had used when he found her. Harry raised their joined hands and pressed his mouth briefly to her knuckles. Draconia went perfectly still. He realised what he had done only after doing it. The Great Hall seemed to lose sound.

It had not. Someone dropped a spoon. A group of Ravenclaws were arguing about curse classification. Fred and George were taking bets beside the doors. Yet none of it reached the space between Harry and Draconia. Colour moved slowly into her face.

"Potter," she said.

"Yes?"

"Was that concussion?"

"I wasn't hit in the head yesterday."

"A delayed consequence from the train, then."

"Could be."

"I shall need to observe you closely."

"For medical reasons."

"Entirely."

Hermione made a quiet sound beside them. Harry turned. She was smiling. It was warm, genuine and just a fraction too careful. The sight caught beneath his ribs. He reached for her hand as well. Hermione allowed it, though one eyebrow rose.

"Am I being medically observed too?"

"You are being escorted to breakfast before you decide tea counts as sleep."

"That is not remotely the same thing."

"No," Harry said. "It isn't."

He squeezed her fingers. Some of the carefulness left her smile. They entered the Great Hall together.

Conversation dipped as Harry passed. Not everywhere. Enough. He could feel questions turning towards his bandage. Sympathy was present, but so was doubt. A few students looked away too quickly. Others watched him with the eager discomfort reserved for public injuries and public scandals, relieved the body carrying both belonged to someone else. At the Gryffindor table, Seamus shifted his bag from the empty place beside him.

"Is it true?" he asked.

Harry sat. "Which bit?"

"The quill."

Harry placed his right hand on the table.

"Yes."

"But they're saying it only marked you because you used it wrong."

"Who is?"

Seamus gestured towards the staff table. A new notice floated above Umbridge's plate in pink-bordered parchment.

The Office of the High Inquisitor wishes to reassure students that no unsafe disciplinary practice has been authorised at Hogwarts. Allegations concerning a standard corrective implement remain under review following improper use, premature interruption and external interference. Students are reminded that rumour may cause unnecessary distress.

Harry read it twice.

"I interrupted being cut," he said. "Very improperly."

Ron, seated opposite, looked up from his toast.

"You should've shown more respect. Bled in an approved direction, maybe."

Neville choked on pumpkin juice. Seamus tried not to smile. It made his doubt look less certain.

"My mum says the Ministry wouldn't approve something Dark," he said.

"The Ministry put Dementors around this school," Harry replied. "They call those guards."

That ended the conversation. Not the question. Questions followed Harry throughout the morning.

Some were whispered behind him. Some were placed directly in his path. A small Hufflepuff girl asked whether the quill could read lies and appeared relieved when Harry said no. Two Ravenclaws wanted the magical classification. A Slytherin boy Harry did not know muttered that Malfoy had probably staged the whole thing to embarrass the Commission. Draconia heard him. She did not turn. Harry did.

"You could ask her," he said.

The boy paled.

Draconia continued walking. "Cowardice is rarely cured by invitation."

"It made him quieter."

"So would Petrificus Totalus."

"You're supposed to be the restrained one."

"I barely slept. My restraint is resting."

The first honest confrontation arrived outside Charms. Zacharias Smith waited with three Hufflepuffs near the classroom door. Cedric stood several feet behind them, expression unreadable.

"Did she force you?" Zacharias asked.

Harry stopped.

"Yes."

"She says you could have chosen a normal quill."

"No, she doesn't. Her notice says I used the one she gave me improperly."

"Same difference."

"It isn't."

Zacharias folded his arms. "You always have witnesses conveniently nearby."

Harry felt Hermione draw breath. Cedric spoke before she could.

"I was conveniently nearby in the graveyard too."

Students turned. Cedric moved to Harry's side, not in front of him.

"I was there," he said. "Harry hid me in the grass beside another grave. They thought I was dead. I could see enough through the roots to know what they were doing to him."

Harry looked down. He had heard Cedric describe it before, at the end-of-term feast. Knowing the account did not make hearing it in a school corridor easier.

"The roots stayed around me while they hurt him," Cedric continued. "Later they found I was alive and Stunned me. I don't remember the last part of the escape. Harry and the healers told me how we got back; I'm not pretending I watched it. Having someone beside him hasn't stopped the Ministry calling him a liar. It just means there are two of us saying they're wrong."

Zacharias's face reddened.

"That isn't what I asked."

"No. You asked whether the existence of witnesses makes Harry less believable. I am telling you that witnesses have not protected him from being called a liar before."

Cedric looked at the white bandage.

"May I?"

Harry extended his hand. Cedric did not touch it. He examined the edge of the dressing, jaw tightening when he saw the red seeping faintly through.

"Do you want me to tell them to go?" he asked.

Harry glanced at the watching students. "I want to get to Charms without having an argument at every door."

"All right. Anything else?"

Harry considered.

"Time."

"You have it."

"And for nobody to challenge Umbridge to a duel before lunch."

Cedric glanced past him. Fred and George stood at the end of the corridor carrying a box that rattled ominously.

"That may be beyond my authority."

"You're in your seventh year. Can't you make it sound authoritative?"

"They've had six years to practise ignoring me. I doubt a seventh will cure it."

"We have considerable experience," Fred said, joining them. "Ignoring Cedric is practically an elective."

George held up the box. "Purely hypothetically, how attached is she to having frogs fall out of her mouth?"

"Very," Harry said. "Which is why you're not doing it."

Both twins stared at him.

"Who are you," Fred asked, "and what bureaucratic instrument have you used upon Harry?"

"If you retaliate now, she gets to stop talking about the quill and start talking about violent students."

"Frogs aren't violent."

"They are if you dislike frogs."

"Everyone likes frogs."

"Professor Snape doesn't."

"Professor Snape dislikes oxygen when Gryffindors breathe it."

George sobered first. "You're asking us to do nothing?"

"I'm asking you to wait until doing something helps."

The words surprised Harry as much as they did the twins. On the train, waiting had felt like weakness. In the office, leaving had felt like surrender. Umbridge relied upon those instincts. She wanted anger before thought because anger could be named for him. Fred studied him for a long moment. Then he put the rattling box back into his bag.

"Forty-eight hours," he said.

"Twenty-four," George amended.

"Thirty-six," Harry offered.

The twins exchanged a glance.

"Done."

"Weasley," Cedric said, "that wasn't permission to do it in thirty-six hours."

"No," Fred replied. "It's how civilisation works."

They disappeared down the corridor.

Ron watched them go. "You do know that gives us until Wednesday evening to find them something useful to destroy."

"I was hoping everyone would forget."

"Have you met my brothers?"

Harry looked at the classroom ahead, at the students still reading Hermione's page, at Cedric beside him and the girls close enough that their sleeves brushed his.

"We may need them," he said.

By Tuesday afternoon, Harry was tired of answering questions. He had answered some badly, refused others, and apologised to a third-year who only wanted to know whether he had dropped his glove outside Transfiguration. The apology embarrassed him almost as much as snapping had. He kept the glove in his pocket until he found its owner again.

McGonagall ended the next question before it began. When a classmate raised a hand to ask about the inquiry, the professor pointed to the beetle upon her desk and informed her that it had no connection to the Ministry unless it successfully applied for a position. For the rest of the lesson Harry was permitted to be ordinary: he turned his beetle into a button, lost its last leg inside the rim, and had to try again.

At three, Madam Pomfrey checked his hand. Hermione and Draconia came for their own follow-up examinations rather than claiming to be there solely for him. They sat in a row outside her office, comparing the homework they had already acquired.

"Six inches on the correct use of a Vanishing Charm," Hermione said.

"Umbridge," Harry offered.

Draconia tilted her head. "That's a practical application. Not the correct use."

"We've been told practical applications are dangerous."

"In this instance, they're tempting."

Hermione tried to look disapproving, then laughed into her sleeve. The sound made Harry turn towards her. He had heard her laugh several times since the train, but he still noticed each one. Pomfrey called Draconia first. When the door closed behind her, Hermione shifted closer to Harry along the bench.

"I was horrible yesterday," she said.

"You've already apologised for the prefect thing."

"I know. I meant afterwards. I kept telling you what to do."

Harry looked down at his bandage. He did not want another hour spent deciding which of them had failed the other.

"I needed you outside the door," he said. "I'm glad you were there."

"But you don't want us everywhere you go."

"Sometimes I do. That's part of what's annoying."

Hermione considered him. A draught moved beneath the office door and stirred a strand of hair against her cheek.

"When I went to my room on Sunday," she said, "I put my books down and couldn't remember why I was there. I'd spent the weekend checking that you were both still there. Then I was supposed to go upstairs and just stop checking."

Harry knew that feeling so precisely he did not need to explain it. He rested his left hand upon the bench between them. Hermione covered it with hers.

"If I tell you I need a minute on my own," he said, "will you let me have it?"

"Yes."

"Even if I look odd."

"You usually look odd."

He turned towards her. She smiled, looking almost surprised that the joke had come out before the worry.

"I will ask when you're coming back," she added. "I think that's fair."

"It is."

They were still holding hands when Draconia emerged, wearing the sour expression she reserved for medical advice she could not dispute. She glanced down at their fingers, then at Harry's face.

"I have been told to walk less, sleep more and stop assuming that being allowed to attend lessons means attending every argument."

"Terrible waste of your talents," Harry said.

Draconia sat on his other side while Hermione went in. She let the back of her head rest against the wall. Harry had rarely seen her do that in a public corridor.

"You can leave it alone for a bit," he said. "The inquiry. Other people have the papers."

"So you've been telling everyone."

"I thought I'd try it on you."

She looked at him, then reached up and took a thread from the shoulder of his robe. Her hand rested there for a moment after the thread was gone.

"I was going to tell you to put me down," she said.

"When?"

"On the train. After you caught me on the roof. I couldn't see how you were going to keep hold."

Harry felt the memory in his left shoulder. Her wrist slipping, the wind pulling at her sleeve. He did not answer quickly.

"You didn't," he said.

"No. I wanted you to keep trying."

She looked ashamed of the admission, and that hurt more than the words themselves. Harry turned his palm towards her. She took it after a moment, carefully avoiding the dressing.

"I'm glad," he said.

"That isn't a sensible answer."

"It's the one I've got."

Her eyes shone. She blinked at the floor until they stopped, and Harry stayed beside her without asking her to look at him. Beyond the windows, a class crossed the lawn towards the greenhouses. Someone tripped over a bag strap; the resulting laughter reached them faintly through the glass. Neither of them made an effort to join it. When Hermione came back, they went downstairs slowly. Harry let her choose the route with the fewest steps, and Draconia carried only one book.

By Wednesday, the boys in his dormitory had learned to ask before launching into the latest version. Dean still brought him the best of them. Neville brought the questions he had been asked by younger pupils, written on a loose sheet Harry could put away when he had had enough. Seamus sent his mother a letter and did not read it aloud.

On Thursday, Harry spent half an hour alone in the library. He chose a table beside a window, opened an ordinary Charms book and read the same paragraph four times. Nobody followed him there. When his concentration finally returned, it brought an uncomfortable realisation: he had wanted the space, but part of him had also been waiting for someone to prove they cared by refusing to give it. He mentioned that to Remus through the mirror that evening. It was easier to say with the library behind him and dinner already over, proof that he had left his friends for a while and found them again. Remus did not appear surprised.

"What did you do?" he asked.

"Read the paragraph a fifth time. Eventually learned the charm."

"And afterwards?"

"Found them at dinner."

"Were they pleased to see you?"

Harry thought of Hermione moving her books out of the spare place and Draconia looking up before she had finished pretending to be interested in her plate.

"Yes."

"Good. You might need to discover that more than once."

The answer was irritatingly small. Harry had hoped for something he could finish practising by Friday. Remus smiled when he said so, then asked about his hand. Harry gave him a fuller description of the pressure he had reported during the inquiry: the anger, the sudden sense of attention against his scar, the way it had faded when he counted McGonagall's buttons. He also mentioned the corridor that had appeared in his dreams over the last few nights, always ending at a closed black door. Saying it now meant Remus could discuss the details with Snape before anyone lifted a wand.

"That helps," Remus said. "Thank you."

"I didn't want to wait until there was another reason for Sirius to arrive through a fireplace."

"An excellent ambition. Shall we work towards a whole week?"

Harry smiled. For the first time, Friday's lesson felt like something he was helping prepare rather than something adults were preparing for him.

By Friday evening, the phrase temporary dermal inscription had appeared in six essays, three conversations with Peeves and a song performed by somebody Harry strongly suspected was Dean Thomas. Umbridge removed every copy of WHAT WAS DONE she found. More appeared.

One was fixed to the wall outside her office with a Sticking Charm so advanced that Professor Flitwick examined it for ten minutes before regretfully declaring himself unable to assist. Another turned up inside the Ministry-approved Defence textbook between the chapters entitled Conflict Reduction Through Verbal Compliance and Recognising When Authority Knows Best. By dinner, students had begun leaving red quills upon empty classroom desks.

Nobody claimed responsibility. Harry did not mistake resistance for universal belief. The school remained divided. Some students lowered their voices when he passed. Several parents sent Howlers demanding that their children be protected from his dangerous influence. The Daily Prophet printed a short article on page twelve beneath the headline POTTER DISRUPTS SAFEGUARDING REVIEW, describing the quill as an ordinary teaching aid and Sirius as an unstable former prisoner, giving far less space to the fact that he had been publicly exonerated for more than a year. Facts had not ended the argument. They had made the argument harder to erase. At ten minutes before eight, Harry stood outside Snape's office with Remus on one side and Sirius on the other.

"You don't have to wait," Harry said.

Sirius looked at the door. "Yes, I do."

"You cannot glare through oak."

"I can practise."

Remus adjusted the leather strap of his bag. "Sirius will remain outside because Harry asked for only one observer in the room."

"I remember what Harry asked."

"Then you are doing very well."

"Don't patronise me, Moony."

"I wouldn't dare."

Harry looked between them.

"You were both easier when one of you was a dog."

Sirius's mouth twitched.

"Cruel," he said. "Accurate, but cruel."

The office door opened. Snape stood beyond it.

"If the family reunion is complete, Potter has a mind to learn how to close."

"See?" Harry said to Sirius. "He makes it sound welcoming."

"I will be here," Sirius replied.

This time Harry understood the sentence for what it was. Not a warning. A fact. He entered the office with Remus.

Snape had changed the room. The office retained its shelves of preserved creatures and the dim green light which made every glass jar appear faintly diseased, but the desk had been moved against the wall. Three chairs stood in the cleared centre, none behind another. A low table held water, chocolate, a Pensieve and a small brass instrument shaped like an opening eye. Harry stopped inside the doorway.

Harry had expected to stand before Snape's desk as he did after a failed potion. Instead there was a chair with room on either side of it. He could see the door without turning his head. His breathing eased before anyone spoke. There was no desk for Snape to command from. No marked place where Harry was expected to stand. The door remained open behind him until Harry closed it himself. Snape noticed him noticing.

"Professor Lupin has been permitted to inspect every object in the room," he said. "No spell will be cast upon you without an explanation and an affirmative response. The door will not lock. Black is positioned outside and has been given an emergency phrase he will undoubtedly misuse at the first opportunity."

"He suggested Snivellus, didn't he?"

"He did. It was rejected. The agreed phrase is 'end the lesson'. If you cannot speak, Lupin will use it for you."

"Unusually restrained of him."

"I am certain it caused considerable suffering."

Remus removed his coat. "A promising beginning. You have been inside for twelve seconds and neither of you has drawn a wand."

Snape gestured to the chairs. Harry selected the one from which he could see both the door and Snape. Remus sat beside him, where Harry could see him without turning. His stick rested against the chair within easy reach. Snape remained standing. A thin line of silver ran around the cleared floor.

"Flitwick tested the barrier this afternoon," Remus said. "I watched him test the cutoff as well. I can end a spell Severus casts if you stop responding. It has not been tested against the scar connection, so we must not assume it will block that."

Snape waited while Harry followed the silver line with his eyes. "We still begin without entry. The cutoff supplements my obligation to stop; it does not replace it."

Harry nodded. Only then did Snape continue.

"Occlumency is frequently described as the art of emptying the mind," he began. "This is inaccurate, impractical and responsible for a generation of mediocre instruction. A mind cannot become empty while its owner remains conscious. Attempting to force it blank merely creates a conspicuous silence around whatever one wishes to hide."

Harry thought of the unfamiliar corridor in his dreams and the black door at its end. The uncertain sense of attention that had touched the edge of his anger at the inquiry.

"So what am I supposed to do?"

"Know what belongs to you."

Snape touched his wand to the brass eye. Its lid opened. A clear sphere rose above the table, empty except for a point of blue light at its centre.

"This instrument responds to recognised forms of Legilimency, compulsion and several varieties of dream intrusion. It cannot identify a distant caster. More importantly, no instrument we possess was designed to measure your connection to the Dark Lord. A blue light is not proof that he is absent. We compare what it records with what you experience."

"And if it turns red?"

"It has detected a pattern consistent with external pressure. Gold usually accompanies a strong memory response. White will identify the controlled pressure I apply during this lesson; I have already calibrated it. None of those colours gives me permission to proceed without asking you."

"What colour is normal?"

"Blue."

Harry stared at the light. It remained blue.

"Before any spellwork," Remus said, "we establish where you are without magic. Five things you can see."

"We've done this before."

"Which is why you already know how."

Harry looked around.

"The brass eye. A jar with something I hope used to be a mushroom. Professor Snape's left sleeve is torn at the cuff. The second shelf is crooked. There's a crack in the floor near my shoe."

Snape glanced down at his sleeve.

"Four things you can feel," Remus continued.

"The chair. My glasses. The bandage. My scar itching."

The blue point flickered. Snape's attention snapped to the instrument.

"What were you thinking?"

"That my scar itches."

"Immediately before."

Harry considered. "Umbridge. The meeting."

"More precise."

"She called refusing to stay in the office distress. At the meeting she said I treated safeguards like persecution. I kept thinking about the door, and I got angry."

The point remained blue.

"That sounds like the response you described to Lupin yesterday," Snape said.

"It felt the same. At the edge."

"Does your scar hurt now?"

"It itches."

Snape paced once around the instrument. Harry watched him rather than the light. It was easier to believe the machine's limitations while an actual person was taking time to consider them.

"So you think it was him at the inquiry."

"Possibly. We do not yet know whether emotion opens the connection, accompanies it or simply makes you notice it. You were right to report the sequence."

Harry looked towards Remus. He had not expected a compliment, certainly not one Snape seemed unwilling to disguise.

"I think that means we can stop arguing about whether I should have said something," he said.

"For the moment. Your capacity to provide a new reason is considerable."

Snape drew his wand. Harry's shoulders locked. The professor lowered it at once.

"I am drawing my wand," he said with exaggerated precision. "I will not cast."

Embarrassment followed the fear, sharp and familiar. Harry hated that his body had reacted. Hated more that everybody had seen it. Remus did not tell him there was nothing to fear.

"What changed?" he asked.

"My shoulders."

"Why?"

"Wand."

"Whose?"

Harry looked at Snape.

"His."

"Not Voldemort's?"

"No."

"Not Pettigrew's or Barty Crouch's?"

"No."

"Then name the room again."

Harry did. Snape waited with the wand held loosely at his side until Harry nodded.

"The first exercise involves no entry," he said. "I will send a controlled pressure towards the outer edge of your mind. It will be comparable to someone placing a hand against a closed door. I will not open it. You will identify the pressure as external, choose a present memory or sensation as an anchor, and refuse access. The instrument should record my calibrated spell. What you experience matters as much as its reading."

"How do I refuse?"

"The same way you refuse anything else. With less talking, preferably."

"Helpful."

"A magical boundary is intent given structure. You have produced shields without understanding the mathematics behind them. This is a shield turned inward. Do not imagine a wall if confinement disturbs you. Choose something with an exit you control."

Harry thought of Hawthorn's kitchen door opening onto the garden. His bedroom window. The motorbike rising above London. None felt right. Then he remembered the Hogwarts Express racing through the afternoon rain, Death Eaters above it and his friends trapped inside. Too much fear.

He moved past it to a different train. First year. Hermione leaning into the corridor to ask whether anyone had seen Neville's toad. Draconia sitting opposite Harry in immaculate robes, examining a Chocolate Frog as if its packaging had personally disappointed her. The landscape running beyond the window while sweets covered the seats. A door he could close or open. A journey he had chosen.

"I have one," Harry said.

"Describe only what is safe to make visible," Snape said. "An anchor is not a precious memory displayed at the gate. It is the shape of a place, a sound, a physical certainty."

"Train window. Fields moving backwards."

"Acceptable. Do you consent to external pressure without entry?"

The question sounded unnatural in Snape's voice. That made it more meaningful, not less. Harry checked the door. Remus. The mirror in his pocket. The blue light.

"Yes."

Snape raised his wand slowly.

"Legilimens Exterior."

Pressure touched Harry's forehead. It was not pain. It was the certainty that someone stood immediately behind him though he could see the room ahead. Every instinct demanded he turn. When he tried, the pressure moved with him, not in space but through attention, searching for whichever thought changed fastest. Harry saw the train window. Fields streamed backwards beneath a grey sky. The point above the brass eye turned white. The pressure found the memory and leaned.

Harry's mind offered it more: the Chocolate Frog box, Hermione's hair in the doorway, Draconia's pale fingers taking a sweet she claimed not to want. He felt Snape's presence recoil before touching them. Not precious memory at the gate. Shape. Sound. Physical certainty. The train window. Wheels beneath the floor. The handle within his own hand. Harry slid the compartment door shut. The pressure remained outside. Not gone. Refused.

"Enough," he said.

It vanished immediately. White became blue. Harry opened his eyes. Snape's wand was already lowered. Nobody told him he had lasted only six seconds. Nobody asked why he had stopped. Remus handed him water. Harry drank.

"Again," he said.

"No," Snape replied.

"I can do better."

"Undoubtedly. You will not do so tonight."

Harry stared at him. "That was the lesson?"

"That was a first controlled contact after experiences we are still investigating, blood magic on Monday and an attack a week ago. Only a fool would proceed directly to forced memory access."

"You used to call me weak for being frightened."

The words entered the room and remained there. Harry had not planned to say them. Snape's tone was too close to a Potions lesson: the certainty that foolishness belonged to somebody else, never to the person holding the wand.

"You said I wanted attention," Harry continued. "When I couldn't sleep. When something hurt. You made it sound like I was doing it on purpose."

Snape's expression closed. Harry remembered entering the dungeons with an excuse already prepared, trying to sound careless enough that his teacher could not hear what mattered. He remembered believing that if he could produce a better joke, the next insult would not follow him out of the room. Remus did not intervene. Harry was glad. He did not want the question softened before Snape answered it.

"Yes," Snape said at last.

Harry waited.

"I mistook familiarity with danger for an appetite for it. I expected you to exploit your history, and interpreted evidence against that expectation as another attempt to do so."

"You thought I'd invented it."

"Some of it. I thought you exaggerated the rest."

The admission hurt even now. Harry looked at the bandage around his hand and made himself look away again. He did not intend to display another injury until his teacher believed him.

"I was wrong," Snape said.

He did not offer a reason yet. Harry had heard enough apologies which put the explanation first and left no room for anger afterwards. For several seconds he let Snape sit with the words.

"Why?" Harry asked.

"Why was I wrong?"

"Why did you dislike me enough to think it wouldn't matter?"

Remus shifted, but Harry kept his eyes on Snape. The professor's face seemed carved from the same pale material as the creatures behind him.

"Because you resemble your father," he said.

"I know that part. Everyone tells me before explaining why I'm about to be punished for something he did."

Snape's mouth tightened.

"James Potter was careless with power. He was praised often enough that he began treating admiration as absolution. When you arrived—famous, protected, reckless—I saw the pattern I expected."

"And when I didn't act like him?"

"You did, with irritating frequency."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the only honest one. You resembled him in ways that confirmed my judgement and differed in ways I dismissed."

Harry thought of Sirius seeing James when Harry flew, when he laughed, when Sirius had thanked Ron beside the stopped train. He thought of Remus carefully separating the son from the memory of his father.

"Do you still?"

"Still what?"

"See him first."

Snape met his eyes.

"Less often."

It was not kindness. Harry trusted it more for not pretending to be.

Remus broke the silence gently. "The instrument changed colour as expected, and you recognised the pressure Severus applied. You stopped it without losing track of the room. We cannot use that to settle every question about Voldemort, but it is a useful beginning."

"Only when I'm angry?"

"Possibly when emotion makes the boundary easier to notice," Snape said. "Possibly he is not initiating the contact at all. A link may transmit pressure in either direction when sufficiently agitated."

Harry's stomach tightened. "So I could be entering his mind."

"At the edge. Without intent."

"Would he know?"

"He may feel whatever you feel: pain, fear, anger. He may mistake it for his own until context distinguishes it. Or the connection may carry only intensity without meaning. We do not know."

"That's reassuring."

"Reassurance unsupported by evidence is merely a polite lie."

"You and Hermione would get on better if you admitted how alike you are."

Snape looked offended in a way Harry found deeply satisfying. Remus hid a smile behind his hand. The blue light flickered. This time no one had cast. A thin thread of red appeared around its centre. Snape's humour vanished.

"My wand is down," Snape said. "Lupin, watch Potter. We are not continuing the exercise."

Harry became aware of his scar again. The itch sharpened into heat. Somewhere beyond the office, Sirius shifted against the door. Harry heard the faint scrape of his boot. The room remained unchanged: green light, crooked shelf, brass eye, Remus beside him. Yet a second place pressed against it. Dark stone beneath cold, unfamiliar lamps. A corridor he had seen in dreams. The black door at its end stood open by the width of one finger. Cold expectancy breathed through the gap. Harry's anger was gone. In its place came hunger so vast and patient that for one disorienting second he believed it belonged to him. Open it. He did not hear the words.

He knew them.

"External pressure," Harry said.

The red thread thickened.

"Anchor," Remus said.

Train window. Fields moving backwards. The corridor remained.

"It isn't working."

"Because you are trying to erase the other image," Snape said. "Do not fight for the entire mind. Name what is yours."

Harry gripped the chair. Wood beneath his fingers. Bandage around his hand. Sirius outside. Remus to his right. Snape in front of him. The black door opened another fraction. Behind it waited shelves. Rows of glass spheres. One glowed faintly with his name. Harry knew this without seeing the letters. He wanted it. No. The hunger wanted it.

"Not mine," Harry said.

The brass instrument whined.

"Again," Snape ordered.

"The corridor isn't mine."

"What is?"

"The train."

"Something you can feel now, Potter. You do not have to stay with the memory."

Harry saw Hermione in the compartment doorway, eleven years old and determined that they help Neville find his toad. Draconia opposite him, eleven and lonely enough to disguise interest as criticism. Ron years later at his back while the train burned beneath them. Cedric flying away with injured children. Sirius upon the motorbike beside the engine. The train had changed. It contained every journey towards and away from safety. Every person who had chosen to return.

"The people on it," Harry said.

The hunger struck the boundary. Pain split his scar. Harry saw a pale hand resting upon the arm of a chair. Long fingers. A dark room. A fire burning low enough that the figure before it had become a shape rather than a face. Then red eyes opened. Not in the room. Inside him. Recognition passed through the connection. Harry slammed the compartment door. The red thread snapped.

Blue light burst across the office. Harry pitched forward. Remus caught his shoulders. Snape seized the brass eye before it fell from the table. The office door flew open and Sirius entered with his wand raised.

"What happened?"

"Uninvited contact," Snape said. "Terminated by Potter."

"You said there would be no entry."

"There was none from me."

Sirius went to Harry. "Look at me."

Harry did. Grey eyes. Human. Afraid.

"I'm here," Harry said before Sirius could ask.

"What did you see?"

"The corridor. The door. Shelves behind it. Something with my name."

Dumbledore's warning at Hawthorn returned: There may come a time when you see something designed to move you.

"And him," Harry added. "He saw me."

Nobody spoke. The brass eye continued shining blue as though nothing had happened. Snape turned it over. A hairline fracture crossed its base.

"The strongest reading came after you identified the people attached to your anchor," he said. "That is a sequence, not yet an explanation."

Sirius rounded on him. "Then perhaps we avoid making him do that again."

"We will review the exercise before attempting it again. What we know is that Potter distinguished the foreign desire from his own and ended the contact. We should not undo that achievement by pretending to know its cause already."

"He is sitting right here," Harry said.

Both men stopped. Remus moved from Harry's side and lowered himself carefully onto the edge of the chair opposite. His stick knocked against a table leg; he caught it before it fell.

"What do you need?"

The question again. This time Harry knew.

"For nobody to tell Dumbledore without me."

Snape's eyebrow rose. "The Headmaster must know."

"I know. I want to be there when he does. No meeting where everyone decides what my mind means while I'm asleep."

"Agreed," Remus said.

Sirius nodded. Snape considered for several seconds.

"Agreed."

Harry released a breath.

"And chocolate."

Remus placed a piece in his hand at once.

"You carry that everywhere," Snape said.

"It continues to prove more useful than bitterness."

"Debatable."

Harry ate the chocolate while they argued quietly above him. His scar still burned. The black door remained vivid whenever he closed his eyes. He was frightened that Voldemort had recognised him. Something in that place had seemed to carry his name, though he had not been close enough to read it. He told Snape that distinction before they left; it went into the notes beside everything else they could not yet prove. Yet another fact stood beside the fear. When the connection came, Harry had recognised it. When he said no, Snape stopped. When something else entered anyway, they had believed him. The lesson had frightened him. It had not trapped him. That difference felt small enough to hold in one hand and large enough to change the shape of the year.

Dumbledore heard the report before midnight. Harry gave it himself. They met in the headmaster's office with Sirius, Remus and Snape present. The silver instruments had been pushed to the edges of the desk. Fawkes watched from his perch, red feathers bright in the lamplight, while portraits pretended to sleep with varying degrees of failure.

Dumbledore listened without interrupting. Harry described the corridor, the black door and the shelves of glass spheres. He described the hunger that had not belonged to him and the instant in which he had felt recognised by the figure on the other side. He could not prove what Voldemort had understood, and said so. He did not make the account neat. Fear interrupted it. Uncertainty remained where he had no answer. Nobody corrected him into sounding braver. When he finished, Dumbledore removed his spectacles.

"I know the place you saw," he said.

Harry waited for the careful refusal he had learned to expect. Dumbledore had told him more this summer, but the habit of withholding things had not vanished from every conversation. He looked towards Sirius once before the headmaster continued.

"It is a corridor within the Department of Mysteries. The door leads to the Hall of Prophecy, where records of certain predictions are stored. One of those records bears your name and Lord Voldemort's."

Sirius's chair creaked beneath his grip.

"What prediction?" Harry asked.

"I will tell you what I know of its contents. Not tonight, while your mind is raw and the connection may remain active. That is not a refusal, Harry. It is a delay until tomorrow afternoon, after rest, with Sirius and Remus present."

Harry studied him.

"Why wait at all?"

"Because I do not know how much he can perceive through the connection tonight. I am concerned that telling you while it remains active could let him overhear me. Waiting does not solve that uncertainty, but it gives us time to examine what happened and you time to rest."

"And if I say I want to know anyway?"

Dumbledore did not answer at once.

"Then I will ask you to wait until tomorrow," he said. "If you still insist, I will tell you. You have been harmed often enough by adults deciding that ignorance is safer without allowing you to dispute the decision."

Harry glanced at Snape. The professor's face revealed nothing.

"Tomorrow," Harry agreed.

"Thank you."

"That doesn't mean I like it."

"Agreement and approval are not identical. The Ministry has spent considerable effort pretending otherwise."

Sirius made a low approving sound. Dumbledore folded his hands upon the desk.

"There is one further matter. Dolores must not learn that you recognised the Department."

"She'll report it to Fudge," Harry said.

"And through channels the Ministry has not yet accepted exist, the information may reach Voldemort."

"You believe someone in the Ministry is serving him," Sirius said.

"I believe the train attack required access to protected travel plans. Whether that access came from a willing servant, coercion or observation remains unclear."

Harry thought of Theodore Nott waiting outside the Slytherin staircase, pressed into reporting to Umbridge because his father had made suspicion hereditary.

"Then we don't tell anyone who doesn't need it," he said.

"We?" Snape repeated.

"Yes, Professor. That is what people call it when information belongs to more than one person."

"You may discover that secrecy requires fewer jokes."

"Then it sounds unbearable."

Remus laid a hand against Harry's shoulder before the exchange could grow teeth. Dumbledore smiled faintly.

"Tomorrow," he repeated. "For tonight, your task is sleep."

"Everyone keeps assigning me that as if I've failed the class."

"Your marks have been inconsistent."

Harry rose. At the door, Dumbledore called his name.

"You recognised the intrusion and ended it. That is no small achievement."

Pride stirred, immediately followed by the suspicion that accepting it would tempt disaster. Harry made himself hold both feelings.

"I had help," he said.

"Yes."

Dumbledore's eyes moved briefly to the men beside him.

"That is not a qualification. It is the point."

Harry slept until ten on Saturday morning. He woke from a dream of the black door to sunlight across his quilt and Ron sitting beneath the window with the Marauder's Map folded beside his knee. The other boys had gone down to breakfast. For a minute Harry listened to the quiet and let the dream become a memory instead of a place he might still be inside.

"How long have you been waiting?" he asked.

"Long enough to consider eating your toast. Hermione and Malfoy are in the visitors' sitting room near the hospital wing. McGonagall said we could use it this morning. They told me to bring you when you woke up."

Harry washed and dressed without hurrying, then walked downstairs with Ron. In the sitting room, Draconia had fallen asleep in an armchair beside the window. Hermione lay along the small sofa with an open book upon her chest. A covered breakfast tray waited on the table between them.

Ron put the map on the table and shut the door. Draconia woke at the soft click, her hand going to her wand before her eyes properly opened.

"It is only me," Harry said.

She focused on him.

"That is not always reassuring."

"Ron says you sent him to collect me."

"You were asleep. McGonagall said we could wait here, and I wasn't going to stand outside Gryffindor Tower all morning."

Ron put down the breakfast cloths. "Neville thought I should wake you. Dean said anyone threatening ten hours' sleep deserved to be hexed."

"You fell asleep waiting for me?" Harry asked.

"This chair is surprisingly comfortable," Draconia replied. "I was testing it. Thoroughly."

Hermione woke more slowly, blinking at the sitting-room ceiling before remembering where she was. The book slid from her chest. Hermione caught it against the sofa cushion before Harry could reach for it.

"Did you sleep?" she asked.

"Nearly ten hours," Harry said. "I checked before I got up."

All three looked impressed.

"I want that witnessed in writing," Harry said.

"Already noted," Hermione replied, reaching for her bag.

"You are terrifying."

"You say that as though it is new information."

They had brought breakfast wrapped in warming cloths. Draconia produced tea, Hermione produced a timetable, and Ron produced six pieces of toast he claimed had entered his pocket accidentally. For a while, they spoke of nothing important. Dean had begun work on another song. Neville's Mimbulus mimbletonia had expelled sap across Seamus's pillow during the night. Pansy Parkinson was taking bets on whether Lucius Malfoy would challenge the Educational Standards Commission to a duel or purchase the building and evict it.

"Father says buying an enemy's office only encourages inflated property values," Draconia said.

"So no duel?" Ron asked.

"Mother has hidden his ceremonial gloves."

Harry laughed. The sound surprised him. Last night remained true. Madam Pomfrey had removed the bandage on Friday afternoon, leaving the letters pale pink across his hand. He still felt them when he moved his fingers. Something had reached him during the lesson and shown him a door inside the Ministry. He could still remember the red eyes, and the terror of feeling noticed. Umbridge was still at Hogwarts, protected by wording written before she ever hurt him.

But sunlight lay across the sitting-room floor. Hermione's hair had flattened strangely on one side during sleep. Draconia was wearing one of Harry's spare jumpers over her uniform because the window let in a draught. Harry could not remember leaving it with her, but she appeared entirely comfortable in it. Ron was explaining why toast became safer after pocket storage. Fear had not disappeared. It had company. Hermione unfolded the timetable.

"We need to decide."

"That sentence has never led anywhere restful," Harry said.

"Students asked you to teach. More will ask after what happened on Monday. If we continue answering individually, Umbridge will identify everyone before the first practical spell is cast."

"So we organise," Draconia said.

Ron stopped defending his toast. "You mean an actual group."

"Not yet," Harry replied.

Hermione looked disappointed.

"I mean not until we know what it is," he continued. "I'm not standing in front of thirty people and pretending I have every answer because I survived things. Mostly I survived because someone else arrived, or because Hermione knew the spell, or Draco noticed what I missed, or Cedric trusted me when I told him to stay down."

"That is why you should teach," Hermione said.

"Because I'm underqualified?"

"Because you understand that Defence is not one brilliant person rescuing everyone."

Harry thought of the graveyard and the binding around Cedric. The train, where Hermione had kept the evacuation moving and Draconia had helped him get the last children out of carriage nine. Ron's shield at his back. Sirius keeping pace beside the engine. The mirror in Umbridge's office.

"Then I don't teach alone," he said. "And we ask people before putting them in charge of anything."

Hermione's expression sharpened with interest.

"We divide it," Harry continued. "I can teach shields, movement and what to do when a plan becomes useless. Hermione handles spell theory and makes sure nobody loses an eyebrow. Draco teaches observation, counters and how to know when someone has built the trap before you enter it. We ask Cedric whether he wants to handle the more advanced defensive magic. He knows more of it than any of us. Ron—"

Ron looked startled to be included. Harry considered him.

"Would you help with moving people and keeping a route clear? You got first-years through the train while everyone else was trying to squeeze the other way. You know what panic looks like before people admit it."

Ron stared at him.

"That sounded almost complimentary."

"Don't become accustomed to it."

"Wasn't planning to. But yes, I'll help. I'd rather know the plan before something explodes next time."

Draconia leaned back in her chair. "We need somewhere private," she said. "And we need to think about the parents. One frightened letter home could tell her everything. We cannot ask anyone to lie to their parents; they need to understand the risk before deciding to come."

"We find somewhere private," Harry said. "Then we check what the map shows. If someone gets hold of the map, hiding the door won't help if it shows every name inside."

Hermione frowned. "That is not a location."

"It's the first problem. Hogwarts has staircases that change direction, doors that vanish and at least one room pretending to be a wall. We shouldn't assume the map explains everything about them. We check it instead of guessing."

"Tomorrow, then," Draconia said. "Unused classrooms first. We can compare them with the map."

"And we test every possibility before inviting anyone," Hermione added. "For exits, listening charms, house-elf access, structural stability—"

"Ventilation," Ron added.

They looked at him.

"Thirty people practising Stunners in a windowless room. Seems relevant."

Hermione wrote it down.

"Cedric should choose the first older students," Harry said. "People trust him."

"People trust you," Hermione replied.

"Some do. Some think I enjoy having dramatic witnesses. Cedric makes it harder to call this Harry Potter assembling an army."

Ron snorted. "Good thing nobody would call it that."

All four went silent. Hermione slowly crossed out the heading at the top of her parchment. Harry leaned sideways to read it.

DEFENCE ASSOCIATION — INITIAL STRUCTURE

"Definitely not that," he said. "And changing the name doesn't make it allowed. We'll still be taking a risk if she refuses permission."

"It was provisional."

"It was evidence."

Draconia took the quill from her and wrote a new heading.

STUDY GROUP

Ron looked appalled. "Nobody will come to that."

"Exactly," Draconia said. "It is perfect."

Hermione reclaimed the quill. "We also need rules."

Harry groaned.

"Consent," she said, ignoring him. "Nobody practises a spell on another student without permission. Any person may stop an exercise. No hexing anyone because they are annoying. We start with low-power practice, supervised pairs and a clear stopping signal. Nobody gets thrown straight into a full Stunner."

"That last rule targets my teaching style," Draconia said.

"It targets your personality."

"Then it is discriminatory."

Ron looked at Harry. "Are they always like this?"

"It gets worse when they agree."

"We are not agreeing," both girls said.

Harry and Ron looked at each other. The laughter began quietly. Ron tried to hide his behind a piece of toast. Harry did not bother. Hermione threw a small sofa cushion at him; Draconia objected to its trajectory and redirected it with her wand, striking Ron instead. Ron retaliated. Within thirty seconds, the first planning meeting of the secret Defence group had dissolved into a battle conducted with pillows, parchment and one Cushion-Charming Jinx Draconia swore was part of the curriculum in respectable homes.

Harry ducked behind the armchair, laughing hard enough that his ribs hurt. For one bright, ordinary minute, nobody in the room was a witness, a survivor, an heir, a liability or a symbol in a Ministry argument. For that minute, being fifteen was enough. They were together. When the last pillow fell, Hermione retrieved her parchment from beneath Ron and smoothed it across her knees.

"Dumbledore this afternoon, first," Hermione said. "Then Sunday, we look for a room. We'll need Cedric's advice before we invite anyone, and we still have to test whatever we find."

Draconia nodded. "And no written list of names."

"Agreed."

Ron glanced towards the door. "What happens when Umbridge finds out?"

Not if. When. Harry looked at his hand. The two words he had written were fading, but he could still find each letter beneath the morning light.

I must.

He had not written the rest. It had seemed like such a small refusal at the time. Harry had a different sentence now.

"Then we make sure everyone knows enough to leave," he said.
