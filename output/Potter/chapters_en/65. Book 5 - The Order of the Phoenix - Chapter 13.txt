13

A silver tail brushed Harry's forehead before he opened his eyes. He woke beneath a tree which had not been there when he fell asleep. For one confused second, its branches seemed to fill the ceiling. Green needles hung above him. Small lights glowed between them, each flame contained inside a glass berry, and something silver moved through the upper boughs with the deliberate suspicion of a creature deciding whether he belonged there. Harry reached beneath his sleeve for the wand in its holster. The blanket caught his wrist. He pushed against it once before the smell of cold ashes reached him and he remembered the fire.

The previous night returned slowly: the sitting-room fire, Sirius beneath two blankets, Remus arriving after midnight with tea. Harry had meant to remain awake until the mugs were empty. At some point, Sirius must have stretched along the sofa again and Harry must have settled against it, because he now lay upon the rug with a cushion beneath his head and a blanket tucked under his chin. The tree had not been there when he fell asleep. Neither had the presents.

They occupied half the room. Parcels leaned against the hearth, climbed the legs of chairs and formed an unstable wall in front of the bookcase. Red paper marked the Weasleys. Silver and midnight-blue belonged to the Malfoys. Several gifts wore newspaper because Sirius had remembered wrapping paper only after the shops closed. Dobby had attached bows to objects which did not require them, including the fireguard, three spoons and a sleeping Fred. Harry let his hand fall from the holster. Home, he remembered. The thought did not arrive naturally enough to avoid effort. It arrived anyway. He turned his head.

Sirius remained asleep above him, one arm hanging over the edge of the sofa. Remus had taken the chair beside the cold fireplace and folded himself into it with the uncomfortable skill of a man accustomed to accepting whatever rest a room offered. A book lay open upon his chest. His fingers still marked the page. Beyond the windows, Christmas morning waited beneath new snow. The wards traced pale gold through the orchard. Every branch carried white, and the path to the gate had disappeared except where fox prints crossed it in a narrow line. Harry listened. No snake moved inside the walls. No voice borrowed his mind. The scar beneath his fringe was only skin. He named what was his.

Breath. Hands. Weight against the floor. Sirius's fingers near his hair. Remus asleep three feet away. Hawthorn Cottage. Christmas morning. He was halfway through sitting up when Sirius's hand closed gently around the back of his jumper.

"Absolutely not."

Harry looked over his shoulder. "You're asleep."

"Guardian magic."

"You grabbed my clothes."

"A very old and respected branch of guardian magic."

Sirius opened one eye.

"What time is it?"

Harry checked the clock. Its hands had been charmed overnight. One pointed to TOO EARLY. The other pointed to CHRISTMAS, which was not a time and appeared pleased with itself.

"Apparently it's Christmas o'clock."

"A perfectly respectable time to stay in bed. Lie down."

"I'm awake."

"So am I now. Try not to spread it any further."

Harry shifted until he sat with his back against the sofa. Sirius's hand moved automatically into his hair, found a knot and abandoned the attempt. Remus spoke without opening his eyes.

"Some of us were awake until one in the morning reassuring an unreasonable child."

"I was the reasonable one," Harry said.

"He means me," Sirius replied.

"I mean both of you."

The kitchen door opened. Dobby entered wearing six knitted hats and carrying a tray large enough to obstruct his entire body. Steam rose above it. The smell of cinnamon reached Harry before the elf emerged around the side.

"Harry Potter is awake!"

The cottage answered. Floorboards thudded overhead. Someone struck a wall. Ginny shouted that Fred had stolen her left slipper. George shouted back that seasonal law permitted it. A door opened hard enough to rebound from its frame. Winky appeared from the kitchen, saw Dobby's hats and informed him that dignity had not been abolished for Christmas. Sirius closed his eye again.

"We could pretend to be dead."

Harry looked at the stairs as the Weasleys descended with enough noise for twice their number.

"They would check."

"Not if we arrange ourselves convincingly. Remus, stop breathing."

"No."

"You never support my parenting decisions."

The room filled before Sirius could make a second attempt. Harry drew his feet beneath the blanket as people stepped over the rug, remembering that nobody here would make him apologise for having fallen asleep in the way. Sirius kept one hand upon his shoulder until Harry was properly sitting up, then passed him the cup Dobby had left nearest the sofa. Ron came down first with his hair pointing in every direction. Harry heard the others laughing on the stairs before he could see them; he drew the blanket over his knees and let himself stay where he was, rather than scramble out of their way. Ginny followed wearing one blue slipper and one bare foot. Fred and George arrived wrapped together inside a blanket they claimed had attacked them. Neville appeared behind them, blinking through sleep and holding the bannister as though uncertain whether he had been invited into the morning or merely swept along by it. He had gone back to his grandmother after the hospital visit, then returned on Christmas Eve with an overnight bag and enough warnings about the twins to make Fred look offended. Augusta had agreed he could stay for a few days; Neville still looked surprised whenever someone included him without asking. Draconia descended last.

She wore dark green pyjamas beneath a long grey dressing gown. Her hair had not been brushed and fell loose over one shoulder, softer without the usual precision. The sight affected Harry with enough force that he forgot the room contained witnesses. Draconia noticed where he was looking.

"Good morning," she said.

Her composure survived. The colour at the tops of her ears did not.

"Morning," Harry answered.

Ron looked between them.

"Do either of you need a separate room, or can presents continue?"

Draconia's gaze moved to him. "You could start by looking at your own presents."

"I'm staying with my family."

"Then you could try not providing a commentary."

Harry smiled before he could stop himself. Narcissa entered from the kitchen carrying plates. She wore blue silk and the expression of someone who had been awake for hours correcting everybody else's preparation. Lucius followed with a pot of coffee in one hand and a Daily Prophet in the other. The front page had been folded away from the room.

"No newspapers," Narcissa said.

"I am not reading it aloud."

"You look as though you're about to announce something dreadful. Put it down until we've eaten."

Lucius considered this accusation, placed the paper face-down upon a shelf and poured coffee. Molly remained at St Mungo's with Arthur. Bill and Charlie had taken the earliest Floo to the hospital. They would return if the healers allowed Arthur home; if not, Christmas would divide itself between Hawthorn and a ward on the fourth floor. Harry had learnt not to ask which possibility people expected. Molly had promised to call when the healers decided. Until then, there were mugs to hold and voices rising from the kitchen. Harry tried to attend to those instead of looking towards the hearth whenever its flames shifted.

Before the Grangers arrived, Sirius escaped to the kitchen with Harry under the pretence of finding more milk. Winky had already filled three jugs. She looked at both of them, decided against asking and pushed a plate of cinnamon toast towards Harry. Sirius leaned against the dresser.

"How did you sleep?"

"On the floor, apparently."

"A traditional family arrangement. I did offer you a cushion."

Harry took a piece of toast. The kitchen windows were pale with the early light, and he could hear somebody upstairs trying to persuade Neville that an enchanted bow belonged upon his head.

"No nightmares," he said.

Sirius nodded without turning the news into a celebration Harry might have to repeat tomorrow. He poured milk into his own tea, thought about it and added more.

"Good."

"You didn't have to stay. Both of you."

"I was in my own sitting room, Harry. I'd have had a rather long journey to get further away."

Harry looked at him over the toast. Sirius gave in with a small lift of one shoulder.

"I wanted to stay. So did Remus. He has a bed; he's quite capable of finding it."

There was no useful answer to that except believing it. Harry ate while Sirius attempted to steal a piece of toast and Winky struck his knuckles with the flat of a serving spoon. The protest he made brought Ron to the doorway. Ron assessed the plate, took two pieces and retreated before anyone could discuss his methods. Sirius watched him go.

"That's what comes of raising children with no respect for authority."

"Did you raise Ron?"

"I am claiming a general influence."

Harry laughed into his cup. From the sitting room, Draconia called to ask whether he intended to miss every present while helping Sirius find milk. He went back carrying a jug, although the original one still stood upon the table. Hermione arrived with her parents shortly after eight, using the visitor hearth Sirius had arranged for them in London. The Floo delivered Mr Granger upright, Mrs Granger laughing and Hermione on one knee with ash across her nose. Crookshanks emerged from the flames behind them, surveyed the unfamiliar number of people and immediately climbed a curtain. Hermione saw Harry beneath the tree. She smiled. For once Harry could see no hesitation in it. She was looking at him, rather than measuring the space between him and Draconia. He tried not to turn that moment into a promise that every later one would be easy. The sight loosened something in Harry which he had not known remained braced after the library. She crossed the room and hugged him.

"Happy Christmas."

"You have soot on your face."

"I came through a fireplace to see you. You could say Happy Christmas first."

"Happy Christmas," Harry said, and wiped the ash with one thumb.

Hermione became still. The room did not. Fred and George had begun distributing parcels according to a method which involved throwing them. Ginny discovered her missing slipper around Dobby's neck. Mr Granger was attempting to explain dental fillings to Lucius, who looked both appalled and interested. A hospital messenger parchment hung above the mantelpiece. Arthur had agreed that Molly could send updates for the household to read; the latest said he had eaten breakfast and was waiting for the healer. Harry had read it twice already. For a second, Harry and Hermione occupied a quieter space inside it.

"There," he said.

Hermione touched the place his thumb had passed.

"Thank you."

Draconia reached them carrying three cups of tea. She gave one to Hermione first.

"Your cat is dismantling the west curtain."

"He's probably frightened. There are rather a lot of people."

"He has swallowed a tassel."

Hermione looked towards the curtain. "Crookshanks. That isn't breakfast."

Harry accepted the third cup. The three of them stood together while the household arranged itself around presents. Hermione asked where she should put her parents' gifts. Draconia moved a chair to make room beneath the tree. Harry carried the parcels while they disagreed about whether one intended for Dobby could reasonably be identified by its size. For a few minutes there was no conversation he needed to prepare for, only wrapping paper to hold and tea going cold in his hands.

Sirius insisted that Harry wait until everyone had settled before opening his gift. This required hiding it because Harry had become skilled at identifying Sirius's parcels through poor wrapping and excessive use of string. The package arrived after the first round of presents had produced torn paper, ribbon and several objects Fred denied purchasing. More parcels still waited under the branches, but Sirius wanted Harry to have room for this one without somebody throwing a box over his head. It was flat, broad and wrapped in brown paper decorated with hand-drawn antlers.

"Remus drew those," Sirius said.

"I did not."

"He supervised."

"I was asleep."

"Moral supervision."

Harry untied the string. A book lay beneath the paper.

The leather cover had darkened with age. No title marked it. Four sets of initials had once been scratched near the bottom and later rubbed almost smooth. Harry could still make out J.P., S.B., R.L. and the curved edge of a final P.

His thumb stopped upon it. Peter had touched this book. So had James. Sirius watched his thumb stop over the last initial.

"I nearly removed his initials."

"Why didn't you?"

"Because James had those years with him too. I didn't want to pretend they hadn't happened."

Harry looked at him. Sirius sat cross-legged upon the sofa, hands clasped between his knees. No performance remained in his face.

"I still had those years," he said. "So did James. I don't want every picture of them to have a hole in it because of what Peter did later."

Harry opened the book. Photographs filled the first page. They were not the formal school pictures Harry knew from albums and newspaper archives. These moved too quickly and badly. In one, James attempted to lean against a tree and missed because the tree had shifted away. In another, Remus slept over three open books while Sirius balanced quills in his hair. Lily occupied the corner of a photograph pretending to read. Every few seconds she lowered the book and smiled at James when she believed the camera pointed elsewhere. Harry could not breathe normally. He turned the page.

His mother danced barefoot in the Gryffindor common room, surrounded by people Harry did not know. James stood upon a table applauding. Peter laughed from an armchair. A dark-haired girl seized Sirius by the tie and dragged him into the dance. Sirius objected for approximately two seconds before lifting her from the floor. Harry looked from the photograph to the man upon the sofa. Sirius had become interested in a loose thread upon his sleeve.

"Who is she?"

Remus lowered his tea.

"Marlene McKinnon," Sirius said.

Harry heard the name and looked up. Around him, the wrapping continued to tear and the twins continued to argue over a parcel, but the adults closest to Sirius had gone quiet. Narcissa glanced towards Lucius. Remus's attention remained carefully upon his cup. Nobody else appeared to notice. Harry looked back at the photograph. Marlene had sharp cheekbones, untidy dark hair and a smile that seemed to begin as a challenge. She landed after Sirius spun her, struck his shoulder and kissed his cheek while he was laughing.

"Were you together?"

"Frequently."

"That isn't an answer."

"It is the only one we ever managed."

Sirius reached across and turned the next page.

Marlene appeared again beside Lily at an Order gathering. She wore scarlet robes and held a bottle by the neck. On the wall behind them, somebody had written NO MARTYRS BEFORE DINNER.

"The papers said her family died," Harry said.

He remembered the name from old reports Hermione collected during third year.

"Most of them did." Sirius's voice lost its warmth. "Marlene survived the first attack. Dumbledore helped her leave Britain before the second. I reached the house afterwards. I never found anything which proved she was among the dead, but by the end of that night I believed it. Then Azkaban gave me plenty of time to keep believing it."

He ran a thumb over the edge of the photograph.

"She spent years believing I betrayed James and Lily. By the time Peter confessed, she had another name and a life abroad. Being told I was innocent did not undo all those years at once."

"Have you spoken to her?"

"Once. In November."

Harry looked up from the album. "I thought she'd died. Those reports—"

"So did I. The name in those reports belonged to a person I couldn't find. She was living under another one."

"And Dumbledore knew?"

Sirius ran a thumb along the album's cloth spine. "He knew where she'd gone. She had asked him to keep her new identity private. He told her when I was cleared, but left it to her to contact me. I'm not especially pleased with how long I was allowed to think she was dead." Sirius's mouth tightened. "That is a conversation for him. This one was her choice."

The distinction left Harry quiet. He could imagine a letter which changed everything without supplying the first word of the reply.

"An Order contact in France asked whether I would take a call," Sirius said. "She still passes information to people here. I hadn't known whose name was behind it. She chose to make the call herself in November. Neither of us was particularly good at it."

"Only once?"

"Harry."

The warning was quiet. Sirius looked down at the album, and Harry recognised the effort it took him to keep talking about something he had not yet worked out for himself. He closed the album gently.

"Thank you."

Sirius's shoulders lowered.

"There are blank pages at the back," he said. "I thought you might want to add some of your own."

Harry opened to them. Twelve clean sheets waited after the past. He touched the corner of the first, already thinking of the picture he would like to put there: everyone crowded round the tree, with Dobby's bow still attached to Fred. Sirius had not handed him a finished account of the family. There was room to add what happened next. An owl struck the sitting-room window. Everyone turned. The bird recovered upon the sill with the offended dignity of something betrayed by transparent glass. A narrow parcel hung beneath it, addressed in unfamiliar handwriting. Sirius went very still. Remus stood to open the latch. The owl entered, circled the room and dropped the parcel directly into Sirius's lap. No sender's name appeared upon the paper. Only two initials marked the corner.

M.M.

Harry looked at the album. Sirius looked at Harry.

"Not one word."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your eyebrows did."

"I've been told my face is expressive."

"I can still send you outside to make a snowman."

Remus examined the parcel over Sirius's shoulder. "France."

"Traitor."

"The postmark is visible."

"You could have respected my privacy invisibly."

Sirius did not open the parcel while anyone watched. He kept one hand over it for the rest of the morning.

Choosing Christmas presents had occupied Harry for most of December. He had made a list, lost it, found it beneath a Defence essay and decided that the list itself was part of the problem. Hermione's gift required evidence of thought without implying that she should perform more work. Draconia's required intimacy without making the intimacy public. Sirius claimed both requirements were impossible and suggested fireworks. Narcissa rejected fireworks on the grounds that neither girl needed encouragement to associate Harry with preventable injury. Remus listened to the argument for ten minutes before asking Harry what each girl did when she was frightened. That question helped. Hermione opened hers first.

The wooden case contained six Muggle fountain pens, each restored by Mr Granger and charmed by Harry so that any colour of ink could be changed with a tap. The lid held a sheet of dark glass. A page laid against it copied its writing once onto a hidden roll of parchment, preserving a record even if the original was confiscated. Harry had made the copied ink vanish from view when the case shut, and spent three evenings checking that Hermione's chosen touch would reveal it again. Hermione stopped breathing.

"The copy only opens for you," Harry said quickly. "You have to mark a page before it will keep it. I didn't want it taking letters or anything you hadn't chosen. I thought, with Umbridge always confiscating your notes—"

"Harry."

"Yes?"

"Stop explaining before you make it less perfect."

She lifted one pen. Her initials had been engraved near the nib by hand. The H leaned slightly because Harry's first attempt at metalwork had gone wrong.

"You made this?"

"Your dad helped with the pens. The crooked bit is mine."

Mr Granger raised his cup. "He learnt quickly."

Hermione put the pen back in its place and hugged Harry so hard that his glasses pressed into her hair. He had practised what to say if she thought the case unnecessary. He had prepared nothing for the way her hands closed round the back of his jumper.

"I love it," she whispered.

Harry understood that she meant the case. The quietness of her voice still made him hold her more carefully, remembering how much she had managed to say without asking him to find an answer for all of it. Harry held her for one heartbeat longer than he needed to.

"Good."

When they separated, Hermione's eyes shone. She did not look embarrassed by it. Draconia's parcel was smaller. She removed the silver paper with exact care and found a black lacquer box. Inside lay three sticks of sealing wax—green, gold and clear—and a seal cut from pale stone. The face carried no crest. It carried three small symbols: a serpent, a lion and an otter arranged around an empty centre. Draconia touched it without lifting it. Harry's confidence vanished.

"I know you already have a seal," he said. "But that's your family's, and I thought you might want one for our letters. Or plans. Things we made ourselves." He glanced towards Hermione. "She had to redraw the otter. Mine looked like a sick ferret."

"It had emotional value," Hermione said.

"It had four legs of different lengths."

Draconia looked up. Her eyes had become too bright.

"Things that belong to us," she repeated.

"Letters. Plans. Anything we don't want altered. The clear wax changes colour if someone opens it."

She lifted the seal. The stone fitted her hand.

"You had this made without a name."

"Names attract attention."

"You have been listening to me."

"Occasionally. It's exhausting."

Draconia laughed once. It came with a breath that almost became something else. She closed her fingers around the seal.

"Thank you, Harry."

She rarely used his first name in a room full of people. Harry heard it clearly despite the conversation around them. It sounded different from Potter in a corridor, softer without becoming uncertain. Draconia did not embrace him. Not there. Instead she extended her left hand between them, palm upward. Harry placed his hand into it. Her fingers closed. That was enough for the room. Her own gift to Harry waited beneath the tree. Harry recognised the green centre of their alarm coin, though almost nothing around it remained the same. Narcissa had asked to borrow his half the previous afternoon to finish testing a modification, assuring him that his mirror would remain within reach while he was at Hawthorn. He had expected another coin. A thin silver cage now held the green core as a pendant, the old fracture filled with something bright enough to resemble trapped lightning. A black cord passed through the setting. Beside it lay a second stone, smaller and grey. Harry stared.

"That's the part that cracked in the graveyard."

"It overloaded," Draconia said. "The summer repair kept our coins working, but Mother wanted a better warning charm. These replace that pair. We kept the cores and changed almost everything around them. Mother rebuilt the casings. Father helped with the warnings."

Lucius looked over his coffee. "Under supervision."

"Extensive supervision," Narcissa corrected.

Draconia ignored them.

"The green one warns you about me," Draconia said. "The grey one warns me about you. They don't pass pain between us, and they don't carry messages. We still use the mirror or the school coins for that."

Harry looked at the silver holding the cracked stone together.

"And this is meant to help find us now? The coins couldn't do that."

"It can give the other wearer a direction to try, if the signal gets through. Only if we agree to that part."

"How do I agree when I'm unconscious?"

"You choose beforehand." She looked towards Hermione. "We wrote the choices down so nobody has to remember what you said."

Hermione passed him the folded parchment. Harry unfolded it, expecting three pages and finding one. The conditions were written plainly: unconsciousness, being taken, or confirmed magical interference. There was space beneath each for a different answer.

"It isn't a listening charm," Hermione added. "There's no recording."

Narcissa leaned forward a little.

"And it isn't certain to recognise every danger. A strong curse may register when exhaustion does not. Any direction comes from the other stone, not from the person. If it has been removed or left behind, that signal cannot tell you where its owner is. Concealing magic or wards can obstruct the location response. It is something else to try, Harry. It cannot replace calling for help, and a quiet stone cannot prove the other person is safe."

Draconia's thumb moved over the grey stone.

"I just want another way to know when I should," she said.

The admission was quieter than the explanation. Harry looked at her, then back at the choices beneath his fingers. Harry picked up the stones. The green one warmed in his palm. He remembered it burning against his chest while Voldemort stood above him. He remembered the burn upon Draconia's hand after the Tournament. She had told him what the old charm carried back to her while he was in the graveyard.

"This won't make you feel it?"

"No."

"Certain?"

"We tested it."

"How?"

"We tested the warnings with controlled charms," Narcissa said. "No injuries, no possession. We used a calibrated signal to imitate the kinds of interference the warning recognises. That tested its response to those signals; it did not establish that it will recognise every real attack."

Draconia looked towards her father.

"His sleeve caught fire."

"A fault in the test apparatus," Lucius said.

"You refused to change your jumper," Narcissa reminded him. "The next test went rather better without the silver cuff links."

Harry laughed. Narcissa showed him the small catch which disabled the location response, then let him switch it on and off himself. With both stones still on the table, Dobby carried one to the far end of the room while Narcissa applied the test signal. Harry watched the faint pointer turn inside its partner. It was an ordinary demonstration of a limited signal, and he was glad of that.

Draconia lifted the cord and fastened it around his neck. Her fingers passed beneath his hair. The green stone settled against his chest, cool at first and then warming to his skin. She held the grey stone. Harry turned the parchment towards Draconia so they could read it together. The idea still frightened him. It also meant that, if he could not speak, she might have something to try before discovering an empty bed or unanswered mirror.

"Unconscious or taken," Harry said. "You can try to find me. I want the same for you, if you want it."

Hermione marked the parchment. Draconia's gaze did not leave Harry's.

"And possession?"

The question barely crossed the space between them. Harry thought of ink disappearing into blank paper. Draconia unable to account for where she had been, defending a book which had begun taking things from her before she understood what it wanted. A snake's satisfaction lodging behind his teeth.

"That too. If something is using me, I want you to have somewhere to start."

"Both ways," Draconia said. "Yes."

Draconia closed her eyes for one second. When she opened them, she fastened the grey stone around her own neck. The cracked green charm pulsed once against Harry's heart.

Hermione's present for Harry made music. The object inside the box was rectangular, black and ordinary enough that almost every witch or wizard in the room assumed it had not yet been enchanted. Two padded earphones folded over the top. A transparent window showed a cassette already fitted inside. Harry lifted it with both hands.

"It's a Walkman."

Ron leaned over his shoulder. "A what?"

"It plays music."

"The wireless does that."

Hermione looked offended. "A wireless plays what the station chooses. This plays what Harry chooses."

She showed him the tapes beneath it. Each had been labelled in her handwriting.

LOUD ENOUGH TO INTERRUPT THINKING.

QUIET ENOUGH TO SLEEP.

SONGS MY FATHER INSISTS ARE IMPORTANT.

SONGS MY MOTHER SAYS ARE ACTUALLY GOOD.

The last tape read FOR JOURNEYS HOME.

Harry ran his thumb across the label.

"Will it work at Hogwarts?"

"Mr Weasley and I modified it," Hermione said.

The messenger parchment above the mantelpiece fluttered as another line from Molly arrived: Arthur was asking whether anyone had tried the breakfast rolls. Harry smiled at it. Even from hospital, he sounded ready to join them.

"Dad's been in hospital," Ron said. "Did you have him dismantling things from bed?"

"He helped me plan it before the holidays. Your dad sent a few more notes after he woke up."

"That sounds like him," Ron said.

Mr Granger examined the silver strip along the battery compartment. "I provided the fire extinguisher."

Hermione took the Walkman from Harry, placed the earphones over his head and pressed a button. Music entered quietly. It was not magical. No instruments materialised above him. The singer did not address Harry by name or alter the words to suit the room. A guitar began in one ear, drums in the other, and a human voice carried something sad without explaining why it had the right. The cottage receded. Not entirely. Harry still saw Draconia speaking to Narcissa near the tree. He saw Ron attempt to open a box with his teeth. Sirius watched from the sofa, the parcel from France hidden beneath one thigh. But the sound created a boundary which belonged to Harry alone. Hermione lifted one side of the headphones and placed it over her own ear. They listened together.

Harry did not know the song. Hermione mouthed several words without making a sound. He watched her recognise what came next and realised she had selected every track, arranged them and considered which order might carry him through a night. The gift was not silence. It was company which did not require him to speak.

"I love it," he said.

Hermione smiled.

"Good."

The same word he had given her. It held more now.

Arthur came home at half past two. The healers permitted it because his latest tests showed the venom responding to treatment. Hawthorn's wards had been inspected, and the potion batches Narcissa helped prepare had been checked by the hospital before being added to his supply. Molly had promised to follow the written instructions about rest, dressing changes and calling for a healer. Arthur had promised to ask before attempting to stand. Nobody at St Mungo's appeared to believe the last condition. They sent an enchanted chair whose brakes locked and warning bell rang whenever Arthur tried to stand without support. He had agreed to use it until the weakness in his leg improved; agreement did not prevent him complaining about the bell. He arrived through the Floo surrounded by green fire. Molly came first, carrying potions and six pages of instructions. Bill followed backwards, guiding the chair by its handles while Arthur protested that he had walked from one end of the ward to the other that morning.

"You reached the door," Molly said.

"The door was at the other end."

"Of your room."

"Distance remains distance."

The room moved towards him. Ginny reached the chair before it cleared the hearth. Fred and George attacked from either side with enough care to reveal how frightened they still were. Ron stopped one pace away, then put both arms around his father without speaking. Arthur closed his eyes. Harry remained near the tree. This time the distance did not come from guilt. Arthur had already named the difference between what entered Harry's mind and what Harry chose to do. The family simply possessed the first right to have him alive. Molly looked over Ron's shoulder.

"You too."

Harry glanced behind him.

"She means you," Ginny said.

"I was checking."

"Coward," Fred told him.

George caught Harry's sleeve and pulled him into the collection of arms. Arthur laughed, winced and informed everyone that ribs were being treated as optional. Molly attempted to release him. Arthur kept an arm around Ron and Harry until Molly reminded him that he could hug them again once he had room to breathe. Fred disentangled his sleeve from George's, and the family drew back by reluctant inches. For several seconds, the enchanted chair protested beneath all of them. Then it gave up and locked every wheel. Lunch became dinner because nobody had eaten at a sensible hour.

The kitchen table extended into the sitting room. Additional chairs emerged from cupboards and argued about where their legs belonged. Dobby and Winky produced food faster than surfaces could hold it. Mrs Granger brought a trifle which attempted to float after George added a Levitation Charm to the bowl; Mr Granger caught it against the ceiling with a serving spoon. Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia. Across from him, Sirius fed Arthur pieces of roast potato whenever Molly looked away. Arthur accepted them with the concentration of a man participating in organised crime.

"Small portions," Molly said without turning.

Sirius withdrew his hand. Arthur continued chewing.

"Remarkable detection," Lucius observed.

"She developed it after raising seven children," Arthur said.

"And you still attempt it," Percy said from the doorway.

Silence crossed the table. Percy stood just inside the sitting room wearing an ordinary brown coat. He carried no Ministry folder. The absence of his badge looked deliberate. Molly's serving spoon lowered. Arthur attempted to stand. The chair's brakes locked with a metallic snap. Molly put a hand on Arthur's shoulder, and he sank back before the warning bell could join the conversation.

"I was told you were home," Percy said.

"You were told correctly," Arthur replied.

No one moved. Percy's gaze passed over the crowded table: Sirius at one end, Lucius at the other, Muggles, elves, Malfoys, children from different Houses and Harry seated where family should have created a boundary.

"I cannot stay long."

"Then sit quickly," Arthur said.

Ginny moved her chair. Percy took the place beside her. He folded his coat across his knees instead of giving it to anyone, as though he still needed to be able to leave without making an announcement. Conversation did not recover at once. Harry heard the alarm from the dining room, and a red line crossed his view of the classroom. Snape called his name. It reached him at the same instant he remembered the word.

"Anchor," Harry said.

The memory released him as Snape cut the viewing charm. Harry had spoken; the adult had acted. He did not have time to work out which had happened first.

He struck the dining-room floor upon one knee. Sirius caught the back of his robes. Remus seized the Pensieve before it overturned. Snape held the collar dark with his wand while Sirius pressed the separate release to keep it disabled. The projection vanished. He closed the detector, though red light escaped its seams for another moment and painted the edge of the basin. Harry dragged air into lungs which felt too small.

"Mine," he said.

"What?" Sirius demanded.

"Body. Mine. Fifteen. Hawthorn."

His mouth tasted of butterbeer he had never drunk. His left knee hurt in two different ages.

"James was—"

Harry stopped. Remus knelt before him. The older face looked nothing like the seventeen-year-old body Harry had occupied and exactly like it.

"You left when you chose," Remus said.

"The detector went red."

Snape opened one side panel. The red thread had vanished. A faint stain remained upon the innermost glass.

"The instrument registered pressure for less than a second."

Harry heard footsteps beyond the doorway. Snape turned his head.

"The exercise has ended," he called. "The detector is no longer registering pressure. Give him a moment."

"We're here," Hermione answered.

Harry could not yet find a reply. Sirius squeezed his shoulder, and the footsteps stopped outside rather than coming closer. Sirius drew his wand.

"Voldemort?"

"The instrument cannot identify a mind. It recorded a pattern consistent with external pressure, distinct from the viewing charm. We compare that with what Potter felt. We do not assign a caster from a colour."

"Did he see it?" Harry asked.

Snape looked at him.

"We cannot establish that. The reading was brief, but duration does not tell us how much an intruder might perceive. We will report the sequence to Dumbledore, with Potter present."

"Which impression?"

"We can describe where the reading began. We cannot tell you which part, if any, another mind received."

James smiling. Remus loved him. Harry wanted him. Harry feared that meeting of feelings had given an intruder something to use. He had no way to tell whether it had; the uncertainty made James's smile harder to remember without the alarm following it. Harry looked towards Remus.

"He knows."

"He already knew you cared for your father," Sirius said.

"Not James. Remus. He might have felt—"

Remus's hands settled around Harry's.

"Look at me."

Harry did.

"He may have known we were close long before today. Being frightened for me isn't something you did wrong in the memory. You don't have to stop loving people to make this work."

"Then what is it?"

"Remembering you were watching, and leaving when you wanted to. You managed that. Even when it became difficult."

Harry looked at their joined hands. They were not the hands from the memory. New scars crossed them. The knuckles were more pronounced. One thumbnail had split near the edge. Time had changed the body without removing the person.

"You thought you were borrowing that Christmas," Harry said.

Remus became still.

"You felt like they had allowed you into it and could decide not to."

Sirius made a quiet sound. Remus looked down.

"That was not the lesson."

"It was in the memory."

"Recorded thought is not objective truth."

"It was true to you."

Remus released one breath through his nose.

"Yes."

"Were you wrong?"

Harry thought of Remus saying he might be sent away for Christmas, and of the relief when he had finally appeared with three cups of tea. He wondered how many times Remus had gone quietly because nobody had realised he wanted to be asked to stay. Sirius answered.

"Completely."

Remus glanced at him.

"Thank you for the clinical contribution."

"Any time."

Harry pushed himself into the chair.

"Why that memory?"

Remus looked towards the Pensieve.

"Because I wanted you to see them happy," Remus said.

Harry looked at him. The admission was so much simpler than the lesson had been that he did not immediately know what to say. Remus placed the closed vial upon the table.

"I told myself it would help with the exercise. There are things in that memory I thought you might recognise, including how certain I was that friendship could be taken back. But I wanted to show you the afternoon as well. That mattered more to me than I admitted."

Sirius had stopped smiling. He rested one hand against the chair behind Harry.

"You could have just shown me," Harry said.

"Yes. From outside, without asking you to separate yourself from it. I should have done that first and left it there today."

Harry looked towards the basin. James had stood on the other side of it minutes earlier, as alive as anybody in the dining room. Leaving the memory had hurt. He did not want Remus to believe that made it something he should never have offered.

"I'm glad I saw them."

"That doesn't make every part of how we did it right."

"I know. But I'm still glad."

Remus nodded slowly. For once Harry could see him accepting an answer rather than helping someone else reach one.

"Next time," Harry said, "could we just watch? Without the collar."

"Yes."

"I'd like to see what happened to the tree."

Sirius let out a laugh.

"Lily made us find a real one. James attempted to improve the process. We lost a window."

"Two," Remus said.

"One was already weak."

Sirius barked a laugh. Even Snape's mouth altered slightly, though it might have been disapproval.

"No more today," Snape said. "We moved to a much more difficult memory before establishing what one simple exercise had cost him. I should not have approved the restricted view. We should have left it at watching the projection."

Remus nodded. "It did."

The admission arrived without defence. Harry watched him put the vial away. Remus had not asked Harry to make him feel better about the mistake. That left room to be glad about the memory and shaken by it without choosing which response to offer first.

"Did I fail?" he asked.

Snape closed the panel upon the detector.

"You identified the differences we asked for, recognised when they became harder to maintain and used the agreed word. We also ended the charm. That is the purpose of having observers; your safety does not depend on a perfect performance." Snape closed the case. "If you require a more ornamental answer, ask Black."

"So no."

"No, Potter. You did not fail. The exercise was harder than it should have been."

Harry smiled faintly. Sirius helped him stand.

"Shall I let the girls in?"

Harry looked at the green stone beneath his shirt. It told him nothing about the warning its partner had given.

"I'll tell them," he said.

Draconia came through the open dining-room doorway with her wand drawn. The grey stone at her throat had returned to its ordinary colour. Hermione followed carrying the written procedure. Ron came behind both with a chess knight still in one hand.

"One second," Snape said before anybody spoke. "The recorded pressure ended as the viewing charm closed. Potter is answering us and knows where he is. Let him speak before adding questions."

Draconia lowered her wand by an inch.

Hermione looked at Harry. "Tell me where you are."

"Hawthorn. Dining room. It's still December twenty-eighth. Snape still thinks I'm irritating."

"Consistently," Snape said.

Hermione let out a breath, though she kept watching Harry. He gave the current phrase as well. She asked a question not written in the procedure.

"What did you draw before the otter? Don't ask Draco."

"A sick ferret."

"All right." Hermione let the answer settle. It was a detail to help Harry find his own surroundings again, not proof that no other mind could know it.

Draconia crossed the room and touched two fingers to the green stone. Harry could feel her hand tremble against his shirt. Harry watched her face.

"I stopped," he said.

"I know."

"How?"

"The warning stopped before I reached the door. That told me less than hearing you speak."

She touched his cheek.

"And the adults are telling me what happened. We still have to check, but you don't have to tell me alone."

Hermione found Harry awake after midnight. The cottage had finally become quiet. Arthur slept in the study, where Dobby had put a temporary bed within easy reach of the downstairs bathroom. Molly sat beside him. The desk had been moved against the wall, and Sirius's half-finished letter to France now occupied a kitchen drawer. The twins had been separated after convincing Neville that mistletoe could detect lies. Crookshanks occupied the top of the Christmas tree and refused negotiation. Harry sat upon the floor of his room with his back against the bed. The Walkman lay in his lap. Music moved through the headphones at a volume low enough that he heard Hermione knock before opening the door. She wore a blue dressing gown and carried nineteen pages. Harry removed one earpiece.

"That looks dangerous."

"It is the revised differentiation protocol."

"More dangerous than I thought."

Hermione left the door ajar and sat beside him. The light from the landing made a narrow strip across the rug. Harry moved his feet so she could settle without resting upon the Walkman's cord, then turned the volume down another notch. The pages had been written with the new fountain pen. Blue ink marked ordinary steps. Red marked failures. Green identified alternatives. Purple comments occupied every margin and several places which were not margins but had been conquered.

"You made this since dinner?"

"Since the lesson."

"That was before dinner."

"Yes."

Harry took the parchment from her.

The seventh page contained a diagram of the Pensieve, three separate emergency signals and a chart titled DEGREES OF OWNERSHIP CONFUSION.

"When did you sleep?"

"Last night."

"That wasn't what I asked."

Hermione looked towards the window. Snow had begun again. Small flakes touched the glass and vanished.

"The detector went red," she said.

"For less than a second."

"That is sufficient time for magical contact."

"I know."

"If the charm had not changed back—"

"It did."

"You remembered the word. What if you hadn't?"

Harry heard what frightened her. Not that the procedure had failed. That it had succeeded because a frightened person remembered every necessary step at precisely the right moment. Hermione had helped build a system, and she was still frightened by the part no diagram could make certain. Harry could hear it in how carefully she said the word remembered.

"I might forget next time," he said. "That's why Sirius and Snape had the release. They weren't waiting for me to get everything right."

Her face tightened.

"That's why I'm trying to give you fewer things to remember. I don't want the whole plan to depend on you being able to speak at the worst moment."

"You can't make me impossible to hurt."

"Of course I know that."

"Sometimes I'm not sure you do."

Hermione reached for the parchment. Harry let her take it, though he kept watching her face rather than the pages.

"There are still things to fix," she said.

"Can you show me tomorrow?"

"Harry, I need to make sure—"

"You gave me music for when my mind won't stop. I'm applying the principle."

"The protocol is not intrusive thought."

"Page sixteen has a heading called secondary failure cascade. That can't have made you feel better."

She gave him a weary look. "It isn't there to make me feel better. It's there because the mirror might fail while somebody's trying to reach the door."

Harry held out the loose earpiece. Hermione glared at it. Then she accepted. They sat shoulder to shoulder while the tape turned. The song was quiet enough to leave room around it. A piano repeated four notes. A woman sang about somewhere she had left and did not know whether she wished to find again. Hermione's breathing slowed. At first she kept one finger beneath the top sheet, marking the place she had been about to explain. Harry did not ask her to put it away. The piano continued, a soft unevenness from the old tape beneath the notes, and after a while she let the pages rest upon her knees. Harry nodded towards the pages. "Leave them here tonight? We can read them in the morning, and you can point out everything I've missed."

Hermione hesitated, then put them beside the Walkman. He slid them under the bed only after she nodded, leaving one corner visible so neither of them would forget.

"You could have used the bedside table," she said.

"I know. But you'd be able to see page sixteen from there."

"I could Summon it."

"You could," he agreed. "I'd rather you stayed for the music."

She did not. After a minute, her head settled against his shoulder.

"Ron said he interrupted you," she said.

Harry looked towards the open door.

"He brought trifle."

"That was his defence too."

There was amusement in her voice, and something he could not answer by making Ron the subject.

"Did you kiss her?" Hermione asked.

Harry had promised honesty.

"Yes."

The answer moved through her body. He felt the small pause in breathing, the decision not to sit away.

"Was it because you were frightened?"

"No."

That hurt more. Harry hated knowing it and did not replace the answer with something kinder and false. Hermione listened to the song until it ended.

"I'm glad," she said eventually.

"You don't have to be."

"I can be glad and still find it difficult. I'm discovering I'm quite good at that."

Harry watched the wheels turning behind the Walkman's small window. He had wanted being honest to stop causing pain once they became better at it. Perhaps it only meant nobody had to suffer the additional hurt of finding out by accident. He was not sure that was enough. He was sure he owed it to her.

"I don't know how to make this easier," he said.

Hermione lifted her head. He did not want to keep her waiting for a promise he could not make. He also could not bear the idea that stepping towards Draconia required stepping away from every ordinary thing he and Hermione had shared. He knew her hand upon his chest had brought him back from the snake. He knew Draconia's mouth against his had felt like a question he wanted asked again. He tried to find words which did not turn either girl into a choice he had to defend against the other.

"I don't want honesty to mean you have to stand there while I tell you things that hurt," he said.

"Nor do I."

"Then what does it mean?"

"You tell me if something changes. You don't have to tell me every time you kiss." Hermione looked down at the earpiece in her hand. "And sometimes I'll need to go somewhere else for a bit. That won't mean I'm leaving you. Either of you."

"You can tell me," Harry said.

"I'm telling you now. Practice listening."

He smiled at that, and she nudged his knee with hers. The pages beneath the bed remained where they were. Neither reached for them. Draconia knocked lightly at the partly open door and waited until Harry answered before entering with a blanket. Her gaze moved from Harry to Hermione, then to the shared headphones.

"Am I interrupting?"

Hermione held out the earpiece.

"You'll have to share. There are only two earpieces."

Draconia looked at the cord. "An obvious flaw. How does anybody listen to music with friends?"

Draconia sat on Harry's other side. She unfolded the blanket across all three laps and leant against him without asking the music to stop. The next song began. Harry offered Draconia his earpiece. She took it, listened for half a verse and informed Hermione that the singer sounded cold. Hermione objected, stole it back at the next song and let Harry have the other. They changed places whenever someone recognised a track, awkwardly passing the cord above the blanket. Draconia leaned against Harry while Hermione tried to explain why the tape was in that particular order. For once the explanation concerned music rather than danger.

At some point Harry stopped noticing whose turn it was. Hermione's head rested against his shoulder; Draconia had tucked one cold hand beneath his. He meant to move them both somewhere more comfortable when the song ended. By then his eyes had closed. The Walkman clicked quietly at the end of the tape, and none of them woke to turn it over.

The Ministry announced its version of Arthur's attack on the second of January.

The Daily Prophet called it an accident involving an unregistered research animal inside a restricted corridor. A Ministry response team had acted promptly. No member of the public had been endangered. The article contained no snake, no vision and no mention of Harry.

Arthur read it at breakfast.

"I dislike being classified as no member of the public."

Molly confiscated the paper before he reached the second page. Cedric's rebuttal appeared that afternoon in three smaller publications and upon hand-copied sheets outside Diagon Alley shops. He had shown Harry the draft through their correspondence before sending it. Amelia had released the rescue timestamps and a limited account of the warning, leaving the Department's protected work out of the statement. He did not claim facts he could not prove. He asked why an unregistered creature had been inside the most secure department in Britain, why Ministry security records showed Kingsley receiving the corridor number from outside the building and why officials attempted to question a protected minor before interviewing their own guards. The Prophet did not print the questions. Students brought them to Hogwarts anyway.

Before the end of the holiday, Sirius and Bill finished two additional mirror pairs. Hermione and Draconia would each carry a small handpiece, with its own answering mirror at Hawthorn. These were new support connections, not replacements for Harry's pair with Sirius; they did not let the children call one another directly. Their families agreed to the arrangement, and the adults tested each pair separately before having the declarations added to the safeguarding record. A second contact could now check something without borrowing Harry's mirror. Harry liked that part more than he liked the prospect of explaining three devices at an inspection table.

They returned to Hogwarts on Sunday, the seventh of January. Platform Nine and Three-Quarters had acquired another row of inspection tables, canvas screens and so many Ministry uniforms that Harry could not see the engine until they passed the first barrier. Aurors walked between the queues. Ministry officers inspected trunks beneath canvas awnings while parents waited behind silver barriers. The repaired Hogwarts Express stood breathing steam into the winter air. New armour covered the driver's compartment. Every carriage window carried wards against shattering, curse entry and forced Apparition. Harry stopped before the nearest inspection table. A pink notice floated above it.

MINISTRY SECURITY DIRECTIVE ELEVEN

ALL ENCHANTED COMMUNICATION DEVICES, LINKED TOKENS, REFLECTIVE CORRESPONDENCE OBJECTS AND UNREGISTERED EMERGENCY CHARMS MUST BE DECLARED BEFORE ENTRY TO HOGWARTS.

Below, in smaller letters:

FAILURE TO DECLARE WILL BE TREATED AS DELIBERATE CONCEALMENT OF A SECURITY RISK.

Hermione read the wording twice.

"They know."

Draconia stood at Harry's right with one gloved hand over the grey stone beneath her coat.

"They know categories," she said. "Not objects."

"Arthur was found because of a mirror," Harry said.

Sirius read the notice over his shoulder.

"And now they would prefer the next warning arrive through an approved delay."

The inspection queue moved. Students surrendered charmed notebooks, paired lockets and one set of toy mirrors which only made the other owner look like a duck. A Ravenclaw sixth-year argued that his linked watch transmitted examination schedules. An officer placed it inside a grey box with everything else. Pansy Parkinson reached the table three places ahead of Harry. The detector reacted to her powder compact.

"That is a mirror," the officer said.

"Astounding."

"Is it enchanted?"

"It improves what it reflects. You may consider the effect aspirational."

The officer opened it. His face appeared with longer eyelashes and flawless skin. Pansy looked towards Draconia.

"Do I declare him improved or merely altered?"

Even Sirius laughed. The officer did not. Lucius stepped beside Harry as the queue advanced.

"Your mirror remains protected beneath the August panel order," he said quietly. "Declare it. Do not surrender it. Force them to document the refusal."

"And if they refuse to let me board?"

"Then the train waits while they explain to two hundred families why the boy targeted in its previous attack was denied a guardian safety device."

Sirius smiled. "I knew there was a reason we kept you."

"Your charm is touching."

They reached the table. The officer was young enough to look unhappy when he recognised Harry.

"Trunk. Wand. Declaration."

Harry placed his trunk upon the brass platform, then laid his mirror beside the declaration form. Hermione and Draconia placed their smaller mirrors beside it. The three dark surfaces reflected the canvas roof and nothing else while inactive.

"One mirror linked to my legal guardian," Harry said. "Two separate support pairs connect Hermione Granger and Draconia Malfoy to Hawthorn. They're registered under the same care order. And one paired emergency charm linked to Draconia Malfoy."

The officer looked at the form.

"Linked tokens require surrender pending review."

"No," Sirius said.

The young man glanced at him. "Sir—"

"I'm his guardian. You have the order in front of you. Read it before you take anything."

The argument attracted attention. Students stopped pretending not to watch. Parents edged nearer to the barriers. Two reporters beyond the platform gates raised Quick-Quotes Quills. The officer called his supervisor. She arrived wearing dark Ministry robes and an expression trained for unreasonable members of the public. Lucius handed her a copy of the panel order before she spoke.

"Paragraph eight," he said. "Continuous access to a guardian contact device which may not be disabled, removed or monitored without agreement of the appointed healer, guardian and minor. Paragraph nine names Miss Granger and Miss Malfoy as direct support contacts and protects the communication required to perform that role."

"Hogwarts security falls beneath the Commissioner's mandate."

"The order was issued by a higher court."

"The new directive concerns national security."

"Then obtain a variation before attempting to override it."

Sirius leant one elbow upon the inspection table.

"We have time. The train appears sturdy."

The supervisor looked towards the reporters. Harry understood the lesson. Law did not become protection merely because the correct words existed. Somebody had to make violating it cost more than obedience. She stamped the guardian mirror declaration and both support-mirror declarations.

"The mirrors are registered as exceptions. The paired charm must be inspected."

Draconia stepped forward and removed the grey stone. Harry drew the green stone out from beneath his shirt and held it where the officer could inspect it, while Draconia placed its partner upon the table. The officer passed his wand above both. Blue light traced the silver cages.

"No audio or visual transmission," he said.

"As declared," Draconia replied.

"Location function?"

"Conditional and consensual."

"Emergency exemptions require school custody outside an active event."

Narcissa joined her daughter.

"You may place that interpretation in writing with your full name, employment number and personal acceptance of liability should my child again become magically compromised while your office holds her protection."

The officer looked at his supervisor. She stamped the second declaration.

"Exception granted pending review."

Draconia fastened the stone around her neck.

"How generous."

Harry tucked his pendant beneath his shirt again. The trio boarded with their safeguards intact. Most students did not. That mattered. Harry watched grey boxes fill along the platform. Every confiscated object represented a parent who could no longer be reached directly, a friend whose warning now required official permission or a frightened child taught that secrecy belonged only to institutions. Hermione watched too.

"Nobody's declaring the coins," she said.

"The directive includes linked tokens," Harry replied.

"It does. I'm hoping they won't identify ours as linked. That still leaves us breaking it."

Draconia closed the compartment door.

"You altered them."

"The message link now closes after thirty seconds," Hermione said. "The wording stays hidden until the owner touches the coin and uses the revealing charm, just as before. But between messages there isn't a live link for a quick inspection to find."

"You changed all of them?"

"I worked it out with my coin and your master, then sent the revised charm through the set yesterday. Everyone still needs to test theirs at the first meeting."

Draconia turned the copied Galleon beneath her fingers. Its visible date remained ordinary.

"A proper examination would find it," she said.

"Yes. And it can be detected while a message is being sent. This only makes the usual search less likely to notice. We still hide them, and we don't use them just because we're bored."

Harry thought of the grey boxes filling upon the platform. He wished a less likely search could be enough to make him feel safe. For now it was better than offering the Ministry a list of everyone who might answer him. He looked back at Hermione.

"You did that while writing nineteen pages about my brain?"

"Twenty-two by the final revision."

"Did you sleep at all?"

"Yes. I also had more than a week, Harry. I didn't finish it in one night."

Draconia sat opposite her. "She did insist the prototype needed testing after midnight."

Hermione looked smug for half a second. Then Harry glanced at the fountain-pen case in her open bag. "Put it away until lunch? I'm beginning to feel tired just watching you work."

"I still need to check the last revision."

"Will a nap stop you being able to do that later?"

"That isn't the point, Harry."

"No. I'm asking you to stop for a while."

"We have the whole journey."

"Excellent."

Draconia moved a book from the seat beside her. "You can take this side. There's less light on your face." Hermione looked between them, then closed the case herself and put it on the luggage rack.

"You are both intolerable," Hermione said.

Harry settled into the seat beside her.

"Was that the bit where I'm supposed to listen?"

Hermione tried to remain angry. The attempt lasted until Ron entered carrying enough food for the entire compartment and asked whether anyone had declared the sandwiches.

Directive Eleven reached Hogwarts before the train. Copies hung from the entrance hall, common-room noticeboards and every corridor containing an owl perch. Umbridge had added school regulations beneath the Ministry text. Students were prohibited from possessing unregistered mirrors, charmed coins, paired journals or any object capable of transmitting information beyond the grounds. Owls would be inspected. Floo access would be restricted to staff supervision. Violations carried confiscation, detention and possible suspension. Harry read it while melting snow fell from his shoes. Above the notice, Neville's bent candlestick remained inside its narrow case.

ELEVEN MINUTES.

Neville had asked Professor Flitwick to help bring it downstairs before they left for the holiday. The brass was real, and the Room had allowed them to carry out the case it built around it. A note inside the glass explained that Neville Longbottom had prevented an injury after the Ministry suspended shield practice. Harry had watched Flitwick choose a place high enough that Peeves could not easily reach it. Umbridge had not removed it. Harry did not know whether she had chosen to ignore it or disliked admitting how much she wanted it gone. Its presence made him look towards the stairs for Neville.

The school gathered around the decree. Whispers travelled outward. Hannah touched the place inside her cuff where she usually carried the D.A. coin. Anthony looked towards the suits of armour, counting patrol reflections. Pansy opened her improved compact and checked the corridor behind her without turning. Networks survived by becoming habits. "The Ministry determines the school's security requirements."

Harry looked at the decree, then at the bent candlestick above it.

Sirius had predicted another attempt to turn care into control. Harry felt anger rise, then noticed the wet stone beneath his shoes and the strap of his bag against his shoulder. Hermione at his left. Draconia at his right. Ron behind him. Entrance hall. January. His body.

Lucius's lessons had become useful in irritating places. Umbridge descended one step.

"It keeps saving people."

She held his gaze. Then students began moving again around them. Not leaving. Repositioning. Neville came down the final stair. Ginny stopped near the doors. Cedric emerged from the passage to the Great Hall with two Hufflepuffs beside him. Witnesses. Umbridge saw the shape before Harry did.

"With notice," Harry said.

She walked away. The noise returned slowly. Hermione stood beneath the new directive, reading every added clause. Draconia watched the corridors rather than the paper.

"They are separating emergency from ordinary communication," Hermione said. "As if people announce which conversation will save a life before beginning it."

"They're watching how we reach people," Draconia replied. "Mirrors. Owls. Floo. Anything we use before asking their permission."

Harry felt the mirror inside his coat.

"They know communication saved Arthur."

"They know people found out they hadn't protected him," Draconia said. "Now they're making it harder to tell anyone if it happens again."

The mirror warmed. Harry stepped behind the broad base of a statue and opened it beneath his sleeve. Sirius's face appeared in the glass.

"Home and safe," he said. "Have you arrived? Your turn."

It was the arrival check they had arranged before leaving the platform. Harry gave the fresh response they had agreed that morning, then asked his own question and waited for Sirius's answer. Hermione opened her new support mirror separately. Narcissa answered from the study, and Hermione asked her to check who was in the kitchen rather than simply repeat what Sirius had said.

Draconia turned so her body blocked the nearest patrol member's view. Harry held the mirror steady while Sirius showed him the kitchen. A moment later Narcissa entered it, carrying the other answering mirror. She confirmed that she had found Sirius there and answered Hermione's question about something they had packed together that morning. It was another check, not a promise that the objects could never be interfered with. Harry was glad nobody called it one.

"Castle," he told Sirius. "Safe. All three of us." He tipped the mirror so his guardian could see the girls beside him, then Ron waiting by the Great Hall doors. Above the decree, the bent candlestick caught torchlight.

"Good. Dinner first, argument with the Ministry afterwards."

Harry closed the mirror with a small smile. Hermione ended her separate call and tucked the new mirror away. Draconia let the patrol student pass before turning towards the Great Hall. Ron was already waiting by the doors, calling that he would not preserve their seats indefinitely. Harry went to join him. The decree remained above the stairs; so did Neville's candlestick. Tomorrow would require another conversation with Umbridge. Tonight, he could tell Sirius where he was and go to dinner with the people beside him.
