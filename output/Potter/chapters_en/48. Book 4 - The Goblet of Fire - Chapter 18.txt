The hedge before Harry divided without sound. One wall slid left while another crossed behind it, leaves passing through leaves as though the plants had become shadows of themselves. The entrance disappeared before the last of the crowd's cheering had finished dying in his ears, and silence replaced it too completely.

No wind reached the path. No voice carried from the stands. The smell of crushed leaves, wet earth and something faintly metallic enclosed him; when Harry drew breath, the sound seemed much too close to his own face. He turned, searching for the seam through which he had entered, but found only towering black-green growth beneath a narrow strip of darkening sky.

He touched the silver band around his wrist. The anchoring runes glowed blue.

Eighty-four per cent.

Professor Flitwick had called that an excellent projection. Hermione had called it an unacceptable margin of failure. Draconia had asked what, precisely, happened to the missing sixteen per cent of Harry. The recollection offered very little comfort, but it brought their voices into the silence with him.

Harry raised his wand. "Point Me."

The tip swung towards his right and held. North. Somewhere beyond the hedges, Cedric had entered from that direction, while Fleur and Viktor occupied the east and west. The Cup waited near the centre, assuming the centre remained in the same place for more than a minute and the four entrances had not already been rearranged around it.

Harry marked the ground with a short silver line. The soil swallowed it. He tried again upon the hedge, but leaves folded around the mark, green spreading over silver until nothing remained.

Draconia had predicted that. Hermione had predicted Draconia predicting it and supplied chalk charmed to renew itself. Harry drew a third line, watching it vanish, reappear and vanish again before it began travelling along the hedge like a determined silver caterpillar.

"Useful," he murmured.

The maze answered with a rustle. Movement travelled along both walls, keeping pace with him before he had chosen a direction. Thousands of leaves turned their paler undersides towards Harry and held there, listening. He went left.

The path curved almost at once. Beneath his boots, packed soil became clipped grass and then flat stones slick with moisture. Harry kept his wand raised near his shoulder rather than pointed forwards, where the first obstacle could knock it from his hand. Moody's advice, learned through Crouch's lesson, remained useful. Harry chose to use it.

At the first junction, one path continued straight beneath floating lanterns. Warm light filled it, and the grass lay smooth and dry. Far ahead, through a sequence of perfectly aligned openings, something silver-blue shone.

The Cup.

It could not be. Harry had walked for less than two minutes, and the sight was too clear, too carefully placed at the end of a passage which appeared to ask nothing of him. The other path narrowed into darkness, where roots broke through the soil like knuckles. Mist gathered close to the ground, and something moved within it with a wet dragging sound.

DO NOT TRUST CONVENIENT PATHS.

Draconia's letters appeared inside his head large enough to cover the hedge. Harry chose the mist and did not look back at the Cup. That required more effort than it should have.

Three paces in, the ground attempted to eat him. Mud closed around both ankles as the apparently solid surface became a black pool beneath the mist, sucking him down with patient strength. Cold seeped instantly through leather and socks. Harry threw his weight backwards and caught a root before the mud reached his knees.

The root uncurled beneath his fingers. It had teeth.

Harry released it. "Of course you do."

The pool dragged him deeper. He aimed his wand at the nearest section of dry earth.

"Incarcerous!"

Ropes burst from the tip and struck the ground, found nothing to bind, writhed once and sank. Harry tried again at the hedge. This time, they wrapped around several thick branches, and he wound the free end twice around his wrist before pulling.

Thorns extended. One cut his palm, another caught his sleeve and pulled until the seam began to tear. The hedge leaned towards him, and Harry stopped pulling, feeling the rope tighten against his wrist. Every solution in the maze appeared to resent being used.

"Glacius."

Frost spread from his wand in a white fan. The mud resisted, boiling once around his calves, warm below and winter-cold across the surface. Then a hard skin began forming around his boots. Harry lifted one foot, planted it upon the new ice and shifted his weight before the spell could fail.

The pool convulsed. A bubble broke near his leg, releasing a smell that reminded him of Hagrid's boots after rain and suggested something had died below with legitimate complaints. Harry dragged himself onto dry ground. Behind him, the frozen surface split, and mud rose in a narrow black column, twisting towards the shape of a hand.

He ran. Ice broke behind him in a rapid series of cracks. The black hand struck once at his heel, missed and collapsed across the stones with the wet slap of something boneless.

The mist ended at another junction. When Harry glanced back, the swamp was gone. Smooth grass stretched along the passage he had just left, floating lanterns casting warm pools of light over it. Something blue-silver shone at the far end.

The convenient path had followed.

Harry's scar prickled. He flexed the cut hand around his wand, adjusting his grip where blood made the wood slippery. Hermione had made him practise changing grips with injured fingers; Draconia had insisted on hexing the practice dummy whenever he dropped it, on the grounds that consequences improved concentration.

He did not drop it. He turned away.

After that, the first ten minutes contained ordinary dangers, although ordinary had become a generous word. A flock of bronze birds attacked from the hedge, their metal wings sharp enough to cut cloth. Harry summoned one into his hand and used it as a shield against the others until they recognised it as a nestmate and tried to retrieve it by removing his fingers.

He transfigured the bird into a stone. The rest dropped at once, all thirty becoming stones around his feet. Then the stones hatched.

Bronze beaks split the grey surfaces from within. Harry escaped beneath a storm of fragments with three cuts across one cheek, a torn cuff and a new dislike of decorative wildlife.

The next path tilted sideways. Gravity followed the hedge instead of the ground, throwing him against the leaves. Vines wrapped around his robes and began pulling him upwards towards a section of sky which had somehow moved ninety degrees to his left.

Harry closed his eyes, his stomach lurching towards the false sky. The body believed what it saw; the spell required the confusion. He forced his attention onto the six inches of earth beneath his palm, feeling grit inside the cut there, smelling damp roots and pressing hard enough for the ground's solidity to become more convincing than the horizon. Only then did he cast the Four-Point Spell through the soil.

His wand indicated north beneath him. Harry crawled without looking until the illusion broke, and gravity returned with enough enthusiasm to drop him upon his shoulder. His teeth met painfully. The sky resumed its proper position as though it had never done anything unreasonable.

He lay in the grass, winded. "Two tasks without breaking this arm," he told the sky. "We had a promising pattern."

Somewhere to the west, the audience roared, the noise reaching Harry in a flattened rush through the hedges. Someone had done something worth cheering. Whether it meant progress or another narrow escape, he could not know.

He sat up slowly. Dampness had soaked one side of his robes, and his shoulder complained when he tested it, though the joint still moved. Fleur or Viktor. Too soon to know which.

Harry checked the bracelet. Blue. His tracking charm remained warm beneath the collar, answering each touch with a faint pulse. The protections still knew where he was, and that mattered more than who led.

He continued.

The cold came gradually enough to pretend it belonged. At first, Harry blamed the setting sun; the walls blocked most remaining light, leaving the paths in a green-black twilight. His breath appeared faintly before him. Then frost feathered across the leaves, forming along their veins before spreading over entire walls, and his wet robes stiffened against his side.

An ache began behind his ribs. Every hopeful thought became difficult to reach. The key against his chest belonged to a door he would never see. Sirius had made the promise because adults said impossible things before children entered danger. Hermione and Draconia's after had been a kindness rather than a future, offered because none of them wished to say aloud that the maze might keep him.

Harry knew those thoughts were being placed inside him. Knowing did not make them feel borrowed. He stopped walking, recognising the terrible absence beneath them before fabric moved at the end of the path.

A hooded figure glided around the corner. Grey, decayed hands protruded from torn sleeves, frost forming beneath it. The hedge blackened wherever its robes passed.

A Dementor.

Harry's wand rose, his injured palm protesting around the handle. As the creature approached, Lily screamed within the dark. The memory carried terror and the desperate movement of a body placing itself between death and a child, reaching him before he could hold on to any words.

He planted his feet. Five things he could see: frost, leaves, torn cloth, the pale length of his wand, one wet stone beside his boot. Four he could feel: wood in his hand, the bracelet at his wrist, the key against his chest, the ground holding his weight. Lupin had taught him that fear occupied a body and therefore a body could answer it. Harry pressed his heels into the soil and reached for the memory he wanted.

Hawthorn Cottage. A brass key warm upon his palm. Sirius missing the lake with a perfectly good stone. Remus pretending tea solved most disasters. Hermione attempting to negotiate with ancient blood magic. Draconia surrounded by ruined coins, laughing while tears still marked her face.

"Expecto Patronum!"

The stag burst from his wand, silver antlers filling the path. Its hooves struck no ground, yet the sound of running echoed between the hedges. Light poured over the frost, turning every crystal white. The Dementor recoiled, one rotted hand rising against the brightness as the Patronus charged.

Light passed through the dark robes, tearing the figure open from throat to hem. No body occupied them. Hundreds of black moths erupted from beneath the hood and surrounded the stag, their wings drinking its silver light. Harry pushed more magic into the charm until his wand trembled between his fingers.

The Patronus reared. Its antlers shortened, the long body becoming smaller, silver narrowing until a doe stood before him. Harry's concentration broke. The moths vanished, and so did the cold.

The doe remained.

Harry stared, his breathing young and broken in the sudden quiet. He did not know whose Patronus wore that shape. The doe glanced over one pale shoulder, and a woman's voice came from beyond the hedge.

"Harry."

He knew it from the final word buried beneath Lily's scream, a recognition that hurt before it gave him anything to remember.

"Mum?"

The doe walked towards the voice. Leaves parted around her silver body, revealing a narrow opening filled with warm light. Harry stepped forwards before deciding to, and the house key pressed against his chest.

Brass. A familiar weight, asking nothing of him. Sirius's voice returned from the lake: You will return. Harry held on to it and looked again at the shape before him.

The doe had appeared only after the maze touched the memory of Lily's death. It had not come from the thought he used to cast. It carried none of the warmth of the stag and left no silver reflection upon the frost. Harry lowered his wand.

"You're not her."

The doe turned its head. For an instant, red light shone inside the silver eyes. Then the opening snapped shut. Branches cut through her body, breaking it into drifting light, and a high, cold laugh moved through the leaves.

Harry's scar burned. He backed away until the opposite hedge stopped him, its branches pressing through his robes. The pain lasted only a second. Long enough.

Voldemort knew he was inside.

The knowledge should have felt new. Instead, it settled beside every protection packed beneath Harry's robes and made them seem suddenly, terribly finite.

The maze gave Harry four routes. He trusted none. One smelled of salt water, another carried distant music, and fresh, cool wind flowed from the third. The fourth ended after six feet at an unbroken wall.

Harry chose the wall. The maze had already offered him what he wanted, what he feared and what he remembered; a route which admitted it led nowhere felt almost honest. He tested it for illusion and found it solid. A Severing Charm parted the outer leaves, revealing branches grown into an interlocking lattice thicker than his arm. Golden runes pulsed within the wood. Tournament magic, protecting a barrier that had never been intended to open.

He examined the other paths from their thresholds without crossing any. The salt passage left a mineral taste upon his lips. Music reached him in separated notes, sometimes close and sometimes impossibly far away. The wind smelled of summer grass beyond Hogwarts, clean enough to make his chest ache.

Salt water meant the lake. Music suggested the three-headed dog or the merpeople's song. Wind offered no obvious memory and was therefore the most suspicious. Harry went towards the music.

The melody remained just beyond recognition. Slow notes travelled through the hedge, played upon something with strings. He knew the tune: Hagrid had hummed it badly while feeding Fluffy. The harp beneath the trapdoor had played it during first year, while three children crept through protections built by adults and assumed surviving proved they had been right to go.

The path widened. An enormous paw rested across the ground, and Harry stopped.

Fluffy's body blocked the junction ahead, three heads lowered in sleep. He was larger than Harry remembered, or perhaps an eleven-year-old's fear had never supplied a reliable measurement. Coarse black fur rose and fell across a ribcage broad enough to fill the path. Each breath moved the hedge behind him, carrying the sour heat of meat, dog and old blood. Drool gathered beneath one mouth in a pool deep enough to reflect the last strip of sky.

The music faltered. Three noses twitched. Harry considered turning around, but the hedge had closed behind him.

"You've grown," he whispered.

One ear lifted. Harry searched his pockets: antidotes, bezoars, whistle, spare wand holster. No flute, because Hermione had decided the chance of encountering a music-dependent three-headed dog was below two per cent. He would mention this if he survived.

The music slowed again. Harry aimed at the grass beside him.

"Avis."

Small birds erupted from his wand. Before they could scatter, he transfigured their voices, and the flock began to sing. Poorly. The tune resembled Fluffy's lullaby in the way Sirius's account resembled accuracy: a few recognisable parts surrounded by confidence.

The nearest head opened one eye. Harry adjusted the charm, softening the birdsong until the eye closed again. He waited through another breath, then moved into the narrow space between the beast and the hedge.

Leaves dragged against his left shoulder while fur brushed his right. Hot breath lifted the back of his robes and flattened them again. The middle head shifted, teeth scraping together inches from his ear, and Harry held still until its breathing settled. One step, then another. The birds circled above Fluffy's heads, repeating their damaged lullaby while he edged past a foreleg thicker than his waist.

At the hind leg, the music stopped. All three heads woke.

Harry ran. The dog lunged, one mouth closing upon empty air behind him while another tore a section from the hedge. The third seized the back of his robes. Cloth tightened across his throat and dragged him backwards; his boots left the ground, and for a moment he could neither breathe nor find anything to push against.

He twisted and fired over his shoulder. "Engorgio!"

The singing birds expanded. Feathers filled the path, and Fluffy released Harry to snap at them. They dissolved before the teeth closed, becoming ordinary wand-light and one bewildered pigeon which escaped over the hedge.

Harry hit the ground, rolled and threw himself around the next corner. The passage sealed, and Fluffy's three roars shook leaves from the walls. He touched the torn back of his robes, where fabric hung from collar to shoulder blades, exposing his shirt. A narrow line marked his throat where the fastening had cut into it.

"Below two per cent," he said breathlessly.

The maze rustled with what sounded disturbingly like amusement. Its laughter followed him to the next junction, where a stone arch waited beneath a sky now the colour of bruised violet.

The creature beneath it possessed the body of a lion, wings folded along tawny flanks and the face of a woman whose eyes had no visible whites. She occupied the path without appearing crowded by it. One paw rested upon the stone threshold, claws sheathed but outlined beneath the fur. A gold collar circled her throat, old Greek letters moving across it like insects and rearranging themselves whenever Harry came close to reading them.

The Sphinx watched him approach. "Champion," she said, her voice vibrating inside the stones.

Harry stopped beyond reach of her forepaws. "Sphinx."

"The path behind me leads inward."

"And the others?"

"Away."

Harry glanced left and right. Two additional passages had appeared while she spoke, one containing sunlight and the other carrying the sound of applause.

"That seems clear," he said.

The Sphinx's tail moved once. "Answer, and I permit passage. Refuse, and you may turn away. Answer falsely, and I attack."

"Do I get to know the question first?"

"You are young to have become suspicious of conditions."

"It's been an educational year."

Her mouth curved without becoming a smile. "What is given before it is earned, broken without being touched, borrowed by liars and restored only by those willing to risk it again?"

Harry knew the answer. That made him distrust it. He considered a promise: promises were given, broken and restored, but liars did not borrow them. A name could be given before it was earned and borrowed, yet a name broken without touch made little sense. The answer he had first thought of remained, unpleasantly suited to the year behind him.

"Trust," he said.

The Sphinx inclined her head. "Pass."

She shifted one wing, revealing the opening. Harry did not move.

"The path behind you leads inward?"

"Yes."

"Towards the Cup?"

The dark eyes studied him. "I promised that it led inward. I did not promise what waited there."

Harry examined the golden runes upon her collar. The Greek letters had stopped moving, and beneath them, revealed in a seam where fur met metal, lay a smaller design which did not belong to the Tournament. A serpent wound around a standing stone.

Nott's seal.

Harry backed away. The Sphinx smiled.

"You answered correctly."

"I didn't say I trusted you."

He turned towards the path containing applause. The Sphinx attacked.

Harry dived as her claws passed above him and cut four grooves through the stone. Wind from the blow struck his back. He rolled beneath one wing, caught a glimpse of ribs beneath tawny fur and drove his wand towards the golden collar.

"Diffindo!"

The metal split. The Sphinx screamed, and dark magic poured from the break like smoke, carrying the smell of singed parchment. The nearest leaves turned grey. Her body convulsed, wings striking both hedges hard enough to crack branches. Harry covered his head and curled around his wand hand while stones fell from the arch, their impacts travelling through the ground beneath him.

When he raised his head, the creature had collapsed. The Nott seal blackened and fell from the collar. She breathed in short, painful gasps, and without the magic holding her upright he could see the raw band it had worn through the fur around her throat.

Harry approached cautiously. "Was that controlling you?"

Her eyes found him. "Guiding."

"Towards what?"

"The path prepared."

The sunlight and applause vanished from the other passages. Only darkness remained, leaving Harry with the sound of her laboured breathing.

"How many creatures have those collars?"

"Not creatures." The Sphinx closed her eyes. "Choices."

The arch began to sink. Harry sent red sparks into the sky, bright enough to paint the leaves crimson. For several seconds, nothing happened. Then the first intervention horn sounded, and white light opened behind the Sphinx, cutting an extraction passage directly through the wall. Two Ministry handlers appeared beyond it with wands raised.

"Injured creature," Harry called. "Foreign control artefact. Nott crest."

One handler knelt beside the Sphinx while the other collected the broken collar without touching it. The security system had answered. Relief made Harry's knees briefly weak.

"I am suspending my attempt," he said. "Foreign magic. The covenant permits a safety pause."

The binding tightened around his ribs.

"Then come through," the second handler said. "We are withdrawing you."

Harry took one step towards the white passage. Pain stopped his breath. He stepped back, and the pressure eased. The official moved his wand between Harry and the opening, repeating the emergency authorisation Bill had tested with them. Gold flared at the edge of the corridor, then darkened. Harry tried again and dropped to one knee.

"Stop." The handler's voice changed. "Do not force it."

Harry pressed the charm beneath his shirt. "Sirius."

Warmth answered, followed by a pull which died beneath the same pressure. He held it until the warmth faded. The Sphinx could be carried through the passage; Harry could not reach it. The corrupted binding was making a distinction between them.

"Tell Dumbledore," Harry managed. "Tell him the pause doesn't work."

"I am doing that now." The handler sent a silver message through the opening, then tried to widen it. Black threaded the white edge. His spell recoiled, throwing him against the hedge and tightening the band across Harry's chest. He broke the spell at once.

The corridor began closing. The first handler drew the Sphinx clear, carrying the collar inside a containment sphere. The second remained at the threshold until only a narrow white line separated them.

"Stay clear of the obstacles. We will open another route."

Harry intended to stay where he was. The instant the line vanished, the binding squeezed again. He forced out the pause declaration, waited for an answer and received only more pain. Turning towards the remaining path loosened it enough to let him stand. He had kept his promise to stop; something inside the task was refusing to let him.

This time, Harry heard a girl scream. The sound came from the path on his left, and every part of him which had spent the year listening for Hermione or Draconia moved before the rest could object. His first step crushed wet leaves beneath one boot.

Harry waited. The scream did not repeat. Branches cracked, followed by a burst of French furious enough that he understood its meaning without knowing the words.

Fleur.

He ran, checking at every turn for drag marks, damaged leaves or spell residue. The voice travelled more easily than the person; the maze wanted urgency without direction. Thorns caught his torn robes as the path narrowed. Twice he struck dead ends which appeared between one step and the next, forcing him backwards while Fleur's voice grew weaker beyond the hedge.

"Fleur!"

"Potter?"

Her answer came from his right now. Harry changed direction, and the maze changed with him. Pursuit was impossible unless he followed the route it wanted.

He stopped fighting the turns. At the next junction, he chose the path which curved away from her voice, walked ten paces and placed his palm upon the hedge. Vibration travelled through the branches. Someone struggled on the other side.

"Stand back!"

"I cannot!" Fleur shouted.

Harry aimed low. "Diffindo!"

The Severing Charm cut through leaves and the thinner outer branches. The hedge screamed, a sound far too clear to mistake for rustling wood. Sap dark as blood ran from the opening and steamed where it touched the grass. The entire wall bent towards Harry, branches snapping at his shoulders as he forced them wider. He threw himself through before the wound sealed around him.

The clearing beyond smelled of lake water. Fleur hung several feet above the ground, black vines binding her wrists and ankles against the hedge. More crossed her waist and throat, tightening whenever she struggled and relaxing when she stopped. Thorns pressed into her robes without piercing them, holding her with deliberate care. Her wand lay below, half-buried in the grass, close enough for her to see without being able to reach.

Three girls stood around her. All wore Gabrielle's face and the pale dress from the lake. Wet hair clung to their cheeks, water streaming from their sleeves into a widening pool beneath their bare feet.

One turned towards Harry. "She left me."

The voice belonged to a child. The accusation did not.

Fleur twisted against the vines. "Do not listen."

The second Gabrielle turned, purple marks circling her throat where the lake ropes had held her.

"She chose the Cup."

The third smiled, revealing a mouth filled with small pointed teeth. "Again."

Harry recognised the structure beneath the spellwork. The false doe had carried the same pressure: memory given shape, then turned towards the choice most likely to hurt.

"None of you are Gabrielle," he said.

All three answered together. "We are whoever you leave."

Their faces changed. One became Hermione, pale and rigid upon the hospital bed after the basilisk's gaze found her through a mirror, her hair spread across the pillow with every curl motionless. The second became Draconia in the Chamber, green robes soaked dark and bloodless hands curled beside the diary. A basilisk fang lay near her fingers. Her eyes opened without seeing him. The third remained Gabrielle.

The maze knew him too. For one fractured instant, the clearing ceased to contain Fleur. Harry was twelve again, arriving after both girls had already been taken from him and understanding that saving one had never guaranteed he would reach the other in time.

Then the false Draconia breathed, and the movement was wrong: too shallow, too carefully timed. Harry cast before it could show more.

"Lumos Maxima!"

White light filled the clearing, turning every falling drop of water into a bright needle. The figures became translucent. Beneath their borrowed faces, bundles of thorn and water were held together by black threads of magic. Harry cut the first thread, and the false Hermione burst apart with the sound of a mirror breaking beneath water.

Fleur flinched as though struck. Harry stopped, his wand already moving towards the next thread.

"They are connected to you," he realised.

"Yes." Her voice shook with effort. "Every spell I cast at them tightens the vines. Every illusion I break returns through me."

The Draconia-shape stepped towards him, bare feet sinking into the flooded grass. Water fell from her hair.

"You left me too."

Harry's scar ached. "you would be more insulting."

The lie smiled with Draconia's mouth. He aimed past it at the vines holding Fleur.

"Incendio!"

Blue fire ran along the black growth. Before the vines began to burn, they contracted around Fleur, jerking her shoulders back against the hedge. The false girls screamed with her voice, and Harry nearly stopped.

Fleur did not. "Continue!"

He fed the flames despite every borrowed scream. Smoke rolled across the clearing, smelling of salt, crushed flowers and the hospital potion used to keep Petrified patients alive. The vines loosened. Fleur tore one hand free, summoned her wand and turned the blue fire gold. Together, they burned through the remaining bonds.

She fell, and Harry caught her badly. They struck the ground in a tangle of robes and elbows, pain shooting through his bruised shoulder. Around them, the illusions lost their bones and collapsed into waves which crossed the grass from impossible directions, washing over Harry's boots before settling into still water.

Fleur pushed herself upright. "But Gabrielle?" she asked.

"At the stands."

"You are sure?"

"I saw her before we entered. She was beside Madame Maxime. Blue cloak. Silver clasp."

Fleur closed her eyes. The Tournament had damaged the line between memory and present badly enough that reassurance required evidence. Harry understood that too well; he waited until her breathing slowed before asking, "Can you stand?"

She tried, but her right leg folded beneath her. One thorn had pierced above the ankle after all. Grey spread through the surrounding skin in branching lines, and Harry reached for an antidote kit.

Fleur caught his wrist. "No. It is dreamthorn."

"I have six antidotes."

"And you may require every one. The poison is not fatal."

"What does it do?"

"Makes the visions continue."

Behind her, the hedge became Gabrielle again. Leaves formed her face first, then wet hair and the pale folds of a dress. The child stood among the branches with both arms extended. Fleur turned her face aside, refusing the sight with an effort Harry could see in her jaw.

"I cannot continue if I do not know what I am seeing."

The admission held judgement rather than defeat: of the poison, of her altered senses and of what continuing would cost everyone forced to trust them. She raised her wand and sent red sparks above the maze.

The horn sounded for the second time. White light opened through the hedge almost immediately, and a Beauxbatons healer appeared within the extraction corridor beside an Auror. Harry's relief lasted until the man crossed towards them. He watched the Ministry identity seal at the Auror's collar flash blue, then checked the revealing charm above the opening. Its light remained steady, with no violet flare of concealed transformation.

Fleur saw him watching. "You are right to check," she said.

The healer knelt to examine her leg. Harry pointed towards the remains of the black threads.

"The obstacle used our memories. It changed Gabrielle into Hermione and Draconia after I arrived."

The Auror cast a diagnostic spell, and his expression changed. "This is not on the approved register."

"Neither was the Nott collar."

The official glanced towards the sealing passage behind him. "I will report both."

"Should the task stop?" Fleur demanded.

"The Headmaster has ordered a pause," the Auror said. "The maze is refusing the override. We are extracting everyone we can."

The healer lifted Fleur onto a floating stretcher. Harry rose beside them and tried to follow her towards the passage. The pressure returned before his foot crossed the light. He gave the safety declaration again. The Auror's wand flashed; black ran beneath the blue seal, and the passage narrowed around Fleur's stretcher.

"It let you reach her," Harry said. "It won't let me leave."

The man tried a second authorisation, then broke it off as Harry's knees buckled.

"I have to get her clear before this closes." He looked directly at Harry. "I am reporting your location and the failed extraction. We are coming back for you."

Fleur's hand caught Harry's sleeve.

Fleur kept him close enough to hear her without raising her voice.

"Do not follow anyone you love."

Harry met her eyes.

"That is what it uses," she said. "Not fear. The direction fear makes you move."

The corridor closed around her. Harry stood alone, ankle-deep in water, with Hermione's face staring up from its surface in place of his own. Her eyes remained open and unblinking. He stepped through the reflection, and the maze absorbed that too.

- - - - - - -

The stands heard two intervention horns. Hermione saw Gabrielle stop breathing after the second; the child stood between two Beauxbatons students with both hands pressed over her mouth until Fleur appeared through the western emergency gate.

Madame Maxime left the judges' platform before the healers finished lowering the stretcher. The crowd rose as one shifting body, benches knocking against stone and hundreds of voices asking questions nobody outside the maze could answer. Cheers began in scattered sections when Fleur lifted one hand.

Hermione did not rise. Relief for Fleur arrived tangled with the knowledge that three lights still burned above the officials' table. She sat beside Draconia in the front row with the Marauder's Map open beneath both their hands.

The parchment had been permitted outside the maze after three Ministry examiners established that it could not display the altered interior. The Quidditch pitch appeared as an empty oval surrounded by labelled stands; four champion names had vanished the instant the hedges closed.

Hermione kept looking anyway. The Map had found Harry in the dragon enclosure on the school grounds, across the lake and inside rooms protected against ordinary tracking. Its blankness now felt like deliberate exclusion. She traced the edge of the empty oval with one finger, as though a name might appear beneath it if she waited long enough.

"The trackers," Draconia said.

Below the judges' platform, four glass spheres floated above a brass table, each containing a different light. Fleur's silver flame had moved to the edge and gone dark after her withdrawal. Viktor's red light travelled slowly through the western half, Cedric's yellow occupied the north, and Harry's green moved east of the centre.

Every few seconds, green doubled. One Harry remained where he had been while another moved several yards ahead. The two lights reunited, separated and exchanged positions.

"It began after his first emergency signal," Hermione said.

"The officials saw."

"They are trying to find which one is real."

Three Unspeakables surrounded the table. Professor Flitwick stood upon a raised chair beside them, passing his wand through the duplicated light, while Bill Weasley read the runes around the brass rim.

Sirius had stopped sitting. So had Narcissa, Lucius and Hermione's parents. They formed an uneven line behind the front bench, each wearing a different version of the same helpless attention. Sirius stood at the barrier with both hands closed around the railing. Remus spoke quietly beside him, but whatever he said failed to make Sirius release the metal. Even at a distance, Hermione could see the whiteness across his knuckles.

"Fleur's obstacle was unauthorised," Draconia said.

"We don't know that."

"Maxime is shouting in French. I consider that evidence."

Upon the field, Dumbledore raised his wand again. The handlers' first report had brought an order to pause the task; the white flare now repeated that command across the sky. Nothing happened. The hedges continued moving, and the audience quieted as people realised the signal had gone unanswered.

Dumbledore cast again. White light spread across the maze like lightning over a dark sea, making every path visible from above for one instant. Hermione saw Harry, a small figure running through a corridor while three other versions of him moved in different directions.

Then the hedges swallowed the light. The brass tracking table cracked, and one Unspeakable stepped backwards.

Sirius crossed the barrier. Amelia was already at the first entrance with two Aurors, presenting the Ministry's authority to the ward. Golden letters rose through the leaves, recognised her and began to part. Then they darkened.

The opening snapped shut. Harry's green light contracted to a pinprick, and Cedric's yellow did the same. Dumbledore swept his wand between Amelia and the hedge. Both lights steadied as her spell broke away.

Sirius stopped beside her. "What happened?"

"It accepted our authority," Amelia said. Her face had gone pale. "Then it used the attempted entry against them."

"The covenant permits intervention," Bagman said from the platform, his amplified voice shaking.

"We know what it permits," Dumbledore said. "Something inside is refusing it."

He turned towards the professors. "The enclosing wards. Leave the champions' bindings intact. We open a way through."

Flitwick sent white light along the outer hedge, exposing a separate line of runes beneath its roots. Aurors spread across the pitch while Sprout and McGonagall joined him. Sirius raised his wand beside Amelia. Hermione watched the first branches begin to split under their combined spells, then knit themselves together farther along the wall. The adults adjusted their positions and struck again.

Draconia lowered her gaze to the coin within her palm. It remained cold. Harry carried the only intact alarm; she had kept the least damaged of the failed discs as a receiver, unable to send a warning of its own but capable of answering his through the remains of the family enchantment. Tournament wards smothered that connection while he remained inside the task. If he passed beyond them, both coins should wake.

Until then, cold metal meant nothing. It certainly did not mean he was safe.

Hermione closed the useless Map. "The maze isn't keeping them away from the Cup," she said.

Draconia followed her gaze towards the broken tracking table. Viktor's light moved north, Cedric's south, both approaching the same junction. Harry's green divided once more, and every copy turned towards them.

"It is bringing them together," Draconia said.

- - - - - - -

Harry found the cupboard beneath the stairs. It waited where no house could possibly stand.

One turn brought him into a narrow hall papered in faded flowers. The ceiling lowered, and the air smelled of boiled cabbage, furniture polish and dust warmed by summer pipes. Somewhere in the sitting room, a clock ticked with the aggressive neatness of a house which disliked evidence that people lived inside it. Number four, Privet Drive surrounded him with perfect, quiet hatred.

Harry stopped upon the mat inside the front door. The maze had vanished behind him. Family photographs lined the walls, Dudley occupying every frame: in a paddling pool, upon a red bicycle, screaming beside a birthday cake because the candles leaned. No image showed Harry. From the kitchen came the clatter of plates.

"Boy!" Vernon shouted. "Cupboard!"

Harry's body obeyed before his mind did. His shoulders drew inward, his chin lowered, and breath shortened until it could make no sound at all. He became aware of mud upon his boots and blood drying along his cheek, each mark transformed into something for which he would be punished. Four years of magic vanished beneath the older calculation of how angry Vernon sounded and whether the kitchen door had closed behind him.

The cupboard door stood open. Darkness waited beyond it.

"You heard me!"

Footsteps crossed the kitchen. Harry gripped his wand, but the wood felt too large beneath his fingers, the handle thick as a broomstick. He looked down at the hand holding it.

His hand had become small. No anchoring bracelet circled the thin wrist. His clothes hung loose and worn, and one toe protruded through a hole in the sock upon the hall carpet.

Seven years old. Perhaps eight. The age before he learned that wit could turn fear sideways, before letters and wands and two girls who watched whether he ate. Before anyone explained that survival was not the same as being unharmed.

The front door stood behind him. Beyond its frosted glass, Privet Drive held a square of ordinary afternoon: clipped lawns, a passing car, the bright red coat of a woman walking a dog. Near enough to see, yet entirely inaccessible. Harry reached for it, but the handle would not turn.

Locks slid into place one after another. Top bolt, chain, deadlock, then the small brass catch Vernon had installed after Harry first learned to reach the handle. His uncle's footsteps grew louder. The cupboard remained open, offering the place where anger could forget he existed.

Harry entered. The door slammed, and darkness closed around him.

His breath struck the low walls and returned warm against his face. Coats pressed from one side, a bucket dug into his ankle, and spiders moved near the pipes. Their tiny legs scratched through dust with a clarity the maze had denied every sound from outside.

The cupboard had remembered everything. The thin mattress smelled faintly of damp foam. A dent in the wall marked where Harry's knees had rested whenever he tried to make himself smaller. Above it, hidden behind the lowest stair, shallow lines counted days nobody had told him would end.

Outside, Vernon passed without stopping. The old relief came first: no hand reached for the latch, nothing hurt, and his uncle had gone past. Then came shame for feeling it.

Harry sat upon the narrow mattress. His wand was gone, along with the whistle, the bracelet and the torn task robes. He wore a shirt discarded by Dudley and the silence expected of him. The maze offered no monster to fight, no riddle, no curse he could block by moving quickly enough. There was only a door which remained closed because part of him still believed hiding was the closest thing he deserved to safety.

He pressed both hands against his knees. He was not meant to defeat this room. That was the trick. The maze had made him small because a small child could be forgiven for staying where he had been put; it had offered relief before asking him to mistake it for a choice.

Something pressed against his chest. Harry reached beneath the oversized shirt and found a brass key hanging from the cord. It remained its proper size, heavy, cool and absurdly solid against his little palm.

Sirius's voice lived inside the memory of giving it: The door remains yours. Somewhere beyond Privet Drive, beyond the Tournament and every protection with a percentage attached, a cottage waited for him to arrive. His room had green walls because he had chosen them. Food remained upon the table after he stopped being hungry. Anger could enter the house without ending it. Doors closed for privacy and opened again.

Harry stood. He fitted the key into the cupboard lock. It should not have matched, but it turned.

Light entered through the widening gap, bringing the bruised evening of the maze into the dark. Harry stepped into it at fourteen. The hallway split from floor to ceiling, wallpaper becoming leaves. The staircase twisted into roots and sank. Vernon's roar stretched into wind moving through the hedge, and the maze returned.

Harry's wand lay in his hand. The anchoring bracelet glowed blue. He leaned against the wall and tried not to be sick. His body had returned to fourteen, but the old obedience remained inside it, shaking through his knees and leaving sweat cold beneath his shirt.

"That," he told the maze when he could breathe, "was not an obstacle."

Leaves whispered above him.

"It was rude."

His voice shook badly enough to spoil the joke. Harry counted five leaves, four places his clothes touched skin, three breaths which reached the bottom of his lungs. The house did not return. He continued anyway.

Water covered the next path, only an inch deep at first, reflecting the hedges and carrying the sound of Harry's steps ahead of him. Spiders fled across the leaves, hundreds of small bodies abandoning the centre in the same direction. He knew before the voice arrived.

Come.

The word travelled through the roots. Harry stopped.

So long.

Parseltongue dragged itself along the inside of his skull, meaning arriving before sound. The old basilisk had spoken those words while moving through Hogwarts pipes, unheard by everyone else. The maze should not know the language as sound, unless something inside Harry had supplied it.

He walked faster. Each step sent a small wave ahead, and each wave returned from the darkness before the next one formed. Water deepened around his boots as soil became fitted stone slabs, slick with years of underground damp. Torchlight turned green. On either side, the hedges hardened into walls carved with serpents whose mouths opened as Harry passed.

Ahead, the path divided. Hermione screamed on the left. Draconia screamed on the right.

Both sounds were real enough to erase thought. Hermione's voice broke upon his name. Draconia's ended in the sharp gasp he remembered from the hospital wing, immediately before her hex struck him and the diary took her to the Chamber. Harry ran one step towards Hermione. Fleur's warning stopped the second.

Do not follow anyone you love.

"Harry!" Hermione cried. "Please!"

"Don't listen to it!" Draconia shouted from the opposite path. "Help her!"

The voices knew them. Of course they did. Crouch had spent nine months observing the trio, the diary had carried Draconia's memories, and Nott had followed their weaknesses for years. Harry stood between the passages, feeling the water move around his legs while everything inside him pulled towards the sounds.

"Which one?" the basilisk whispered beneath the water, its voice coming from everywhere the reflections touched.

"Harry, it's here!" Hermione's terror tore through the left-hand hedge.

Draconia's voice became small. "You always choose her."

Pain opened across Harry's chest, older than the contract and less honest. He imagined both girls alone beyond the walls: Hermione waiting because he had gone right, Draconia bleeding because he had gone left. One of them discovering that danger had finally made him rank what neither had dared ask him to name.

The maze did not need him to believe either voice completely. It needed him to move before doubt could become choice. Harry brought his wand up and pointed directly ahead.

"Reducto!"

The spell struck the hedge between the passages. Water leapt around his knees, branches exploded, and the screams stopped. He cast again, filling the water with leaves and splintered wood. Golden Tournament runes ignited within the damaged wall, resisting him; the recoil drove through his bruised shoulder and nearly turned his wrist.

"You don't get to ask that."

The words belonged to the maze, to Voldemort and to every old fear which understood love only as a way to decide where to place the knife. Harry cast a third time. The runes broke one after another, each releasing a note like a bell struck underwater. Darkness opened through the centre, and he climbed over the ruined hedge rather than choose either path.

On the other side, the water ended. Hermione and Draconia stood together at the far end of the passage, no longer screaming. They held hands exactly as they had in the stands before Harry entered. Their eyes were black.

"Wrong," they said.

The figures dissolved, their expressions remaining until the last moment. Behind Harry, both false paths snapped shut. The way forwards remained open. For the first time, the maze had not wanted him to take it.

It ran straight into a passage with no lanterns and no silver light waiting at the end. Harry distrusted it immediately. The hedges pressed close enough to brush both shoulders, their leaves rough and ordinary beneath his torn sleeves. After the cupboard and the Chamber, even that ordinariness felt staged.

He checked the Four-Point Spell. North lay behind him, and the path carried him west. Harry continued because the walls had left no turn to refuse. After fifty paces, his tracking charm cooled.

He touched the small disc beneath his collar. Since entering the maze, it had pulsed warmly every few seconds in answer to the brass table outside. Now it felt like dead metal. The silver bracelet still shone blue.

Harry raised the emergency whistle to his lips, then hesitated. Using it would summon an official and might end his attempt; he had no evidence yet that the tracker had failed rather than entered a section protected against observation. He was still trying to decide whether that distinction mattered when spells struck ahead.

One impact flashed red through the leaves, another purple. Someone shouted, the voice cut short by an explosion which drove hot air along the passage. Harry ran.

The path opened upon a circular clearing already torn apart by fighting. Scorched rings marked the grass. One section of hedge had crystallised into black glass, fragments dropping from it with small, musical chimes. Cedric lay near the opposite wall, blood covering one side of his face. His wand remained in his hand, a thin shield raised above him while Viktor Krum crossed the clearing with the slow certainty of someone walking in a dream.

"Viktor!" Harry shouted.

Krum turned. His eyes were open and empty. A narrow metal collar lay against his throat, so thin it vanished beneath the shadow of his jaw. At its centre, the Nott serpent twisted around a standing stone.

He cast without speaking. Harry shielded, but the violet light struck hard enough to drive him into the hedge. Thorns tore across his back through the ruined robes, reopening earlier cuts and adding deeper ones beneath them.

"He's controlled!" Harry called.

Cedric rolled away from a second spell. "I noticed!"

Krum moved between them. His boots passed through scorched grass without hesitation, even where embers caught the hems of his robes. Harry had watched him practise beside the ship, forceful, direct and economical. Now he attacked without preserving himself, every movement designed to finish the nearest target even if the next curse struck him.

Cedric aimed for the collar. Krum let the Disarming Charm hit his shoulder, bone shifting beneath the impact, but kept his wand level long enough to send a cutting curse beneath Cedric's shield. Cedric cried out as red opened across his thigh.

Harry struck Krum with a Stunner. The spell hit his back, the collar flashed, and he remained standing.

"It's holding him up," Harry said.

"Then take it off."

"Excellent plan."

"Thank you."

Krum attacked again. Harry and Cedric moved apart, crossing curses between them and forcing him to turn. Harry aimed high, Cedric low. Krum blocked one spell and jumped the other, turning in the air to cast at both before landing.

Harry's shield caught the first curse. The second passed through the place Cedric's head had occupied before he dropped, striking the hedge and stripping every leaf from a circle three feet across. Krum continued moving with unnatural precision, but terror remained trapped behind his empty eyes. Sweat ran from his hairline despite the cooling air, and his jaw strained against whatever command the collar forced through him.

Harry remembered the Imperius Curse spreading through his mind like warm light, leaving no fear or uncertainty, making command indistinguishable from desire. Krum knew what his body did. The collar refused to let him stop.

"Viktor can hear us," Harry said.

Cedric blocked another curse. "Useful to know."

"Viktor, Stop."

Krum's gaze flickered. His knees bent for half a second before the collar burned black. He straightened and sent two spells at Harry, who ducked the first. The second cut across the anchoring bracelet and opened a shallow line along his forearm.

Silver runes flared. The band held; one rune dimmed, recovered and shone blue again. Eighty-four per cent became a prayer.

"Again," Harry told Cedric.

"He'll know."

"So will Viktor."

Cedric understood. He circled right, limping visibly. "Krum!"

The champion turned towards him, and Harry moved left. Cedric raised his wand.

"Drop."

Krum fought the collar's command. Every muscle in his neck tightened as the black metal pulsed. His wand hand jerked towards Cedric while the rest of his body tried to fold in the opposite direction. He bent only a little, but enough.

Harry cast the Severing Charm over his back. It struck the Nott seal, and the collar snapped. Magic left Krum's limbs all at once. He fell without putting out his hands, his final curse leaving the wand as he hit the ground. It tore upwards through the hedge, setting leaves alight. For a moment, Harry saw the night beyond and one distant tower of Hogwarts. Then branches knitted over the gap.

Cedric reached Krum first. "Breathing."

Harry kicked the broken collar away. Its halves crawled towards each other through the grass, leaving blackened lines wherever the cut metal touched. He blasted them apart. Most fragments charred where they landed, but one thin strip remained hooked beneath Krum's robes, too close to the burned skin for Harry to tear free safely. Its magic had gone dark.

"Viktor?"

Krum opened his eyes. Pain returned to them before recognition.

"Diggory." His gaze moved to Harry. "Potter."

"What happened?" Cedric asked.

Krum swallowed. A black burn circled his throat, the skin beneath carrying dozens of fine punctures where the metal had fed its commands directly into him.

"Door."

"What door?"

"In hedge. Headmaster calling." His accent thickened as breath shortened. "I open. Something behind."

"Crouch?" Harry asked.

"No face. Hands."

Krum's eyes closed. Harry sent red sparks above the clearing. They rose, but no horn sounded; the sparks faded slowly against an invisible ceiling, red reflected across all three boys' faces. He cast again. This time, they struck a surface several feet above the hedge and scattered back upon them.

"That's new," Cedric said.

Harry drew the silver whistle. Its sound came thin and distant, as though someone blew another whistle miles away. No emergency corridor opened.

The clearing began to shrink. Hedges moved inward, branches scraping across grass, giving them less room with every second. Cedric pointed his wand at the sky.

"Periculum!"

His sparks failed too. Harry closed his fingers around the cold tracking charm.

"We carry him."

Cedric attempted to stand, but his injured leg failed. Harry caught his arm, feeling him lean more heavily than either of them wanted to acknowledge.

"You're bleeding."

"Observant."

The cut ran from Cedric's hip nearly to his knee, shallow enough to spare the bone but long enough that blood had soaked the fabric around it and gathered inside his boot. Harry gave him a bezoar. Cedric stared.

"Was the spell poisonous?"

"No. I have three."

"That isn't a reason."

"Hermione put you up to the speech about self-sacrifice. You can't object to irrational preparation now."

Cedric pocketed the stone. Harry opened the outer antidote case and found the clotting potion; two drops sealed the worst of the wound. Cedric bound it while Harry turned to Krum. The walls had moved close enough to touch the unconscious champion's boots.

"Mobilicorpus."

Krum rose several inches, but the spell trembled. Harry's magic felt thick here, every charm pushing through resistance that had not existed near the entrance. The body rose unevenly, one shoulder lower than the other, and Harry had to correct the spell twice before Krum's head stopped lolling.

Cedric limped beneath his other arm. Together, they moved towards the only remaining path. The clearing closed behind them, swallowing the scorched grass, the collar fragments and every sign that a fight had occurred.

They carried Krum for what felt like half a mile. The maze allowed no junctions, every turn leading deeper. Harry checked north until his wand began spinning continuously. Cedric scattered dust for invisible barriers, but it floated behind them, forming arrows which pointed forwards.

"It's guiding us," Cedric said.

"I preferred when that was a theory."

Krum stirred between them, his feet dragging across the ground. Fever radiated through his robes.

"Put down," he muttered.

"Not yet," Cedric said.

"You cannot fight carrying."

"Neither can you."

Krum forced his eyes open. "Signal not work."

Harry shifted beneath his weight to see his face. "We noticed."

"Then leave me where officials find."

"They can't find us."

"Maze wants you. Not me."

The words were too close to Harry's own thoughts. He adjusted Krum's arm across his shoulders. The champion was taller and heavier than either boy, and every third step sent a hot line through the cuts across Harry's back.

"That sounds like its problem."

The path opened. A white emergency gate stood in the hedge, light shining beyond it. Harry could see the edge of the pitch, Aurors running towards the opening and crowded stands rising beneath the night sky. The sight struck with such force that his body leaned towards it before suspicion caught up.

Sirius stood nearest. "Harry!"

His voice crossed the distance clearly. Cedric stopped, but Harry kept supporting Krum and approached only close enough to examine the frame. No revealing charm glowed above it.

"Harry, move!" Sirius shouted. "The maze has been compromised!"

Only Harry. The man beyond the gate had not asked whether Cedric could walk or told them how to bring Krum through. Sirius could become unreasonable where Harry's safety was concerned, but he did not stop seeing everyone else.

Krum tried to lift his head. "Is real?"

Harry studied his godfather beyond the gate: the same blue robes, hair escaped from its tie in precisely the right places, one hand gripping his wand while the other reached through the opening. An old silver ring circled the first finger of that hand.

Sirius did not wear rings. He claimed they interfered with a proper grip and made every wizard resemble a suspect in an inheritance dispute. Harry levelled his wand at the gate. Concern remained around the false Sirius's mouth while the eyes emptied of it.

"Finite."

The emergency gate vanished, taking the stands, Aurors and night beyond with it. A vertical mouth remained in the hedge. Green lips peeled back from teeth made of thorns, and rot breathed from the opening.

The mouth snapped. Harry threw all three of them sideways, and branches closed where their heads had been.

"Not real," Krum said from the ground.

"He was wearing a ring," Harry replied.

Cedric stared at him. "That was your proof?"

"Sirius thinks jewellery is a conspiracy."

"That explains nothing," Cedric said.

"It explains the ring."

The hedge-mouth opened again. Cedric fired into it, his Stunning Spell vanishing down the throat. The entire wall convulsed. Harry grabbed Krum beneath the arms.

"On three."

"Three," Cedric said.

They dragged him as the hedge bit through the path behind them. One, two—branches struck the ground, and Harry felt thorns tear across the heel of his boot.

The passage ended at a circular stone platform marked with a white cross. Unlike the gate, it carried a Ministry revealing charm around the edge, pale blue light travelling through the carved channels.

"Retrieval point," Cedric said.

"Or another mouth."

Krum pulled his arm free and rolled onto the cross before either could stop him. White light erupted. The hedge-mouth struck, its teeth closing upon empty stone, and Krum vanished. VIKTOR KRUM burned across the cross, fading with the retrieval light.

The horn sounded for a third time. Real. The walls recoiled from the platform, and Harry's whistle warmed, confirming an official extraction.

Cedric sank against the hedge, breathing hard. "He made it."

"Yes."

"We can use the platform," Cedric said.

Harry approached the white cross. "Emergency equipment failure. We are withdrawing."

The revealing charm remained blue, but beneath it the carved letters shifted. The contract tightened around Harry's ribs before his foot reached the stone. He stepped back, caught his breath and tried again. The same pressure met him. Cedric limped forward beside him; his face drained as the binding caught him too.

"It took Viktor," Cedric said.

The platform had accepted Viktor's name. It would not even let Harry and Cedric step onto its surface, though they had declared an emergency in the words Bill had taught them. Whatever had interfered with the maze was choosing which rules it would obey.

Harry pressed the charm beneath his shirt. "Sirius."

Warmth answered, followed by a pull which vanished as the binding clenched around his chest. He kept hold of the charm until its warmth faded. The official platform remained a yard away, blue and empty.

Behind them, the hedge-mouth opened. Cedric raised his wand, and Harry turned with him. They could not stand here long enough to argue with magic which had stopped answering them. The only open passage led away from the mouth; Harry caught Cedric's arm and helped him towards it.

Cedric steadied himself against Harry until he could stand without pulling at the sealed cut. He remained close as they moved away from the platform.

"We stay together now," he said.

"Inside the maze, we separate."

"I am revising the agreement."

"Hermione will want a process."

"Draconia will add one afterwards."

Harry faced the path ahead. For the first time since entering, someone walked beside him rather than waiting inside an obstacle. It helped. That frightened him too.

The retrieval platform disappeared at the first turn. By the second, Cedric's leg had worsened. The clotting potion had sealed the cut, but it could not restore blood already lost; his face appeared pale beneath dirt and drying blood, and every few steps his left foot dragged. Harry slowed without mentioning it. Cedric noticed without objecting.

He removed what appeared to be a knot of dried grass from an inner pocket and breathed upon it. Pale fibres uncurled between his hands, braiding themselves into a flexible cord.

"Greenhouse Four," he explained when Harry stared. "It roots towards separated pieces of itself."

"You brought friendship rope."

"I brought practical rope."

They tied the Hufflepuff grass-rope between their belts. It stretched far enough to let them fight independently and tightened if the maze attempted to place a wall between them.

It attempted three times. The first hedge rose from the ground beneath the rope. Harry and Cedric jumped to opposite sides before it reached their knees, then climbed while the living wall carried them upwards. At the top, they crossed through the branches and dropped together into the same passage.

The second wall descended from above. Cedric transfigured the rope into a steel chain before the leaves cut it. The hedge struck the chain, failed to divide them and folded sideways with visible irritation.

The third tried subtlety. A girl with pale hair appeared behind Harry.

"Potter," she said.

Draconia's voice sounded exhausted: the thin, uncertain voice she used only after nightmares, with none of her public impatience to conceal it. Harry did not turn.

The thing used her voice again. "I cannot tell whether these thoughts are mine."

The words found the exact memory they had been made to hurt. He had heard them once in darkness after the Chamber, when Draconia could not bear to look at him while admitting how much of Riddle remained inside her recollection. Harry kept his eyes upon the path, feeling the chain shift against his belt with each step Cedric took beside him.

Cedric glanced back. His expression hardened. "Keep walking."

The illusion followed for six turns. Sometimes it sounded like Draconia, sometimes Hermione. Once, it used Cho's voice and told Cedric that his father had collapsed in the stands. Neither looked. Eventually, it became Harry.

"Cedric," his own voice pleaded behind them. "The rope broke."

Cedric continued. Harry glanced sideways at him.

"How did you know?"

"You would have said something sarcastic first."

"Good."

"Also, you are directly beside me."

"Less impressive."

The false voice stopped. The link between them remained.

Several turns later, they smelled the creature before seeing it. Rot, damp hair and venom turned the air thick. The passage widened gradually, its floor covered by pale threads which caught moonlight in narrow silver lines. Harry stopped before his boot touched the first.

"Web."

Cedric examined the hedges above. "Acromantula?"

"Probably."

"You sound disappointed."

"Hagrid's descriptions were larger."

"Of course they were."

"They involved several warnings about pincers."

"Useful."

The path behind them sealed. Ahead, webs thickened into sheets which closed away most of the sky. Shapes hung inside them: bronze birds, broken broomsticks and the white bones of animals too large to have entered accidentally. Moisture jewelled the finer strands. The larger ones were thick as cord and flexed in time with something breathing beyond them.

Something clicked above. Harry and Cedric raised their wands together.

The first spider dropped behind them without disturbing a single strand. It was the size of a desk, hair covering eight jointed legs, pincers opening beneath a cluster of shining eyes. Cedric's Stunner struck its thorax and threw it backwards into the hedge. The leaves rejected it, and it hit the ground with its legs folded beneath it. A moment later, it rose again, reflecting Harry's wandlight in eight separate points.

Three more descended.

"Fire?" Cedric asked.

"The hedges react badly."

"Spiders react worse."

"Fair."

They cast together. Flames raced across the webs, turning the corridor gold and casting the shadows of dozens more bodies across the hedges. Acromantulas screamed, their clicking voices becoming human enough to turn Harry's stomach.

The webbing contracted in the heat. Instead of burning away, every strand drew tight towards the centre. Hung bones struck one another, the stunned spider slid across the grass, and a line too fine to see caught Harry's ankle, wrenching it from beneath him.

He hit the ground hard enough to bite his tongue. Copper filled his mouth as the chain pulled Cedric sideways. His injured leg folded, and both fell.

The largest spider emerged from the burning sheets ahead. Its body nearly filled the path. One leg ended in a metal blade stamped with the Triwizard crest, and articulated armour protected its eyes and thorax without restricting movement. Every plate fitted closely enough to reveal deliberate workmanship. Venom dripped from pincers long enough to close around Harry's chest, hissing wherever it struck the heated web.

"That wears their crest," Harry said.

"How can you tell?"

"Expensive."

The blade-leg struck with a sound like an axe splitting wood. They rolled in opposite directions, the chain snapping taut and dragging Harry clear as metal buried itself where his spine had been. Cedric blasted the leg, but the armour deflected the spell into the hedge. Branches exploded outwards, and smaller spiders poured through the gap.

Harry cut the web around his ankle. The giant acromantula lunged, and he aimed for the unarmoured mouth.

"Impedimenta!"

The creature slowed without stopping, its body continuing forwards with terrible momentum. Legs carved trenches through the ground while Harry scrambled backwards on hands and heels. Its shadow covered him before its body arrived.

Cedric sent a binding charm beneath it, turning grass into ropes around four legs. The spider tore two free, but the others held. Its body twisted sideways. The blade passed Harry and struck the chain connecting the boys.

Steel rang. Both halves broke and recoiled towards their original knots, grass fibres reaching uselessly across the new distance. The maze moved at once, sending a hedge upwards between them.

"Cedric!"

"Here!"

The wall rose above Harry's head. The giant spider remained on his side; every smaller one remained with Cedric. Human design lay beneath the division. Harry had been given the greatest visible danger because the maze expected him to fight it. The greater actual danger had gone to the injured champion who could no longer move quickly.

It had watched Harry choose people all night. Now it tried to turn that instinct into delay.

He turned from the armoured creature and ran at the new hedge. The spider pursued. Harry counted its steps through the tremors beneath his feet—three, two, one—then threw himself flat.

The blade-leg passed overhead and struck the wall. Metal tore through living wood, opening a narrow split, and Harry pointed into it.

"Deprimo!"

The compressed blast widened the break. He forced himself through, branches raking his ribs and catching at the tears across his back. Beyond the opening, Cedric stood inside a ring of fire. Dozens of spiders surrounded him, and his wand moved constantly, maintaining flames while he balanced upon one leg. A small acromantula clung to his robes, its pincers worrying at the fabric above his spine.

Harry seized it with magic and threw it into the swarm. Cedric's ring opened for half a second to admit him, then closed behind his heels.

"Behind you," Cedric gasped.

Harry turned. The armoured head crashed through the gap.

"Do you have a plan?" he asked.

"You brought it."

"I was hoping you did."

The creature tore the hedge wider. Cedric tipped his head towards the webs covering the tops of both walls, thick ropes connecting them and supporting the nest above the path.

"Bring it forward."

"That isn't a plan."

"It is the part you need."

Harry cast at the creature's mouth and ran. The acromantula followed, its armour crushing smaller spiders beneath it. Cedric waited until Harry passed, then aimed above.

"Diffindo!"

The support webs snapped in a sequence of deep, resonant cracks. Layers of sticky silk collapsed over the armoured spider, driving it to the ground beneath the falling nest. Its legs thrashed so violently that the corridor trembled. The blade cut through one layer and became tangled in three more; armour which had defeated their spells trapped the creature inside the mass.

Harry and Cedric cast together. "Stupefy!"

Both spells entered the open mouth. The spider's legs folded, and silence reached the corridor in pieces: first the blade stopped striking, then the smaller creatures retreated into the hedge. Last came the low clicking beneath the nest, slowing until it ceased.

Harry bent over with his hands upon his knees. Cedric sat down where he stood.

"You went through the hedge," he said.

"It was nearer than going around."

"There was no around."

"Even nearer."

Cedric laughed breathlessly. Harry sat beside him, heat from the burned webs pressing against one cheek while night air cooled the other. For one minute, neither moved. Then he saw the puncture in Cedric's sleeve, two small marks darkening the skin beneath.

"It bit you."

Cedric twisted his arm to see. "I wondered why my arm went numb."

Harry opened the second antidote case. "Acromantula venom."

"You have that?"

"Hagrid contributed to the list."

"Why does Hagrid have acromantula antivenom?"

"Long story."

"Is every answer about your life a long story?"

Harry poured the clear potion over the wound. "The short ones are worse."

Cedric's fingers twitched as feeling returned. Harry kept the remaining half-bottle, closing the stopper carefully before replacing it.

"Your turn," Cedric said.

"It didn't bite me."

"Your back."

Harry had forgotten the thorns. Adrenaline had reduced his back to a single broad heat; now each separate cut returned. When Cedric peeled the torn robes away, blood had soaked the shirt beneath them. Four cuts crossed Harry's shoulder blades.

"Dreamthorn?" Harry asked.

"Ordinary."

"Wonderful."

Cedric cleaned them with the last of the water in his flask and sealed the deepest cut. Harry kept his jaw closed through the sting, concentrating upon the stopper lying beside his boot.

"You know," Cedric said, "this would be easier if either of us accepted help before bleeding significantly."

"I accepted."

"After I pointed at the blood."

"Still counts."

They stood. Harry repaired the rope in silence, and Cedric fastened his end. The path beyond the nest opened towards the centre. Neither pretended it was a coincidence.

When Viktor appeared upon the retrieval platform, Hermione saw at once that the maze had not merely eliminated him. He lay unconscious and burning with fever, one hand clenched around grass which had travelled out with him. The skin above his collar was blistered black.

Madam Pomfrey reached him before Karkaroff. The Durmstrang Headmaster was forced to stop at the edge of the medical ward she cast around the platform, his face white with a fury which looked uncomfortably like fear. A twisted strip from the collar remained hooked beneath Viktor's robes. It began to twitch as soon as the retrieval light released him, part of the serpent-and-stone seal visible along one edge. Bill Weasley crossed the pitch at a run.

"Where are Potter and Diggory?" Amelia asked.

Krum's eyes opened beneath the healer's wand. "Inside."

"Can they signal?"

"No."

The answer passed through the nearest stands as the crowd repeated it in frightened fragments. Sirius stopped arguing with the Aurors. Dumbledore knelt beside Viktor.

"What did you encounter?"

"Door. Voice like yours. Hands put collar." Krum fought for breath. "Maze makes us together. Potter sees false Black. Saves me."

The fragment twisted against his shoulder. Bill contained it beneath a ward, watching the dark metal move within the light.

"This isn't merely laid over the Tournament enchantments," he said. "It is drawing power from them."

Hermione and Draconia had reached the barrier without either remembering to ask permission. Narcissa followed close enough for one hand to hover near Draconia's back. Lucius stood beside Sirius, every remnant of their ordinary hostility made temporarily irrelevant.

"The completion circuit," Hermione said.

Every adult turned towards her. She faced the maze centre, where only the top of the stone plinth was visible above the hedges, the Triwizard Cup casting a faint blue glow into the dark.

"The contract powers the task," she continued. "If Nott attached his magic to its choices, the maze cannot distinguish sabotage from its own function."

Bill's face changed. "If it can alter what the task recognises—"

He ran to the broken tracking table and pulled the original contract towards him. Golden thread connected its eighteen sheets. Near the final clause, something black moved inside one strand, spreading through the gold as the live circuit drew more power. Hermione followed. She had examined that passage in this same light two days earlier; there had been nothing there to see.

"The Cup accepts a champion's living magic."

"Then the contract carries the completed circuit somewhere." Bill traced the strand without touching it, darkness moving beneath his fingertip as though it noticed. "Not a Portkey placed upon the Cup. A destination inserted into the moment the task ends."

Flitwick climbed onto the platform. "The Cup passed every transport test."

"And the dormant contract passed its examinations," Bill said. "This only shows itself while the circuit is drawing power. He hid it inside the authorised working."

Hermione found the golden thread connecting their safety covenant to the original. It had begun to blacken too.

"They are allowed to wait," she said. "It accepted that."

Bill's wand followed the spreading dark. "It is cutting the interpretation away from their bindings."

"Then sever that strand," Sirius said.

"With both boys still attached, the force goes through them." Bill looked up. "That is what happened when the Department tried to remove the Cup. We have to reach them first."

Sirius turned on Dumbledore. "Tell him."

The Headmaster's phoenix Patronus erupted above the judges' platform. It flew towards the southern entrance, wings stretched wide enough to silver the faces nearest the field. The hedges closed over it. Light flashed within the maze, travelled several yards and went out.

Sirius drew his wand. Behind Hermione, Mrs Granger inhaled; Mr Granger took her hand without looking away from the field.

"Open it."

"The contract—" Bagman began.

"Open it."

Dumbledore raised both arms, and every professor moved with him. Flitwick, McGonagall, Snape, Sprout, Maxime and Karkaroff formed a line along the pitch. Aurors filled the gaps, their wands aimed towards the outer hedge.

"On my signal," Dumbledore said.

The maze shifted before he could give it. Harry's remaining green lights vanished from the cracked tracking spheres. Cedric's yellow followed. Draconia closed both hands around the receiving coin, pressing its edge into her palms.

It remained cold.

The inner maze contained no obstacles. Harry preferred the spiders.

Hedges opened before him and Cedric in long, measured sections, each wall moving only after they reached it. The route never branched. Roots withdrew from beneath their boots, the ground became level, and even the wind died, leaving warm, stale air around their faces. Blue light brightened beyond each turn, close enough now to silver the upper leaves.

The Cup waited ahead. Harry checked the tracking charm again. Cold. The repaired grass-rope still connected him to Cedric, its pale fibres darkened by blood near both knots. Cedric leaned more heavily upon it with every step.

Harry lifted the emergency whistle and blew. No sound emerged. He felt the effort leave his lungs, but the maze swallowed it before it became noise. Cedric tried his; the silver instrument split along its side with a tiny metallic sigh.

"We turn around," Cedric said.

They did. The hedge had sealed behind them so neatly that its leaves had already gathered dust. Cedric cast a diagnostic charm across the wall, revealing golden runes woven closely enough to resemble scales.

"Tournament barrier."

"We broke one before."

"Before the centre."

Harry struck it with a Blasting Curse. The spell rebounded before reaching the branches, and Cedric shielded them. Golden light poured around the charm, pushing both boys backwards along the path.

Towards the Cup.

The contract tightened beneath Harry's ribs with the intimate certainty of a hand closing around his lungs. His next breath stopped halfway. Cedric bent beside him, one hand pressed to his chest.

"The pause," he gasped. "It should let us stop."

Harry forced enough air into his lungs to speak. "Emergency equipment failure. We are waiting for assistance."

They had practised those words in Dumbledore's office. The binding had loosened then, allowing him to turn away without pain. Here, it tightened until his knees struck the ground.

"Something has changed," Cedric said.

Harry looked back at the wall. The diagnostic runes still shone beneath its leaves, but black flecks moved between the gold, joining and separating as the hedge knitted itself more firmly together. He tried the pause again. Cedric did too. Neither received an answer except the pressure beneath their ribs.

Harry turned forwards, and the pressure eased enough to let him stand. Cedric caught his sleeve. They stayed there for a moment, looking at the path the maze had left them, before following it towards the open space ahead. If they could not get through the hedge, Harry needed somewhere to send another signal. He kept his wand raised and his free hand near the Portkey beneath his shirt.

A circular clearing lay beneath open sky. Four passages entered at equal points, each sealed by hedge the moment the boys crossed its boundary. White stones formed a ring around grass cut so evenly that every blade appeared the same height. No creature waited, and no spells moved through the air. After hours of manufactured terror, the stillness carried its own threat.

The Triwizard Cup stood upon a waist-high plinth. Blue fire filled its shallow bowl without heat. Silver handles curved from either side, polished where generations of hands had closed around them. Names of former champions travelled around the base, appearing and vanishing beneath age-darkened metal. Some remained visible long enough to read; others dissolved after the first letter, swallowed back into the Cup's history.

No serpent marked it. No standing stone. After everything the maze had done, Harry distrusted its stillness. The Cup looked exactly as Flitwick had described it, but reaching an object which had passed inspection was not the same as finding a way out.

He and Cedric stopped at the white boundary.

"Checklist," Cedric said.

Harry drew the folded card from his inner pocket. Moisture had softened the creases, and one corner carried a smear of blood. Hermione had written the procedures in numbered order. Draconia had added three more in the margins, then arrows correcting the order when she decided Hermione's sequence left too much room for assumptions. Moody had placed a final instruction across the bottom in block capitals:

IF EVERY TEST SAYS SAFE, ASK WHO BENEFITS FROM YOU BELIEVING IT.

Harry read it twice. "We test anyway."

Cedric nodded. Neither crossed the stones.

They began with detection, using the passive tests they had practised with Flitwick. Each read magic already radiating from the Cup without sending theirs into it; direct contact by skin, wand or spell would complete the circuit. Harry cast the observing form of Specialis Revelio, and pale symbols appeared above the metal, listing every recognised enchantment before fading. Cedric followed with a Hufflepuff charm designed to expose magic bound to an object's history; yellow light traced decades of handling and revealed nothing added during the present school year. Flitwick's transport test outlined the Cup in orange, the approved colour for Tournament placement, and showed no red trace of Portkey enchantment.

They tested the plinth and found stone. The grass held three fertilising charms, a moisture ward and traces of the spell which moved the hedges. Harry tested for Nott's ritual signature, but the serpent-and-stone detector remained clear.

Cedric transfigured a pebble into a mouse and sent it across the white ring. The animal sniffed the plinth, placed both forepaws upon its base, ignored the Cup and began cleaning one ear. Harry summoned it back.

"Cruel to leave it."

"Your concern is touching."

"I contain multitudes."

Cedric tapped the mouse with his wand. It became a smooth grey pebble again, which he pocketed carefully.

"If we return, remind me I owe it cheese."

"When."

Cedric met his eyes. "When we return."

They sent red sparks. The magic struck the open sky and flattened against something invisible, crimson spreading above the clearing like paint beneath glass. Harry tried the whistle again. Silence.

He pressed one thumb to the emergency charm beneath his shirt. "Sirius."

It warmed. For one hopeful second, something pulled behind his navel. Then the contract closed around his ribs, the pull vanished, and the charm cooled without breaking.

"It should allow emergency departure," Cedric said.

"It should allow us to wait too." Harry kept his thumb against the charm until its last warmth faded. "Apparently it has forgotten both."

They waited. Completing a task under threat was not the same as choosing to complete it, and Harry was not ready to let the contract make those things indistinguishable. One minute passed, then two. He counted both by the beating beneath his scar. Cedric shifted his injured leg every few seconds, trying and failing to find a position which did not pull at the sealed cut.

Beyond the hedges, a deep impact shook leaves from the walls. The adults were trying to enter. Harry turned towards the sound, hope arriving so quickly that he had to swallow before speaking.

"They know."

"Something," Cedric said. "Not necessarily where we are."

Another impact followed. White light flickered above the invisible ceiling and disappeared. The Cup continued burning blue, its fire undisturbed by the strikes, the names around its base travelling at the same patient speed.

The contract tightened again. Pain drove Harry to one knee, and the white boundary stones blurred. Air entered only as far as his throat before an invisible band forced it out. Cedric caught the plinth without touching the Cup, his injured leg shaking beneath him.

"It wants completion."

"It can want."

Harry gripped his chest. The pressure increased, each beat of his heart striking against it. The binding did not need to move their hands. It could make breathing, standing and thought progressively more expensive until touching the Cup resembled a decision. Whatever had corrupted the binding still required their living magic to finish the circuit. It was forcing them towards the act which would supply it.

Harry understood Crouch Senior better then. Obey. Work. Sleep. Obey. A command repeated through pain until relief became indistinguishable from choice. Draconia would have recognised it, and that made Harry hate the Cup more than any visible curse could have done.

"The Cup is clear," Cedric said through clenched teeth. "The binding isn't."

Harry looked at the silver handles. Touching one would complete the task. They knew that much because Bill had translated the condition three times, and because Flitwick had made them practise the passive tests until they could perform them without feeding magic into the object. Everything those tests could read remained exactly as it had been before the maze closed.

"If we finish," Cedric said, "the binding releases us."

"If it still obeys that part."

Cedric's eyes moved towards the invisible ceiling. Neither mistook the silence for reassurance. Beyond it, another spell struck the maze; the white light vanished before Harry could see where it had landed.

"I can take it," Cedric said. "You stay behind the stones. If the pressure stops, use the Portkey immediately."

Harry looked at his injured leg, at the hand gripping the plinth to keep him upright. Cedric was offering to discover whether the last condition still worked by leaving Harry outside its reach.

"And if whatever is wrong with the task reaches you when you finish it?"

"Then you can get out."

"You don't know that. Neither do I."

Cedric's jaw tightened. "One of us has to try."

Harry pressed the emergency charm again. Nothing pulled. He lifted the whistle and forced breath through it; the effort brought pain and no sound. The grass-rope between them trembled as Cedric's knee began to buckle.

"We're not taking turns finding out who it hurts," Harry said. "We keep hold of each other."

"Harry—"

"We tested it. We tried to leave. We called for help." His voice thinned as the contract tightened again. "If we could wait, I'd wait."

Cedric looked at him for a long moment. He had been in the office when Harry said he did not want to continue. There was no prize left in this clearing that either of them wanted badly enough to endure another minute of it.

"The contract ends when we finish," Harry said. "That's when the emergency charm is supposed to work again. If something tries to move us first, I still have the bracelet." He raised his wrist. Blue light remained inside the silver runes. "You hold on to me. We use whatever works."

"And if nothing does?"

Harry looked towards the hedge. He could hear the adults striking it, could picture Sirius refusing to stop. He wanted to give them another minute. Cedric was struggling to breathe beside him; Harry's own pulse kept stumbling against the pressure in his chest. He could not know how much longer either would stay conscious.

"Then I don't know," he said. "But I don't think we have another way to give them time."

Cedric lowered his eyes to the rope fastening their belts together, checked his knot with shaking fingers and nodded once.

The hedge shuddered under another external strike. A thin crack of white light appeared above the northern wall, and Harry waited for it to widen. The maze repaired it.

The contract squeezed. Cedric made one pained sound and nearly fell; the grass-rope tightened between them before Harry caught his sleeve. He held out his hand.

"Together?"

"Yes." Cedric took it, his grip cold and slick with sweat. They crossed the white stones one uneven step at a time, hands locked between them. The pressure eased just enough to reward every movement towards the plinth.

Every protection upon Harry reacted. The tracking disc warmed, the anchoring bracelet brightened from blue to white, and the emergency charm beat once against his chest. The Malfoy alarm coin pulsed green beside Sirius's key, its magic waking as the task approached completion.

No warning appeared. Every charm told the truth it had been designed to tell.

The Cup was clean. The contract was not.

Cedric positioned his hand around the left handle without closing his fingers. Harry did the same upon the right. The metal radiated no heat, yet sensation climbed his arm before he touched it, like the moment preceding a lightning strike. They met each other's eyes.

"Three," Cedric said.

They closed their hands together. Blue fire went out.

For half a heartbeat, nothing happened. The pressure beneath Harry's ribs vanished, and air filled his lungs so quickly that it hurt. The maze became silent. Cedric laughed once in disbelief.

Golden light travelled from their hands into the Cup, bright, orderly and ancient, resembling every approved line of the contract. It entered the metal, crossed the plinth and flowed through the white stones in a perfect circle.

Then the dark strand reached it. Gold turned black.

Harry pressed the emergency charm. "Sirius!"

Red light seized him from behind, attempting to pull him towards the person whose name he had spoken. The Cup pulled forwards. The two forces met inside his body, and Harry screamed.

The anchoring bracelet flared. Runes rose from the silver band and wrapped around his arm, fastening him to the earth with lines of blue fire. Grass tore beneath his boots, the soles of both shoes beginning to lift. The plinth cracked from top to base.

Cedric grabbed Harry around the chest without releasing the Cup. Their grass-rope drew tight between them, pale fibres blazing yellow where the competing magic struck it.

"Hold on!"

"That was the plan!"

The maze vanished into white light. For one impossible second, Harry saw outside through every wall at once. Dumbledore stood beyond a shattered hedge with every professor and Auror behind him. Sirius was running. Hermione held the Marauder's Map against her chest, and green fire surrounded Draconia's hand.

Their faces turned towards him. Sirius's mouth formed his name.

The silver bracelet broke, blue runes snapping one by one. The emergency charm cracked against Harry's chest. The Cup took both boys.

Draconia's coin burned. She dropped it and caught it again before it struck the ground. Green flame climbed between her fingers without consuming skin, bright enough to expose every line in her palm. Upon the coin's face, the serpent crest split open.

"He crossed the wards," she said.

The words did not sound like hers. They belonged to the part of her which had spent the year preparing for proof and had never truly believed preparation would prevent it. Hermione was already moving towards the centre barrier.

Dumbledore brought his wand down. The combined spell struck the maze with enough force to send a pressure wave through the stands. Spectators ducked, and glass burst somewhere behind the judges' platform. Every outer hedge collapsed at once. Stone paths, creatures and illusions flickered beneath the sudden absence of walls, revealing the centre beneath a dome of black light.

Harry and Cedric occupied it for less than a second. Draconia saw Harry's hand around one handle, Cedric's arm around his chest and silver exploding from his wrist. She opened her mouth.

Then the Cup disappeared. Both boys vanished with it, the black dome broke, and the maze opened upon an empty plinth.

Sirius reached it first, dropping to both knees beside the cracked stone as though there might still be something to seize.

Harry fell through cold. There was no direction inside Portkey travel, no air or ground. Light stretched into colours he could not name, then vanished entirely. Cedric's grip remained around him, the only proof either possessed a body.

The emergency charm pulled once more. The contract's black magic reached through Harry and caught it, drawing him towards two destinations at once. His body thinned between them like thread pulled too tight.

Something tore. Then earth struck him.

Harry landed upon his side. The impact drove every remaining breath from him and ground damp soil into his cheek. The Cup hit nearby with a metallic crash. Cedric rolled across wet grass, still attached by the repaired rope around their belts.

For several seconds, neither moved. The world returned as separate sensations: cold beneath Harry's palms, water soaking one knee, iron somewhere in the air and insects moving invisibly through long grass. He tasted blood.

The anchor bracelet hung open from his wrist, its silver blackened and every rune dead. A burn circled the skin beneath it. Under his shirt, the emergency charm had split neatly down the middle. Sirius's key remained whole, and Harry closed one hand around it.

"Cedric?"

A groan answered. "Alive."

"Good."

Harry forced himself onto one elbow. Gravestones surrounded them, leaning from long grass beneath a colourless sky. Some listed sideways; others had sunk until only their rounded tops remained above the earth. Marble angels watched through weathered faces, lichen obscuring names. A yew tree spread black branches over the nearest row, and beyond it stood a ruined house with blind windows.

No village light showed beyond the churchyard wall. No road sounded nearby. Mist collected close to the ground and moved around the stones without wind. Harry recognised the place from his vision before clouds shifted from the moon and revealed the name upon the largest stone.

TOM RIDDLE

Cedric sat up. He saw the grave, then the Cup, then Harry's face.

"This isn't Hogwarts."

Harry's scar opened in pain, carrying the same recognition that had reached him through the maze. Footsteps moved beyond the yew tree. Someone approached carrying a bundle beneath dark robes.

The shape inside it breathed, high, shallow and eager. The sound crossed the graveyard more clearly than the footsteps carrying it. Harry reached for his wand.

A voice came from the darkness.

"At last."
