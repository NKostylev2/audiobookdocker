12

The rat returned on Tuesday morning, carried into breakfast between Ronald Weasley's hands like a piece of evidence. The Great Hall was bright with a washed-out spring sky, and the ordinary clatter of cutlery made Ron's approach easy to hear before Harry saw him. Voices rose along the Gryffindor table. Students twisted upon the benches. Neville nearly poured pumpkin juice into his lap when Ron forced himself between him and Dean and dropped into the seat opposite Harry.

"Look."

Harry looked. Scabbers crouched between Ron's hands, shaking hard enough to disturb the whiskers around his narrow face. The rat appeared to have spent the previous six weeks being dragged backwards through a hedge. His grey fur had thinned along the spine, dirt darkened his paws and one ear was torn near the tip. He had lost enough weight that Ron's fingers met beneath his body.

Alive, however.

Unmistakably.

Scabbers's sides moved quickly. His eyes remained fixed upon the Great Hall doors.

"Where did you find him?" Harry asked.

"Inside my trunk."

Ron lifted the rat higher.

"Buried under my winter robes. Must've got back into the dormitory and hidden."

Hermione approached from the end of the table carrying a bowl of porridge and three books. She stopped when she saw Scabbers. Relief crossed her face.

"You found him."

"No thanks to your cat."

Crookshanks sat beneath Hermione's usual place, orange tail curled around his paws. At the sound of Ron's voice, he looked up. Scabbers screamed. The rat fought so violently that Ron nearly dropped him. Crookshanks rose.

"Don't," Hermione warned.

The cat stared at Scabbers. Not hungry.

Intent.

Harry knew the difference now. Draconia watched from the Slytherin table. She placed down her teacup, following the direction of Crookshanks's gaze. Ron shoved Scabbers inside the front of his robes. The rat continued struggling beneath the fabric.

"You still think Crookshanks ate him?" Hermione asked.

"He tried."

"But he didn't."

"Because Scabbers escaped."

"So the blood on your sheet—"

"Could've been his."

"Did Madam Pomfrey examine him?"

Ron looked at her as if she had proposed taking the rat before the Wizengamot.

"He's a rat."

"Being a rat doesn't mean he can't be hurt."

"He's fine."

Scabbers bit him through the shirt. Ron yelped. Harry reached across the table before he could strike the animal instinctively.

"Let him out."

"He'll run."

"He can't breathe inside your robes."

"He's breathed there before."

"That doesn't mean he likes it."

Ron pulled Scabbers free and placed him upon the table. The rat immediately tried to escape beneath a plate. Harry caught him around the middle. Scabbers's heart hammered against his palm. Up close, the missing toe on the left front paw was obvious. A small pale scar crossed the place where it should have been.

Peter Pettigrew had left one finger behind. The fact surfaced again. Harry turned the rat carefully.

"What are you doing?" Ron demanded.

"Checking him."

"For what?"

"I don't know."

The honest answer earned a suspicious look. Hermione leaned closer. Scabbers exposed long yellow teeth.

"How old is he?" she asked.

"We did this already."

"You said Percy had him first."

"So?"

"How long?"

Ron looked towards the ceiling, calculating.

"Four years. Maybe five."

"And you've had him for three."

"He's old."

"Very."

Draconia appeared beside Harry. Ron frowned.

"Do you ever stay at your own table?"

"When it is interesting."

"This isn't."

Draconia looked at Scabbers. The rat went rigid. His eyes fixed upon her. She lifted one hand. Scabbers squealed and twisted free of Harry's grip. He struck the table, scattered a dish of marmalade and disappeared beneath Ron's sleeve.

Draconia's hand stopped in the air.

"I wasn't going to touch him."

"He knows a Malfoy when he sees one," Ron said.

"So do most mammals. They tend to be impressed."

Ron rolled his eyes. Draconia sat beside Hermione.

"He is frightened of people," she said.

"He's been hiding in a trunk for weeks," Ron replied.

"He was frightened before."

"Crookshanks kept trying to kill him."

At the sound of his name, the cat placed his front paws upon the bench. Scabbers thrashed beneath Ron's sleeve. Crookshanks stared. Harry watched the cat's eyes rather than the rat. Crookshanks did not track the movement beneath the fabric as prey. His gaze moved to Ron's face, then the doors, then back to the concealed animal. The behaviour looked almost impatient.

"You should put Scabbers somewhere secure," Hermione said.

"Meaning a cage?"

"Yes."

"So your cat can stop terrorising him?"

"So he can recover."

Ron pulled his sleeve closer.

"I'll take him to the dormitory."

Crookshanks jumped onto the bench. Scabbers screamed again. Half the Great Hall turned. Professor McGonagall looked up from the staff table.

"Mr Weasley," she called, "remove that animal from the dining table."

"He's not on the table."

A sausage vanished beneath Ron's sleeve. McGonagall's eyebrows rose.

"Not entirely," Ron amended.

He stood. Crookshanks watched him leave. Hermione placed one hand upon the cat's back.

"Don't follow."

Crookshanks sat. Draconia reached for the toast.

"That rat is wrong."

Hermione looked at her.

"Because it dislikes you?"

"Most creatures possess that instinct. His is excessive."

Harry stared towards the doors.

"Peter Pettigrew was missing a finger."

Neither girl spoke. The noise of breakfast continued around them. Hermione's hand stilled upon Crookshanks's fur. Draconia lowered the toast.

"What?" she asked.

"When Sirius killed him," Harry said quietly. "They found one finger."

Hermione looked towards the doors through which Ron had disappeared.

"Lots of people are missing fingers."

"Scabbers is missing a toe."

"That is not a finger."

"I'm aware."

"It could be coincidence."

"Yes."

Draconia studied Harry.

"You do not think it is."

"I don't know what I think."

Hermione drew a breath.

"Scabbers is a rat."

"Strong start," Harry said.

"A human cannot simply become a rat."

Draconia looked at her.

"Animagi exist."

"They are registered."

"Illegal Animagi also exist."

Hermione's expression tightened.

"Yes, but—"

"But?" Harry asked.

"But Peter Pettigrew is dead."

Across the Hall, the staff doors opened. Professor Lupin entered. He looked pale and moved carefully, one hand brushing the edge of the table before he sat. A covered goblet already waited beside his plate. The moon had been full the night before. Harry watched Snape push the goblet towards him without looking up from the Daily Prophet.

Draconia followed Harry's gaze.

"Later," she said.

Hermione nodded. They returned to breakfast, though Harry caught each of them looking towards the doors before the meal ended. Crookshanks never stopped.

By Wednesday, Scabbers's return had altered the castle in ways no one but the trio seemed to notice. Ron began carrying the rat everywhere.

Scabbers travelled to Charms inside a pocket, slept through History of Magic in Ron's schoolbag and appeared during Care of Magical Creatures with his tail wrapped around Ron's wrist. He refused to eat unless the doors were closed. Sudden noises sent him burrowing into the nearest fabric, and whenever Crookshanks entered a room, the rat became nearly impossible to hold. Ron interpreted this as proof of trauma inflicted by Hermione's cat. Hermione stopped trying to correct him. Draconia began recording observations.

"He does not behave like prey," she said one evening.

They sat in a courtyard warmed by the final sunlight of the day. Ivy had begun returning to the walls, small green leaves emerging along the stone. The fountain in the centre had been repaired after winter, and four carved ravens spat water into the basin with varying enthusiasm. Harry rested against the low wall with the Marauder's Map inside his pocket. Hermione read from an Animagus reference book. Draconia's loose parchment lay across her knees.

"What should prey behave like?" Harry asked.

"Frightened of predators."

"He is frightened of Crookshanks."

"He is more frightened when Sirius Black is mentioned."

Hermione lowered the book.

"That may be because everyone becomes agitated when Sirius is mentioned."

"The rat understands conversation?"

"Animals recognise sounds."

"Then perhaps he thinks Sirius is a type of cat."

Harry leaned towards Draconia's notes. She had written:

Scabbers: estimated age eight to eleven years.

Average domesticated rat lifespan: two to three.

Missing digit.

Unexplained return following disappearance.

Extreme response to Crookshanks, Black's name and unfamiliar adults.

"You made a suspect list for a rat."

"An observation list."

"The title says suspect."

Draconia placed one hand over it.

"That is a later addition."

Hermione turned several pages.

"Animagi retain human intelligence in animal form."

"That would explain why Scabbers behaves unlike an ordinary rat," Harry said.

"He'd have to be an unregistered Animagus. And have survived the explosion, framed Sirius Black and spent twelve years pretending to be a rat."

Harry considered.

"That is less convenient."

"It is absurd."

Draconia looked towards the courtyard entrance.

"Most of our lives would sound absurd if summarised correctly."

"That does not make every absurd theory true."

"No. But it does remove absurdity as disqualification."

Hermione frowned at the book. The Animagus registration list occupied an appendix near the back. Only seven witches and wizards had registered during the twentieth century. Their forms, markings and identifying features had been recorded beside their names. Peter Pettigrew did not appear. Neither did Sirius Black.

"Nothing," Hermione said.

"Does that help?" Harry asked.

"It means neither was registered."

"Both could still be Animagi."

"So could Professor Flitwick."

Draconia looked across the courtyard.

"His animal form would be very small."

Hermione closed the book.

"We need evidence."

"We have a rat old enough to attend Hogwarts," Draconia said.

"That proves only that magical rats may live longer."

"Scabbers came from a Muggle pet shop," Harry said.

Hermione turned.

"How do you know?"

"Ron said Percy bought him after finding him in the shop window. I remember because he claimed Scabbers was discounted."

Hermione counted backwards beneath her breath. "That would place the purchase four or five years after Pettigrew died."

"So the dates do not fit perfectly," Harry said.

"They do not disprove it either. If Scabbers is Pettigrew, we have no idea where he was before the pet shop."

Draconia stared.

"Peter Pettigrew escaped an explosion, transformed into a rat and arranged to be sold at a reduced price?"

Harry lifted one shoulder.

"Perhaps he lacked confidence."

Hermione fought a smile. The fountain splashed behind them. For several minutes, the theory felt like one of their early investigations: impossible pieces arranged on parchment while the castle continued around them. No one had been Petrified. No voice moved inside the walls. A rat was only a rat until proven otherwise. Then Harry remembered Sirius standing beside his parents.

"If Peter's alive," he said, "Sirius didn't kill him."

Hermione's smile vanished.

"He could still have betrayed your parents."

"He could."

"And killed the Muggles."

"He could."

"But the evidence would be wrong," Draconia said.

Harry looked towards the castle. One lie inside the official story did not prove the rest. It opened a door.

Over the next two days, the portrait password changed seven times. Sir Cadogan announced each new word with the excitement of a general distributing battle plans.

At breakfast, the password was Crimson sabre. Before Charms, it became Unhand that maiden. By lunch, Gryffindor students were expected to remember A pox upon the cowardly knees of my enemies, which even Sir Cadogan forgot halfway through and refused to admit. Neville began writing them down. He kept the list folded inside the pocket of his robes and updated it whenever the knight changed his mind. By Thursday evening, the parchment contained three crossed-out challenges, two insults and a sketch of a horse Percy had added while waiting for the portrait to open.

"You should copy it," Neville told Harry.

They stood outside Gryffindor Tower with six other students. Sir Cadogan insisted the current password contained the word turnip. No one else remembered it.

"I'd lose the list," Harry said.

"So do I."

"That weakens the suggestion."

Neville searched his pockets. His face fell.

"I had it at dinner."

Percy made a sound of despair.

"You lost the password list?"

"No. I put it—"

Neville checked the same pocket again. Crookshanks emerged from behind a tapestry. A piece of parchment protruded from his mouth. Hermione stared.

"Crookshanks."

The cat turned.

"Bring that here."

Crookshanks ran. He passed between Percy's legs, bounded down the staircase and vanished around the corner.

"That was mine!" Neville said.

Hermione followed. Harry and Neville ran behind her. Draconia waited near the foot of the tower staircase, having arranged to meet them after dinner. Crookshanks crossed in front of her. She moved to intercept him. The cat changed direction without slowing and slipped through a narrow gap beneath a suit of armour.

Draconia stopped.

"That animal is unnaturally strategic."

Hermione knelt beside the armour.

"Crookshanks."

A low purr answered from behind the metal base.

"Give me the parchment."

A corner appeared. Hermione reached. The paper vanished.

"There are passwords on that," Harry said.

"I know."

"If he carries it outside—"

"I know."

Draconia drew her wand.

"Stand back."

"You cannot hex my cat."

"I intend to move the armour."

"Oh."

Draconia levitated the suit. Crookshanks was gone. A dark opening showed in the wall behind it. Hermione lit her wand. The gap extended farther than expected, narrowing into an old service passage.

Harry took out the Marauder's Map. He tapped it. Ink spread. The corridor appeared. The space behind the armour did not.

"It isn't shown," he said.

Draconia leaned closer.

"The passage where Scabbers escaped was missing too."

Hermione stood.

"The map may not show routes too small for people."

"That one is large enough for a child," Harry said.

"Or a very large dog," Draconia added.

They looked at one another. Harry crouched near the opening. Cold air moved from within.

"Crookshanks?" Hermione called.

No answer came from the passage. Harry sent his wandlight as far as it would travel, revealing rough stone, old cobwebs and a bend too tight for any of them to see beyond. They searched the adjoining corridors until curfew forced Draconia towards the dungeons and Percy ordered the Gryffindors back through the portrait hole.

Crookshanks returned an hour later. He slipped into the common room behind a pair of fifth-years, jumped onto Hermione's lap and circled twice before going to sleep. Hermione checked beneath his chin, around his paws and finally beneath the nearest armchair.

The password list was gone.

Sirius Black entered Gryffindor Tower at four minutes past three the following morning.

Harry woke because Scabbers screamed.

The sound cut through the dormitory with enough force to drag Harry upright before he understood where he was. It was not the brief squeal the rat made whenever Crookshanks entered a room. This cry continued without breath—high, raw and horribly close to human.

Darkness filled the space beyond Harry's curtains. The fire had collapsed into a red seam beneath the grate, and rain whispered against the tower windows. For one confused second, the scream belonged to the Chamber. Then a shape moved near Ron's bed. Fabric ripped from its rail. Ron shouted, and the mattress struck the wall as he scrambled backwards.

Harry reached beneath his pillow and seized his wand.

"Lumos."

Light struck the dormitory. A man stood beside Ron's four-poster. He was taller than Harry expected and so thin that his clothes hung from him like material draped across bone. Tangled black hair reached his shoulders. One hand gripped the torn bed curtain. The other held a knife. Sirius Black.

He looked older than the photograph. Azkaban had hollowed his face. Dirt marked his skin, and his eyes burned from deep sockets with an intensity too alive for the rest of him. Ron pressed himself against the headboard. Scabbers fought inside his hands. Black was not looking at Ron.

His gaze fixed upon the rat.

"Give him to me."

His voice scraped through the room. Ron stared.

"What?"

"The rat."

Scabbers screamed louder. Harry pushed his curtains aside. Black's head turned. Their eyes met. The world narrowed.

Harry saw the man from the wedding photograph beneath the ruin. The line of the mouth. The dark brows. The grey eyes which had once laughed while baby Harry pulled his hair. Sirius stopped breathing. The knife lowered.

"Harry."

His name sounded broken. Harry raised his wand.

"Expelliarmus!"

Black moved. The spell struck the bedpost and shattered one carved corner. Students woke around them. Curtains opened. Dean shouted. Neville rolled from his mattress and hit the floor. Sirius lunged—not towards Ron's face, but towards the frantic shape struggling against his chest. Harry cast again.

The knife flew from Black's hand and struck the far wall. Ron threw himself sideways. Scabbers escaped. The rat landed among the blankets, raced across the bed and vanished through a tear in the canopy. Sirius seized the fabric.

"Peter!"

The name tore from him. Harry froze. Black ripped the curtain aside. Scabbers disappeared beneath the bed. Sirius dropped to one knee and reached after him.

Ron kicked. His heel struck Black's shoulder. Sirius turned with a snarl. For one second, the man resembled the moving photograph from the Daily Prophet: teeth bared, face emptied of everything except fury. Harry stepped between them.

"Stay away from him."

Sirius looked at Harry. The rage vanished. Not softened.

Replaced.

Pain entered his face with such force that Harry almost lowered his wand.

"I'm not here for you," Sirius said.

Behind Harry, Scabbers squealed. Sirius's attention snapped towards the sound. Harry fired a Stunning Spell. Black twisted aside. Red light struck the wall.

The dormitory door flew open. Hermione stood in the doorway wearing a dressing gown and holding her wand.

"Stupefy!"

Sirius ducked. The spell passed over him and struck Seamus's pillow. Feathers exploded through the room. Sirius snatched the knife from the floor. He did not attack. He cut through the remaining bed curtain, searching.

Scabbers darted from beneath Ron's bed towards the open door. Hermione saw him. So did Sirius. The rat ran between her feet, and Hermione turned too late to move out of the doorway.

Sirius charged after the rat. To Harry, it looked like an attack upon Hermione. His body moved before thought. He caught Sirius around the waist, and the impact carried both of them into a bedpost hard enough to shake the canopy. Pain crossed Harry's shoulder.

Sirius could have thrown him aside. He did not. His hands caught Harry's arms, steadying him. For one suspended instant, they stood close enough that Harry saw the tears inside the man's eyes.

"You look like him," Sirius whispered.

Harry struck him. His fist hit Black's mouth. Sirius staggered backwards. Harry raised his wand. The dormitory door slammed.

Black had gone through it. Hermione followed. Harry ran behind her. They reached the common room in time to see the portrait swinging open. Crookshanks stood beside it.

Sirius crossed the threshold. Before disappearing, he looked back. Not towards Harry. Towards the staircase Scabbers had taken. Then the portrait closed.

Silence held the room. Hermione aimed her wand at the canvas. Sir Cadogan swung into view upon his pony.

"An intruder!" he cried. "A gaunt rogue of villainous aspect! Fear not—I permitted him passage so that I might engage him upon favourable terrain!"

"You let him in?" Hermione demanded.

"He possessed the passwords."

Harry looked at Crookshanks. The cat sat beneath the portrait, tail curled around his paws. The parchment list was no longer in his mouth. Harry's wand pointed towards him. Crookshanks met his eyes.

"Where did he go?" Harry asked.

The cat blinked. The dormitory erupted behind them. Students poured down the stairs. Percy shouted for everyone to remain calm while becoming visibly less calm with every word. Ron appeared carrying a shoe in one hand and bleeding from a scratch along his cheek.

"Scabbers," he said.

Harry turned.

"Where is he?"

"I don't know."

Ron looked towards the boys' staircase.

"He ran."

The portrait hole opened. Professor McGonagall entered with Snape, Flitwick and three suits of armour behind her. Her eyes took in the feathers, the students and Harry standing in the centre with his wand raised.

"What happened?"

"Sirius Black," Hermione said. "He was in the dormitory."

McGonagall's face drained of colour. Snape drew his wand.

"Where?"

"He left through the portrait."

Sir Cadogan lifted his sword.

"I granted him entrance!"

McGonagall turned with terrifying slowness.

"You did what?"

"He had every word of passage!"

Neville made a small sound. The room looked towards him. His face became white.

"My list."

Snape's gaze cut towards Hermione.

"And how did Black acquire it?"

Hermione looked towards the place beneath the portrait where Crookshanks had been sitting.

The cat had disappeared.

The castle did not sleep again. Gryffindor students were moved to the Great Hall while professors searched every corridor, passage and tower. Ghosts travelled through walls. Suits of armour sealed the staircases in clanking pairs, and Filch tested every external door and window twice. The enchanted ceiling had lost its stars beneath a bank of low grey cloud. Beyond the real windows, Dementors gathered along the boundary until frost spread across the glass from the edges inward.

Harry sat upon the stone ledge beneath one of the high windows while the Hall filled with blankets, frightened whispers and the smell of cooling candles. Hermione occupied the place beside him. Neither had changed from their nightclothes. Harry's pyjama shirt had torn along one shoulder, and dried blood marked his knuckles where he had struck Sirius. Each time he closed his hand, he remembered the shock of bone beneath his fist—and Sirius catching him before that, when it would have been easier to throw him aside.

Professor McGonagall questioned them separately.

Harry repeated the facts. Black stood near Ron's bed. Black demanded the rat. Black said Peter's name. Black saw Harry and lowered the knife.

McGonagall paused over that detail.

"He recognised you?"

"Yes."

"And did not attack?"

"No."

"Perhaps your spell prevented him."

"He had time."

McGonagall's quill stopped. Harry continued.

"He could have hurt me when I grabbed him. He didn't."

Her face became unreadable.

"You must not interpret restraint as innocence."

"I'm not."

"Mr Potter—"

"I'm telling you what happened."

McGonagall studied him. Then she wrote it down. Snape was less patient. He questioned Harry near the doors while two Hufflepuff prefects moved sleeping bags away from the windows.

"You claim Black shouted Pettigrew's name."

"He did."

"The name of a man he murdered."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"I don't know."

Snape's eyes narrowed. Harry heard the thought in his tone.

Lupin.

"Professor Lupin was ill," Harry said.

"I did not ask."

"You were going to."

Snape's mouth tightened.

"Black entered through the portrait using stolen passwords. His method requires no assistance from Lupin."

"Then leave him out of it."

"Your attachment to that man has developed quickly."

"So has your obsession."

Snape leaned closer.

"Do not mistake Lupin's softness for honesty."

Harry held his gaze.

"Do not mistake your hatred for evidence."

The words struck. Snape's face changed into something colder than anger. Before he answered, Dumbledore entered the Hall. Every voice stopped. The Headmaster's robes moved behind him, dark blue against the candlelight. His expression held no trace of his usual warmth.

"Sirius Black has left the castle," he announced. "The grounds remain under search. All students will stay here until breakfast."

Ron stood.

"My rat's missing."

Several people stared at him. Ron's face reddened, but he continued.

"Black was trying to get him."

Dumbledore looked towards Harry.

"You witnessed this?"

"Yes."

"Did Black say why?"

"No."

"Did he use Mr Pettigrew's name?"

The direct question sharpened the room. Harry nodded. Dumbledore's gaze moved briefly towards McGonagall. Something passed between them.

"Find the rat if you can," he told Ron. "Do not leave the Hall."

Ron sat. The teachers withdrew to continue searching. Harry looked towards the far doors.

"Draco doesn't know," he said.

Hermione followed his gaze.

"The Slytherins are in their common room."

"She'll hear rumours."

Hermione took the silver mirror from the pocket of her dressing gown.

"You first."

Harry accepted it. He whispered Draconia's name. The surface brightened immediately. She appeared fully dressed, pale hair pulled back and wand in hand. Green light moved across the Slytherin common-room wall behind her. Students crowded near the doorway.

"What happened?"

"Sirius got into our dormitory."

Draconia went white.

"Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Hermione?"

"I'm here," Hermione said.

Draconia looked between them inside the reflection.

"Where is Black?"

"Gone."

"Did he reach you?"

Harry hesitated.

"Yes."

Her fingers tightened around her own mirror.

"He didn't hurt me."

"That is not reassuring."

"He wasn't there for me."

Draconia stopped.

"What?"

"He went to Ron's bed."

"Why?"

"For Scabbers."

Silence.

Behind Draconia, Pansy shouted a question. Draconia turned.

"Go away."

"But—"

"Now."

The reflection shifted as she moved into an empty dormitory and closed the door.

"Tell me everything," she said.

Harry did. He included the knife, the name Peter and Sirius's words.

I'm not here for you.

Draconia listened without interrupting. When he finished, she stared beyond the mirror.

"The rat has disappeared again."

"Yes."

"Crookshanks stole the password list?"

"We think so," Hermione said.

"And opened the portrait for Black."

"The portrait opened because Black had the passwords," Harry said. "Crookshanks was there."

Draconia looked at him.

"You are defending the cat."

"I'm describing it."

"Distinction noted."

Hermione glanced towards the teachers.

"We need to speak somewhere private."

"The castle is locked down," Draconia replied.

"Tomorrow."

"No," Harry said.

Both girls looked at him. He felt the Marauder's Map folded inside his pocket. It had remained there throughout the attack. He had almost forgotten it.

"I have the map."

Hermione understood first.

"You can search for Black."

Harry took it out.

"And Scabbers."

They opened the map beneath Harry's Invisibility Cloak. The three of them could not fit comfortably beneath it, particularly while sitting against a wall in a crowded Great Hall, but Harry and Hermione managed to conceal the parchment across their knees. Draconia watched through the mirror, her face hovering in one silver corner. Harry tapped the map.

"I solemnly swear that I am up to no good."

"Understatement," Draconia said.

Ink crossed the parchment. The castle unfolded. Hundreds of names occupied the Great Hall. Students overlapped so densely that most became impossible to read. Teachers moved through the corridors in pairs. Snape crossed the fourth floor with Filch. McGonagall searched the Astronomy Tower. Dumbledore's name stood motionless inside his office. No Sirius Black.

"He's gone," Hermione whispered.

"Or the map can't detect him."

"It might still detect him if he's an Animagus," Harry said.

"How do you know?"

He did not. The map had never been tested upon one. Harry searched the grounds anyway. Hagrid remained inside his hut with Fang. Professor Flitwick moved along the boundary. Dementors did not appear.

"Animals aren't named," Hermione said. "Crookshanks never appears."

"But an Animagus is human."

"Possibly."

They examined Gryffindor Tower. Ron's dormitory stood empty. No Scabbers. No Peter Pettigrew. Harry felt foolish.

The theory had already grown larger than its evidence. An old rat, a missing toe and Sirius shouting the name of a dead man did not change twelve years of history. Hermione shifted beneath the Cloak.

"Wait."

"What?"

She pointed towards a narrow line near the seventh-floor corridor. A dot moved inside the wall. The letters were cramped by the passage. Harry tilted the map.

P.

The dot crossed beneath the North Tower.

E.

It paused.

T.

Hermione stopped breathing. The label became clear.

Peter Pettigrew.

As the name moved, fresh ink uncurled across the wall around it, sketching a passage that had not existed upon the parchment a moment earlier. Harry's hands went numb.

"That's impossible," Hermione whispered.

Draconia leaned towards the mirror.

"What do you see?"

Harry turned the parchment so the mirror reflected it. The silver surface reversed the words, but Draconia read them anyway.

"Peter Pettigrew."

The name reached a junction. It disappeared. Not faded. Gone beyond the edge of the enchantment. Harry looked at Hermione.

She looked back. The Great Hall continued around them. Students whispered. Candles burned. Teachers searched for a murderer who had broken into a child's bedroom with a knife. The map lay across Harry's knees and claimed the murdered man had just crossed the seventh floor.

For twelve years, the story had possessed the hard, finished shape of history. Peter was dead. Sirius had killed him. Harry's parents had trusted the wrong man and paid for it with their lives. Now one line of ink had loosened every certainty at once. Harry wanted to unfold the map again, find the hidden passage and run until he caught the name himself. He also wanted to shove the parchment beneath the nearest suit of armour and let the adults keep their simpler answer.

Instead, he folded it.

"Tell no one," Hermione said.

Harry stared at her.

"That is usually my line."

"I mean until we can verify it."

"We verify it together," Draconia added. "No following the dot alone."

Harry looked at the mirror.

"I wasn't going to."

Both girls remained silent.

"I brought you the map."

Hermione's expression softened. Harry heard the congratulations before she could give them.

"Don't," he warned.

"I hadn't said anything."

"You were about to."

Draconia studied her through the mirror. "He is correct. It was becoming visible."

"I am being criticised for a sentence I deliberately chose not to say."

"I'm saving you the trouble," Harry replied.

Despite the cold, the attack and the impossible name, Harry almost laughed. The sound caught halfway out. Peter Pettigrew was alive. If the map told the truth, every adult had been wrong for twelve years. And Sirius Black had entered Gryffindor Tower for the rat.

They met in the abandoned music classroom after breakfast. Harry had slept for forty minutes inside the Great Hall. Hermione had not slept at all. Draconia arrived through the eastern corridor carrying three cups of tea, a wrapped bundle of toast and the sharp expression of someone who had spent the night frightening Slytherin prefects into cooperation. Harry took one cup.

"Did you threaten anyone?"

"Only administratively."

"That is not a recognised category of threat," Hermione said.

"It achieved recognition."

The chairs still stood in the triangular arrangement used for the memory spell. The repaired basin had been removed, but a pale ring remained upon the floor where moonstone water had dried between the stones. No one suggested sitting in the same formation. Harry dragged one chair towards the windows; Hermione placed hers beside a desk, and Draconia chose the edge of another desk near the wall.

None of them mentioned why the altered geometry made the room easier to breathe in.

Harry opened the map. Peter's name had vanished. They watched the corridors for several minutes, following dots they already recognised, but nothing moved through the hidden passage near the North Tower.

Hermione spread parchment across the nearest desk.

"Let's work out what it could mean."

"He's alive," Harry said.

"The map may be wrong."

"It knew everyone else."

"It could identify a ghost or portrait by the same name."

"Ghosts move differently," Draconia said. "And portraits do not enter walls beyond their frames."

"Another Peter Pettigrew could be at Hogwarts."

Harry shook his head.

"A student?"

"There is no student by that name," Draconia said. "I checked the register."

Hermione looked at her.

"When?"

"Before breakfast."

"How?"

"Slytherin prefects possess access to House records."

"You are not a prefect."

"I possessed access to a Slytherin prefect."

Harry drank his tea.

"You threatened someone administratively."

"Precisely."

Hermione wrote:

Possibility one: different person with same name—unlikely.

"Professor?" she suggested.

"No Pettigrew on the staff," Harry said.

"Visitor?"

"The castle was sealed."

Hermione added:

Possibility two: map error.

Harry watched her.

"You don't believe that."

"No."

"But you wrote it."

"We need to consider it."

"Fine. Possibility three: the dead man is not dead."

The quill paused. Hermione wrote it. The words altered the room. Draconia unwrapped the toast. She gave one piece to Harry without comment.

He accepted it.

"How could he survive the explosion?" she asked.

"Shield Charm," Harry said.

"Witnesses said Sirius cast."

"Witnesses saw the aftermath," Hermione corrected. "They heard Peter accuse him before the explosion."

"Peter could have cast it," Harry said.

"And transformed."

Draconia looked at Hermione's notes.

"Into Scabbers."

Hermione drew a line beneath the theory.

"We need to prove that."

"Missing finger," Harry said.

"Toe."

"Equivalent in animal form, perhaps."

"Animagus markings reflect features of the human body," Hermione said. "McGonagall's markings resemble her spectacles."

Draconia added the missing digit.

"Age."

"Scabbers appeared after Pettigrew died," Harry said. "Percy bought him years later, but he still came from nowhere and has already lived several times longer than an ordinary rat."

Hermione marked the gap upon the timeline. "That prevents the pet-shop story from proving when he transformed. He could have lived elsewhere first."

"It weakens the sequence," Draconia said, "but not the identification. The missing digit and the Map matter more."

"Can we confirm the date?"

"Ron might know."

"He will not answer if we ask whether his pet is a fugitive," Draconia said.

"Then we don't ask that."

Hermione wrote the timeline.

"Sirius knew Scabbers was Peter."

Harry saw the dormitory again. Black beside Ron's bed.

Give him to me.

Peter.

"He wasn't trying to kill me."

Neither girl contradicted him.

"He saw me," Harry continued. "He had the knife. I grabbed him."

Draconia's face tightened.

"He could have hurt me."

"Yes," Hermione said.

"He didn't."

"That does not prove he is safe."

"No."

"Then why did he go straight to Ron's bed?" Draconia asked. "He's tried to get into the tower twice now. Both times Scabbers was up here."

"He could have come for both," Hermione warned.

"Then why leave Harry standing?" Draconia asked.

The question remained unanswered. Harry looked towards the rain-dark windows.

"Because he knew me."

His godfather's face returned. Not the laughing man in the photograph. The gaunt prisoner lowering his knife.

You look like him.

Harry's anger had prepared itself for a murderer. It did not know what to do with grief. Hermione looked at him carefully.

"What do you want this to mean?"

"That's not evidence."

"No."

Harry folded his arms.

"I want him not to have done it."

The confession felt dangerous. Draconia lowered her gaze. Hermione stopped writing. Harry continued before either could offer comfort.

"I want my parents not to have been wrong about him. I want one person they chose to have stayed what they thought he was."

"That might make you want to believe it," Hermione said.

"I know."

"But you're not inventing what's on the map."

Harry looked at her.

"Is that your way of agreeing with me?"

"My way of saying we should continue."

"Painful, wasn't it?"

"Very."

Draconia pushed the plate of toast towards Hermione. She took a piece without noticing.

"What about Crookshanks?" Harry asked.

Hermione's attention moved towards the door, almost expecting the cat to appear.

"He stole the passwords."

"For Sirius," Draconia said.

"We do not know that."

"He carried food to the black dog," Harry replied.

Hermione frowned.

"Which may have been Sirius."

"The dog was at Privet Drive before the Knight Bus came," Harry said. "It appeared beside the Quidditch pitch. Crookshanks sat with it."

Draconia's eyes sharpened.

"Sirius is an Animagus."

Hermione stared at the map.

"If he is, it explains Azkaban."

"How?" Harry asked.

"Dementors cannot see in the ordinary sense. They sense human emotion. An animal mind may register differently."

"They didn't know to guard against a dog," Draconia added.

"The Ministry registration list didn't include him."

"Unregistered."

Harry thought of the black dog waiting at the edge of Magnolia Crescent. It had not attacked. The Knight Bus had nearly hit it when Harry fell. The dog had watched him leave.

"The Grim," he said.

"What?" Draconia asked.

"Trelawney kept seeing a black dog in my future."

Hermione's expression became deeply unimpressed.

"She saw a shape in some tea leaves. She thinks everything means you're going to die."

"She wasn't wrong about the dog."

"She was wrong about the supernatural omen."

"We haven't proved that."

"You are not dying to validate Divination."

Draconia looked between them. "Could we restrict ourselves to one impossible subject?"

Hermione returned to the parchment.

"If Sirius is the dog, Crookshanks has been helping him."

"And if Peter is Scabbers, Crookshanks has been trying to catch him," Harry said.

"Not kill him," Draconia added. "Herd him."

The first-floor chase returned. Crookshanks directing Scabbers towards the hidden passage. A cat trying to deliver a rat to a dog. Hermione looked sick.

"He let Sirius into Gryffindor Tower."

"Yes," Harry said.

"That is extremely poor judgement."

"He's a cat."

"He understood enough to steal Neville's password list."

Draconia's mouth moved.

"Your cat has a better plan than the Ministry."

"This is not funny."

"A little," Harry said.

Hermione glared at both. Then she looked down at her notes.

"If we are right, Sirius Black spent twelve years in Azkaban for a murder Peter Pettigrew staged."

"And Peter spent twelve years as a pet," Harry said.

Silence.

Draconia stared at the words as a pet.

"Damn rat."

Harry looked at her. She rarely swore. Hermione appeared too tired to object.

"What do we do?" Harry asked.

The old answer would have been investigate. Follow Peter's dot. Find the passage. Force Scabbers to transform before anyone could stop them. Harry wanted to do all three.

The treaty inside his pocket seemed to grow heavier. Hermione saw his hand move towards it.

"We tell an adult," she said.

Draconia looked unhappy.

"We agreed," Hermione reminded her.

"I know."

"Which adult?" Harry asked.

"Professor McGonagall."

"She thinks Sirius is guilty."

"She can still look at what we've found."

"Dumbledore," Draconia proposed.

Harry shook his head.

"Lucius."

"He is in London."

"He can reach Hogwarts."

"Not before Peter disappears again."

Hermione looked towards Harry.

"Lupin."

His chest tightened.

"He knew all of them," she continued. "He may know whether Peter could become an Animagus."

"And if Sirius fooled him?" Draconia asked.

"Then he may recognise how."

Harry stared at the map. Lupin had left the most important truths until Harry discovered them elsewhere.

Godfather.

Werewolf.

Past friendships turned into locked rooms. But Lupin had also remained in the office while Harry slept. He had named the Dursleys' abuse without using it to take Harry's choices. He had told the truth about his own cowardice when a kinder lie would have protected him. Harry folded the map.

"Lupin."

They could not speak to him immediately. The search continued through Friday morning, and Professor Dumbledore kept Lupin with the other members of staff while Ministry officials inspected Gryffindor Tower and argued about the Dementors at the gates. Snape taught Defence in his place that afternoon and spent the lesson criticising the curriculum, the class and Lupin's decision to introduce third years to "advanced protective charms before they have mastered the difficult art of remaining conscious."

Harry watched the professor. Snape knew Sirius and Peter. He had attended Hogwarts with them. More than once, Harry nearly asked whether Peter Pettigrew could become a rat. Each time, the hostility in Snape's face stopped him.

After class, Snape called Draconia back. Harry and Hermione waited outside. Through the door, Snape's voice reached them in fragments.

"—Black entered a tower filled with children."

"I know."

"Your association with Potter places you within any danger surrounding him."

"That has been true for three years."

"This is not amusing."

"I wasn't amused."

A silence followed. Snape spoke more quietly.

"Your father's change in political posture has produced enemies."

Harry looked at Hermione. She moved closer to the door. Draconia replied.

"Do you think Black is connected to them?"

"No. I think chaos permits unrelated enemies to act unseen."

"Father knows."

"Lucius believes knowledge and control are synonyms."

"They often are."

"No."

The answer held enough experience to silence Draconia. When she emerged, her face was thoughtful.

"What did he want?" Harry asked.

"To tell me Father has made people angry."

"That describes most days."

"Different people."

Hermione lowered her voice.

"Old Death Eaters?"

Draconia looked down the corridor.

"He did not say."

"But you think so."

"Yes."

Harry thought of Lucius's changing loyalties, the rejected diary and the Malfoy family's increasingly public protection of a Muggle-born girl and Harry Potter. The war had ended before any of them could remember it. Its loyalties remained alive beneath everything.

"Tell Lucius about Peter," Harry said.

Draconia looked at him.

"After Lupin."

"He may know how to verify Ministry records."

"Then yes."

The decision represented more trust than Harry would have offered Lucius a year earlier. Draconia heard it. She did not make the moment unbearable.

"Agreed," she said.

Lupin returned to dinner that evening. He looked exhausted but otherwise well. The cut along his cheek had nearly healed. He ate little, spoke briefly with Professor Flitwick and left before dessert. Harry waited ten minutes. Then the trio followed. The corridor outside Lupin's office smelled of woodsmoke and the bitter residue of Wolfsbane. Light showed beneath the door.

Harry knocked.

"Come in."

They entered together. Lupin sat behind his desk marking essays. A teapot steamed beside his elbow, and the curtains remained drawn despite the early evening. His eyes moved from Harry to Hermione and Draconia.

"This appears organised."

"It is," Hermione said.

"That rarely means good news."

"We need to show you something," Harry replied.

Lupin placed down his quill.

"Is anyone injured?"

"No."

"Has anyone used prohibited mental magic?"

"No."

"Illegally altered time?"

Hermione coloured.

"No."

"Then this is already an improvement."

Draconia closed the door. Harry remained standing before the desk. He felt the map beneath his robes. Once he showed it, the object would no longer belong only to the trio. It contained every passage they had used, every teacher's location and evidence which could change the story of his parents' deaths. Lupin waited.

Harry took it out. The parchment was blank, its creases softened from years in other people's pockets. Something changed in Lupin's face before Harry unfolded it. His eyes caught upon one frayed edge and remained there, as though the empty parchment had spoken first.

Recognition.

Immediate and complete. He rose.

"Where did you get that?"

Harry looked at Hermione. She saw it too.

"The Weasley twins," Harry said. "They took it from Filch."

"When?"

"Years ago."

Lupin came around the desk. His hand extended towards the parchment, then stopped.

"May I?"

Harry gave it to him. The surrender felt larger than passing over a sheet of parchment. The map contained the castle as Harry knew it: its hidden staircases, its secret doors and the moving names of everyone within its walls. Lupin accepted it with both hands. His thumb moved across one stained corner with the unconscious certainty of someone finding an old mark in the dark.

"You know what it is," Draconia said.

He looked at her.

"What do you believe it is?"

"A map of Hogwarts."

"More than that," Hermione said. "It shows every person inside the castle."

Lupin's gaze snapped towards her.

"Every person?"

"As far as we know."

Harry watched his face.

"You've seen it before."

Lupin looked down at the parchment.

"Yes."

The room became very quiet. Harry drew his wand and touched the surface.

"I solemnly swear that I am up to no good."

Ink spread beneath Lupin's hands. The words formed.

Messrs Moony, Wormtail, Padfoot and Prongs are delighted to present the Marauder's Map.

Lupin stopped breathing. His fingers closed around the edges.

"Professor?" Hermione asked.

He read the names again. His thumb came to rest beneath Moony before he seemed to realise what he was doing and drew it away. Grief, fear and disbelief crossed his face too quickly to separate. Harry pointed to the second.

"Wormtail."

Lupin looked at him.

"That was Peter, wasn't it?"

No answer.

"Padfoot was Sirius," Harry continued.

Lupin lowered the map.

"How do you know?"

"The black dog."

The colour left Lupin's face.

"You saw it?"

"At Privet Drive. Near the Quidditch pitch. Outside Hagrid's hut."

"Why did you not tell me?"

"I told Hermione and Draco."

"That was not my question."

Harry heard the fear. Not suspicion. Lupin moved towards the window and pulled the curtain aside. Dusk covered the grounds. The Whomping Willow stood black against the final light.

"What happened?" he asked.

Harry told him about the break-in. Not the abbreviated account given to McGonagall.

Everything.

Sirius demanding the rat. Calling Peter's name. Lowering the knife when he saw Harry. Catching Harry rather than throwing him aside. Crookshanks waiting beside the open portrait. Lupin listened with one hand braced against the window frame. When Harry finished, Hermione placed her notes upon the desk.

"Scabbers returned earlier this week," she said. "He is at least eight years old and has lived far beyond the normal lifespan of a domesticated rat. The Weasleys acquired him years after Peter Pettigrew supposedly died, but he appeared without any known history in a Muggle pet shop."

"He is missing a toe," Draconia added.

"The same hand on which Peter lost a finger."

Lupin turned. Harry had never seen him look afraid in this way. Not during the Dementor lessons. Not after the Fat Lady attack. Not while admitting what he had failed to do after James and Lily died. This fear belonged to hope. Hope was more dangerous.

"That is impossible," Lupin said.

Harry unfolded the map fully across the desk.

"We saw him."

"Peter?"

"His name."

Lupin approached.

"Where?"

"Inside a passage near the seventh floor. After Sirius left."

Lupin searched the parchment. Names moved through the castle. Professor Sprout crossed the entrance hall. Filch stood near the kitchens. Dumbledore remained inside his office. No Peter Pettigrew. Lupin's shoulders lowered.

"It may have been a flaw."

Hermione stared at him.

"You know how the map works."

"Yes."

"Can it mistake one person for another?"

"No."

The answer came too quickly. Harry's pulse quickened.

"Can it show dead people?"

"No."

"Ghosts?"

"Yes, but they are identified correctly."

"So if it showed Peter—"

Lupin closed his eyes.

"He was alive."

The words barely emerged. Draconia looked towards Harry. He gripped the desk.

"Then Sirius didn't kill him."

"No."

Lupin opened his eyes. The years inside them had changed.

"Professor," Hermione said, "could Peter become an animal?"

Lupin's gaze moved to the word Wormtail. He did not answer. Harry understood.

"He could."

Lupin's fingers pressed into the desk.

"Where is the rat now?"

"Missing," Harry said. "It escaped during the attack."

Lupin searched the map again. Every line. Every passage. His attention stopped near the grounds. A dot had appeared beside Hagrid's hut.

The label was too small to read from Harry's side of the desk. Lupin bent closer. The dot moved into the shadow of the pumpkin patch. Harry came around the desk. Letters formed.

Peter Pettigrew.

Alive.

At Hogwarts.

Lupin's hand began to shake. The name vanished beneath the edge of Hagrid's hut, but Harry was no longer looking at the grounds. His attention had moved to the four names written across the top of the parchment.

Moony.

Lupin, disappearing with the full moon.

Wormtail.

A rat whose tail had wrapped itself around Ron's wrist.

Padfoot.

The black dog waiting at the edge of Privet Drive.

Prongs.

A silver stag turning its antlered head towards Lupin.

The names were not signatures chosen merely for secrecy. They were portraits.

Harry looked up at Lupin.

"You know this map," he said.

Lupin remained fixed upon Peter's last position.

"You know why it calls them that."

"Yes."

"How?"

The professor's face held twelve years of grief rearranging itself around one living name. When he finally answered, his voice was barely more than breath.

"Because I helped make it."
