1

Lucius Malfoy's evening had begun inconveniently and was rapidly acquiring the shape of something worse.

He had intended to spend it alone in his study, untangling several weeks of Ministry correspondence before dinner. Instead, his work lay abandoned beneath the hands of an exceptionally unwelcome guest. Heir Nott stood on the opposite side of the desk, uninvited, unsmiling and apparently in no hurry to leave.

Between them rested a slim black book.

The object was completely unimpressive given the immediate hostility. Its leather cover had been scuffed at the corners, and years of handling had softened its edges. No title marked the cover or spine. When Nott opened it, the yellowed pages revealed no writing or obvious enchantment.

Lucius examined the blank paper before raising his eyes to his guest.

"A tatty diary," he said, making no effort to conceal his disdain.

"It belonged to someone whose possessions were rarely without purpose."

"So you have said."

Rain tapped lightly against the high windows. The summer evening beyond them had faded into a dull wash of grey, leaving the study illuminated by the green-shaded lamp upon Lucius's desk and a fire burning unnecessarily in the grate. Several Ministry notices lay beside the diary, all concerning Arthur Weasley's proposed inspections of old magical homes.

Nott rested one hand upon the book.

"My father received it during the war."

"Your father collected a great many things during the war. Most of them became liabilities once it ended."

"This was given to him."

Lucius's gaze lifted.

"By whom?"

Nott did not answer directly. He did not need to. Lucius looked again at the diary. Nothing about it changed. It remained an old, blank book resting between men who possessed far more valuable objects than it.

"I told you during your last visit," Lucius said, "that I want nothing associated with Voldemort inside this house."

Nott's expression tightened at the name.

"You speak very boldly now that he cannot hear you."

"He is dead."

"You cannot be certain of that."

"He has been gone for nearly eleven years. I will not spend my daughter's life waiting for him to return."

The insult settled between them. Nott's fingers moved over the diary's cover.

"You found your principles quickly enough once the war was over."

"I had a family to protect. I still do."

"And you have survived very comfortably."

Lucius leaned back in his chair. "If you came here to admire my furniture, you might have requested a tour."

"I came because Arthur Weasley intends to search our homes."

"He intends to search yours. My solicitors have already restricted the scope of any inspection conducted here."

"For now."

"Then I suggest you employ better solicitors."

Nott's restraint thinned.

"Several families remember that your influence was not built alone."

"Several families mistake being present for being useful."

"Some of us can prove where you were."

Lucius's hand became still upon the silver head of his cane. There it was.

The threat had existed beneath both of Nott's visits, carefully concealed behind shared history and political concern. Speaking it aloud did not make it stronger. It merely made further civility unnecessary.

"What do you want?" Lucius asked.

Nott pushed the diary an inch across the desk.

"Weasley's daughter begins at Hogwarts this year."

Lucius looked from the book to him.

"And?"

"There will be opportunities before term begins. Flourish and Blotts, perhaps. The station, if necessary. Children misplace things. Parents fail to inspect everything placed inside a cauldron."

"You want me to put this among the girl's belongings."

"I want Arthur Weasley's enthusiasm for searching other people's homes to become embarrassing."

Lucius did not touch the diary.

"If a prohibited object were discovered in the possession of his daughter," Nott continued, "questions would follow. Questions about his household, his judgment and the peculiar collection of Muggle objects he keeps without proper registration. His inspections would end before they reached another ancestral door."

"And when he explains that the object was planted?"

"He will sound exactly like every wizard he has accused."

Lucius's eyes narrowed.

"You have not told me what the diary does."

"It need not do anything. Its history is sufficient."

"What history?"

"One the Ministry would find troubling."

"That answer might satisfy a fool."

"Then stop treating me like one."

The fire snapped.

"What was done to it?" Lucius asked.

Nott's gaze sharpened. "You are becoming repetitive."

"And you are avoiding the question."

"My father never explained its enchantments."

"Yet you want it placed beside an eleven-year-old girl."

"You object to her age?"

"I object to employing an object you cannot identify in a scheme you cannot control."

"Concern for a Weasley child. How progressive."

"My daughter attends that same school."

Nott smiled without warmth.

"Yes. She does."

Lucius straightened. His eyes travelled once over Nott, then towards the door.

"I suggest you take your... filthy diary and leave."

"You have not considered the consequences."

"I considered them during your first visit. The answer was no then. It has not improved with repetition."

"Arthur's inspections could uncover enough to remove you from the Board of Governors."

"But they will not."

"The old families may hear that Lucius Malfoy has begun refusing friends."

"You did not enter this room as a friend."

Nott's hand closed around the diary.

"And what of Draconia?"

The temperature inside the study seemed to fall.

Lucius's voice lowered. "Choose your next words carefully."

"People have noticed her loyalties."

"She is a twelve-year-old."

"She is a Malfoy. That has never permitted the luxury of being merely twelve."

Lucius knew the lesson. He had taught versions of it himself. Nott continued.

"She spent her first-year crossing House lines for Harry Potter. She befriended a Muggle-born girl openly enough for the whole school to see. Now I hear she writes to both throughout the summer."

"My daughter's correspondence is not your concern."

"It becomes our concern when others begin asking whether the Malfoys still remember what they are."

Lucius came around the desk.

"My daughter has forgotten nothing."

"Then perhaps she should be encouraged to demonstrate it."

The study door opened. Narcissa stood upon the threshold.

She rested one hand on the handle and the other beneath a stack of sealed envelopes. Draconia stood just behind her, holding a second collection against her chest.

Narcissa took in the room quickly: her husband beside the desk, Nott facing him and the black diary gripped in one hand.

Her gaze lingered upon the book for no more than a second.

"Are we interrupting?"

Nott's mouth tightened.

"Your husband and I have finished."

"You should have left several minutes ago," Lucius said.

Nott snatched his gloves from the desk.

As he passed Narcissa, she moved aside without offering him the courtesy of a farewell. He stopped when he reached Draconia.

The girl held his gaze.

She had known Theodore Nott for most of her life, although knowing Theodore had never required familiarity with his father. The Nott family had attended the same gatherings as the Malfoys, stood in the same drawing rooms and spoke with the same people. They belonged to the background architecture of their world: always present, rarely considered.

Tonight, Heir Nott looked at her as though she had become personally disappointing.

"Miss Malfoy."

"Mr Nott."

His attention moved to the letters in her arms. Harry's name was visible upon the uppermost envelope. Something ugly stirred behind his eyes.

Draconia drew the letters closer. Nott smiled. Then he walked away.

Narcissa watched until his footsteps disappeared along the corridor before entering the study. Draconia followed, and the door closed behind them.

Lucius remained standing.

"What did he want?" Narcissa asked.

"To waste my evening."

"Was it another of his artefacts?"

Lucius looked at her.

"You knew about that?"

"I knew he brought an object which frightened Dobby badly enough to destroy a tray of eighteenth-century crystal."

"The tray was replaceable."

"The elf has since vanished."

Draconia placed the letters upon the desk.

"Is that why Dobby ran?" she asked.

"I do not know why Dobby ran."

"These might explain part of it."

She released the bundle. The letters spread across Lucius's desk, some bearing Draconia's narrow handwriting and others Hermione's more careful script. Every seal remained intact. Several envelopes had been cut along one edge without being opened, while others bore faint grey marks left by owls turned forcibly from their routes.

Lucius selected the nearest letter.

"What am I looking at?"

"Thirteen attempts to contact Harry," Draconia said. "None reached him."

Lucius lifted the envelope towards the lamp. A thin shimmer moved along its edge. His expression altered.

Narcissa noticed. "House-elf magic."

"Yes."

"Dobby?"

"No other elf connected to this family is missing."

Draconia folded her arms. "Did you order him to stop the letters?"

"No."

"Did Nott?"

"Nott possesses no authority over a Malfoy elf."

"Then why did the letters stop the day after his first visit?"

Lucius placed the envelope down.

Narcissa moved closer to the desk. "What did Dobby hear?"

"A tedious attempt to involve this family in Nott's dispute with Arthur Weasley."

"With an object?"

"Yes."

"What is it?"

"He claims it belonged to Voldemort. That is all I know."

Draconia looked towards the door through which Nott had departed.

"He brought something belonging to Voldemort into our home?"

"Allegedly belonging to him."

"And you didn't think that mattered?"

"I refused to take it."

"That was not my question."

Lucius's eyes narrowed. Narcissa picked up another letter before the disagreement could harden.

"What did Nott intend to do with it?"

"Plant it among the belongings of Arthur Weasley's daughter. He believes its discovery would discredit the family and end the proposed searches."

"Does he know whether it is dangerous?"

"He claims its history is the only useful thing about it."

"And you believed him?"

"No."

"Yet you told no one."

"There was nothing to report. Nott possessed an unidentified item, an unverifiable story, and an ill-conceived plan I had already refused to join."

"Dobby thought there was more to it," Draconia said.

"Dobby is easily frightened. Old magic has always disturbed him."

"He disobeyed you."

Lucius fell silent. Draconia stepped towards him.

"Something frightened Dobby enough to run away. Now there's his magic on our letters. What if he's gone to Harry?"

"Has Potter told you this?"

"No. Harry has told no one anything because nobody can reach him."

The force of the answer caused one of the loose envelopes to slide across the desk. Narcissa steadied it with two fingers.

"The Grangers attempted to telephone his home," she said. "A man answered and denied that Harry Potter lived there. Their Muggle post has also been returned."

"His relatives may have taken him elsewhere."

"He would have sent Hedwig."

"Assuming the elf did not prevent her leaving."

Draconia's face tightened.

"That is supposed to make this better?"

"No."

Lucius looked again at the letters. Dobby had not merely intercepted incoming post. The marks upon the envelopes suggested a barrier laid around Harry's home, one broad enough to repel owls and persistent enough to remain active while its caster was absent.

Maintaining it for weeks would have required considerable strength and desperation.

"Dobby would not be able to tell Harry what he heard," Narcissa said slowly. "You forbade him from repeating the conversation."

"I forbade him from discussing private business."

"If he's trying to keep Harry from me, he can't even explain why."

Lucius recognized the likelihood and disliked it. Draconia didn't give him time to retreat into silence.

"What did Nott say about Harry?"

"Very little."

"He spoke about him before he left."

"He questioned this family's loyalties."

"Because I'm friends with Harry and Hermione."

Lucius did not answer. Draconia understood.

"He threatened you."

"He reminded me that certain families possess long memories."

"And what did you do?"

"I instructed him to leave."

"What will he do now?"

"That is what I intend to discover."

Narcissa placed both palms upon the desk.

"You may discover it after we find Harry."

Lucius looked at her.

"The Grangers are meeting us tomorrow morning," she continued. "They have Harry's address and records of every failed attempt to contact him."

"Then his guardians may explain the situation."

"His guardians locked Hedwig's cage before any of this began."

Lucius recalled Draconia's description of the Dursleys after her first meeting with Harry. Incompetent. Unpleasant. Frightened of magic. At the time, he had considered the arrangement beneath his interest. It was becoming increasingly difficult to maintain that position.

"Why involve ourselves?" he asked, though the question sounded less certain than he intended. "If the boy's relatives object to magical correspondence, that remains a matter for Dumbledore."

"Dumbledore left him there," Draconia said.

"He may have had a reason."

"Then he can explain it after Harry is safe."

Lucius studied his daughter. She had inherited his composure and Narcissa's ability to make stillness feel like pressure. Beneath both, fear showed in the way her fingers curled against her sleeves.

"Nott threatened this family," Lucius said. "Harry Potter is part of the reason he believes our loyalties have changed. Removing the boy from his relatives may appear to confirm that belief."

Draconia's expression sharpened.

"Is that meant to matter more than finding him?"

"It means helping him carries consequences."

"Then carry them."

The response arrived without hesitation. Lucius looked at Narcissa. She made no attempt to correct their daughter.

"Harry is her best friend," she said. "Dobby belongs to our household. Whether or not we created this situation, our name is already part of it."

Lucius looked down at Harry's name repeated across thirteen envelopes.

He thought of Nott's diary, Dobby's terror and the threat concealed beneath a courteous farewell. He thought of his daughter standing at King's Cross beside Harry Potter and Hermione Granger, refusing to behave as though the distance between their Houses mattered.

Helping Harry could be interpreted as weakness. Leaving him isolated because Nott might disapprove would be worse.

"When?" he asked.

"Ten tomorrow morning," Narcissa said.

Draconia's shoulders loosened. Lucius noticed.

"I will come," he said.

"Why?" Draconia asked.

The question surprised him.

He could have answered with politics. Harry Potter's survival remained important to their world's stability. He could speak of Dobby's interference creating a risk to the family, or of Nott's interest in the boy making investigation necessary.

All were true.

None were the answer Draconia wanted.

"Because someone has used a servant of this house to frighten your friend," he said. "And because you asked me to."

Draconia regarded him for a moment. Then she nodded. It was not forgiveness, nor gratitude exactly. But it was enough.

Hermione waited at the Leaky Cauldron with her parents, a notebook and every letter which had returned to Oxford.

She rose the instant the Malfoys entered the private room Tom had provided. Relief crossed her face when she saw Draconia, followed immediately by the apprehension she had been holding in check all morning.

"Is there any news?"

Draconia stopped across the table from her.

"Nothing from Harry."

Hermione's shoulders fell.

"But," Draconia continued, setting her bundle of returned letters beside Hermione's, "we know the failures weren't accidental. The problem isn't with our owls or the wards around the Manor."

"So it's happening at Privet Drive."

"That appears most likely."

"And your parents are going to help us reach him?"

Draconia glanced towards Lucius. "Father has agreed to be useful."

Lucius's gaze settled upon the back of his daughter's head.

"Useful? How generous of you."

"You took some persuading."

"I needed to know what had happened."

"You had thirteen letters."

"And now we shall do something about them."

Hermione looked between them, deciding that whatever argument had taken place at Malfoy Manor could wait until Harry had been found.

"His uncle denied that he lived there," she said. "He didn't say Harry was unavailable or that they had gone away. He said there was no Harry Potter at the address and hung up when I identified myself."

Draconia's expression hardened.

"Then he was lying."

"Yes. I think so too."

"When did you telephone?"

"Three days ago. We tried twice more afterwards, but nobody answered."

Mr and Mrs Granger rose as Narcissa approached. She introduced herself first, accepting Mrs Granger's hand with practised grace before turning to her husband.

Lucius regarded Mr Granger's offered hand for the smallest moment longer than politeness allowed. His eyes briefly examined the man's ordinary suit, wristwatch and conspicuous lack of a wand.

Narcissa glanced at him.

Lucius completed the handshake.

"Lucius Malfoy."

"David Granger. Thank you for coming."

"I understand your daughter has assembled a record of the situation."

"She has assembled several."

"I wanted to make certain we hadn't missed anything," Hermione said.

She opened the notebook across the table. Dates divided two pages into neat rows, each attempt at contact recorded beside its method, result and most likely explanation. The first two exchanges had proceeded without difficulty. Hermione's letters reached Privet Drive, and Harry's replies returned within several days.

After the seventh of July, every line ended in failure.

Draconia moved to Hermione's side and bent over the pages.

"You included the telephone calls."

"And the Muggle letters. The first was returned as undeliverable. The second disappeared."

"You assigned deliberate interference ninety-eight per cent."

"I allowed two per cent for an explanation we haven't considered."

"That was generous."

"I thought so."

Lucius read the table from the opposite side. His attention paused upon the date of Harry's final successful reply, though nothing in his expression explained why.

Hermione noticed the pause.

"So?" she asked.

Lucius looked up.

"So what, Miss Granger?"

"Do you know why the letters stopped?"

"No."

It was not the complete truth, but it was not quite a lie. He knew enough to suspect interference and too little to explain its purpose.

Hermione studied him for a moment before closing the notebook.

"Then we need to ask Harry."

Draconia gathered the returned letters into a single pile.

"First," she said, "we need to reach him."

Mr Granger looked towards his wife.

"We can take our car," he said. "If the situation requires Muggle authorities, we'll be able to contact them. The girls remain outside until we know the house is safe."

Lucius appeared offended by the implication that he required assistance entering a suburban home. Narcissa answered before he could.

"Agreed."

Hermione closed her notebook. Draconia reached for the letters. Their hands met upon the bundle. For a moment, they looked at one another.

"We'll bring him back together," Hermione said.

Draconia nodded. "Together."

After several days of captivity, Harry had learned the exact geography of his room. There were eight steps from his bed to the bedroom door, five from the door to the window and three between the desk and Hedwig's cage. If he moved diagonally, he could manage eleven uninterrupted steps before having to turn. He knew because he had counted them repeatedly, usually during the longest hours of the afternoon when heat gathered beneath the ceiling and the house offered no indication that time remained interested in passing.

The cat-flap opened twice on good days.

On bad ones, it opened once.

Harry had stopped measuring meals by breakfast, lunch and dinner. A bowl of soup pushed through at nine in the morning could be expected to last until night. Bread arrived irregularly. Water came in a plastic cup which Uncle Vernon filled only when Harry returned the previous one.

Hedwig drank first.

The room smelled of warm dust, old soup and the damp flannel Harry kept draped over her cage. Her confinement distressed him more than his own. She paced constantly, feathers dulled by heat, and attacked the padlock whenever footsteps passed the door.

"Save your strength," Harry told her.

Hedwig struck the bars harder.

"All right. Ignore me. It's worked for everyone else."

His voice sounded unused.

The first two days had been filled with attempts. Harry tried the door spindle, the hinges, the window frame and every damaged toy Dudley had left behind. He considered accidental magic and rejected it each time. The Ministry's warning remained vivid in his mind. Another incident might give them reason to expel him, and losing Hogwarts was the one punishment he could not risk.

By the fourth day, the attempts had become less frequent.

By the seventh, Harry spent long periods upon the floor beside Hedwig's cage without deciding to sit there. He would look up and discover that the light had moved across the carpet while he remained still.

The old heaviness had returned. It did not hurt sharply enough to demand attention. It simply removed the purpose from things. Standing required a reason. So did eating. Harry saved half his bread beneath a cloth because the possibility of needing it later felt more urgent than hunger now.

Dobby had shown him the letters.

Hermione and Draconia had written. They had not forgotten him, reconsidered their friendship or decided Hogwarts was easier without the trouble Harry carried into it. On the first nights, that knowledge had been enough to keep hope close.

After a week, hope required more effort.

Perhaps they knew something was wrong but could not find him. Perhaps Dobby's magic continued redirecting every attempt. Harry touched the brass compass lying beside him.

"Hermione."

The needle revolved.

"Draconia."

It spun again.

"Hogwarts."

Nothing.

"Front door."

The needle pointed through the floor.

Harry closed the lid.

"Excellent. I'll walk through the ceiling."

Hedwig gave a low hoot.

A car stopped outside.

Harry did not move at first. Cars had come and gone throughout the week, their doors closing beneath his window before engines carried them away. Number Four remained separate from the ordinary world moving beyond its walls.

Then another car arrived. Voices carried from the pavement. One he knew for certain belonged to Hermione. Harry was upright before he realized he had stood.

The room tilted. He caught the desk and waited for the blackness at the edges of his vision to recede, the curse of not eating enough in weeks. Hedwig began shrieking.

Harry stumbled towards the window. Two cars stood beside the pavement. Mr and Mrs Granger were crossing the road with Hermione between them. Behind them walked Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy, dressed severely enough for a funeral, with Draconia several steps ahead.

Harry gripped the bars.

"Draco!"

All six looked upwards.

Draconia stopped.

Hermione's face changed.

For one second, nobody moved. Then both girls ran for the gate. Vernon opened the door before they reached it. He had been watching from the sitting-room window and possessed enough experience with unusual visitors to recognise a threat before it knocked.

His eyes moved past Hermione and Draconia towards their parents.

The Grangers looked respectable. That made them dangerous in a way Vernon understood. Lucius and Narcissa looked wealthy. That made them important.

Vernon attempted to close the door.

Mr Granger put one hand against it.

"We're here for Harry."

"No one by that name lives here."

From above came Harry's voice.

"That seems ambitious while I'm visible."

Hermione looked upwards.

Harry stood behind a thin sheet of glass and iron bars; one hand pressed against the window. His face looked too pale against the room's darkness, and the shirt hanging from his shoulders was several sizes too large.

Mrs Granger stopped beside the gate.

"Oh my God."

Draconia stared up at him. Her hand found the gate latch and fumbled against it.

"Those are bars."

Vernon pushed harder against the door. "This is private property."

Lucius stepped forward.

"Then you should have been more careful about what could be seen from the street."

Vernon's attention caught upon the silver serpent crowning Lucius's cane.

"I don't know what that boy has told you, but he's dangerous. We've had to protect ourselves."

"He has not told us anything," Hermione said. "That is the problem."

Narcissa looked towards Harry's window. "How long has he been confined?"

"That is none of your business."

"I am making it mine."

Her voice was quiet. Vernon took one involuntary step backward.

Mr Granger entered first. "Girls, stay by the door."

"No," Hermione and Draconia said together.

"Stay here until we've checked," Mrs Granger replied.

"Harry is upstairs," Hermione said.

"And we will reach him."

Narcissa crossed the threshold.

Something in the house's protection stirred around her, old magic recognizing another mother without welcoming her. She felt it gather beneath the wallpaper and floorboards, immense but curiously narrow in purpose. It guarded Harry from dangers outside the bloodline.

It had never been designed to protect him from the people sharing it. Her expression hardened.

"Show me the room."

Vernon blocked the staircase. Lucius removed the wand from his cane. Vernon looked at it for a short second before moving. Harry's bedroom door was bolted from the outside. The handle had been removed, leaving four uncovered holes in the wood. A cat-flap had been fitted near the floor.

Mrs Granger reached the landing behind Narcissa. For several seconds, both women stared.

"Is there a fire risk?" Vernon demanded from below. "Because if you're inspecting the property, I can assure you—"

Mr Granger turned on him. "Stop talking."

Narcissa drew her wand.

"I informed the Ministry that adult magic might be used at this address," she said, though no one had asked. "There will be no warning."

The bolt tore away from the frame. The door flew open with a sudden force that nearly ripped it from the hinges. Heat rolled into the landing.

Harry stood beside the desk with one hand gripping its edge. A week's worth of sparse meals showed in the sharpened line of his face and the hollows beneath his eyes. A bruise faded yellow near his collar. Another marked his wrist.

Behind him, Hedwig battered herself against a cage wrapped in chain.

Narcissa's first instinct was to enter. She stopped herself. Harry had drawn backwards at the sight of her raised wand.

The movement was slight, nearly hidden beneath his attempt to stand straighter. Narcissa saw it nonetheless. She lowered the wand.

"You are not in trouble," she said.

Harry blinked.

"I didn't use magic on the masons, I swear."

"I know."

"Dobby—"

"We know about Dobby. May I come inside?"

The question confused him more than the magic had.

"It's not my house."

"I asked whether I may enter your room."

Harry looked around, as though the answer might belong to someone else.

"Yeah. Of course."

Narcissa crossed the threshold.

Mrs Granger followed and immediately opened the window as far as the bars permitted. Her gaze moved over the plastic cup, the dried soup on the carpet and the bread wrapped beneath a cloth.

"How long?" she asked.

"Since dinner."

"How many days?"

Harry had to think.

"Nearly ten. I think."

Mrs Granger's hand tightened upon the windowsill. "They locked you in here for ten days?"

"The door was already locked before that."

Narcissa turned. Harry misunderstood her expression.

"It had a handle then."

The clarification made it worse. Footsteps sounded on the stairs.

Hermione and Draconia appeared despite every instruction to remain below. Mr Granger followed, evidently having decided that preventing them required more force than he was willing to use.

Harry looked towards them. Relief reached his face first. Then embarrassment replaced it, as though being discovered in the room were something for which he owed an apology.

Hermione crossed the distance and wrapped both arms around him. Harry stumbled. Draconia caught them both.

For several seconds, the three remained tangled together, Harry holding Hermione with one arm and gripping Draconia's sleeve with the other.

"You came for me," he whispered.

Hermione pulled back.

"Of course we came."

"I knew you would."

His voice broke upon the last word. Draconia put both arms around him before he could pretend it had not.

Harry closed his eyes.

"You took your time though," he murmured against her shoulder.

"We couldn't find you," she said. Her voice shook. "We tried everything, Harry. Don't—don't make a joke of it yet."

"I tried the door."

"There is no handle."

"That's why it didn't work."

Nobody laughed.

Harry looked between them. "I thought that might help."

Hermione touched the bruise near his collar. Harry flinched before stilling himself.

"Did your uncle do this?"

"That one's not so bad now."

"How often?"

"It wasn't—" Harry stopped. Every person in the room was watching him. "Not often anymore, I swear."

Draconia's fingers closed around her wand.

Harry saw.

"Don't."

"Why?"

"Because you'll get expelled."

The answer was immediate and entirely sincere. Something in Draconia's face fractured. She lowered the wand because Harry had asked, not because Vernon deserved the restraint.

Mrs Granger crouched beside the bread.

"When did you last eat?"

"Yesterday morning."

"What did you have?"

"Soup."

"And before that?"

"Uh, the day before."

"What time?"

Harry frowned, searching his memory. "Morning, I think."

Hermione went very still.

"You have been eating once a day?"

"Sometimes twice."

"Sometimes?"

"I wasn't doing anything," Harry said. "I didn't need as much."

Harry looked from face to face. No one answered him.

"I saved some," he added, pointing to the bread. "I wasn't going to run out."

Mrs Granger closed her eyes.

Narcissa crossed to Hedwig's cage because if she remained facing Harry, her composure would not survive. One movement of her wand opened the padlock. The chains fell away.

Hedwig exploded into the room. She circled once, struck Vernon's framed photograph from the wall and landed heavily upon Harry's shoulder. Harry swayed beneath her weight.

Draconia steadied him.

Narcissa conjured a deep bowl of clean water. Hedwig abandoned Harry so quickly that her talons scratched his shoulder through the shirt.

"Sorry," Harry told her.

The owl submerged half her face in the bowl.

Hermione stared at him. "Why are you apologizing to her?"

"I let her get locked up."

"You did not lock her cage."

"I couldn't get it open."

"That is not the same thing."

Harry looked away. Narcissa recognized the expression. He did not believe Hermione, but he understood that agreement would end the conversation sooner.

She had seen it upon Dobby's face. Her grip slipped on her wand. She tightened it, looking at the boy who was still waiting for someone to tell him what he had done wrong. She would have to think about Dobby later. Harry was here now.

"Harry," Narcissa said.

He looked at her.

"Nothing done in this room was your fault."

He gave a small, automatic nod. She could tell the words had not reached him. Lucius appeared in the doorway.

His gaze swept across the room and stopped upon Harry. For a moment, he looked not at the famous scar or the political figure Nott feared, but at a boy the same age as his daughter standing beneath an owl because nobody had taught him that care did not need to be repaid with apology. Then Harry took an unconscious step away from the door. Lucius realized he was blocking the exit.

He moved aside immediately.

"We are leaving," he said.

Harry glanced towards the landing. "Will Uncle Vernon let me?"

Narcissa turned away, pressing her fingers to her mouth. Lucius kept his eyes on Harry.

"Your uncle's permission," he said, each word unnaturally calm, "has ceased to interest me."

Harry looked uncertain whether that was allowed. Draconia touched his hand.

"You're coming with us."

"Oh...Really?"

Hermione tightened her hold upon his sleeve. "You are not getting rid of either of us."

A faint smile appeared.

"Good."

Harry's school trunk, broom and wand had been locked inside the cupboard beneath the stairs.

Lucius opened it with a flick of his wand while Vernon protested from the sitting room. The door swung back to reveal the trunk, several cleaning supplies and a narrow, stained mattress pushed beneath a shelf.

Narcissa stopped. Mrs Granger reached the bottom of the stairs behind her and followed her gaze.

"What is that?"

Harry came down between Hermione and Draconia. He saw the open cupboard.

"Oh. That's mine."

Every adult in the hallway turned towards him. Harry mistook their attention for confusion.

"My old bed," he explained.

Draconia looked inside.

There was barely room for the mattress. A few scratched lines remained upon the wall where Harry had once marked his height. A plastic soldier lay near the skirting board, abandoned by Dudley years earlier. The bolt was still fitted to the outside of the door.

"You slept in there?" Hermione asked.

"Until Hogwarts wrote."

"As a punishment?"

"No. It was just my room."

The distinction silenced the house. Harry glanced at the cupboard again, trying to understand what they were seeing that he did not. It looked smaller than he remembered. He had been smaller too.

"I fit better then," he said.

Mrs Granger covered her mouth. Mr Granger turned away and pressed one hand against the wall. His shoulders rose once beneath his jacket.

Hermione made a sound Harry had never heard from her before—not quite a sob, because she stopped it before it became one. Draconia stepped towards the cupboard.

Her face had emptied of everything except disbelief.

"For ten years?"

Harry shifted uncomfortably. "Not all the time. I had to use the bathroom."

Nobody moved.

"I was trying to be funny," he said.

"It wasn't funny," Hermione whispered.

"Oh."

Harry looked down.

"I know."

Narcissa entered the cupboard and crouched beside the mattress. Her pale dress touched the dusty floor. She placed one hand upon the thin bedding, then looked towards the height marks scratched into the wall.

Draconia had possessed a nursery larger than this space. When nightmares woke her, Narcissa had crossed an entire wing of the Manor to sit beside her until she slept again. She had worried about childhood fevers, unsuitable tutors and the possibility that Draconia might be lonely at Hogwarts.

Harry's aunt had closed this door.

Narcissa stood.

"Where is his aunt?"

Aunt Petunia waited inside the kitchen, rigid beside the table. Dudley hovered behind her with a sandwich in his hand. He had been eating when the visitors arrived and had not thought to put it down.

Narcissa looked at the sandwich, then at Harry.

Harry noticed and immediately said, "He gets hungry more than I do."

Dudley stopped chewing. Draconia stared at Harry as if he had spoken in another language.

"How would you know?"

"He's bigger."

"That is not how hunger works," Hermione said.

"It's how portions work here."

Lucius closed his eyes. Only for a moment. When he opened them, he entered the kitchen.

Vernon followed from the sitting room. "You've broken into a locked cupboard, damaged my door and threatened me inside my own home. I'll have the police here."

"Please call them," Mr Granger said.

Vernon faltered.

Mr Granger stepped between him and Harry.

"I would like to hear you explain the barred window, the locks and the starvation to someone carrying a warrant."

"He wasn't starved!"

"He has eaten once a day for a week."

"We fed him this morning."

Lucius stared at Vernon. "You consider that a defense."

"He is not normal. Things happen around him. Dangerous things. We had our own son to protect."

Dudley continued holding the sandwich. Lucius's gaze passed over him.

"Your son appears to have survived the sacrifice."

Petunia found her voice. "You don't know what Harry was like. Strange things happened before we knew what he was. He brought danger into this house from the moment he arrived."

"He was fifteen months old," Narcissa said.

Petunia flinched.

"You knew my sister?"

"No. I know what she did."

The colour drained from Petunia's face.

"She died to keep her child alive," Narcissa continued. "You answered that sacrifice by teaching him that survival was a debt he could never repay."

"That isn't fair."

"No," Narcissa said. "It is not."

Harry stood near the stairs, listening with an expression of growing distress. Their anger frightened him.

Not because it was directed at him, but because all trouble inside Number Four eventually became his responsibility. The visitors would leave. The Dursleys would remain. Punishment merely waited until there were no witnesses.

Draconia felt his hand tighten around hers. She looked at him.

"Can um...can we go?" he whispered.

The request erased every speech she had prepared. She had wanted Vernon to understand what he had done. Looking at Harry, she realized Vernon's understanding did not matter nearly as much as getting Harry beyond his reach.

"Yes."

Harry exhaled. Lucius saw the exchange. He removed a folded document from inside his coat and placed it upon the kitchen table.

Vernon eyed it. "What's that?"

"Permission for Harry to reside elsewhere for the remainder of the summer, including authority for travel and medical treatment."

"We're not signing your abnormal paperwork."

"Then Mr Granger will contact the local authorities while my wife informs the Ministry that a protected magical child has been illegally confined and abused."

"The Ministry sent us the warning!"

"The Ministry warned Harry against performing magic. It did not authorize you to imprison him."

"He belongs here."

Harry flinched. Lucius's wand was in his hand before anyone saw him draw it. The lights throughout the kitchen dimmed.

"He belongs to no one. He, no matter how much you may want him to be, is not a slave"

The quiet fury in Lucius's voice left no room for interruption.

Nott's accusation returned to him: Your daughter's friendships have made people curious.

Lucius had thought of Harry as an alliance, a danger, a symbol around which political power might gather. Vernon Dursley had treated him as an unwanted possession. Both reduced a child to the purpose he served in someone else's life.

Lucius disliked the resemblance.

"My daughter calls him her best friend," he said. "Miss Granger crossed two worlds to find him. Your only claim rests upon blood you have spent eleven years dishonouring."

Petunia stared at him. Lucius pushed the document towards Vernon.

"Sign."

Vernon looked at Mr Granger, who had already lifted the telephone receiver. Then he looked at the barred window visible through the kitchen doorway and the cupboard standing open beneath the stairs.

Fear of scandal accomplished what shame could not. He took the pen. Petunia caught his wrist.

"Will he have to come back?"

Harry looked at her. The question did not sound hopeful or frightened. It sounded like something Petunia had tried not to ask herself for years. Narcissa felt the blood protection stir around them.

"That will be decided before next summer," she said. "He will not return during this one."

Petunia released Vernon. He signed. The ink flashed once, binding the consent.

Harry retrieved his wand from the top of his trunk. His fingers closed around the familiar holly, and some part of him returned to its proper place. Hermione watched his shoulders straighten.

Draconia watched him immediately conceal the change, as if even relief were something the Dursleys might punish if he displayed too much of it.

Narcissa touched his arm.

"Harry."

He looked at her.

"You are leaving."

"I, um, I know."

"Now."

"And I'm not in trouble?"

Six faces turned towards him. Harry's expression became wary.

"For the Ministry warning," he explained. "And the dinner. Uncle Vernon said—"

"You did not perform the magic," Hermione said.

"I know, but it happened because Dobby came to see me."

"That does not make it your fault."

Harry glanced at Narcissa. She remembered saying the same thing upstairs and watching the words fail to reach him.

This time, she crouched so their eyes were level.

"You haven't done anything wrong," she said. "You don't have to earn your meals, Harry. Or a bed. And your friends came because they wanted you here. Do you understand?"

Harry hesitated. He understood every word. Believing them was different.

"I think so."

Narcissa did not force him to promise more.

"That will do for now."

Lucius lifted Harry's trunk with his wand. Mr Granger carried Hedwig's cage, though the owl chose to remain upon Harry's shoulder. Mrs Granger kept one hand near Harry's back without touching him.

At the front door, Harry stopped. The polished hallway stretched behind him. The open cupboard remained visible beneath the stairs. Vernon stood in the kitchen with the signed document, while Petunia stared through him towards something years away.

Dudley took another bite of his sandwich.

Harry waited for the familiar pull—the belief that leaving would cause consequences he ought to remain and manage.

Draconia moved to one side of him. Hermione took the other. Neither told him to hurry. Harry stepped outside.

The afternoon air felt cooler than the bedroom despite the summer heat. For several seconds, the open sky was too large, the pavement too bright. He had spent seven days measuring the world in eleven diagonal steps.

Hermione took Hedwig when Harry's balance faltered. Draconia caught his hand.

Nobody commented.

Behind them, Lucius looked back at the barred window. Something had changed in his expression since entering the house. The concern with which he arrived had been political, shaped by Nott's threat, Dobby's disobedience and the danger gathering around his daughter.

What he carried away was simpler. Anger.

Nott had threatened to use Harry Potter because he was famous. The Dursleys had harmed him because they believed he was powerless. Both had mistaken the boy for something they were entitled to use.

Lucius knew too much about that kind of mistake. The door to Number Four closed.

Harry walked between Hermione and Draconia towards the waiting cars.

For the first time in his life, he left Privet Drive because someone had noticed he should not be there.
