16

Sirius slept through the first sunrise of his freedom with Harry's hand still caught loosely in his. Freedom was not yet the official word for it. The Ministry preferred temporary suspension of detention, a phrase long enough to avoid admitting that twelve years in Azkaban had been a mistake. Sirius remained confined to Hogwarts. His wand had not been returned. An Auror stood beyond the hospital doors, and a thin blue line surrounded his bed, ready to sound an alarm if he attempted to leave. None of that changed the fact that the Dementors could no longer take him.

For the first time since he was twenty-one, Sirius slept without hearing them beyond a door. Harry remained awake beside him. Morning gathered slowly in the hospital wing. The highest windows brightened from black to blue, revealing dust suspended above empty beds. Beyond the screens at the far end of the ward, Madam Pomfrey moved around Lupin. Glass clinked. Water ran into a basin. Once, Harry heard a muffled groan and the Healer ordering her patient not to move. Hermione slept with one arm outside the blankets.

Her fingers curled near the edge of Harry's mattress, no longer reaching for him but close enough to prove she had. The bandage around her hand was clean. The curse wound across her side had closed, though Madam Pomfrey promised it would ache for several days and had threatened to tie Hermione down if she attempted to attend Ancient Runes before Monday. Draconia occupied the chair between their beds.

She had folded herself sideways during the night, one shoulder against Hermione's mattress and her cheek resting on the back of the chair. Lucius's cloak covered her from neck to ankle. Dried mud remained beneath her fingernails. Without the careful arrangement of her hair or the guarded composure she wore through most waking hours, she looked thirteen. Harry wondered how often people forgot that. Adults called them brave when they survived situations adults should have prevented. Professors discussed their instincts, resourcefulness and unusual magical ability. Newspapers printed Harry's name when danger required a recognisable face. None of those words made them older.

Harry looked down at Sirius. He did not look old enough either. Azkaban had aged his body without giving him any of the years it took. Beneath exhaustion and scars remained the uneven edges of a young man who had entered prison before learning how to live without his friends. Harry had spent most of the night wondering what one did with a godfather. The role sounded important. It implied birthdays remembered, Christmases shared and an adult who knew when to interfere. It did not explain how to begin after twelve missing years and one extremely poor introduction involving a knife.

Sirius stirred. His fingers tightened around Harry's before his eyes opened. They found the ceiling first, then the blue alarm line, the hospital beds and Harry.

"Still here," Sirius murmured.

"So are you."

"Bit disappointing for the Ministry."

"They'll recover."

Sirius turned his head. He took in Hermione and Draconia, lingering upon the bloodstained sleeve protruding from beneath Lucius's cloak.

"They came back?"

"They said they would."

"Good."

His gaze returned to Harry.

"Did you sleep?"

"Yes."

Harry answered too quickly. Sirius's brows rose.

"What?"

"You're terrible at lying."

"You've known me for less than a day."

"I knew James."

"I'm not James."

"No," Sirius said. "James added details."

Harry looked towards the windows.

"I wasn't tired."

"That's another bad one."

"Would you prefer I improve?"

"I'd prefer you didn't need to lie."

The response removed the joke before Harry could use it. Sirius shifted. Pain crossed his face. Harry reached towards the water, but Sirius caught the glass first with his uninjured hand. He drank, spilling a little down his chin, and pretended not to notice the tremor in his fingers. Harry pretended too. They were going to require practice.

"Peter?" Sirius asked.

"Moody took him somewhere secure."

"Where?"

"They wouldn't tell us."

"Good."

Harry frowned.

"He's spent years hiding as a pet. If I know where he is, anyone can know."

Sirius placed the glass upon the cabinet.

"And Remus?"

"Alive. Human again."

"Has he woken?"

"I don't know."

Sirius's gaze moved towards the screens. A dozen thoughts crossed his face too quickly for Harry to separate them. Guilt remained longest.

"He thought you were guilty," Harry said.

"So did everyone."

"You thought he was the spy."

"Yes."

"Are you angry with him?"

Sirius considered.

"I've had twelve years to be angry."

"That doesn't answer it."

"No."

Harry waited. Sirius rubbed his thumb over the blanket.

"I'm angry that he doubted me. I'm angrier that I gave him reasons. I spent months disappearing on Order missions and refused to explain where I went. Every time he asked, I made a joke because I thought suspicion was insulting."

"Where were you going?"

"Checking on Regulus."

Harry had heard the name from Draconia.

"Your brother."

Sirius's expression tightened.

"He joined Voldemort. Then he started behaving strangely. Frightened. I thought he wanted to leave, but he wouldn't speak to me."

"What happened to him?"

"My family said he died."

"How?"

"They didn't say."

The lack of an answer remained alive between the words. Sirius looked towards Lupin's screens again.

"Remus knew I suspected him. I knew he knew. Neither of us would say it. Perhaps we were afraid of finding out we'd been wrong."

"Everyone keeps doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Choosing not to know things because the answer might hurt."

Sirius looked at him for a long time.

"Do you?"

Harry's first instinct was to say no. The lie would have been poor.

"Sometimes."

"What are you choosing not to know now?"

The question entered more deeply than Harry expected. He knew what waited beneath it. Whether Sirius wanted him because he was Harry or because he was the last piece of James. Whether the hearing would return freedom or discover another reason to take it away. Whether living through the night had changed anything when Harry still had to return to Privet Drive at the end of term. He looked towards Draconia and Hermione.

"Too many things."

"That sounds more honest."

Madam Pomfrey emerged from behind the screens. She carried a basin filled with water darkened by blood and potion. Her sleeves had been rolled beyond her elbows, and deep lines marked the skin around her mouth.

"Mr Potter, why are you awake?"

"I'm not."

She placed the basin upon a trolley.

"Mr Black, why are you encouraging him?"

"I was asleep."

"You were quieter then."

She examined Sirius's eyes, checked the bones in his injured arm and pressed two fingers against his ribs. Sirius attempted not to react. His entire body stiffened.

"You have three cracked ribs, one healed fracture that has broken again, a damaged shoulder and the nutritional condition of a man who has considered food an optional rumour."

"I've been busy."

"You will remain in this bed."

"For how long?"

"Until I cease wanting to curse you unconscious."

"That seems subjective."

"It is."

She turned towards Harry.

"You will sleep."

"I have questions."

"You can ask them after breakfast."

A soft sound came from the screens. Sirius sat upright. Madam Pomfrey pushed him down with one hand.

"Remus?"

"He is awake."

"I need to see him."

"You need bones."

"Poppy."

"Do not 'Poppy' me. I treated both of you as students, and you were considerably less trouble when one of you became a wolf every month."

Harry stared.

"How is that possible?"

"We had more staff."

The screen shifted. Lupin stepped through. He wore hospital pyjamas and a grey dressing gown several sizes too large. Bandages covered one forearm and disappeared beneath his collar. New scratches crossed his face, and exhaustion had hollowed the skin around his eyes. Madam Pomfrey turned.

"What did I say?"

"That I should not attempt to stand alone."

"And yet?"

"I used the bedframe."

"That is not the improvement you believe it to be."

Lupin barely heard her. He saw Sirius. Neither man moved. Harry felt the years between them enter the ward: four boys beneath a school roof, two men on opposite sides of a war, one prisoner and one friend who had not asked enough questions. Sirius swung his legs from the bed.

Madam Pomfrey reached for her wand. Harry caught her sleeve. She looked down at him, outraged.

"Give them a minute."

"They are both injured."

"They've been injured for twelve years."

Her expression changed. She lowered her wand. Sirius stood. His knees nearly failed. Lupin caught him, which caused both of them to stagger into the cabinet. The water glass fell. Harry caught it before it struck the floor. Then Sirius and Lupin held one another.

There was nothing graceful in it. Sirius's good hand twisted into the back of Lupin's dressing gown. Lupin buried his face against Sirius's shoulder. Both shook with the effort of remaining upright and the failure of doing anything else.

"I thought you did it," Lupin said.

His voice broke against Sirius's robes.

"I know."

"I let them take you."

"You couldn't have stopped them."

"I didn't try."

Sirius closed his eyes.

"I thought it was you."

Lupin drew back. Sirius looked at him.

"I thought you were passing information. I told James not to trust you."

"I know."

"I didn't tell you about the switch."

"I know."

"I should have."

"So should I."

Years of blame passed between them and found nowhere simple to settle. Sirius's hand tightened upon Lupin's shoulder.

"I went after Peter."

"You thought he'd killed all of them."

"I wanted him dead more than I wanted Harry safe."

Harry lowered his gaze. Lupin looked at him.

"You were twenty-one."

"So were James and Lily."

"They made mistakes too."

"Not that one."

"No."

Lupin did not soften the truth. Sirius seemed grateful for it.

"I'm sorry," Lupin said. "It is too late and too small, but I am."

Sirius looked at the scars upon his face.

"I'm angry with you."

"You should be."

"I don't know what to do with that yet."

"You don't need to know today."

"Very sensible."

"One of us has to be."

Sirius laughed. The sound became a groan as his ribs protested. Lupin attempted to support him and nearly collapsed in turn. Madam Pomfrey pointed both hands towards their beds.

"Enough."

Neither argued. They separated reluctantly. Harry helped Sirius sit. Madam Pomfrey guided Lupin back towards the screens, muttering about reckless men, transformed bones and patients who confused emotional revelations with medical clearance. Lupin stopped beside Harry. His gaze settled upon him.

"The stag."

Harry had been waiting for this.

"It's mine."

"Yes," Lupin said.

No surprise entered his face. Harry looked up.

"I thought you might say it was my father's."

"James's Patronus resembled the same animal. That does not make yours an inheritance."

"You saw it?"

"Not clearly. I remember light. Antlers."

"You nearly ate Sirius."

"So I have been informed."

"He says that was normal."

Lupin's eyes closed briefly. "Sirius possessed an unusual understanding of normal."

"He protected me."

Lupin looked towards his friend.

"I know."

"He protected you too."

Sirius frowned. "When?"

"He didn't let the Patronus hurt you."

"It wouldn't have."

"You didn't know that."

Sirius looked away. Lupin understood.

"Thank you," he said.

Sirius examined a crack in the wall. "You scratched my face."

"I apologise."

"And broke my arm."

"Also regrettable."

"You ruined an excellent coat."

Draconia spoke without opening her eyes.

"It was not an excellent coat."

Everyone turned. She remained curled in the chair beneath Lucius's cloak, but one grey eye had opened.

Sirius looked offended. "You were asleep."

"Your reunion lacked restraint."

Hermione stirred.

"Is everyone alive?"

"Yes," Harry said.

"Peter?"

"Still caught."

"Good."

Her eyes closed again. Draconia shifted towards the edge of Hermione's mattress, making space without acknowledging she had done so. Hermione's hand found the cloak and drew part of it across Draconia's shoulder. Lupin watched them. Then he looked at Harry.

"You did not go alone."

"No."

"A meaningful improvement."

"We still disobeyed every professor available."

"Yes."

"Does that reduce the improvement?"

"Considerably."

Sirius leaned back against his pillows.

"I think it shows initiative."

Lupin gave him a tired look. "You are not helping."

"I've only just started."

Despite herself, Madam Pomfrey smiled. It disappeared the instant she realised.

"Sleep," she ordered them all.

This time, the hospital wing listened. Narcissa Malfoy arrived at eight carrying enough food for a minor siege. Two house-elves followed her through the hospital doors with baskets balanced above their heads. Fresh bread, fruit, pastries, soup and several covered dishes appeared upon every available table. Madam Pomfrey attempted to object. Narcissa gave her a small blue jar labelled St Mungo's Restorative Salve for Magical Lacerations.

The objection ended. Mrs and Mr Granger arrived ten minutes later. Hermione's mother crossed the ward before the doors had fully opened. She hugged Hermione, checked each bandage and listened with tightening lips while Madam Pomfrey described the curse that had nearly struck her daughter. Mr Granger stood at the foot of the bed. He said nothing until Hermione finished explaining that she was safe.

Then he turned towards Lucius.

"Where is the man who did it?"

"In Auror custody."

"Secure custody?"

"Alastor Moody considers six wards, three guards and an anti-transformation chamber barely adequate."

Mr Granger looked at Draconia.

"He went after both of you?"

Draconia nodded.

"And Harry?"

"Repeatedly."

Mr Granger removed his glasses and cleaned them.

"Good," he said.

Hermione frowned. "Good?"

"That he's secure."

Harry suspected Mr Granger had intended another word. Narcissa reached Draconia last. Her restraint lasted until she touched the newly healed cut at her daughter's temple. Then she pulled Draconia against her. Draconia stiffened from surprise.

"Mother."

"I know."

"People are watching."

"They may develop manners."

Draconia's arms slowly closed around her. Harry looked away before the moment became one Draconia would resent him for witnessing. Narcissa released her and turned towards Hermione.

"You saved her."

Hermione shook her head. "She saved me first."

"Then I shall thank you in the order events occurred."

She hugged Hermione too. Mrs Granger watched. A year earlier, Hermione's parents would have been told only that their daughter suffered a magical accident. Lucius would have regarded their presence at Hogwarts as an inconvenience. Narcissa might have comforted Draconia without looking directly at the Muggle-born child beside her. Now the two mothers stood on opposite sides of Hermione's bed, comparing instructions from Madam Pomfrey and discovering that both their daughters lied about pain with exactly the same expression. Lucius remained near the window.

He had changed into clean robes, though one cuff bore dried blood he had failed to notice. When Narcissa finally turned towards him, he straightened.

"You allowed them to pursue an armed traitor."

"I encountered them after the pursuit began."

"You did not prevent it beforehand."

"I was in London."

Narcissa held his gaze. Lucius seemed to reconsider the defence.

"I should have anticipated the possibility."

"Yes."

Harry smiled into his tea. Sirius noticed.

"You enjoy watching Malfoy suffer?"

"A bit."

"A healthy instinct."

Lucius heard. His gaze moved towards Sirius's bed. The two men looked at one another across the ward.

"Black," Lucius said.

"Malfoy."

"You appear terrible."

"You always did know how to welcome family."

"Your incarceration has not improved your manners."

"Your freedom hasn't improved yours."

Harry looked between them.

"Good. You've met."

Narcissa poured tea.

"They have known one another since childhood."

"Sirius once transfigured Lucius's dress robes into curtains during my engagement dinner," she added.

"They improved the room," Sirius said.

Lucius's expression chilled. "He was nineteen."

"You were wearing lace."

"It was formal embroidery."

"It had sleeves large enough to shelter livestock."

Harry laughed. Lucius looked betrayed.

"You encouraged him."

"I wasn't alive."

"Your influence is retroactive."

Narcissa carried a cup towards Sirius. He stopped smiling.

"Hello, Cissy."

The childhood name changed her face. Narcissa placed the tea down. Then she slapped him. The sound cracked across the ward. Madam Pomfrey spun from her office.

Sirius touched his cheek.

"I may have deserved that."

"That," Narcissa said, "was for entering a school with a knife and frightening my daughter."

"Fair."

She hugged him. Sirius went completely still. Narcissa's arms tightened around his shoulders.

"This is for being alive."

His eyes closed. Harry watched one hand rise uncertainly to the back of her robes. They were cousins. Family, though Sirius had run from the one bearing his name and Narcissa had remained close to it. The war had placed them on opposite sides without ever fully severing the old connection. Lucius looked away first. When Narcissa released Sirius, she wiped beneath one eye and immediately began criticising the hospital's tea.

No one mentioned it.

Dumbledore asked to speak with Harry and Sirius after lunch. The headmaster's office smelled of smoke, old books and the restorative soup Narcissa had insisted Sirius carry with him. His portraits pretended to sleep. Fawkes sat upon his golden perch, his fire-bright plumage a warm contrast to the silver phoenix Harry remembered crossing the grounds. Sirius occupied the chair beside Harry. Madam Pomfrey had allowed him to leave the ward for one hour under four conditions, two of which involved returning immediately if he felt dizzy. He had agreed with the sincerity of a man already planning to ignore them.

A blue Ministry bracelet circled one wrist. He kept turning it over. Dumbledore watched him do so.

"The restriction will remain until the hearing."

"I know."

"It is designed to prevent Apparition beyond the grounds."

"I recognised it."

"Then you will also recognise that attempting to remove it—"

"Sets my hair on fire. Yes."

Harry looked at the bracelet.

"You tried?"

"Gently."

"How do you gently remove a magical restraint?"

"Experience."

Dumbledore's eyes glimmered. Harry did not smile. He had not forgotten the conversation in the forest. Dumbledore noticed that too.

"You have questions," the headmaster said.

"A lot."

"Then we should not delay."

Harry looked towards Sirius.

"Who is my guardian?"

Sirius became still. Dumbledore folded his hands upon the desk.

"James and Lily's will named Sirius."

"Then why did I go to the Dursleys?"

"The will addressed legal guardianship. It could not account for the protection created by Lily's sacrifice."

Harry had heard fragments of the explanation before. None had ever included choice.

"What protection?"

"When your mother died to save you, her sacrifice placed an ancient shield upon you. Voldemort could not touch you without suffering harm. By sending you to live with Lily's blood relative, I extended part of that protection around her dwelling."

"Privet Drive."

"Yes."

Sirius's chair scraped backwards.

"You sent him there for a ward?"

Dumbledore looked at him. "Yes."

"To Petunia?"

"Yes."

"You met her."

"I did."

"Then you knew."

"I believed she would protect Lily's son despite their difficult relationship."

"Difficult?" Sirius repeated. "She hated Lily because Lily had magic."

"I underestimated that resentment."

"You didn't check?"

Dumbledore's silence answered. Sirius stood. The Ministry bracelet flashed warning blue.

"You left him there and didn't check."

"Arabella Figg observed Harry from nearby."

"Mrs Figg thought making me enjoy visits would stop the Dursleys sending me back," Harry said. "She knew they didn't like me. She didn't know about the cupboard."

Sirius turned.

"The what?"

Harry wished immediately that he had chosen another example. Dumbledore's face closed with regret. Sirius looked between them.

"What cupboard?"

"It doesn't matter now."

"Harry."

He had learned the tone quickly. Harry examined Fawkes's perch.

"I slept under the stairs when I was little."

"How little?"

"I don't know. Until my first Hogwarts letter."

"Where did you sleep before that?"

"The cupboard."

Sirius did not move. Dumbledore spoke carefully.

"I learned the full extent only after Harry's first year."

"And sent him back."

"The wards require him to return."

"You sent him back."

"Yes."

The word carried no defence. Sirius crossed the room. Harry stood before he reached the desk.

"Sirius."

"He knew."

"I know."

"He left you there."

"I know."

"You had parents."

"No," Harry said.

Sirius stopped. Harry hated the hurt the words caused, but he could not take them back.

"I had people who were dead or in prison. That isn't the same."

Sirius's anger collapsed inward. Harry continued before guilt could silence him.

"I want to live with you."

The room changed. Sirius stared at him.

"You don't know me."

"I know."

"I don't have a house."

"You own one."

"I have no intention of taking you to Grimmauld Place."

"Good. Draco says the drawing room has severed heads."

"House-elf heads," Sirius said.

Harry looked at him.

"That was meant to improve it."

"It didn't."

Sirius sat slowly.

"You want to live with me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

Harry had expected enthusiasm, perhaps an immediate plan involving a house, a motorbike and no adult judgement whatsoever. He had not expected Sirius to ask why.

"Because my parents chose you."

Sirius's face tightened.

"And because you listened when I said the stag was mine. You protected Lupin when you were angry with him. You didn't lie about going after Peter."

"That's not enough to build a home on."

"No."

Harry looked at the bracelet around Sirius's wrist.

"But it's enough to start."

Sirius turned away. He pressed his hand to his mouth until he could speak.

"All right."

The words came roughly.

"We start."

Dumbledore waited. Sirius faced him.

"How much time must Harry spend with Petunia?"

"The protection does not depend upon you loving the house or believing it is home," Dumbledore said. "It depends upon Petunia continuing to give Lily's son shelter and upon you physically returning to live beneath her roof. The bond would otherwise endure until you come of age. It could end sooner only if the blood magic itself were fundamentally altered."

Harry thought of every summer spent trying to make himself small enough that number four forgot he existed.

"How long must he remain there?" Sirius asked.

"I have studied the ward's renewal since Harry's first year. Twenty-one consecutive days and nights are sufficient to anchor it through the next school year. Afterward, he may spend the remainder of the summer elsewhere. Unless the nature of the protection changes, he will need to renew it again next year."

"You knew the whole summer wasn't necessary?" Harry asked.

Dumbledore's expression bore the weight of a decision examined too late.

"I knew a shorter stay might be sufficient. Because Voldemort survived, I chose the strongest possible interpretation rather than risk weakening the shield."

"For my body," Harry said.

"Yes."

The answer hurt because it was honest. Dumbledore had protected Harry Potter as a necessary figure in a future war. He had protected Lily's sacrifice, the scar and the life Voldemort might return to finish. He had failed to protect the child who lived inside all of them.

"Then I'm not going back," Harry said.

The refusal arrived with eleven summers behind it. For one sharp second, relief flashed across Sirius's face.

Then it disappeared.

"Yes, you are," Sirius said.

Harry turned on him. "You just heard what he did."

"I heard him admit he left you there without checking whether the protection cost more than it saved. That doesn't make the protection worthless."

"You want me to live with you."

"More than anything."

"Then—"

"I went after Peter because I wanted revenge more than I thought about keeping you safe. I am not making that choice twice simply because this time the thing I want is better."

Harry stared at him.

Sirius looked ill beneath his determination, as though every word argued against the part of him already planning to carry Harry out of the castle and never stop moving.

"Twenty-one days and nights," he said. "On the twenty-second morning, I collect you myself. Until then, you return under conditions."

Harry wanted to be angry. It would have been easier if Sirius had been reckless, if freedom meant an adult finally agreeing that Harry should never have to endure anything unpleasant again.

Instead, Sirius was choosing Harry's safety over being wanted by him.

Harry looked down at his hands.

"Three weeks," he said.

"Three weeks," Sirius agreed. "Not a day more."

He leaned forward.

"No locks on his door."

Dumbledore nodded.

"No bars on his window."

"Yes."

"His owl remains with him."

"Yes."

"He keeps his wand."

"Ministry law forbids underage magic except in immediate danger."

"That wasn't the condition."

Dumbledore considered. "His wand remains accessible."

"Food whenever he wants it."

Harry shifted. Sirius saw.

"And someone checks he eats."

"I eat."

"Poorly."

"You've spoken to Lucius."

"Narcissa."

"Worse."

Sirius continued.

"Daily contact."

"Letters can be intercepted," Harry said.

"Then not letters."

"I may have a solution," Dumbledore said.

"No vague headmaster solutions. Name it."

"A pair of enchanted mirrors once used by members of the Order. One was recovered from the Potters' belongings at Godric's Hollow. At my request, Alastor located the other among the property sealed after Sirius's arrest."

Sirius went quiet.

"Mine," he said.

"One belonged to you. James carried the other."

Harry looked between them. Dumbledore opened a drawer and removed two square objects wrapped in cloth. The first mirror was cracked along one corner but otherwise intact. The second had been restored with thin seams of gold running through the glass. Sirius picked one up.

"We used these during separate detentions."

"Separate?" Harry asked.

"McGonagall learned."

"Eventually," Dumbledore said.

Sirius handed Harry the repaired mirror.

"Say my name while looking into it. If I have the other, we can see and hear each other."

"What if you don't answer?"

"Then call Remus. Or Dumbledore. Or Hermione. Or Draconia. We can make more."

Dumbledore lifted one brow at the casual expansion of difficult magic. Sirius ignored him.

"Someone visits at least every second day," he continued. "Not watches from across a street. Enters the house. Speaks to Harry alone."

"Agreed," Dumbledore said.

"And if they touch him—"

"We determine a response within the law."

Sirius's smile held no warmth. "Certainly."

Harry looked into the mirror. For years, Privet Drive had remained powerful because it was complete. Once Vernon locked the door, the magical world ceased to exist. Letters could be taken. Hedwig could be caged. No one saw the bruises that faded before September. Now a narrow square of glass connected his room to someone who would answer. The change was small enough to hold in one hand. It felt enormous.

"There is another matter," Dumbledore said.

Sirius's attention remained upon Harry.

"Your legal position cannot be restored until the hearing. Even afterward, questions will be raised regarding the effects of Azkaban and your ability to provide a stable home."

"Of course they will."

"I will testify on your behalf."

"You testified against me by not asking for a trial."

Dumbledore lowered his head.

"Yes."

Sirius looked at Harry.

"Remus?"

"If he is willing."

"He will be."

"Narcissa and Lucius Malfoy have also offered."

Sirius stared.

"No."

"You need witnesses," Harry said.

"I would rather remain homeless."

"Narcissa is your cousin."

"Lucius is Lucius."

"He helped catch Peter."

"He spent the first war wearing a mask."

"He admitted he wasn't under Imperius."

Sirius's expression changed.

"To whom?"

"Moody."

"That must have gone well."

"Apparently no one died."

"A disappointing standard."

Harry leaned back.

"You don't have to like him."

"Excellent."

"But he is helping."

"For Draconia."

"At first."

Sirius studied him. Harry thought of Lucius standing between them and Fudge, refusing to hand over the cage. He had done more than Draconia had asked of him.

"This family is complicated," Sirius said.

"I've noticed."

"We may need rules about Malfoys."

"Draco writes the rules."

"Absolutely not."

"You can appeal."

"To whom?"

"Hermione."

"That's worse."

Harry smiled. For the first time, the future did not arrive as a blank stretch of summer he had to endure. It contained three weeks, a mirror, visits and an argument about where Sirius might live. It contained dates. That was enough to make it real.

The hearing took place six days later.

Cornelius Fudge attempted to hold it inside the Ministry. Dumbledore refused to move Sirius through a building connected to the Dementor transport network. Moody refused to reveal Peter's secure location until independent wards had been established. Lucius informed three members of the Wizengamot that the Ministry had concealed evidence in a case involving an heir to the House of Black. By noon, the hearing had been relocated to Hogwarts. The Great Hall changed for it.

The House tables vanished. A semicircle of high-backed chairs rose where the staff usually sat, each occupied by a robed member of the Wizengamot. Purple banners bearing the Ministry seal hung between the windows. A Pensieve rested upon a stone pedestal in the centre of the room beneath layers of protective magic. Students had been forbidden to attend. This ensured every corridor outside became crowded. Harry entered through the side door with Hermione and Draconia.

They wore school robes. Hermione had attempted to persuade him to dress more formally until Draconia pointed out that presenting Harry as a student rather than a political symbol worked in Sirius's favour.

"Let them see how young you are. They're meant to look after children," she had said.

Harry did not understand entirely. Lucius did. He had approved. Sirius waited near the centre of the hall. Madam Pomfrey had cut his hair.

The change startled Harry more than it should have. Without the matted strands surrounding his face, Sirius looked less like the photograph in the Daily Prophet and more like the boy in Lupin's frame after grief had aged him. He wore plain black robes borrowed from Dumbledore and the blue Ministry bracelet upon his wrist. Two Aurors stood behind him. Neither carried a hood. Sirius saw Harry. Tension left his shoulders.

Only slightly. Lupin sat in the first row of witnesses. His injuries had faded beneath potions, but he still moved carefully. A cane rested beside his chair. Narcissa occupied the seat beside him. Lucius stood behind them, speaking quietly with Moody and a tall Auror Harry recognised as Kingsley Shacklebolt. Professor Snape remained alone at the far end of the row. He had agreed to testify.

He had not agreed to sit near Sirius. Fudge called the hearing to order. Peter was brought in within the transparent enclosure. His hair had been washed, his wounds treated and plain grey robes placed upon him. None of it altered the way his body curved inward. Chains bound his hands. When he saw Harry, he began to cry. Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia.

Neither looked at Peter. The evidence took three hours. Moody described Peter's capture and submitted the spell-sequence analysis performed upon his recovered wand. McGonagall gave her account of the confession outside Hagrid's hut. Hermione presented the map's enchantments, its identification of Animagi and the sequence of Peter's movements. Draconia explained how Peter had forced a curse around the cage's restored containment circuit until the repaired joint ruptured. Harry described the Shrieking Shack. He kept his voice steady.

He told the Wizengamot that Sirius had possessed multiple opportunities to harm him and had not. He described Peter changing his explanations whenever one failed. He repeated the confession Peter made before Veritaserum and the details concerning the Potters' home. Several members attempted to ask Harry about his parents' deaths. Dumbledore stopped them.

"The witness did not observe those events."

"He carries a magical memory," one witch argued.

"He carries trauma," Dumbledore replied. "You will not use it as evidence."

Harry looked at him. Dumbledore did not look back. Peter's memories were examined by three independent specialists. Each confirmed they showed no sign of alteration. The Pensieve filled the Great Hall with ghosts. James and Lily stood within a memory of their own sitting room. Harry saw his father older than in Lupin's photograph, holding a sleeping baby against his chest. Sirius paced before the fire. Peter sat at the table. Lily listened from the sofa.

"We use Peter," memory-Sirius said.

James's face hardened. "No."

"Voldemort expects me."

"That is why we chose you."

"It is why choosing me is obvious."

Peter looked frightened. Even in his own memory, he had arranged himself to appear reluctant.

"I can't," he whispered.

James turned towards him. "You don't have to."

Peter looked at Sirius. Sirius knelt beside him.

"He'll come for me. Everyone will watch me. You stay hidden, and no one knows."

Lily studied Peter.

"You understand what we're asking?"

"Yes."

"If you say no, we find another way."

Peter hesitated. Then he looked towards the baby in James's arms.

"I'll do it."

The memory changed. A dark room replaced the Potters' home. Voldemort stood beside a narrow window. His face was not yet the ruined mask Harry knew, though little humanity remained within it. Peter knelt before him.

"The Potters are hiding in Godric's Hollow," he said. "The cottage beside the churchyard."

He gave the precise location. Voldemort smiled. The memory dissolved. Sirius made no sound. Harry could not look at him.

The final memory showed the Muggle street. Peter shouted his accusation. Sirius approached with his wand lowered, demanding to know why. Peter smiled through tears, cut his finger behind his back and cast the curse into the road. The explosion filled the Hall. When it cleared, memory-Sirius knelt within the crater. He looked at the severed finger.

Then he began to laugh. Harry understood the laughter now. It sounded like a mind breaking. The Pensieve darkened. No one moved for several seconds.

Fudge shuffled his papers. Bureaucracy returned because someone had to invite it. Snape testified last. He walked to the centre of the Hall without looking at Sirius.

"Did you believe Sirius Black betrayed Lily and James Potter?" a wizard asked.

"Yes."

"On what evidence?"

"The information distributed to members of the Order and later repeated by the Ministry."

"Did Black ever confess this crime to you?"

"No."

"Did Peter Pettigrew?"

"Yes."

The wizard glanced towards Peter.

"Do you consider Pettigrew's confession credible?"

Snape's eyes found the man inside the enclosure.

"I consider his continued breathing regrettable."

Fudge cleared his throat.

"That is not an evidentiary assessment."

"No," Snape said. "The evidence is considerably clearer."

A murmur travelled around the semicircle. Another witch leaned forward.

"Professor Snape, you have personal history with Mr Black."

"Yes."

"Hostile history."

"Yes."

"Do you believe he is a good man?"

Sirius looked towards him. Snape's face became unreadable.

"No."

The single word landed heavily. Harry gripped his chair.

The witch continued. "Yet you believe him innocent of the charges for which he was imprisoned?"

Snape looked at Peter.

"Yes."

Sirius lowered his gaze. Snape returned to his seat. The Wizengamot withdrew. They did not leave the room. Privacy wards descended around their chairs, turning voices into indistinct noise. Fudge argued with a thin wizard near the centre. Two members watched Sirius while speaking. Another read Moody's report for the third time. Waiting became worse than the evidence.

Harry counted the seams between flagstones. Hermione's knee bounced beside his. Draconia sat perfectly still, the nervousness visible only where one thumbnail pressed too hard against her finger. Sirius faced the empty staff table. He did not look at Harry.

"What if they refuse?" Hermione whispered.

"Lucius will appeal," Draconia said.

"That takes months."

"Then we make it public."

"It's already public."

"More public."

Harry barely heard them. A verdict could not make Sirius innocent. He already was. It could only decide whether the world intended to stop punishing him. The privacy wards lifted. Fudge stood. He looked older than he had six days earlier.

"Sirius Orion Black."

Sirius turned.

"Upon review of the testimony, physical evidence, Auror spell-sequence report and verified memories placed before this body, the Wizengamot finds that your detention followed a materially incomplete investigation and occurred without the lawful hearing required to establish guilt."

Sirius's jaw tightened.

"Speak normally," Moody called from the witnesses' row.

Fudge ignored him.

"The charges concerning the betrayal of James and Lily Potter, the alleged murder of Peter Pettigrew and the deaths of twelve Muggles are dismissed."

Harry forgot to breathe.

"Effective immediately," Fudge continued, "your arrest warrant is withdrawn. Your wand and frozen assets will be returned following standard identity confirmation. Restrictions placed upon your movement are lifted."

The blue bracelet around Sirius's wrist opened. It fell to the floor. Sirius stared at it. No applause followed. The moment was too large and too strange for celebration. Members of the Wizengamot arranged papers. Quills scratched. Peter began sobbing inside his enclosure. Twelve years ended with a small piece of metal striking stone.

Sirius bent and picked it up. His hand shook. Fudge continued reading.

"The Ministry acknowledges that Mr Black did not receive the hearing required under ordinary criminal procedure. The emergency statutes applied during the conflict will be reviewed."

"A review," Lucius murmured.

Narcissa touched his sleeve.

"Not now."

"Financial compensation will be assessed—"

Sirius laughed. Every head turned. It was not the broken laughter from Peter's memory. This sound held disbelief.

"Compensation?"

Fudge stiffened. "A standard calculation based upon wrongful detention."

"How much is twelve years?"

"That will be determined."

"Does it cost extra when the guards eat happiness?"

"Sirius," Lupin said quietly.

Sirius looked towards him. The humour left.

"I don't want their money."

"Take it," Lucius said.

Sirius's gaze snapped towards him.

"Use it to make their mistake expensive."

The Hall went quiet again. Sirius looked at the fallen bracelet in his hand. Then he placed it upon Fudge's papers.

"Fine."

Fudge recoiled.

"My godson needs a house."

Harry's eyes burned. He looked down before either girl could notice. Hermione noticed anyway. Her hand closed around his. Draconia's followed from the other side. Fudge cleared his throat.

"The guardianship issue remains subject to a separate welfare review."

Sirius's expression sharpened. Lucius rose.

"So does the Minister's continued handling of this hearing."

Fudge went pale. Narcissa closed her eyes briefly. Harry almost felt sorry for him.

Almost.

The chains around Peter tightened. Kingsley stepped towards the enclosure.

"Peter Pettigrew will remain in secure Auror custody pending trial on twelve counts of murder, conspiracy to murder James and Lily Potter, treasonous service to Lord Voldemort, obstruction of justice, use of an Unforgivable Curse and unlawful Animagus activity."

Peter pressed both hands against the transparent wall.

"Harry!"

Harry did not turn.

"Harry, please!"

Sirius looked towards him. Harry held his gaze instead. The enclosure vanished in blue fire. Silence remained where Peter had been. Fudge gathered his papers.

The members of the Wizengamot began filing from the Hall. Some avoided Sirius. Others offered stiff nods. An elderly wizard stopped to apologise and ruined it by explaining how convincing the evidence had seemed at the time. Sirius listened until the man left. Then he looked at Harry.

"Well."

"Well," Harry agreed.

"I'm innocent."

"You were yesterday."

"Yes, but today it has paperwork."

Harry stood. So did Sirius. Neither seemed to know what came next. Hermione solved the problem by pushing Harry forward. He stumbled into Sirius.

Sirius caught him. The embrace began accidentally and became real before either could become embarrassed. Sirius's arms closed around Harry. He held him carefully at first, mindful of injuries, then with twelve years of grief behind the movement. Harry gripped the back of his robes. Sirius smelled of hospital potion, smoke and the soap Madam Pomfrey had used to force Azkaban from his hair. No memory accompanied the embrace.

Harry had never been held by Sirius before. That made the moment entirely theirs.

"I've got you," Sirius whispered.

Harry closed his eyes. It would have frightened him once—the promise implied in those words, the possibility of losing the person offering them. The fear remained. Harry held on anyway. Behind them, Lupin laughed softly.

Narcissa cried without bothering to hide it. Lucius complained that the Wizengamot had failed to clarify the restoration of Sirius's voting rights, which everyone understood was his method of giving the moment privacy. When Harry released Sirius, Hermione hugged him next. Sirius looked startled.

"You kept him alive," she said into his robes.

"He did most of it."

"Yes. It's an irritating habit."

Draconia waited. Sirius opened his arms. She stared.

"I am not hugging you in front of the Wizengamot."

"They've left."

"Father's still here."

Lucius examined a parchment with unnatural concentration. Sirius smiled. Draconia stepped forward and hugged him quickly.

"If you frighten Harry again," she whispered, "innocence will not protect you."

"Understood."

She released him. Harry looked around the Great Hall. Peter was gone. The Dementors were gone. Sirius stood without restraints.

The past had not changed. Everything after it had.

News of Sirius's innocence reached breakfast before they did. Owls flooded the Great Hall carrying special editions of the Daily Prophet. Papers struck tables, landed in porridge and knocked over two jugs of pumpkin juice. The headline occupied half the front page.

BLACK CLEARED: MINISTRY ADMITS WRONGFUL IMPRISONMENT

Beneath it, a new photograph showed Sirius leaving the hearing with Harry beside him. Sirius looked alarmed by the camera. Harry appeared to be telling the photographer to move. A second headline asked:

PETTIGREW ALIVE—WAR HERO OR SERVANT OF YOU-KNOW-WHO?

Hermione folded the paper.

"Why is that phrased as a question?"

"Because newspapers enjoy uncertainty when facts are less exciting," Draconia said.

"He confessed."

"The question mark protects them from having to develop principles before the next edition."

Harry turned the page.

An article described Peter as a former associate of the Potters and suggested the Ministry had acted with "wartime urgency" based on "the best information then available."

"They make it sound like weather," he said.

Sirius read over his shoulder.

"Scattered injustice with periods of administrative regret."

Harry laughed. Students continued looking at them. The staring had changed. Sirius was no longer a threat they expected to attack between courses. He had become a story people wanted to touch. A fourth-year Ravenclaw approached and asked for an autograph. Sirius said no.

A third-year asked whether he had truly escaped by turning into a dog. Sirius said yes. Within twenty minutes, someone had begun selling sketches of his Animagus form for two Sickles each. Draconia confiscated one.

"For evidence?" Harry asked.

"The proportions are insulting."

The artist had given Sirius six-foot legs and glowing red eyes.

"I look impressive."

"You look anatomically impossible."

Hermione read the small print beneath the Ministry article. Her expression changed.

"What?"

She turned the paper. A narrow column appeared beside the main report.

PARENTS QUESTION WEREWOLF'S EMPLOYMENT AT HOGWARTS

The article named Lupin. It described the transformation on school grounds, his history as a werewolf and his childhood connection to Sirius. No mention appeared of the Wolfsbane Potion, the missed dose during an emergency or the months Lupin had spent teaching safely. Harry stood.

"Who told them?"

Draconia took the paper.

"'A senior Ministry source.'"

"Fudge."

"Possibly."

"Snape knew."

Hermione looked at him. "So did McGonagall, Dumbledore, Madam Pomfrey, Moody, Lucius and everyone at the hearing."

Harry saw Snape at the staff table. The professor read the same article without expression.

"He threatened Lupin all year."

"That is not proof," Hermione said.

Harry hated hearing his own arguments returned to him. Sirius folded the paper.

"Where is Remus?"

His seat remained empty.

They found him packing. Lupin's office had been stripped of most personal objects. Books filled two cases near the door. The jars of creatures had already been returned to the storeroom. His patched teaching robes lay folded inside an open suitcase. The photograph remained upon the mantel.

Harry stopped in the doorway.

"No."

Lupin looked up. Sirius entered behind him, followed by Hermione and Draconia.

"Harry."

"You're not leaving."

"I have resigned."

"Why?"

Lupin glanced towards the newspaper in Sirius's hand.

"That's why they printed it."

"To make you leave," Harry said.

"Yes."

"Then don't."

"It is not that simple."

"It is exactly that simple."

Lupin closed the suitcase.

"Seven parents withdrew their children before lunch. More letters are arriving. The governors have been called."

"Lucius will make them let you stay," Draconia said.

Lupin's smile carried no amusement. "That may be the least reassuring defence available to me."

"He frightens them."

"I know."

"Dumbledore won't accept your resignation," Hermione said.

"He already has. Effective upon the final day of term."

Harry stared. Lupin came around the desk.

"He argued. I insisted. In return, he made the governors agree that I may finish the year under strengthened safeguards."

"Why?"

"Because I transformed on the grounds."

"You missed one dose because we found Peter."

"That does not alter what I nearly did."

"You weren't yourself."

"I am responsible for preparing for the nights when I am not."

"You saved me all year."

"And nearly harmed you last week."

"Sirius stopped you."

"This time."

The answer angered Harry because it held truth. Sirius stood near the shelves, face tight.

"Don't make this penance."

Lupin looked at him.

"You always do that," Sirius continued. "Take whatever anyone might use against you and punish yourself with it first."

"And you mistake consequences for punishment."

"The school needs you."

"It needs confidence from parents who send their children here."

"Most parents are idiots."

Draconia nodded. "Not an entirely weak argument."

Hermione gave her a look. Lupin sat on the edge of his desk.

"I am not disappearing."

Harry laughed once.

"That's what adults say before leaving."

The sentence stopped everyone. Harry looked away. He had not intended to make the fear visible. It sat within him anyway: Lupin packing, Sirius nearly taken, Hermione returning to her parents and Draconia to the Manor. People always promised correspondence before distance made forgetting convenient. Lupin approached him. Harry stepped backwards.

"Don't."

Lupin stopped.

"You don't get to tell me this is good for me," Harry said.

"I would not insult you by trying."

"You're leaving."

"Yes."

"Then it isn't good."

"No."

The agreement weakened the anger. Lupin folded his hands.

"I can't teach Defence here next year. I can still teach you."

"How does that work?"

"I have been offered research work connected to Wolfsbane accessibility. It will keep me near London for part of the summer."

"Who offered?"

Lucius cleared his throat from the corridor. Everyone turned. He entered carrying a stack of documents.

"I have developed unfortunate charitable impulses."

Sirius narrowed his eyes.

"What do you want?"

"Advice from Professor Lupin about the laws concerning Dark creatures."

"You're hiring him?"

"I am funding research."

"You don't do anything without wanting something."

Lucius looked at him. "I want competent advice before the Ministry converts fear into another policy affecting my family."

"Draconia isn't a werewolf."

"No. She is friends with several people the Ministry considers inconvenient."

Harry looked at Lupin.

"You accepted?"

"Provisionally."

"And you'll be near London?"

"For several weeks."

"Where?"

Lupin took a piece of parchment from the desk and handed it to him. An address appeared in neat writing. Beneath it were dates. Every Sunday afternoon. Harry read them twice.

"What is this?"

"Lessons."

"Patronus?"

"If you wish."

"Defence?"

"Yes."

"Stories about my parents?"

Lupin hesitated. "Within reason."

Harry folded the parchment.

"That's an appointment."

"I thought it might be more useful than a promise."

The distinction found the place Harry needed it to reach. He placed the parchment inside his pocket.

"I'm still angry."

"You may continue to be angry on Sundays."

"Good."

Lupin looked relieved. Sirius lifted the photograph from the mantel.

"You're keeping this."

"I intended to."

"I mean upright."

Lupin took it. The four boys waved from behind the glass.

"It has spent long enough facedown," Sirius said.

Lupin's thumb moved over the frame.

"Yes."

Hermione looked towards the half-empty shelves.

"Who gave the information to the paper?"

"Not Professor Snape," Lupin said.

Harry's head lifted.

"You know?"

"He came here before breakfast to warn me."

Sirius's expression darkened.

"Why would he warn you?"

"Because the leak came from the Minister's office."

Harry thought of Snape reading the article in the Great Hall. He had looked neither surprised nor satisfied.

"I thought it was him," Harry said.

"I know," Hermione replied.

He glanced at her.

"You made the face."

"Both of you need new material."

Draconia folded one of Lupin's robes more neatly.

"What happens to Defence next year?"

"Dumbledore will appoint someone else."

"That has gone well historically," Harry said.

Lupin almost smiled.

"I will finish this term."

"You're still teaching until June?"

"Yes."

"And the Patronus lessons?"

"Yes."

Harry let out a breath. The goodbye had moved farther away. Not vanished. Harry no longer required that lie. Farther was enough.

Harry found Snape in the dungeons after dinner. The Potions classroom stood empty. Evening light reached the high windows only as a thin green line, disturbed by the lake water outside. Cauldrons hung above the workbenches. Ingredients floated in dim glass jars along the walls.

Snape sat behind his desk marking essays. He did not look up when Harry entered.

"If you have caused another catastrophe, put it in writing."

"I came to apologise."

Snape's quill stopped.

"That is even more suspicious."

Harry approached the desk.

"I thought you told the Prophet about Lupin."

"You expressed that accusation widely enough that I am aware."

"I asked Hermione and Draco."

"In a corridor containing forty students and Peeves."

"That was unfortunate."

"That was habitual."

Harry placed both hands in his pockets. Snape returned to the essay.

"Was that the apology?"

"No."

"Then improve it or leave."

Harry hated apologising to Snape. The feeling made it more necessary.

"I was wrong."

Snape's quill moved again.

"I thought you cared more about hurting Sirius than finding out the truth."

"I did not ask for your opinion."

"You testified for him."

"I testified regarding Pettigrew."

"You said Sirius was innocent."

"He was."

"You also stopped Lupin from attacking us."

"A teacher protecting students is not a revelation, Potter."

"Sometimes it is."

The quill stopped again. Harry regretted the words the instant they left him, but not enough to take them back. Snape looked up. The green light from the windows sharpened his face.

"No," he said. "I suppose your experience has been inconsistent."

Harry did not know what to do with the acknowledgement. Snape placed the essay aside.

"You wished to apologise."

"I'm sorry I assumed it was you."

The silence lasted long enough to become uncomfortable.

"Your apology is noted."

Harry waited.

"That's all?"

"What did you expect? A biscuit?"

"Lupin has chocolate."

"Professor Lupin encourages dependency upon confectionery."

"He says it helps after Dementors."

"He says many things."

Harry moved towards the door.

"Potter."

He turned. Snape's hands rested flat upon the desk.

"You were correct about one point."

Harry waited.

"My judgement concerning Black was not objective."

"That's a strange apology."

"It was not an apology."

"Obviously."

Snape's eyes narrowed.

"I knew him at school. He was arrogant, reckless and cruel."

"He told me what happened with the Willow."

A small change passed across Snape's face.

"Did he?"

"He said it was his fault."

"An accurate assessment."

"He wants to apologise."

"I do not want it."

"That doesn't mean he shouldn't."

Snape rose.

"Do not imagine that one exoneration transforms Black into a decent man."

"I don't."

"Do not confuse his affection for your father with proof he will care for you responsibly."

"I don't."

"Do not begin every answer with—"

"I'm not James."

The sentence struck more deeply than Harry intended. Snape went still. Harry held his gaze.

"I know Sirius did terrible things to you. My father too. I know you look at me and remember them."

"You resemble him."

"That isn't my fault."

"No."

"I shouldn't have to become him every time you're angry."

The lake water moved beyond the windows, sending shadows over Snape's face. Harry expected punishment. Instead Snape looked away.

"No," he said again.

It was not enough to repair two years of cruelty. It was not an apology. Nothing changed visibly between them. But Snape had heard him. Harry turned towards the door.

"Five points from Gryffindor," Snape said.

"For what?"

"I just did."

"That ruins the moment."

"I am devastated."

"You were listening."

"Door was open."

"It wasn't."

"Old habits."

"No."

"I should speak to him."

"He doesn't want you to."

"It should start."

"You and Remus are becoming repetitive."

"No."

"Good."

"If you intend to loiter outside my classroom, Black, I will summon Filch."

"We need to talk."

"No."

"About the Shack."

"No."

"You're right."

"I'm sorry."

The apology remained in the corridor. Snape looked first at Sirius, then at Harry.

"I do not forgive you."

"I know."

"I may never."

"I know."

"That went well."

"You apologised to Snape."

"Don't tell anyone."

"Your reputation would never recover."

Harry smiled.

Hermione returned the Time-Turner on a Thursday afternoon. Harry and Draconia waited outside McGonagall's office. They had not been invited. Hermione had asked them to come anyway.

Inside, voices rose and fell beyond the closed door. Harry could not distinguish words. Draconia could and pretended not to. She sat upon the windowsill with one foot against the wall. Spring sunlight brightened the corridor, warming the stone beneath them. The grounds outside had recovered from the night of Peter's capture. Snow had melted. Hagrid had replanted the vegetables crushed during the pursuit and placed a sign beside them warning fugitives to use the path. Harry looked at Draconia.

"How long does returning a necklace take?"

"As long as admitting why it must be returned."

Hermione had carried the Time-Turner all year beneath her robes. Harry had occasionally seen its gold chain near her collar, but he had not understood what it cost until her memories forced him into the truth: days stretched beyond their proper length, meals missed because two classes occupied the same hour, exhaustion treated as insufficient discipline. She had been everywhere. No one had noticed she was disappearing.

"She thinks she failed," Harry said.

"She completed every subject."

"That doesn't matter to her."

"It will."

Harry leaned against the wall.

"What if McGonagall tells her to keep it?"

"She won't."

"How do you know?"

"I spoke to her."

Harry looked at Draconia.

"When?"

"Yesterday."

"Without Hermione?"

"Yes."

"What did you say?"

"That if she allowed Hermione to continue fracturing her days, I would inform the Board that a professor supplied a thirteen-year-old with unsupervised access to temporal magic."

Harry stared.

"That sounds like a threat."

"It was phrased as concern."

"Was it convincing?"

"Professor McGonagall removed fifteen points from Slytherin."

"So no."

The office door opened. Hermione emerged. Her eyes were red. The chain no longer circled her neck. Harry straightened.

Draconia came down from the windowsill. Neither asked how it went. Hermione closed the door and stood between them, hands empty.

"I dropped Divination and Muggle Studies."

Harry nodded seriously.

"A tragic collapse."

She glared.

"You were taking too many classes," Draconia said.

"I could have managed if I organised better."

"No," Harry replied.

"You do not know how I organised."

"I saw it."

Hermione went quiet. The memory spell still sat awkwardly among them. They had agreed not to use what they saw as weapons. They had not agreed to pretend it had never happened.

"You were tired in every memory," Harry said. "Even the good ones."

Hermione looked down at her empty hands.

"I wanted to prove I could."

"You did," Draconia said.

"I stopped."

"You chose to stop."

"That feels worse."

"Only because continuing was easier than admitting it hurt."

Hermione looked at her. The words belonged to more than a Time-Turner. Draconia knew it. Harry did too. Hermione reached for their hands.

"We're holding hands in a corridor," Draconia said.

"There's no one here."

"The portraits have eyes."

A painted knight above them leaned visibly out of his frame. Draconia glared. He retreated. Harry closed his fingers around theirs.

"What happens next year?" he asked.

"I take a reasonable number of subjects."

"How many?"

"Ten."

"Hermione."

"Nine."

"Six," Draconia said.

"Eight."

"Seven."

Hermione considered. "Eight with one independent study."

"That is nine."

"It is not scheduled."

Harry sighed. "We need another amendment process."

Despite the tears upon her face, Hermione laughed. The sound filled the sunlit corridor. McGonagall listened from the other side of her door. On her desk, the Time-Turner lay inside its velvet case. Beside it sat Hermione's original schedule and a letter the professor had begun twice.

Miss Granger,

Ambition is not measured by the amount of yourself you are willing to spend.

McGonagall read the sentence. Then she signed it and placed it among Hermione's end-of-year papers.

Draconia wrote to the Mind Healer three days later. The decision required six pieces of parchment. The first letter sounded like a legal challenge. Privet Drive waited with its clean pavements, narrow rooms and silence trained to erase him.

But Sirius stood behind the car with one hand resting upon the motorbike. Hermione and Draconia remained beside their families. No one had left while Harry faced the door. He climbed inside. Dudley pressed himself against the opposite side.

Vernon started the engine. As they pulled away, Harry looked through the rear window. The motorbike followed. Sirius rode close behind them, black hair tearing free in the wind. Lucius's car moved into the lane after him. The Grangers followed that. A ridiculous procession left King's Cross.

Harry began laughing. Vernon glared into the mirror.

"What is so funny?"

"Traffic."

"That man cannot follow us home."

"He already knows the way."

The mirror inside Harry's pocket warmed. He drew it out. Draconia's face appeared first, crowded immediately by Hermione's.

"You left your chocolate," Hermione said.

"No, I didn't."

Lupin's bar remained inside his robes.

"She is testing the mirror," Draconia explained.

"It works."

"Clearly."

Sirius's face entered from one side, distorted by the angle of the mirror attached to the motorbike.

"Eyes on the road," Harry said.

"I am on the road."

Vernon looked into the rear-view mirror. The motorbike slipped neatly between two lanes and drew level with the car. His face turned purple. Harry settled against the seat. Privet Drive had not changed.

Harry watched the motorbike through the rear window, following the car beneath the summer sky. He was returning to Privet Drive, but he wasnt going back alone.
