10

Harry had spent lunch wondering whether he would still want dinner after the meeting. At three o'clock he stood on the moving staircase outside Dumbledore's office, watching the stone steps carry him towards the explanation promised the night before. Sirius stood close enough for their shoulders to brush whenever the stone turned. Remus followed one step below, his presence marked by the faint tap of his walking stick against each stair.

Stone ground softly against stone as they climbed. Harry watched the toe of Sirius's boot appear beside his own at each bend, then disappear as the steps carried them higher. There had been no hurried explanation on the way here, no instruction to leave the girls behind because what he was about to hear was beyond them. Hermione knew where he was. Draconia had told him she would be in the library afterwards, then repeated it until he understood that he was meant to come and find her. Nobody was hiding the meeting from him. He still could not stop wondering what would be different when he came back down.

Harry kept his injured hand inside his pocket. Madam Pomfrey had removed the bandage on Friday afternoon and pronounced the cuts closed, but the words remained faintly visible beneath the skin. When he flexed his fingers, I must appeared in thin pink strokes and faded again. Temporary, according to the Ministry. Harry wondered whether fear became temporary when nobody else could see it. The gargoyle closed behind them. Dumbledore's office waited in the grey light of a wet afternoon, crowded with books, silver instruments and sleeping portraits. Rain moved against the high windows. Fawkes perched beside the desk with his head beneath one wing, though one bright eye opened as Harry entered.

Dumbledore had placed four chairs near the fire, close enough for them to speak without the broad desk dividing the room. A tray stood upon a low table between them. The teapot had begun to steam; beside it, four cups waited with their handles turned neatly outwards. Harry noticed the arrangement and disliked that he was grateful for it. Someone had thought about how to make this easier. That did not mean the thing itself would be easy.

"Thank you for coming," Dumbledore said.

"You made it sound fairly important," Harry replied.

"It is."

Sirius sat beside Harry. Remus took the chair opposite, leaving Dumbledore the one furthest from the door. No parchment waited in anyone's lap. No recording quill floated nearby. Harry had been asked whether he wanted one and declined; Remus would help him put the important parts in his own notes afterwards.

"Before I begin," Dumbledore said, "I must tell you what I will not explain today. There are details concerning your mother's protection, the magic used in the graveyard and the consequences of Voldemort taking your blood which I do not yet understand well enough to present as fact."

Harry's scar prickled at the name.

"You're researching it."

"Yes."

"And when you know?"

"You will know."

"Before you use it in a plan?"

Dumbledore accepted the edge in the question without pretending not to hear it.

"Before I ask anything of you because of it."

Harry nodded once. The headmaster looked older than he had the previous night. Fatigue sat differently upon him than it did upon Remus. Remus settled his stick against the chair and shifted until his recovering leg was comfortable. Dumbledore took a moment longer to sit than Harry remembered. Neither seemed inclined to pretend that the previous week had been easy.

"The prophecy was made shortly before your birth," he said. "Its speaker was Sybill Trelawney. She did not understand what she had said and retains no conscious memory of saying it."

"Professor Trelawney?"

Sirius stared at him. "You appointed the woman who predicted this?"

"I appointed her partly because the knowledge placed her in danger. I also hoped that teaching might reveal abilities beyond that single moment."

"Has it?"

Dumbledore's eyes moved briefly towards the ceiling, in the approximate direction of the North Tower.

"Not consistently."

Harry might have laughed on another day.

"What did she say?"

The fire shifted. One log collapsed into sparks. Dumbledore did not recite the prophecy as though it were scripture. He translated it slowly into ordinary language, stopping wherever the old phrasing might be mistaken for certainty. A child would be born as July ended. His parents would have defied Voldemort three times. Voldemort would identify that child as his equal and, by attacking him, create the person capable of defeating him. The power needed to do it would be something Voldemort neither understood nor possessed. The prediction said that one would die at the other's hand, and that neither could truly live while the other survived. Dumbledore gave Harry those words before offering any interpretation; he did not want a gentler summary mistaken for the whole prediction. When Dumbledore finished, the rain still moved across the windows.

Harry waited for the room to change. The instruments continued turning, and Fawkes tucked his head beneath his wing again. One of the portraits snored with unnecessary force. Harry could hear the tiny click of a brass wheel somewhere behind Dumbledore's desk, the rain and Sirius shifting in his chair. It seemed unreasonable that such ordinary sounds could continue after someone had explained why his parents had died. He tried to remember being eleven and hearing Hagrid say his name in a hut on a rock. There had been a whole world waiting beyond the Dursleys then. Now there was another piece of it he had not known existed, and this one had apparently been waiting for him before he could walk.

"That's it?" Harry asked.

Sirius leaned towards him. "Harry—"

"No, I mean—" Harry stood because sitting had become impossible. "That's what he wants? A glass ball saying one of us has to win?"

"He knows only the beginning," Dumbledore said. "The person who overheard the prophecy was discovered before hearing its conclusion. Voldemort learned that a child born at the end of July to parents who had defied him might one day threaten him. He does not know the nature of the power described, nor the precise consequence of marking the child."

"But he knows it was me."

"He chose you."

Harry stopped.

"Chose?"

Dumbledore's gaze remained steady. "There were two children to whom the first portion might have referred. Both were born as July ended. Both had parents who had opposed Voldemort repeatedly. The other was Neville Longbottom."

Neville's face appeared in Harry's mind: rounder at eleven, frightened of Snape and ashamed of forgetting passwords; older now, standing straighter beside a plant that spat foul-smelling sap at anyone who approached it incorrectly. Neville, whose parents remained alive without knowing him.

"Does he know?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because Voldemort did not choose him. The prophecy does not govern Neville's life merely because he once fitted its opening conditions."

"It governs mine because Voldemort believed it?"

"In part. Prophecies do not issue commands. They describe possibilities, often through language broad enough to become dangerous when treated as instruction. Voldemort heard that a child might possess the power to defeat him. He acted to prevent it and, in acting, gave you the mark by which the prophecy recognised you."

Harry touched his scar. He had imagined fate as something ancient and enormous. A road built before he arrived, too high-walled to leave. This was worse in a smaller way. Voldemort had selected him from two babies. Not because Harry had been stronger, or because he had done anything to deserve it. Voldemort had made a choice before Harry was old enough to recognise his own name. Harry could not yet understand why.

"Why me?"

Dumbledore's face tightened.

"I cannot know his thought with certainty. My belief is that he considered you the greater resemblance and therefore the greater danger. You were, like him, a half-blood. Neville came from an old pure-blood family. Voldemort chose the child whose history reflected his own."

Sirius swore under his breath. Harry walked to the window. Rain blurred the grounds into watercolour: dark forest, silver lake and the lawns falling away beneath the tower.

"So Neville's parents—"

"Their attackers were trying to discover what had happened to Voldemort after his fall," Remus said quietly. "Frank and Alice were Aurors and members of the Order. We have no evidence that the attack was an attempt to fulfil the prophecy through Neville. What happened to them belongs to their family, Harry. It is not something you caused by surviving."

Harry heard the correction inside the kindness. He had already begun trying to carry it.

"Does Neville deserve to know he was nearly chosen?"

"That is a difficult question," Dumbledore said. "The information concerns him. It also places a burden upon him which no longer alters Voldemort's purpose. I would value your thoughts before deciding."

Harry turned from the window.

"Mine?"

"You understand better than I do what such knowledge costs at fifteen."

That might have sounded like another responsibility placed in his hands. Instead Dumbledore waited, leaving the question open.

"Not yet," Harry said. "Not because he can't handle it. Because I don't know what good it does him. But if the Ministry or Voldemort could find out and use it against him, then he should hear it from us first."

"Agreed."

Harry returned to the chair but did not sit.

"What is the power?"

No one needed to ask which power he meant. Dumbledore folded his hands.

"Love."

Harry stared at him.

"That's not funny."

"No."

"He tortured me. I loved people then. It didn't stop him."

Sirius reached towards Harry and stopped before touching him. Dumbledore did not soften the answer.

"Love is not a shield against pain. It did not prevent your parents from dying or you from being harmed. To call it protection without acknowledging that would be cruel."

"Then what does it do?"

"It permits choices Voldemort cannot predict. Your mother did not defeat him by feeling love. She made a deliberate sacrifice when she had the chance to stand aside. That act produced magic because it was freely chosen. In the graveyard, your attachment to Cedric led you to bind your own strength to his concealment. He saw a tactical problem where you saw a person you would not abandon. We should not mistake that difference for a reason to ask you to endure more pain. It is a reason to understand why your choices are difficult for him to predict."

Harry looked at Sirius.

"That sounds like a useful way of saying I make bad decisions."

"Some of your decisions are appalling," Sirius said. "The reason you make them matters anyway."

"Thanks."

"Any time."

Remus leaned forward, forearms resting upon his knees.

"The prophecy is not saying affection makes you invulnerable," he said. "It may be saying that connection changes what you are capable of doing and what Voldemort is capable of understanding. That includes accepting help, Harry. It cannot mean only dying for people."

Harry remembered Sirius beside him in the hospital wing on Monday, relieved that he had called instead of remaining at Umbridge's desk. Accepting help had been a choice too.

You called.

Harry finally sat.

"Does it literally mean one of us has to kill the other?"

"Death is the plainest reading," Dumbledore said. "I will not conceal that from you. But a prophecy does not give us a set of instructions, and I cannot tell you exactly how its words may be fulfilled. We must not turn it into an order for you to kill, or assume it explains every choice still available to us."

"But Voldemort thinks it means death."

"Almost certainly."

"And he wants the sphere to hear the rest."

"Yes. Only those named by a prophecy can safely remove its record. He could enter the Ministry and retrieve it himself, but doing so would expose his return beyond the government's ability to contain. Using you offers him both secrecy and the pleasure of forcing his intended victim to deliver the weapon."

Harry saw the black door open by the width of one finger. The hunger behind it had felt like his. He still did not know whether Voldemort had deliberately pushed that feeling towards him or whether the connection had carried a desire already present. Either possibility frightened him.

"Can we destroy it?"

"The record, yes. Destroying it would not undo the prediction or what I remember of it. An unexplained removal or destruction could also alert a compromised observer that we know Voldemort seeks it. It may also drive him towards more direct methods before we understand the connection well enough to protect you."

"So we leave it there as bait."

Sirius stood.

"No."

Dumbledore looked at him.

"You are not using Harry as bait," Sirius said. His voice remained low, which meant anger had passed beyond shouting. "Not deliberately, not by omission and not because a prophecy has provided poetic language for it."

"I have no intention of doing so."

"You said moving it might make him change his methods. That leaves Harry exposed to the method we already know he wants."

"I said moving it may accelerate his actions. The distinction matters."

"Not if Harry is the route he takes."

"Then we make it harder for him to use me," Harry said.

Both men turned.

"Occlumency," Harry continued. "The mirror. Verification before anyone acts on a vision. We already made rules at Hawthorn. We check them against this now, while nobody is screaming."

Remus watched him with an expression Harry could not read.

"What rules?" Sirius asked.

Harry began counting upon his fingers.

"If I see one of you in danger, I use the mirror before leaving Hogwarts. If the mirror fails, I can send a Patronus or ask McGonagall, Dumbledore or Snape to contact the duty coordinator. A failed connection is not proof the vision is true. Nobody goes anywhere alone, and I don't leave to investigate until two independent checks agree."

"And if the vision shows there isn't time?" Remus asked.

The question was gentle. It hurt anyway. Harry imagined Remus beneath a wand. Sirius behind the black door. Hermione falling on the train. Draconia unconscious in the Chamber while words promised her body would remain there forever. Every memory insisted that delay killed.

"Then I still check," he said.

"Why?"

"Because that's what he'll use."

The room became very quiet. Harry hated the rule already. That probably meant they needed it.

Dumbledore drew a sheet of parchment towards him. "We will make the procedure exact. We will retain the daily verification checks agreed at Hawthorn and make sure every duty coordinator knows them. A private answer may help us recognise a real contact, but we cannot assume it is secret from Voldemort merely because we changed it. It must be supported by an independent confirmation."

"If Voldemort is inside my mind when I hear it?"

"Then the phrase alone is insufficient," Hermione would have said.

Harry nearly heard her voice.

"There has to be someone answering me in the present," he said. "Not just saying the phrase. I need to ask a question I haven't prepared and hear them answer it. Then someone else has to confirm where they are."

Remus nodded slowly. "A live exchange, then confirmation through a separate person and channel before anyone travels because of a vision. The same person repeating an answer does not count as two checks."

"And you tell Hermione and Draco," Sirius said. "They will notice if you begin finding reasons the rules do not apply."

"They'll write more rules."

"Exactly. We might as well let her put that notebook to work."

"I intend to tell her you said that."

"She already assumes worse."

Dumbledore wrote as they spoke, then read the procedure back so Harry could correct it. They kept the agreed check-in times and the instruction to contact the duty coordinator immediately after a missed check. Harry disliked imagining an emergency in which he had to wait for an answer, but he knew whom to ask now, and what to do if the first person did not answer.

Remus walked Harry back to Gryffindor Tower. Sirius remained with Dumbledore to argue about relocating the prophecy, a task he described as a calm exchange of views and Remus described as the reason several portraits had abandoned the office before they began. The rain had stopped. Windows along the seventh-floor corridor stood open to air washed clean and cold. Water shone upon the stone ledges. Far below, students crossed the grounds towards dinner, their robes dark marks against grass brightened by the storm. Harry slowed near one window. Remus stopped with him.

"You knew," Harry said.

"Part of it."

"Which part?"

"That a prophecy concerned you and Voldemort. That he attacked your family because of what he believed it meant. I never knew the complete wording."

"Did my parents?"

"They knew enough to understand that you were a target. Not enough to know another child had fitted the description."

Harry leaned against the cold stone.

"Dumbledore said they defied him three times."

"Yes."

"How?"

A smile touched Remus's face. Not happiness. Memory carrying warmth across grief.

"The first time, James drove a stolen Ministry carriage through the wall of a recruitment house while Lily evacuated six Muggle-born families through the kitchen."

Harry stared.

"He drove a what?"

"The carriage was technically borrowed."

"From the Ministry."

"Without permission."

"That is the definition of stolen."

"Your father felt definitions became unnecessarily rigid during emergencies."

"I am beginning to understand why Professor McGonagall looks tired when she says his name."

Remus laughed softly.

"Lily had designed the evacuation. James was meant to wait beyond the wards. He decided the wall was taking too long."

"Did it work?"

"Spectacularly. The families escaped. The carriage lost two wheels. Moody threatened to arrest James and then asked whether he could reproduce the manoeuvre under controlled conditions."

Harry pictured his father not as a photograph or a body upon a cottage floor, but young and terrified and driving through stone because waiting had become unbearable. The resemblance was uncomfortable.

"What about the other times?"

"One involved Lily exposing a poisoned Floo network. The other belonged to both of them and is a longer story than the walk to your common room."

"Convenient."

"I will tell you."

Remus's gaze moved to the grounds.

"They were brave," he said. "They were also frightened, angry and sometimes wrong. The prophecy did not transform them into symbols until after they were dead. Do not let it do the same to you while you are alive."

Harry looked at him.

"Is that what you think I'm doing?"

"I think you heard that your capacity to love may defeat Voldemort and immediately began calculating how much of yourself you might be required to spend."

Harry looked through the open window. Two younger students were running across the wet lawn below, one carrying a bag which appeared to be leaking feathers. Their shouting reached the tower faintly. A professor would catch them if they kept it up. Harry envied the certainty of that small, approaching disaster. Remus waited beside him. He had always been good at letting a question sit without reaching in to answer it on Harry's behalf; today, Harry found the patience almost unbearable. It left him nowhere to hide except the truth.

"He has to be stopped."

"Yes."

"If I'm the one who can—"

"Then you will need to be rested enough to think, and willing to let other people do what you cannot. You do not become better prepared by wearing yourself out first."

Remus said it without drama. That made the sentence harder to escape. They resumed walking. At the portrait of the Fat Lady, Harry gave the password and then remained outside.

"Professor?"

Remus raised an eyebrow. "You only call me that when you are worried or attempting to manipulate me."

"Can it be both?"

"Experience suggests yes."

"The Defence group. I said I'd teach."

"I heard."

"Sirius told you."

"You told Sirius about the plan through the mirror at lunch, remember? I was at the kitchen table. Narcissa was near the door and asked whether Draconia would be helping. We discussed the lesson, not a list of pupils."

Harry pushed his glasses higher.

"How do I teach people without making them think I know more than I do?"

Remus considered.

"Begin by telling them what you just told me. Then teach the first thing you needed during every dangerous moment and did not have."

"Help?"

"A way out."

Harry thought of the office door refusing to open.

"Most Defence lessons begin with attack spells."

"Most Defence lessons are designed by people imagining a duel with rules. Real danger is often a room you should leave, a friend you should reach or a second in which surviving matters more than winning. Teach them exits. Then teach them how to create enough time to use one."

Harry nodded.

"Will you help me plan it?"

Remus smiled.

"Of course. Bring what you've worked out. We can argue about the rest."

Harry smiled, relieved by the prospect of an argument he would be allowed to lose. He had spent the afternoon hearing about a future Voldemort wanted to choose for him. Next week, Remus would look over a lesson plan. Harry found he wanted that small appointment in his diary as much as he wanted any answer Dumbledore could give him.

On Sunday morning, the castle refused to cooperate. Harry, Hermione, Draconia and Ron began on the seventh floor with the Marauder's Map spread between them. The parchment showed corridors, classrooms, cupboards, staircases and the occasional professor moving through rooms no student was supposed to know existed. It showed several large classrooms, but none whose privacy or access looked suitable for twenty people practising spells. A room being empty on the Map did not mean it would remain empty once they began. They inspected two abandoned classrooms, an old music room and a chamber containing nothing except seventeen cracked chamber pots arranged in a circle.

"Perhaps they were holding a meeting," Ron said.

Hermione closed the door. "We are not practising here."

"They seem friendly."

"One of them moved."

Ron stopped smiling. The music room had better ventilation but shared a wall with Professor Vector's office. The first classroom possessed three exits, all of which opened onto heavily travelled corridors. The second had no windows and a portrait of a seventeenth-century headmaster who promised not to report them with an enthusiasm nobody trusted. Draconia rejected the broom cupboard before Harry finished opening it.

"We could fit six people," he said.

"If none of them required arms."

"That would complicate the spellwork."

"Slightly."

By noon, Hermione had developed a table ranking each room by privacy, access, escape routes, structural integrity and likelihood of attracting Peeves. Every option had failed at least two categories. Ron had developed hunger.

"We could use an empty Quidditch changing room," he said as they climbed another staircase.

"Too exposed," Draconia replied.

"The boathouse."

"Visible from the castle."

"The Forbidden Forest."

"Forbidden," Hermione said.

"That's never stopped Harry."

"It has occasionally slowed me down."

"The Shrieking Shack?"

Harry looked at Ron.

Ron raised both hands. "Bad history. Fair enough."

They returned to the seventh floor because the Map contained a peculiar stretch of outer wall opposite a tapestry of Barnabas the Barmy. The corridor was longer on parchment than it appeared in stone. Hermione measured it twice, first with footsteps and then with a length of enchanted string.

"There should be twelve feet between this wall and the exterior," she said.

"Old castles lie," Ron replied.

"Not if you take them properly. I checked twice."

"They do if the castle moves."

Draconia examined the tapestry. Barnabas wore a broad hat and an expression of doomed optimism while attempting to teach ballet to three trolls. One troll had lifted him by the ankle. Another appeared to be applauding.

"There is no seam," she said, running her wand along the stone beside it. "No hinge, concealment charm or displaced mortar."

Harry opened the Map. Four labelled dots stood in the corridor. Nothing occupied the blank space beyond the wall.

"Maybe there isn't a room," he said.

Hermione crouched to inspect the floor. "Then the outside of the castle is twelve feet closer than it should be."

"Perhaps it dislikes being measured."

"Buildings do not dislike things."

The nearest staircase rotated away from them.

Hermione glared after it. "That proves nothing."

Ron sat beneath the tapestry. "Could we prove nothing after lunch?"

They had nearly agreed when Filch appeared at the end of the corridor. Mrs Norris moved ahead of him, tail raised.

"You lot," Filch called. "What're you doing up here?"

Hermione folded the Map before he could see it.

"Walking," Harry said.

"Back and forth?"

"We wanted to be thorough."

Filch's eyes narrowed. A new pink badge had been pinned to his coat beneath the Hogwarts crest: INQUISITORIAL SUPPORT STAFF.

"Professor Umbridge says suspicious congregation is to be reported."

"We're walking between classrooms," Hermione said. "We haven't held a meeting or practised a spell."

"Depends what you're congregating about."

Draconia stepped forward with the expression she used when a shopkeeper attempted to overcharge her.

"We are assessing disused spaces for a cross-House academic study group. Our preliminary survey has been conducted openly during permitted hours. Directive One concerns organised discussions of disputed events involving more than three students. We have not discussed an investigation or held a practice session here. Professor Umbridge has repeatedly encouraged theoretical education."

Filch looked uncertain. Draconia continued before he could recover.

"Of course, if the High Inquisitor wishes to state formally that students may not study with members of another House, I am sure the Board of Governors and the Daily Prophet would appreciate the clarification."

"I didn't say—"

"Excellent. Then your report may note our cooperation."

She turned away. Harry followed. Hermione and Ron moved with them. They did not hurry until the next corner hid them.

"That was terrifying," Ron whispered.

"That was administration," Draconia replied.

"Same thing."

They descended one flight and waited until Filch's dot reached the opposite staircase. Harry and Draconia returned beneath the Cloak first. Hermione and Ron followed at a distance after checking the corridor, and joined them beside the tapestry. All four tried crowding under the fabric when footsteps sounded on the floor below. It was an immediate mistake. Hermione walked bent beneath the centre. Ron's left shoulder and Draconia's right remained visible at opposite edges. Harry was pressed between the girls closely enough to feel Hermione's hair against his cheek and the line of Draconia's arm along his side.

"Three of us fitted better when we were eleven," Hermione whispered.

"We were smaller," Harry said.

"We also had fewer shoulders," Draconia murmured.

"I had two."

"They were theoretical."

Ron stepped on the back of Harry's shoe. They lurched as one misshapen invisible creature.

"Sorry."

"If we survive this war," Harry whispered, "it will be despite you."

"I helped on the train."

"You have used up your competence for the month."

They reached the blank wall. Harry walked the length of it, thinking. The castle had always opened certain secrets only when students needed them: staircases arriving late, passages hidden behind portraits, a hat producing a sword for someone willing to ask for help. Hogwarts could be careless, dangerous and infuriatingly convinced that riddles counted as supervision. It could also listen.

Harry passed Barnabas once, thinking about the unused classroom whose portrait had been so eager to promise discretion. They needed somewhere Umbridge could not enter merely because she possessed a title. Somewhere frightened students could make mistakes without finding their names in a report. He pictured a floor wide enough for shields and cushions to fall onto, then remembered the lock in Umbridge's office and added doors which opened from the inside.

They disentangled themselves once the footsteps had passed. Harry turned at the window and walked back. Hermione was bent over the Map. Ron folded the Cloak before somebody trod on it again; Draconia held her side clear of a suit of armour whose visor had begun to follow their progress. Harry scarcely noticed them. Every room they had seen came with another reason to say no. Somewhere in this enormous, ridiculous castle, there had to be a place they could use. On his third pass, his foot caught against something which had not been there before. A narrow strip of brass lay across the bottom of the wall. Harry stopped, catching Hermione's sleeve before she walked into him. Beside the tapestry, a handle was pushing slowly out of the stone.

The four of them stared at the new handle. Hermione lowered the Map without folding it.

Ron stared at the door. "Was that there?"

"No," Hermione said.

"Excellent. I was worried the chamber pots had affected me."

Draconia drew her wand.

"We test it before opening."

The first detection spells produced no warning. Draconia tried again with a different charm, then looked at Hermione rather than lowering her wand. A quiet result did not tell them everything about a door which had grown out of a wall. It told them only that neither girl had found an immediate danger. Hermione cast three spells twice. Draconia checked the hinges, frame, handle and stone above it. Ron inspected the tapestry in case the trolls began paying attention. Harry placed his hand upon the brass.

"Ready?"

"Not until we agree how we're doing this," Hermione said. "One person looks from the threshold. Nobody goes inside until we can see the way back."

Draconia nodded. "I'll keep the frame clear."

Ron drew his wand. "That sounds better than probably."

Harry waited for Hermione's nod, then opened the door. The room beyond was enormous. Harry stopped on the threshold long enough for Ron to collide with his shoulder. The wall opposite stood much farther away than the outside of the castle allowed, and high windows poured light across a wooden floor with no sign of rain beyond the glass. Outside, the afternoon was grey. Here, the light had the colour of a late summer morning.

"Don't," Hermione said before anyone asked her.

"I hadn't said anything," Ron replied.

"You were going to ask how it fits. I don't know."

He looked pleased to have reached a question she could not answer. Harry checked the threshold from both sides before moving aside. Draconia held the door open while Hermione followed him; Ron stayed in the corridor until they had tested opening it from inside. Only then did all four enter. The floor gave a little beneath his foot. It was solid, but not hard enough that falling onto it would hurt in quite the same way as falling onto stone. He took two quicker steps, stopped and looked down.

"It gives under you," he said.

Draconia tested it for herself, then went back to the door and pushed it open again. The corridor remained exactly where they had left it. She held the frame with one hand while looking back into the room, as though she needed both places in sight before accepting either. Shelves lined one side, holding cushions, practice targets, bandages, bottles of water and protective equipment in sizes ranging from first-year small to Hagrid unlikely. The floor was smooth wood broken by broad padded squares. Training dummies stood at the far end beneath a wall marked with distance measurements. Three doors occupied the opposite side. Harry crossed to them.

The first opened onto the corridor outside. The second opened onto an adjoining corridor, its doorway about fifty feet from the first but beyond the corner leading towards the west stair. The third revealed a small quiet room containing chairs, a basin and a cabinet with a promisingly useful label: REFRESHMENTS. Harry opened it. Nothing occupied the shelves.

"Nearly," he said.

Hermione peered over his shoulder. "It may be able to give us somewhere to put food without providing the food itself."

"I can work with that," Ron said. "Provided someone else brings it."

The chairs, at least, looked comfortable. One faced the open doorway instead of the wall. Harry imagined Remus settling into it, putting down his book and waiting until whoever had come in was ready to speak. They could bring water and chocolate themselves. Hermione stood near the entrance, eyes wide.

"It created exits."

"You were thinking about exits, weren't you?" Draconia asked Harry. "You checked every door in the other rooms."

They looked at him.

"I didn't say anything."

"You were walking in front of a wall three times while thinking violently," Ron replied. "That's practically a speech."

The room changed while they watched. Words appeared above the three doors.

MAIN CORRIDOR. SECONDARY CORRIDOR. QUIET ROOM.

Hermione approached the shelves. "It responds to need."

"Then ask it for lunch," Ron said.

Nothing happened.

"It has standards," Draconia observed.

For the next two hours, they attempted to make the room betray them. Hermione tested the exits from both sides and marked the secondary corridor on a separate sketch, because it did not open beside the tapestry. Each of them practised reaching it from the stairs before relying on it as an escape route. Draconia tried the surveillance charms Narcissa had taught her. None carried a signal through the walls, though that could only tell them about the spells she knew how to test. Harry left and tried to summon the entrance while thinking only of inspecting an unauthorised gathering. He could not become Umbridge simply by imagining her; they were testing how the room responded to a different request. The door refused to appear. Ron remained inside and confirmed that the room had not vanished, sealed its exits or replaced him with a cupboard.

The Marauder's Map showed no labelled dots for them while they stood inside. Harry held it closer to the light, checking the adjacent classrooms and the space beyond the tapestry. Nothing. Ron stepped through the main door, and his name appeared in the corridor. When he returned, it vanished. Hermione repeated the test before allowing herself to look pleased. He asked the room to dim the lights and remove one corridor door, forgetting to mention the alarm he imagined with it. The room dimmed. One marked door vanished. Cushions expanded into waist-high barriers across the floor. A low alarm sounded from the walls—not the guardian scream from Umbridge's office, but enough to make several students flinch. Ginny froze. So did Draconia. Harry stopped the alarm immediately.

"Too much," he said.

Nobody moved. Harry lowered his wand. A few pupils looked towards Ginny, then stopped when Cedric asked them to check their own partners. Nobody needed an audience while deciding whether to continue. Hermione crossed to Draconia while Harry went to Ginny. Neither asked what memory the sound had reached.

"Do you want it removed?" Harry asked.

Ginny stared at the floor. "Lower. It sounded like the train for a second. And warn us before it starts."

"All right."

Across the room, Draconia nodded at something Hermione said. Harry addressed the group.

"That was my mistake. We agreed no surprise spellwork. The room changing counts. Next time you get a warning and a choice."

"You're the teacher," Ernie said. "You don't have to apologise."

"Then the teacher should be the first person who follows the rules."

Draconia looked at him. Something in her expression warmed. They repeated the exercise after warning everyone exactly what would change, and offering a quiet version to anyone who preferred it. This time the alarm remained quiet enough to resemble a distant bell. Students found their partners, identified the changed route and moved. Nobody received points for reaching the exit first. A pair succeeded only when both crossed. Neville and Pansy were slowest. Neville attempted to lift a padded obstacle which the room had made too heavy. Pansy told him to leave it. Neville refused because Luna's robes had snagged beneath one edge as her pair went past. Ron was beside her, keeping the fabric from pulling tight while she tried to free it. Pansy stayed with Neville rather than calling him away.

"Finite," Harry called.

The obstacle did not move.

"It isn't enchanted onto her," Hermione said. "The weight is physical."

"Can I cut the hem?" Neville asked. Luna nodded at once. He abandoned the lifting charm, cut the caught fabric clear of her ankle and helped her step free. Ron and Pansy waited while she checked that she could walk. All four crossed last.

"Correct decision," Harry said.

Neville stared at his feet. "We lost."

"No one was left behind."

"Everyone else was faster."

"Everyone else had a different problem. Defence isn't an examination where the room promises fairness. You solved yours."

Luna examined the missing strip from her robes. "I have been intending to shorten them."

Pansy looked at her. "They are uneven."

"Yes."

"Give them to me after the meeting."

Luna smiled. "Thank you, Pansy."

Pansy appeared alarmed by her own kindness. The first shields came after that. Hermione called everyone back to the padded squares while Draconia moved the practice dummies farther from the doors. People were louder now. Ernie had started explaining to Susan that he had identified the secondary corridor immediately; Susan reminded him that he had first tried a cupboard. Zacharias bent to retie a shoe which had somehow lost its lace during the evacuation.

Harry had expected to be grateful when the room became quiet. Instead he found he liked the noise. They were talking about what they had done, disagreeing and going back to look at things they had missed. Neville stood beside Luna while she examined the cut in her robes. Pansy appeared to be giving instructions about the hem, with Luna accepting only every second suggestion.

Cedric took the centre of the floor beside Harry. "Ready for the next bit?"

Harry nodded. His mouth was no longer dry. He could still feel every person looking at him, but he also knew where each of them had been a few minutes ago. Teaching twenty strangers had become practising with people he was beginning to recognise.

"A shield does not need to overpower the attacking spell," he explained. "It needs to redirect, absorb or delay it long enough for you to act. A perfect shield cast too late is decoration."

He demonstrated with Harry. Cedric warned him before casting a low-powered Disarming Charm. Harry raised his wand.

"Protego."

Gold light curved between them. Cedric's spell struck and slid sideways into a padded wall.

"Again," Cedric said.

This time Harry cast first. Cedric shielded while moving towards the secondary exit. He did not remain planted for the sake of looking strong.

"That," Harry said, "is the important part. The shield creates a second. Use it."

Pairs spread across the room. The next hour filled with shouted incantations, flashes of light and the repeated thud of harmless spells hitting cushions. Hermione corrected wand angles. Cedric adjusted footwork. Draconia taught Pansy and Anthony to watch an opponent's shoulder rather than the wand tip. Ron discovered he could produce a broad, unstable shield strong enough to cover Neville as well as himself.

"Again," Harry told him.

Ron did. The shield appeared faster.

"Again."

"Are you going to say anything else?"

"Eventually."

On the fourth attempt, the silver barrier held without shaking. Ron lowered his wand, breathing hard.

Harry grinned. "That one."

Pride transformed Ron's face before he disguised it.

"Wasn't difficult."

"Of course not. Do it while moving."

"I liked you better three seconds ago."

Neville struggled. His first shield barely sparked. The second formed at the wrong angle and sent Ginny's spell into a bucket of water. On the third attempt he jerked his wand down before the shield formed. Ginny stopped her weak spell, and Neville knocked his knee against the practice stand while trying to step away. Laughter moved through the nearest students. Neville bent at once, pretending to check his knee. Harry saw his shoulders draw in and recognised the attempt to occupy less space before anyone could notice how much he already wished to leave. Across the room, Pansy had stopped casting. She was watching Neville too, but she did not laugh. The floor smelled of warm wood and the faint sharpness left by spent spells. Harry remembered how that smell changed in a classroom where someone else was waiting for you to fail.

Harry remembered a Potions classroom. Melted cauldrons, Snape's contempt and the way failure grew larger when an adult expected it.

"Hold your spells a moment," he said. "Cedric, can you look at the pair by the window?"

Cedric nodded and drew the nearest students back to their own work. Harry went to Neville while Hermione corrected Hannah's grip, giving the rest of the room something useful to watch. He did not need twenty people waiting for Neville's next attempt.

"Show me your grip."

Neville held out his wand. His fingers were locked around it.

"You're bracing for the spell before it arrives," Harry said.

"Isn't that the point?"

"Not if it makes you too stiff to cast. Who are you protecting?"

Neville glanced at Ginny.

"Her."

"No."

Neville looked confused.

"Not yet," Harry said. "Right now you're learning the shape. Protect the space in front of you. Nothing else depends on it."

"But if this were real—"

"It isn't. You are allowed to practise before it becomes real."

Neville swallowed. Harry showed him the looser grip on his own wand, then waited while Neville adjusted his fingers.

"Again."

Ginny cast.

"Protego!"

The shield appeared. Small. Crooked. Entirely present. Ginny's spell broke against it. Neville stared. Ginny grinned at him. Pansy gave a brief nod, and a few nearby pupils clapped before returning to their own spells. Neville looked startled, then pleased; nobody was waiting for him to repeat the success for the whole room. Neville's eyes shone.

"Again," he said.

They continued until every student produced something capable of interrupting a weak spell. Not everyone managed a complete barrier. Hannah's shield appeared in scattered plates. Zacharias's was strong but so narrow that Cedric hit his shoulder by aiming six inches to the side. Luna's curved backwards around herself like an eggshell and remained after she lowered her wand.

"Is it meant to do that?" Ron asked.

"Probably not," Hermione said.

Luna tapped the inside. It rang.

"I like it."

At nine, Hermione called for the last attempt. Several pupils asked for another, and she gave them five minutes with Cedric watching the remaining pairs. At nine ten, the targets went dark as agreed. Draconia reminded everyone that departures had to be staggered if they meant to get back before patrols reached the upper floors. Wands went away, reluctantly but without an argument. Students gathered their bags. Hermione checked that every charmed coin still received the test message and repeated the secrecy rules. No one discussed membership outside the room. No one brought another student without approval. If questioned by a professor, they did not use elaborate lies which could be disproved; they asked for their Head of House or guardian.

"What do we call it?" Ginny asked.

Hermione looked pained. "We do not require a name."

"Every illegal group requires a name," Fred said.

"We are an unapproved study group," Hermione said. "I know that breaches the decree. We do not have to announce it with a name."

George pointed towards the decree folded on the table.

"The wall disagrees."

"Defence Association," Cho suggested. "It matches the initials already on Hermione's first notes."

"That will look splendid in her report," Draconia said. "Could we choose something less helpful to her?"

"Study Group," Harry offered.

Ron groaned. "Still terrible."

Ginny turned the coin between her fingers.

"The Ministry thinks Dumbledore is assembling students against them, doesn't it?"

"That is one of their more dramatic claims," Hermione said.

"Then Dumbledore's Army."

The room became quiet. Harry looked towards the door. Naming it made the danger real. It also transformed the Ministry's accusation into a joke they owned.

"Dumbledore didn't organise this," Ernie said.

"Exactly," Ginny replied. "It's their accusation. We might as well make a joke out of it."

Cedric smiled. "The name stays inside this room."

One by one, students agreed. Harry handed Hermione the master coin. She altered the tiny lettering along its edge, showed him the result and returned it.

D.A. — FIRST MEETING

The copies changed in twenty other hands. Pupils turned the coins over, comparing the new letters before putting them away. Harry looked at the small message and tried to remember how empty the room had seemed half an hour before they arrived.

The students left in pairs through the two corridor exits, waiting between departures while Hermione checked the Map for movement in the outside corridors. It could not count who remained inside, so Cedric and Draconia counted the departures themselves. Cedric sent the Hufflepuffs out two at a time, then followed them. Cho left with Anthony. Angelina took Fred with her; Alicia followed with George after the first pair reached the staircase. Both twins were loudly discussing an imaginary Gobstones dispute. Pansy departed with Theodore after warning him that if he betrayed them, she would ensure his formal robes fitted badly for the remainder of his natural life. Luna and Padma waited for Hermione's nod before taking the other corridor. Ron waited for Neville and Ginny near the main door.

"Coming?" he asked Harry.

"In a minute."

Ron looked at Hermione and Draconia, both still packing materials. Understanding crossed his face with suspicious speed.

"Right," he said. "A minute."

Harry threw a rolled practice cloth at him. Ron ducked and disappeared through the door laughing. Hermione watched it close.

"He is improving."

"At Defence?"

"At leaving."

Draconia fastened the case of practice quills. "One might argue he has received excellent instruction."

Harry sat on the edge of the platform. Now that the room had emptied, exhaustion arrived all at once. His hands shook faintly. The wound from Umbridge's quill pulsed with each beat of his heart despite being healed. Hermione noticed first. She always did when the evidence was physical.

"You should have stopped ten minutes earlier."

"Everyone was still practising."

"That is not a defence."

"It was a Defence meeting."

"Harry."

"I'm fine."

Draconia came to stand between his knees.

"That phrase has lost all meaning through misuse."

"I'm tired."

"Then say that next time. We can work with tired."

Hermione sat beside him and placed a piece of chocolate in his hand.

"Remus?" Harry asked.

"Narcissa. She packed an entire box in Draco's bag."

Draconia looked offended. "Mother believes every difficulty can be improved through appropriate provisions."

"Your mother is often right."

"Do not encourage her."

Harry ate the chocolate. For several minutes they sat without speaking, watching the room clear the scattered equipment. Cushions floated back to the walls. Practice marks vanished from the floor. A cracked target repaired itself with a soft wooden sigh.

"You were good," Hermione said.

Harry looked at her. "Cedric was good."

"Cedric was excellent. So were you."

"Neville did the spell."

"Because you worked out what stopped him."

Praise still felt like a trapdoor. If he stood upon it too confidently, the next failure would open beneath him. Hermione seemed to recognise the thought.

"You do not have to become the best teacher in Britain because one meeting went well," she said. "You are only allowed to be pleased."

"Allowed?"

"Required."

"That sounds like Umbridge."

"All right. You can be pleased or not. I'd still like you to hear me when I say you did well."

Harry laughed softly. He leaned his head against her shoulder. Hermione went still, then relaxed beneath the weight. Her cheek rested against his hair.

"Thank you," he said.

"For what?"

"Building the parts I wouldn't have thought of."

"Someone has to prevent your revolutions becoming scheduling disasters."

"I knew there was a reason."

Draconia remained in front of them. Her expression was composed, but Harry recognised the slight distance entering it. Not anger. Not quite jealousy alone. The old fear that affection had become a room whose door might close before she crossed it. Harry held out his uninjured hand. Draconia looked at it.

"That is an exceptionally obvious gesture," she said.

"I've been told subtlety isn't working."

"By whom?"

"Everyone. Constantly."

She took his hand. Harry pulled gently until she sat on his other side. The three of them occupied the platform shoulder to shoulder. Hermione's head remained against his. Draconia's fingers stayed threaded through his, hidden between their robes. It did not resolve anything. Harry no longer thought every good moment had to.

"The room opened because you asked for somewhere safe to fail," Draconia said after a while.

"You already said that."

"I was wrong."

Harry turned towards her.

"You made room for Neville to try again," Draconia said. "If you'd made that mistake, you would have decided the whole meeting was ruined."

The observation went through him with uncomfortable ease. Hermione lifted her head.

"Then we amend the requirement," she said.

The words above the blackboard changed.

EVERYONE MAY LEARN HERE.

Harry stared at them.

"The room likes Hermione," he said.

"The room has judgement," she replied.

Draconia squeezed his hand.

"Next week," she said, "you will permit Cedric to teach for longer than twelve minutes."

"Were you timing him?"

"I time many things."

Harry remembered the Yule Ball.

"Apparently."

Colour rose faintly in her cheeks. The main door opened. Ron stood beyond it, breathing hard.

"Umbridge," he said. "Second-floor staircase. Filch with her. They're checking bags."

The room changed at once. The practice equipment vanished. The blackboard became a blank wall. The quiet room filled with shelves of cleaning supplies. Their bags arranged themselves beside the secondary exit. Harry stood.

"Did everyone else get clear?"

"Cedric's group did. Fred and George doubled back to distract her."

"How?"

A distant explosion answered. Green light flashed through the corridor beyond the main door. Filch shouted. Something began singing in a voice like an operatic goat.

"Efficiently," Draconia said.

Hermione checked the Map. Umbridge's dot moved towards the seventh floor. Another dot travelled beside hers. Theodore Nott. Harry looked at Draconia. Her face closed.

"We don't know why he's with her," Harry said.

"No."

"We knew she might question him."

"Yes."

The coin in Harry's pocket warmed. Tiny letters moved around its edge.

DO NOT USE MAIN EXIT. SHE KNOWS TAPESTRY. — T.N.

Draconia read them over Harry's hand. Her expression did not soften. It became something more difficult. Trust, beginning before certainty.

"Secondary corridor," Harry said. "Ron and Hermione first. Draco with me."

"Why are you last?" Hermione demanded.

"We all use the secondary corridor," Harry said. "Ron knows the route and can check ahead. Draco and I come straight after you. We don't stay to watch the door—we already tested that."

"That is not—"

"I'll be beside him," Draconia said.

Hermione looked between them, then towards the secondary door. There was no reason to wait at the main entrance and watch Umbridge approach.

"Mirror open," she said.

Harry drew it from his pocket and activated the connection. "Patrol outside the room," he told Sirius quietly. "We're leaving by the route we tested." He set the sound ward to carry their voices without broadcasting Sirius's into the corridor, then returned the mirror to his jacket.

"Open."

Hermione and Ron left through the second door. The corridor beyond ran in the opposite direction from Umbridge's approach. Draconia followed Harry to the threshold. The hidden room had already begun dimming behind them.

"Thank you," Harry whispered to it.

The last lamp went out. They stepped into the corridor. The door disappeared. Stone sealed behind them without a seam. Footsteps struck the staircase at the far end. Harry and Draconia ran. The secondary corridor curved behind a row of armour and ended at a staircase descending towards the west tower. Hermione and Ron had already vanished below. Harry reached the first step when the staircase groaned and swung away from them. Empty air opened where escape had been.

"Of course," Harry said.

Draconia looked back. Umbridge's voice carried around the corner.

"—certain you saw them on this floor?"

"I saw several students use the western stair," Theodore replied.

He sounded bored. Harry pulled the cloak from his bag. It covered two people easily now that neither was Ron. They moved behind the nearest suit of armour, drew the fabric over themselves and pressed into the narrow recess between its plinth and the wall. Draconia stood with her back against Harry's chest. His injured hand held the cloak closed near her shoulder. Her hair brushed his mouth when she turned her head.

"If you make a joke about my shoulders," he breathed, "I will leave you visible."

"I was going to compliment their development."

"That would be worse."

Footsteps approached. Harry felt Draconia's breathing change. Not from closeness. Umbridge's shoes struck the stone with deliberate little taps. Filch dragged one foot behind her. Theodore moved more quietly than either of them. Harry looked down through the cloak's translucent fold. The pink edge of Umbridge's robes appeared ten feet away. His scar did not hurt. The back of his hand did. Two faded words burned beneath the skin as Umbridge stopped beside the armour.

"There is no western stair on this landing," she said.

"There was when I came through," Theodore replied.

"Staircases do not move that quickly."

The empty space beside them creaked. Far below, the missing staircase continued rotating towards another floor. Theodore looked over the railing.

"Perhaps it is responding to increased supervision."

Filch made a suspicious noise. Umbridge moved closer. Harry's fingers found Draconia's beneath the cloak. He did not remember reaching for them. She turned her hand and held on.

"Mr Nott," Umbridge said, "your value to this office depends upon accurate information."

"My value appears to depend upon what you want the information to mean."

Silence. Draconia's fingers tightened. Umbridge's voice lost its sweetness.

"Your father remains wanted for crimes which may expose your family to seizure, review and the permanent suspension of your inheritance. Cooperation could demonstrate that his disloyalty did not pass to his son."

"You ask whether it passed to me every time I cooperate."

Harry could not see Theodore's face. He heard what the boy had understood. There was no amount of information which would make the Ministry stop using his father. Compliance did not end leverage. It fed it.

"Where did Miss Malfoy go?" Umbridge asked.

Theodore paused.

"She returned to the dungeons after dinner."

Draconia closed her eyes.

"And Potter?"

"I do not monitor Gryffindors voluntarily."

Umbridge stepped so close that the hem of her robes brushed Harry's shoe beneath the cloak. He did not breathe. Neither did Draconia. Mrs Norris appeared around Filch's legs. The cat stopped. Her yellow eyes fixed upon the recess. Harry stared back. Mrs Norris approached, whiskers forward. Draconia's wand shifted inside her sleeve. A crash sounded below. The staircase completed its rotation. Peeves rose through the gap wearing both of Fred's false moustaches and carrying a bucket of luminous green foam.

"Found the pink professor!" he sang.

Umbridge turned. Peeves emptied the bucket. Chaos filled the corridor. Filch shouted. Mrs Norris fled. Umbridge's Shield Charm sent the foam across three portraits, each of whom began screaming advice. Theodore moved aside with impressive speed. The stair below swung back into line with their landing. Harry saw the stone edge meet the wall and touched Draconia's arm. They moved through the returning staircase entrance together. They descended beneath the cloak while Peeves launched into the second verse of a song concerning temporary dermal inscription and permanent stupidity. By the fourth floor, Draconia began laughing. She tried to silence it. The effort made it worse. Harry felt each breath against his chest as they stumbled into an empty alcove and lowered the cloak.

"Did you ask Peeves?" she whispered.

"No."

"The twins?"

"Probably."

"The song rhymed classification with defenestration."

"Dean may have helped."

Draconia leaned against the wall, still holding Harry's hand. The laughter faded slowly. Harry lifted the mirror and told Sirius they were clear of the patrol and heading straight back to their Houses. Sirius asked whether either of them was hurt. They answered together, then Harry closed the connection and returned it to his jacket.

"Theodore lied," she said.

"Yes."

"He may still tell her later."

"Yes."

"You trust too easily."

Harry thought of the boy standing beside Umbridge, offering just enough cooperation to remain useful while refusing to surrender the people she wanted.

"Sometimes."

"That was not agreement. It was criticism."

"I know."

Draconia looked down at their joined hands.

"You kissed this one the other morning," she said.

Harry's thoughts abandoned Theodore, Umbridge and defensive organisation with remarkable efficiency.

"I did."

"Was that also because you trust too easily?"

"No."

Her gaze lifted. Harry could have said more. He wanted to say more. Instead the castle clock began striking ten, reminding them that they had reached curfew while a Ministry official still searched for evidence of their unapproved group. The Cloak lay gathered over Harry's arm, ready to cover them again if footsteps sounded.

"We should go," Draconia said.

She did not release his hand.

"Probably."

Neither moved until the final bell faded.

Educational Decree Number Twenty-Six appeared at breakfast. It established the Inquisitorial Patrol: selected students empowered to report prohibited gatherings, inspect bags with staff supervision and recommend point deductions for approval by the supervising professor. Montague wore the first silver badge. Two Ravenclaw prefects received others. Warrington also received one, as did a Hufflepuff seventh-year Harry barely knew and three students whose parents worked directly for the Ministry. Theodore did not. Umbridge watched him from the staff table while the badges were distributed. He ate breakfast without looking up. Across the hall, Neville dropped his fork. Peeves swooped from the rafters and released a water balloon above the Gryffindor table. Neville's wand entered his hand.

"Protego!"

A crooked silver shield sprang above him. The balloon burst against it. Water flew sideways, soaking Seamus and half a platter of eggs. Students laughed. Neville stared at his own wand. Then he looked towards Harry. Not for approval. To share the fact that it had worked. Harry raised his cup. Across the Great Hall, other members of the group noticed. Cedric smiled into his tea. Ginny kicked Ron beneath the table before his reaction became obvious. Pansy looked away from Neville with suspicious speed. Luna applauded once, softly. At the staff table, Umbridge had turned away from Neville to pin another silver badge onto Warrington's robes. If she had noticed the smiles passing between the tables, she gave no sign of it.

Harry felt the charmed coin warm inside his pocket. Cedric had sent a reply asking about next week. Beneath the table, Harry touched his wand to the master coin, changed the question into an answer and passed it on to the others.

AGAIN NEXT WEEK.

The Ministry had given its patrol badges. Hogwarts had begun teaching itself.
