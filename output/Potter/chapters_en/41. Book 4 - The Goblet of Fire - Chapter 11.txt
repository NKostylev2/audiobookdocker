11

By breakfast, Harry had acquired six new friends and three conflicting accounts of his own bravery.

The friends were students who had scarcely spoken to him before the first task. The accounts involved increasingly impossible versions of the dragon. In one, Harry had broken the restraint with his bare hands. In another, he had flown through the Horntail's fire because phoenix feathers made him immune. The third credited him with deliberately throwing the golden egg towards the judges' platform as a protest against the Tournament.

That version was his favourite.

"You missed," Seamus told him as they descended towards the Great Hall. "But the intention was clear."

"I'll aim for Karkaroff next time."

"That's the spirit."

Harry walked carefully, keeping one hand near the banister. Madam Pomfrey had sealed his ribs and covered the burns with a transparent dressing that pulled whenever his shoulders moved. The worst pain had diminished into a broad, constant soreness, but the stairs kept discovering injuries he had not noticed while lying still. Even doors became difficult when someone held them open and expected him to hurry through.

The bruises attracted less sympathy than the story. At the foot of the marble staircase, a fifth-year Hufflepuff clapped him between the shoulders. The boy meant it kindly; Harry knew that from his grin, his breathless well done and the way he hurried after his friends before waiting for an answer.

Harry's body knew only that something had struck him from behind. He spun with one hand halfway to his wand, pain flashing beneath the dressing. For a moment, the moving robes and wet shoes around him seemed impossibly crowded. By the time the corridor returned to its proper shape, the Hufflepuff had disappeared and Hermione was watching Harry rather than the stairs.

"You all right?" she asked.

"Fine."

The word came too quickly. Hermione heard it, but did not challenge him with half the school flowing around them and three second-years openly discussing whether dragon fire could melt bone. Harry lowered his hand and waited until his breathing settled before continuing.

Yesterday, people had watched him as though they expected him to be exposed as a fraud. Today, they wanted pieces of the story: a joke, a scar, the exact sound the Horntail made when it charged. The attention had changed direction without becoming any less exhausting. Students waited on landings to congratulate him, portraits applauded when he passed, and Sir Cadogan pursued him through four frames demanding a detailed account of the beast's defeat. Another knight eventually shut him inside a painted cupboard.

Harry thanked people until the words stopped sounding sincere. He had wanted the school to believe him; he had not realised belief would be this loud.

Hermione intercepted him outside the Hall.

"Why are you carrying your bag?"

Harry looked at the strap over his less painful shoulder. "It contains my books."

"You are not attending lessons."

"Madam Pomfrey said I could."

"Madam Pomfrey said you could attend two lessons provided you did not carry anything."

"She was speaking generally."

Hermione removed the bag. "I don't think she was."

Harry let her take it. Arguing required more energy than surrender, and he had already discovered that she was prepared to spend considerably more of it than he was.

"Have you seen Draco?"

Hermione's expression changed by a degree. "At the Slytherin table."

He followed her gaze through the open doors. Draconia sat beside Daphne Greengrass, already dressed for Potions. She appeared restored: her hair arranged, her posture corrected and every visible trace of tears removed. She was reading the Daily Prophet while Pansy spoke near her ear. As she turned a page, her eyes lifted and found Harry across the Hall, then returned immediately to the paper.

"She's avoiding me," he said.

"She is sitting in her usual place."

"She looked away."

"People do."

Harry glanced at Hermione, who adjusted the weight of his bag unnecessarily.

"You're helping her."

"I told her you would not mention yesterday in public."

"I wasn't going to."

"She knows."

"Then why is she avoiding me?"

Hermione looked towards the Slytherin table. "Because you saw her."

Harry frowned. "I see her every day."

Her mouth moved as though several answers had presented themselves and all had been judged unsafe.

"Not like that."

They entered the Hall and took their usual places. Hermione set Harry's bag safely beneath the bench before sitting beside him, but before he could ask what she meant, an owl struck him with a newspaper. It dropped the rolled copy directly onto his head, circled once and landed beside Hermione's plate with an air of professional satisfaction.

Harry caught the paper before it reached the floor.

"I didn't order this."

Hermione saw the green ribbon and groaned. Harry unrolled the special edition beneath his hands, revealing Rita Skeeter's smiling photograph beside the byline.

DRAGONHEART HARRY: LOVE GIVES POTTER WINGS

The main photograph showed him crossing the arena boundary on the damaged Firebolt. Fire filled the background, and his face was streaked with soot and twisted in pain, but the angle transformed it into determination. In the lower corner, almost lost behind the barrier, Draconia stood with both hands locked around the rail.

The article did not lose her. Rita described the warded chain at Harry's wrist as "an intimate token from devoted pure-blood heiress Draconia Malfoy". Hermione became "the brilliant but increasingly overshadowed friend whose careful planning enabled Potter's triumph". Fleur's kiss appeared in a paragraph concerning his "growing international appeal".

The actual dragon received four sentences.

"Increasingly overshadowed?" Hermione repeated.

Harry lowered the paper. "You planned most of it."

"That is not the point."

"I know. I only meant you aren't overshadowed."

Hermione looked at him. The irritation shifted into something softer, although she kept one finger pressed against the offending paragraph.

"I know that too."

Across the Hall, Pansy pressed a finger to Draconia's newspaper and whispered something. Draconia folded the paper once, then again, reducing it to a narrow rectangle beneath her hands. She placed it upon her empty plate and set fire to it with her wand. Flames consumed Rita's photograph while Snape continued eating as though spontaneous journalism combustion occurred every morning.

"Subtle," Harry said.

"She learned from Sirius," Hermione replied.

The owl stole a piece of her toast.

Draconia left Potions before Harry had finished packing his cauldron. She usually waited near the dungeon stairs: if Snape dismissed Slytherin first, she reorganised her bag until Harry and Hermione emerged; if Gryffindor left first, she appeared within five minutes and claimed coincidence. Today, Harry looked up in time to see the last movement of her robes at the corridor's far end.

He abandoned his scales to Hermione.

"Where are you going?"

"To see someone who apparently doesn't see me."

Hermione nearly dropped a brass weight. Harry did not notice. He was already following Draconia, walking as quickly as his leg would allow, and caught her near the unused storerooms beneath the Great Hall.

"Draco."

She continued walking. "You are meant to be resting."

"I'm walking."

"Poorly."

"You usually wait."

Draconia stopped. The corridor was cool and dim, lit by narrow windows at ground level. Rain-dark shoes crossed the courtyard beyond them, their owners hidden above the stone. A suit of armour leaned crookedly beside the nearest door, one gauntlet missing after the Firebolt's journey through the castle.

Draconia turned to face him.

"You have Hermione."

"I usually have both of you."

Harry had not considered the answer before speaking. Draconia appeared to consider it enough for them both. Her gaze dropped to his wrist, where a narrow red line remained after the heated chain. She had reclaimed the charm before returning to the dungeons the previous night, but Harry still felt its absence whenever he moved his hand.

Draconia covered her own wrist. Harry noticed.

"Let me see."

"It is nothing."

"That phrase is banned."

"By whom?"

"Sirius. He applied it to me, but I'm expanding enforcement."

Harry held out his hand. Draconia studied it as though accepting involved an agreement she had not read, then placed her fingers in his. He led her into the nearest empty classroom and closed the door with his foot.

Dust covered the desks. A row of narrow cupboards occupied one wall, their doors marked with the faded names of potions no longer stored there. High windows admitted bars of colourless daylight, showing every disturbance in the dust as Harry pulled two chairs together. He drew the burn salve from the inner pocket where Madam Pomfrey had ordered him to keep it.

"Sit."

Draconia lifted an eyebrow. "Is that what I sound like?"

"Worse."

She sat. Harry took the chair beside her and extended his hand again, waiting until she surrendered her wrist.

The mark was worse than his. An angry red circle crossed the pale skin, darkest beneath the green stone, with small blisters following the line of the chain. Harry's chest tightened as he turned her hand gently towards the light.

"This happened when I burned."

"That was the intention."

"The intention was to warn you, not injure you."

"Mother calibrated the link for distance within the school. The arena was closer than expected."

Harry opened the jar. "And you didn't take it off."

Draconia said nothing. He dipped two fingers into the salve, cold enough to numb his fingertips, and paused above the worst of the blisters.

"This might hurt."

"You have cracked ribs."

"That wasn't an invitation to compete."

He spread the salve carefully across her wrist. Draconia inhaled, but her hand remained still inside his. The room seemed quieter than an unused classroom had any right to be. Harry became aware of small things he would usually have overlooked: the movement of her pulse beneath his thumb, the clean scent of her soap beneath the medicinal salve, a pale strand of hair escaped near her ear.

Draconia watched his face rather than his hands.

"You do not have to do this."

"Neither did you."

"That is different."

"Why?"

Her answer did not arrive. Harry smoothed the salve over the final blister, taking care not to press against the broken skin.

"Yesterday wasn't embarrassing."

Her fingers tensed. "I did not say it was."

"You set fire to a newspaper and fled Potions."

"The first was reasonable. The second was scheduling."

"You're avoiding me."

"I am sitting in an empty classroom while you hold my hand."

Harry looked down. He was still holding it. He did not release her.

"You know what I mean."

Draconia's composure wavered. She looked towards the cupboards before speaking, her voice lower than it had been in the corridor.

"You saw me lose control."

"I've seen that before."

"Not voluntarily."

"Draco." Harry waited until she met his eyes. "You didn't become someone else because you cried."

"It did not feel dignified."

"Most things involving me aren't."

Her mouth softened. Harry moved his thumb once across the uninjured skin inside her wrist.

"I'm glad you trusted me enough to stop pretending."

Something changed in her face. The distance between their chairs had become very small, and her knee rested against his without either of them moving it. Harry could count the dark lashes around her eyes and see the faint place where cosmetic magic had failed to conceal yesterday's tears.

She leaned closer. Perhaps she only intended to inspect the cut along his cheek; her free hand rose, hesitated beside it, then touched the edge of the healing line. Harry remained still.

"This will scar," she said.

"It has competition."

"I like this one less."

"Why?"

Her gaze moved from the cut to his eyes. Harry forgot the pain in his ribs, and the answer he had expected no longer seemed particularly important.

Footsteps approached the classroom. Draconia withdrew her hand, Harry released her wrist, and the door opened.

Hermione stood on the threshold holding both their bags. Her eyes moved from Harry to Draconia, then to the open jar of salve between them.

"Madam Pomfrey said you were not to carry anything," she told Harry.

"I wasn't."

"You left your bag in the Potions dungeon."

"I only carried it to Potions."

"That counts."

Hermione entered and placed the bags upon a desk with unnecessary care. Draconia examined the silver coating across her wrist, turning it slightly before looking at Harry.

"Thank you."

The moment before the door opened remained in the room, altered but not erased. Harry still did not understand what might have happened. The care with which Hermione avoided looking directly at either of them suggested that she did.

The golden egg resisted scholarship.

Hermione approached it as a linguistic problem. She copied its sounds onto parchment, slowed them with a recording charm and compared the results against dictionaries of magical languages. Goblin, Mermish, Troll, several runic ciphers and a chapter concerning the mating songs of frost salamanders were eliminated by the end of the week. The last left her looking personally offended by the salamanders.

Draconia treated the egg as an object designed by people who enjoyed unnecessary secrecy. She examined the hinges, tested the metal for concealed writing and made a list of conditions that might alter sound. Harry opened it periodically to confirm that it remained unbearable.

It did.

"Perhaps the clue is that we should destroy it," he suggested.

Hermione stopped the recording charm, cutting the distorted screaming off halfway through a note.

"The second task is on the twenty-fourth of February. We have three months."

"Exactly. Plenty of time to destroy it carefully."

"We are not destroying the only clue."

Draconia held the egg towards a beam of winter sunlight. "We could remove one panel."

"That is destruction with better vocabulary."

"Which generally improves things."

They worked in the library, abandoned classrooms and once in a greenhouse, until the egg's voice caused several Fanged Geraniums to bite through their restraints. Professor Sprout banned it from the greenhouses afterwards, and Hermione added another unsuccessful experiment to a list that was beginning to require its own folder.

Ordinary school life collected around the mystery. Harry returned to a full timetable after three days, although the burn still tightened beneath his robes during long lessons. His ribs ached whenever he laughed, which Fred and George treated as an irresistible challenge. Professor Flitwick excused him from practical work until Harry demonstrated a Shield Charm strong enough to throw a cushion through a window.

The damaged Firebolt remained with Sirius. Without it beside his bed, Harry woke twice convinced that the Summoning Charm was still travelling towards him. He would sit in the dark, reaching for the absent handle before he was properly awake, and find the arena waiting in the space where the broom should have been. On those mornings, he used the mirror before breakfast.

Sirius never asked whether he had dreamt of the dragon. He asked whether Harry wanted company. Sometimes Harry said no, meaning that he did not want to explain anything. Sirius seemed to understand the difference; he stayed with him anyway, speaking about unimportant things until dawn loosened the darkness around the curtains.

The school's attention altered rather than disappeared. Harry no longer heard accusations in corridors, but students asked about the Firebolt, the Summoning Charm and whether the Horntail's breath smelled different from ordinary fire. Younger Gryffindors followed him between lessons. Two third-year Ravenclaws attempted to calculate his speed from witness accounts and argued over whether the dragon's tail had accelerated him.

The Hufflepuff badges remained. SUPPORT HOGWARTS'S CHAMPIONS became common enough that even Professor Sprout wore one beneath her cloak. Cedric's face appeared when the badge was pressed once; Harry's appeared on the second press, usually looking surprised about it.

Ernie Macmillan apologised outside Charms. He had prepared the words in advance, which Harry could tell because he spoke without breathing and included the phrase "failure of personal judgement inconsistent with Hufflepuff values".

"It's all right," Harry said when he finished.

"It isn't."

Harry considered him, and the careful speech he had evidently rehearsed before coming over.

"No. It wasn't."

Ernie looked startled.

"But you apologised." Harry held out his hand. "So we can stop carrying it."

Ernie shook it, relief making his grip a little too enthusiastic. Harry found that he preferred this to pretending nothing had happened.

Rita's article continued causing trouble. Pansy began referring to Harry's "international appeal" whenever Fleur entered a room. Lavender asked Hermione whether overshadowing had affected their friendship. Two Slytherin boys bowed when Draconia passed and offered to carry her intimate tokens. Draconia hexed the first boy's tie into his mouth; the second carried his own things.

Hermione responded differently. She answered Rita's claims with facts detailed enough to kill conversation. When someone asked whether she resented Draconia's gift, she explained the principles of paired wardstones for eleven uninterrupted minutes. Harry listened beside her, noticing that she never quite said no.

On the first Saturday in December, Professor McGonagall permitted Harry to spend the afternoon at Hawthorn Cottage. The permission arrived with six conditions, two signed declarations and a reminder that Sirius's legal innocence had not improved his capacity for supervision. Hermione and Draconia intended to come until McGonagall assigned Hermione a meeting about the independent-study schedule devised after she surrendered her Time-Turner, and Snape ordered Draconia to attend an overdue review of Slytherin conduct.

Harry suspected the conduct belonged to the boy who had swallowed his tie. He travelled alone.

Hawthorn Cottage stood beyond Hogsmeade, sheltered behind the same hedges Harry had first seen heavy with summer flowers. The long journey north in July had ended at this gate; now he could reach it on an afternoon away from school. Snow had fallen during the night, softening the roof and filling the narrow garden, while smoke rose steadily from the chimney. Harry could see his footprints following him along the path. Sirius opened the door before he reached it.

"You're late."

Harry checked his watch. "By fourteen seconds."

"I suffered."

"You look recovered."

Sirius pulled him inside. The cottage smelled of woodsmoke, bread and the polish used on broom handles. Harry's Firebolt occupied the kitchen table, its broken tail removed. Tools, replacement twigs and coils of binding wire surrounded the exposed handle, leaving just enough space for two mugs.

"You're fixing it here?"

"We are."

Sirius took Harry's cloak. A blue jumper stretched across his shoulders, one sleeve marked with varnish, and his hair had been tied back with what appeared to be a strip torn from an old Gryffindor scarf. Harry stared.

"What?"

"You look domestic."

"Take it back."

"There's flour on your face."

Sirius wiped his cheek and found a white streak on his fingers.

"Remus did that."

"I have been reading for the last hour," Remus called from the sitting room.

"Vindictive man."

Harry smiled. The cottage was warm enough to fog his glasses, and when he finished cleaning the lenses, a mug of hot chocolate waited at his place beside the Firebolt. No one announced that it had been made for him. He sat down and wrapped his fingers around it, feeling the warmth reach his hands through the china.

A cupboard opened behind Sirius, and Winky emerged carrying a folded tea towel. She wore the small knitted dress Narcissa had sent rather than the stained garment from the World Cup, and the shadows beneath her eyes were no longer quite so deep. Even so, she stopped as soon as she saw Harry. Her gaze travelled to the dressing visible above his collar, then to the healing cut on his cheek.

"Harry Potter fought the dragon," she said.

It was not a question. Harry wondered how many versions of the story had reached a house hidden behind protective hedges.

"The dragon did most of the fighting."

Winky's ears lowered. "Dragons is not meant to be fighting children."

"I agree. You should write to the judges."

For one uncertain second, he thought she might smile. Then Sirius reached for the towel, and she drew it protectively against her chest.

"You don't have to work here," Sirius reminded her. His voice had lost its usual carelessness. "Not to earn the room. Not to earn the food. Not for any reason."

"Winky knows."

She said it with the solemn patience of someone repeating a lesson she had memorised but did not yet believe.

"Winky is helping because Winky chooses."

Sirius accepted the distinction with a nod. "Then thank you."

Winky looked startled by that too. Harry lifted the mug.

"And thank you for this."

Her eyes flicked towards the chocolate and away. "Master Remus made it."

"Remus put the milk in a pan," Sirius said. "Winky prevented him from burning it."

"I was reading," Remus called.

"You're always reading when something goes wrong."

Winky retreated towards the sitting room, still holding the towel. At the doorway, she glanced back at Harry's injuries.

"Harry Potter came back," she whispered.

She vanished upstairs before he could decide whether she meant from the arena or from somewhere else.

Sirius showed him how to strip the damaged varnish without cutting into the ash, examine the grain for fractures and bind new tail twigs so they responded as one rather than several hundred separate pieces. The split in the handle had already been repaired, leaving a faint seam beneath Harry's fingers. He worked around it carefully, turning the wood towards the light whenever Sirius wanted him to look more closely.

The work demanded a patience Sirius claimed not to possess and demonstrated anyway. Harry liked the quiet of it. He liked being taught something that did not concern survival, with no curses cast towards him and no timer measuring how quickly he could answer. When he wrapped the first binding incorrectly, Sirius undid it and showed him again.

"James hated maintenance," he said. "Wanted every broom to remain perfect through appreciation."

"Did it work?"

"He was very appreciative."

Harry bent over the tail. "You always tell me things he was bad at."

Sirius's hands paused on the wire. "Does that bother you?"

"No." Harry considered the question. "Everyone else makes him sound finished. Like a portrait. You make him sound like someone who left socks beneath furniture."

"He did. Lily once threatened to charm them into his pillow."

"Did she?"

"No. She used mine."

Harry laughed, his ribs objecting less sharply than they had a few days before. He tried to picture his mother gathering abandoned socks and planning her revenge, and found that it was easier to imagine her alive when she had something ordinary to be irritated about.

Remus joined them for lunch. Sirius had attempted bread, producing a crust hard enough for Hagrid to serve with tea around a centre that remained an unexplained liquid. They ate soup instead, with Sirius insisting that the loaf had been an experiment rather than a failure.

Afterwards, Remus spread several documents across the cleared end of the table.

"We have news about Crouch."

Harry set down his spoon. "You found him?"

"No."

Sirius leaned against the counter. "We found evidence that he's either conducting Ministry business from a sealed house while violently ill, or someone wants it to appear that way."

Remus turned the first parchment. It contained three samples of Barty Crouch's handwriting, their strokes identical even to Harry's untrained eye.

"Orders arrive at eight every morning," Remus said. "They are written on parchment kept inside Crouch's office and sealed with his wand."

"Can someone copy a wand seal?"

"Not easily."

"Imperius Curse?"

Sirius's expression hardened. "Possible."

"Polyjuice?"

"Would not reproduce his magical signature."

Harry studied the samples, following the same sharp curve at the end of each signature.

"Could the orders be written before eight and sent automatically?"

Remus looked at Sirius.

"Hermione?" Sirius asked.

"Me."

"Terrifying."

Remus selected a fourth parchment. "That is what we believe. Several instructions reference events occurring after Crouch stopped appearing publicly, but each could have been predicted or reported to him by an accomplice."

"And Winky?"

Sirius looked towards the ceiling. The floorboards above them had stopped creaking.

"She knows more than she can tell us," he said quietly. "Or more than she believes she is allowed to tell us."

Harry remembered her shaking beneath the Dark Mark. Young master. The words had sounded frightened then; remembering them inside the warm kitchen did not make them feel any safer.

"But Crouch dismissed her. His orders shouldn't still bind her."

"The household bond is gone," Remus said. "That does not make years of obedience disappear. Nor does it necessarily undo every command laid before the dismissal."

"You think he ordered her not to speak?"

"We think someone did." Sirius folded his arms. "She confirmed that Crouch carried the Goblet into the Ministry himself. She also recognised the outer wards around his house when we showed her the diagrams."

"But?"

"But she says they feel wrong now," Remus answered. "Altered after her dismissal, perhaps. Whenever we ask who might possess the authority to change them, she begins blaming herself and cannot finish the sentence."

Harry looked towards the stairs. The fire crackled behind him, filling a silence none of them seemed willing to disturb.

"Have you tried asking about her young master?"

Sirius's expression tightened. "Once."

That single word told Harry how the attempt had ended.

"We won't do it again unless she asks us to," Remus said. "A frightened person is not a locked cabinet. More force does not make the right answer fall out."

Harry thought of Draconia in the hospital tent, apologising for a curse cast through her own hand.

"No," he said. "It usually breaks something."

He looked at the repaired portion of the Firebolt, then back at the papers.

"The Goblet was kept at the Ministry. Crouch transported it. Someone hid my name inside it before the monitoring charms began. Then Crouch disappeared."

"Yes," Remus said.

"That sounds conclusive."

"It sounds arranged," Sirius replied. "Which worries me more."

Harry looked at him.

"Crouch may be involved. He may also be the next thing the person responsible wants us to find."

Moody's advice returned to Harry: play to his strengths, use the broom. Each piece of information had come with enough explanation to make accepting it feel reasonable. He had been grateful for that at the time.

"And Moody?"

Sirius's mouth flattened. "Dumbledore trusts him."

"That wasn't my question."

Remus's expression showed approval too slight to be called a smile.

"Moody's movements before the school year remain difficult to confirm," he said. "That is not unusual. He is famously private."

"He knew Hagrid showed me the dragons."

"Hagrid's secrecy is famously public," Sirius pointed out.

"He also knew what advice I needed."

"Which could make him a competent teacher."

"Or involved."

Sirius came to the table. "We're checking."

Harry recognised the deliberate use of we. He wanted to object, to insist that the questions belonged to him because his name had been put into the Goblet. Then he remembered the Horntail's teeth closing around his shoe, and how easily he could mistake danger for responsibility.

"Tell me what you find."

"We will."

Sirius held his gaze. Harry believed him, and let the papers remain where they were as they returned to the Firebolt.

By dusk, the new tail was secured. Sirius carried the broom into the garden and held it while Harry mounted, the snow reflecting the last light beneath a wall of bare hawthorn branches. Harry kicked off gently, giving himself time to feel how the repaired handle answered him.

The balance had shifted a little. The new twigs responded more slowly during the first turn, and he adjusted his weight, circling until the broom settled beneath him. On the second circuit, it flew like itself.

Sirius stood in the snow watching. Harry dived low over the hedges, climbed above the chimney and returned with the cold pulling tears from his eyes. There was no dragon below him, no egg to take and no crowd waiting to decide whether he had been brave enough. Smoke rose from the cottage, a light burned in the kitchen, and Sirius laughed when Harry landed badly enough to cover them both in snow.

Harry fell onto his back, breathless with laughter. Sirius dropped beside him, sending another spray of snow over his jumper. For several minutes, Harry could think of nothing beyond the cold beneath him, the open sky above and the fact that nobody was scoring the result.

Winter reached Hogwarts properly the following week. Snow covered the grounds overnight, burying the last black marks left by the dragons. Frost webbed the classroom windows, the lake hardened at its edges, and pale morning light turned the corridors blue before the torches warmed them. Students brought the weather indoors on their cloaks, leaving trails of melting snow between lessons.

The Yule Ball was announced on a Thursday. Professor McGonagall waited until the end of Transfiguration, when everyone had packed their books and begun thinking about lunch.

"A final matter."

The class settled with varying degrees of reluctance. Harry's matchbox had acquired four legs and a tail but refused to become any recognisable species. It scratched at the inside of his bag. Hermione's guinea fowl stood perfectly upon her desk, while Draconia's had escaped through an open window ten minutes earlier and was last seen crossing the courtyard.

McGonagall moved to the front of her desk.

"As part of the Triwizard Tournament, Hogwarts will host the traditional Yule Ball on Christmas night. It is an opportunity for students and guests to socialise under considerably less hazardous circumstances than the first task."

Harry heard the qualification. McGonagall's eyes found him briefly before she continued.

"Students in fourth year and above may attend. Younger students must receive an invitation from an eligible attendee. Dress robes are required."

Excitement travelled across the room. Lavender seized Parvati's sleeve, Dean turned towards Seamus, and several Slytherins began whispering before McGonagall finished. Harry looked at Hermione, who had gone very still. On his other side, Draconia examined her quill with sudden concentration.

"The Ball will begin at eight o'clock," McGonagall continued, "and conclude at midnight. The champions and their partners will open the dancing."

Harry stopped looking at either girl. His matchbox creature escaped from the bag, ran across the desk and launched itself onto the floor, disappearing beneath Neville's chair.

"Mr Potter," McGonagall said.

"Sorry."

"You understand that your participation is expected."

"In the Ball?"

"In the opening dance."

"That seems more dangerous than the dragon."

Laughter moved through the classroom. McGonagall did not smile.

"You will select a partner and attend the champions' rehearsal on the twenty-third."

Select a partner. Harry made the mistake of looking left, where Hermione's eyes met his, then right, where Draconia was already watching. The space between one heartbeat and the next became crowded.

He could ask Hermione. The thought arrived with alarming completeness: her beneath candlelight, hair free of its usual tie, her hand in his while hundreds of people watched. Then Draconia shifted beside him, and he imagined asking her instead. Her surprise carefully hidden. Pale fingers placed in his. The look she would give him if he stepped on her shoes.

Choosing one image meant doing without the other. His stomach tightened.

"Questions?" McGonagall asked.

Harry had several. None belonged to Transfiguration.

The bell rang, releasing the conversation everyone had been holding back.

"You have to ask someone," Seamus announced before Harry stood.

"Thank you. I missed that."

"Quickly," Dean added. "Before everyone good is gone."

Hermione snapped the clasp of her bag.

"Girls are not library books, Dean. They do not become unavailable stock."

"I only meant—"

"We know what you meant," Draconia said.

Dean looked to Harry for assistance. Harry was still trying to understand why the idea of either girl going with somebody else felt like losing something he had never possessed.

"You're on your own."

They left the classroom together. Students moved around them in excited groups, invitations already being proposed, analysed and rejected before they reached the stairs. A Ravenclaw fifth-year asked another boy beside the statue of a one-eyed witch. Two Hufflepuff girls agreed to attend together before anyone else could ask them.

Harry walked between Hermione and Draconia. Neither mentioned the Ball, which made it impossible to think about anything else.

He discovered that he could face a dragon before several thousand people but could not ask one girl whether she wanted to spend Christmas evening with him. The comparison became worse when there were two.

He tried to begin after lunch. Hermione was beside him on the staircase, and Draconia had remained in the Hall to answer a note from Narcissa.

"Hermione?"

"Yes?"

Harry looked at her. She waited while students passed in both directions and a suit of armour behind them hummed a carol with none of the correct notes.

"Do you—"

Hermione's hand tightened on the strap of her bag. Harry imagined Draconia watching them open the dance together, her usual place beside him occupied by somebody else.

"—think the egg might need to be heated?"

Hermione stared at him. "Heated."

"Sound travels differently through metal at different temperatures."

"That is technically true."

"We should test it."

"Yes," she said after a pause. "We should."

She continued up the stairs. Harry followed, hearing disappointment in her voice and wishing he had thought of something less obviously unrelated to the question he had begun.

He tried with Draconia before dinner. She stood alone beneath an arch overlooking the courtyard, reading Narcissa's letter. Snow drifted beyond the stone in slow white pieces, and the wind lifted the ends of her hair.

"Everything all right?" he asked, stopping beside her.

"Mother has begun discussing dress robes."

"Already?"

"She considers three weeks a crisis."

Harry looked out at the snow, gathering the words while Draconia returned to the letter.

"About the Ball—"

She folded the parchment. Her face became carefully neutral, but she turned towards him so quickly that he knew she had been waiting for the subject.

Harry thought of Hermione on the staircase, her hand tightening when he began to ask. If he invited Draconia now, would Hermione understand? Would she believe he had considered her first? Would Draconia wonder the same if he chose Hermione?

He had faced the Horntail by creating more room. Here, every move seemed to place one girl farther away.

"I don't know how to dance," he finished.

Something closed behind Draconia's eyes.

"Of course you don't."

"Do you?"

"My mother began teaching me before I could read."

"Could you show me?"

Draconia studied him. "You want me to teach you how to dance with someone else."

Harry heard the question hidden inside the statement and could not find an answer that would be safe for both of them.

"I have to open the Ball."

"Yes."

"And I'd rather not injure anyone."

"A concern you might have considered before the first task."

Harry smiled carefully. After a moment, Draconia sighed and slipped the letter into her pocket.

"Seven o'clock. The trophy room."

The trophy room offered polished floors and an audience incapable of spreading rumours. Silver cups watched from glass cabinets, old Quidditch shields covered the walls, and moonlight crossed the floor through high windows. Harry recognised some of the engraved names. The others belonged to people who had apparently managed to attend Hogwarts without becoming catastrophically confused by Christmas.

Draconia arrived at precisely seven. She had removed her outer robe, and the white sleeves of her shirt had been rolled once at the wrists, revealing the fading circle left by the paired wardstone. Her hair remained loose from the wind outside. Harry forgot his prepared apology for arriving early.

She noticed him looking.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You are poor at nothing."

"Your hair is different."

Her hand rose towards it. "The clasp broke."

"I like it."

Draconia became still. Harry felt heat reach his face and searched for a way to make the remark sound less revealing.

"I only meant—"

"Do not ruin a competent statement through panic, Potter."

He closed his mouth. Draconia drew her wand and sent a small silver sphere into the air. Music unfolded from it, strings and piano carrying a measured rhythm Harry immediately distrusted.

"A waltz is counted in three. You step on the first beat."

"Which is the first?"

"The one before the second."

"Helpful."

She held out her hand. Harry took it, grateful to be given something definite to do.

"Your other hand goes here." She touched the space above her waist. "Not my shoulder. Not my ribs. Here."

Harry placed his palm against her side. Draconia rested her free hand on his shoulder, avoiding the last tender edge of the burn without needing to be reminded. They stood closer than he had expected, the position leaving very little room to pretend he was thinking only about his feet.

"Now?" he asked.

"Now you move your left foot."

He moved his right. Their shoes collided, and Draconia closed her eyes.

"A dragon," she said. "You outmanoeuvred a Hungarian Horntail."

"It had larger feet."

"Again."

Harry counted aloud as they began, the numbers tangling with the music. Watching his shoes made him lean forward, and he struck Draconia's forehead with his own. She corrected his posture by placing two fingers beneath his chin.

"Look at me."

Harry did. The next step went wrong for an entirely different reason.

"Concentrate."

"You told me to look at you."

"Most people can do both."

"Unreasonable."

They tried again. Gradually, the pattern stopped feeling like a succession of instructions. Harry began to recognise movement as he recognised flight: pressure, balance and a response that came before words became necessary. Draconia followed when he remembered to lead; when he forgot, she guided him without drawing attention to the correction.

They crossed the floor. Moonlight silvered her hair, the music covered their breathing, and her thumb moved once against the back of his shoulder. For a complete circuit, neither spoke. Harry imagined the Great Hall transformed for Christmas, Draconia in dress robes and her hand in his exactly like this.

Come with me.

It seemed simple while it remained unspoken. Harry opened his mouth, and Draconia's eyes lifted to his.

Then he thought of Hermione: at the arena rail, carrying his books, waiting on the staircase while he turned an unfinished invitation into a discussion of heated metal. He missed the next step.

His heel caught Draconia's shoe, and they turned badly, momentum carrying them towards a display cabinet. Draconia struck the glass with her back. Harry lifted his hand from her waist to brace it beside her head, stopping himself before he followed her into it.

The music continued. Neither moved. Their joined hands had lowered between them, and Draconia's other hand remained on his shoulder. Her breath touched his face.

"Harry," she said.

She rarely used his first name when they were alone. Hearing it now removed every clever answer he might have found. He could ask, and she might say yes. The possibility was there in the way she watched him, waiting for something he could no longer pretend not to understand.

Then Hermione would know he had chosen.

Harry stepped back. "Sorry."

Draconia's hand fell from his shoulder.

"You were doing well."

"Until the cabinet."

"The cabinet survived."

The distance returned too quickly. Harry looked at the silver sphere, trying to find his way back to the easy mistakes of a few minutes before.

"Could you show Hermione too?"

Draconia went completely still. "Hermione?"

"She might need to dance."

"Yes," Draconia said. "She might."

With one movement of her wand, the music stopped. The silence that followed was worse.

"We have done enough for tonight."

She collected her robe. Harry watched her leave, understanding too late that he had answered a question she never asked.

Draconia did not come to the library the following evening. At twenty-five minutes, Hermione checked the clock. At twenty-six, she unfolded the Marauder's Map beneath the edge of a book, shielding it from the other tables.

Harry raised an eyebrow.

"We agreed not to use that because someone was late."

"We agreed on twenty-five minutes unless danger was announced."

"I thought that was thirty."

"Draconia negotiated it down."

"Of course she did."

Hermione found the small labelled dot inside the Slytherin common room. It had not moved for several minutes. Draconia was safe, then, and choosing to remain where they could not follow. Hermione refolded the map more sharply than necessary.

"What happened in the trophy room?"

Harry looked at the golden egg, standing between three towers of books, closed and blessedly silent.

"She taught me to dance."

"I know that."

"How?"

"She told me."

"When?"

"This morning. She asked whether I required instruction too."

Harry remembered Draconia leaving beneath the stopped music.

"Did you?"

"That is not the point."

"It might be if I step on you."

Hermione's expression tightened. "Did you ask her to the Ball?"

The question landed without warning. Harry looked down at his parchment, where a line of notes had ended in an inkblot.

"No."

Hermione became very still. "Oh."

Harry heard relief, then disliked himself for being relieved by it. He turned the quill between his fingers without writing anything.

"Did she think I was going to?"

Hermione opened a book. "You would need to ask her."

"She isn't here."

"No."

Pages turned between them. Harry tried to read a chapter about resonant enchantments, but the same paragraph passed beneath his eyes four times without becoming language. Hermione's silence beside him grew more difficult to ignore.

"Are you angry with me?"

Her gaze remained on the book. "Why would I be?"

"You're using your calm voice."

"I have several calm voices."

"That one means you're arranging an argument."

The book closed. Madam Pince looked towards them from her desk, and Hermione lowered her voice.

"Draconia thought you were going to ask her."

"She said that?"

"She did not need to."

"I didn't mean to make her think—"

"What did you mean?"

Harry had no answer that did not lead directly to the thing he had failed to say. Hermione saw that too.

"Right," she said, reopening the book.

They worked until Madam Pince extinguished half the lamps. Snow brushed against the windows, and most students had left for dinner. Krum occupied his usual table near the foreign-language shelves, though he had not turned a page in nearly an hour. Whenever Hermione stood to retrieve another book, his gaze followed, moving away before she noticed.

Harry noticed. The awareness irritated him for no reason he was prepared to defend.

Hermione copied a section about acoustic concealment, her handwriting deteriorating line by line. When she reached for the ink, her fingers trembled. Harry closed her book before she could begin the next paragraph.

"That is becoming a habit," she said.

"You're tired."

"We have three months."

"You said that like it was plenty of time."

"It was before the egg rejected every known system of encoded sound."

Harry removed the quill from her hand. "Come on."

"Where?"

"Somewhere with food."

"Dinner ended twenty minutes ago."

"Hogwarts contains house-elves. Food continues existing."

Hermione glanced at the golden egg, still reluctant to surrender the evening to something as ordinary as supper.

"It will still be horrible tomorrow," Harry said.

That persuaded her. They locked their books inside a cabinet and descended towards the kitchens, leaving the egg secured with the notes. The castle had quietened, snow pressing white against the dark windows. Their footsteps alternated along the stairs until Harry deliberately changed pace and matched hers.

Hermione noticed.

"Your leg hurts."

"A little."

"You could have said."

"We were busy discussing your exhaustion."

"Those are separate matters."

"Convenient."

At the landing below the library, Hermione stopped beside a stone bench beneath the window.

"Sit."

"That is what Draco said."

"She often has good ideas."

The words carried something Harry could not identify. He sat, and Hermione knelt before him, lifting the edge of his trouser leg. The healing burn had reddened where his sock rubbed against it. She touched the skin beside the dressing, her fingers careful and cool.

Harry looked down at her bent head.

"You don't have to do that."

Her hands paused. "You said the same thing to her."

It was not a question.

"She told you?"

"No. I saw the salve."

Harry leaned back against the window recess. "She was hurt because of me."

"So are you."

"That's different."

Hermione looked up. "Why?"

He recognised the question. He had asked Draconia the same thing in the empty classroom and had no better answer now that it had been turned on him.

Hermione opened the small healing kit she carried and replaced the edge of the dressing. Her fingers moved tentatively, but with such care that Harry scarcely felt the bandage settle against his skin.

"Rita was wrong," he said.

"About several things."

"You aren't overshadowed."

Hermione concentrated on fastening the bandage. "You said that already."

"I don't think you believed me."

She sat back on her heels. Moonlight and snow turned the landing colourless around them. Away from the library's books and the Great Hall's noise, Hermione looked younger and more tired. Ink stained the side of her hand, and a shallow line marked the bridge of her nose where reading glasses rested during long evenings, though she refused to admit she needed them.

Harry reached down and rubbed the ink with his thumb. It did not come away. Hermione watched him try, her hand resting loosely against his.

"You are the first person I look for," he said.

Her breath changed. Harry continued because stopping would leave the sentence exposed between them, and because he had meant it even if he had not planned to say it.

"When something doesn't make sense, I look for you. When I wake up and don't know where I am, I know what you'd tell me to count. When the dragon—" He swallowed. "You were in the stands, and I knew what to do because I knew what you would say."

Hermione's eyes shone. "Harry."

"That doesn't feel overshadowed."

For a moment, she remained kneeling before him, one hand resting lightly beside his injured leg while his thumb stayed against the ink on the other. Somewhere below them, a door closed, but the sound seemed distant. Harry thought of the Ball: Hermione beneath floating candles, her hand in his, hearing her name asked rather than included as an afterthought.

He could do it now. He wanted to.

"Would you—"

She looked at him with hope too clear to misunderstand. Harry saw Draconia against the trophy cabinet, waiting in much the same way, and the question divided in his throat.

"—want to go down to the kitchens before they stop serving anything warm?"

The hope disappeared. Hermione stood, carefully folding the healing kit before putting it away.

"Yes," she said. "That would be sensible."

Harry rose more slowly. He knew he had done something wrong and could not see how to repair it without asking the question he had just refused. They continued downstairs with less to say than before.

At the kitchen corridor, Krum waited beside the painting of the fruit bowl. Harry stopped, and Hermione did too. Krum's expression suggested he would rather face another dragon than explain why he was there. Harry disliked him less for that.

"Hermy-own-ninny," Krum began.

"Hermione," she corrected automatically.

"Hermione." He said it carefully. "Could I speak vith you?"

His eyes moved to Harry, making the rest of the request clear. Harry looked at Hermione. She had folded both hands before her, and the disappointment from the landing had not completely left her face.

"I'll get the food," he said.

"Harry—"

"It's all right."

He tickled the pear and entered the kitchens alone. The portrait swung closed behind him, shutting away whatever Krum intended to ask.

The kitchens contained more food than Harry could carry and no useful place to avoid thinking. House-elves moved around him with silver trays, copper pans scrubbed themselves above deep sinks, and the four long tables corresponding to those in the Great Hall stood empty beneath drifting lamps. Their surfaces were already being prepared for breakfast, clean cloths appearing wherever an elf passed.

An elderly Hogwarts elf approached with a plate of sandwiches balanced above her head.

"Sir is wanting supper?"

Harry took the plate and thanked her. The elf stared at him, her long ears lifting.

"Sir Harry Potter is thanking Pipkin."

"You made it."

"Pipkin is making food because Pipkin likes making food."

"Then thank you for doing something you like."

That appeared to present a philosophical problem. Pipkin looked at the plate, then at Harry, and finally settled on a cautious smile. He thought of the documents spread across Sirius's kitchen table.

"Pipkin, do house-elves know when an old order has stopped being magic?"

The kitchen changed around him. Knives continued chopping and pans continued scrubbing themselves, but the nearest elves stopped speaking. Several pairs of enormous eyes turned towards Harry. Pipkin lowered her voice.

"Sir is asking about Winky."

"I know where she is. She's safe with Sirius."

Pipkin folded her hands into her tea towel. The tension eased by degrees: an elf beside the ovens resumed humming, and somewhere behind Harry a whisk began beating eggs again.

"Winky is speaking to Dobby sometimes," Pipkin said. "Not much. Dobby is carrying things to Hawthorn Cottage. Tea. Blankets. Socks Winky says she is not wanting."

"Dobby sends everyone socks."

Pipkin nodded gravely. "Dobby believes feet is an emergency."

Harry crouched, ignoring the protest from his ribs so he could speak without Pipkin craning her neck.

"Winky was dismissed. If Mr Crouch ordered her not to talk before that, could the order still stop her now?"

Her ears lowered. "Old family magic is ending when an elf is dismissed."

"Then why can't she speak?"

Pipkin twisted the tea towel once.

"A command can live in magic," she said. "A command can also live in the elf."

Harry went still. Draconia's voice returned to him: my hand held the wand. Winky's old household bond and Riddle's possession were different kinds of magic, yet he recognised the guilt that remained after both were gone.

"Has she told you anything?"

"Winky says Winky is bad. Winky says Winky failed her family. Winky will not speak of young master."

"Tell her she isn't in trouble with me."

Pipkin studied him. "Winky believes she is in trouble with everyone."

"Then tell her I know what that feels like."

The elf's grip loosened on the towel. "Pipkin will tell her."

The portrait door opened behind Harry. Hermione entered alone, and Pipkin lifted the sandwich plate back into his hands before returning to the ovens. Harry stood, looking past Hermione towards the doorway until it closed.

"What did Krum want?"

Directness seemed safer than pretending. Hermione looked at the trays rather than at him.

"He asked me to the Ball."

The answer hurt in a place Harry had not prepared.

"Oh."

"I told him I would think about it."

She waited. Harry could still ask; the possibility remained between them, painfully clear now that she had made a point of leaving it open. Hermione had not said yes. She was looking at him as she had on the landing, before he turned his invitation into warm food.

He imagined saying the words. Then he imagined telling Draconia.

"Krum seems all right," he said.

Hermione's shoulders lowered. "Yes. He does."

An elf offered her a mug of tea, which she accepted with both hands. Harry looked away. The opportunity closed so quietly that he almost convinced himself it had never existed.

He divided the sandwiches between two plates.

"There's something else."

Hermione looked over the rim of her tea. He told her what Pipkin had said, and disappointment gave way to concentration, though he could still see its traces. She listened without interrupting until he repeated Winky's refusal to speak about her young master.

"She may know who cast the Dark Mark, what happened inside the Crouch tent and why Crouch disappeared," Hermione said.

"She might."

"We should speak to her when we visit Hawthorn Cottage."

Harry hesitated, and she leaned towards him.

"Harry, if she knows something about the person who entered you—"

"Sirius and Remus already asked. She couldn't answer."

Hermione looked back at him. Harry remembered Draconia's hand shaking inside his and Winky protecting a tea towel as though it were the only reason anyone would let her stay.

"She thinks she failed everyone," he said. "If the three of us arrive and start asking the same questions again, we prove that being rescued only gave more people access to her."

Hermione lowered the mug. Her urgency did not leave, but she looked towards Pipkin before answering, considering what Harry had told her rather than only what it might reveal.

"We tell Sirius and Remus what Pipkin said about commands surviving in memory," she said. "And Draconia."

"Yes."

"No one from the Ministry."

"Definitely not Percy."

"He would report it to Crouch."

Hermione tore a clean strip from the bottom of a sheet of parchment and borrowed Harry's quill. She wrote one line before folding it twice.

"Would Pipkin send that with the next parcel?" Harry asked.

Hermione held up the note.

You do not owe us answers before you deserve help.

Pipkin accepted it with both hands.

"Winky will hear," she promised.

Harry and Hermione carried their plates into the corridor, holding the pear painting open long enough to hear the promise before it closed.

Blaise Zabini asked Draconia the next afternoon. He did it properly, and Harry knew because he was present for every unbearable second.

The trio had left Herbology arguing about whether water could alter an enchantment carried through sound. Snow covered the path to the castle. Hermione walked ahead with her scarf over her mouth, while Harry and Draconia followed more slowly, choosing their steps around patches of ice.

Blaise waited beneath the stone arch. He was tall for fourteen, impeccably dressed and composed in a way that seemed to require none of the effort Draconia put into it. His mother belonged to the same old circles as Narcissa. Harry knew him from the Slytherin table and shared classes, although Blaise rarely joined the louder cruelty of the House.

"Draconia," he said.

Her full name settled easily in his voice, without the clipped familiarity of Draco or the distance of Malfoy.

"Blaise."

He held a narrow black box. Draconia looked at it, then at him.

"You have acquired confidence."

"I borrowed some."

"From whom?"

"No one who required it today."

Harry disliked that he was funny. Blaise opened the box, revealing a corsage of small white flowers and silver leaves. The petals moved faintly despite the still air beneath the arch, a charm keeping them open against the cold.

"Would you attend the Yule Ball with me?"

There was no panic, no discussion of eggs, dancing lessons or heated metal. He had asked a question that could be answered without anyone first having to decide what it meant.

Draconia looked at Harry. Her eyes held his for one second, then two, giving him time to say something if he wanted to. He remembered the cabinet, her hand on his shoulder and the way she had said his name. He remembered Hermione telling him Krum had asked, leaving the answer unfinished while she waited for him.

The two choices pressed against one another until neither could move. Harry smiled because Draconia was watching and he did not know how to conceal everything else.

"You should go."

He saw the words hurt her before she turned back to Blaise.

"Yes," she said. "I will."

Blaise's relief appeared only in the slight movement of his mouth. He removed the corsage and offered it to her rather than fastening it himself. Draconia accepted the flowers, holding them carefully by the silver leaves.

"Thank you."

"I'll write regarding the details."

"Naturally."

Blaise inclined his head to Harry.

"Potter."

"Zabini."

He left beneath the arch. Farther up the path, Hermione had stopped, her gaze moving from the flowers in Draconia's hand to Harry's face.

Draconia placed the corsage inside her bag.

"We should continue."

They walked to the castle. No one resumed the argument about water.

Hermione accepted Krum's invitation that evening and told them during dinner. Harry sat between her and the aisle at the Gryffindor table. Draconia had joined them with Snape's reluctant permission, though the white flowers remained absent from her robes.

"Viktor asked whether I had decided," Hermione said. "I told him yes."

Harry put down his fork. "Right."

Draconia looked at Hermione. "You are attending with Krum."

"Yes."

"Do you like him?"

Hermione's cheeks coloured. "I barely know him."

"That was not the question."

"He is thoughtful. Quieter than people assume. He asked about my research rather than Harry."

The final words escaped before she could soften them. Harry felt them land, although he knew she had not meant to hurt him. He had watched enough people approach her with questions that were really about him to understand why the difference mattered.

Hermione looked at him. "I didn't mean—"

"It's fine."

The banned phrase. All three recognised it, and Harry picked up his fork rather than leave his hands empty under their attention.

"You should go with him."

"I am."

Across the table, Draconia's fingers rested on the strap of the bag where she had hidden the corsage.

"And you're going with Blaise," Hermione said.

"Yes."

"Do you like him?"

Draconia understood the returned question.

"I have known Blaise since childhood. He is intelligent, tolerable and unlikely to embarrass me in public."

"A sweeping romance," Harry said.

Her gaze cut towards him. "He asked."

Harry heard the accusation. Hermione did too. They sat within the noise of the Great Hall, closer than most people and suddenly unable to cross a few inches of table.

"Who are you taking?" Hermione asked.

Harry looked at his plate. "I haven't asked anyone."

Draconia's expression changed. "No one?"

"No."

For a dangerous moment, hope returned to both girls. Harry could see it, which made the relief he felt a moment later even harder to bear. They had accepted other invitations. The choice had been removed from him; he could no longer hurt one by asking the other.

He had also lost both.

"There's time," he said.

Hermione looked away first. Draconia took the corsage from her bag and set it beside her plate, the white flowers opening beneath the candlelight.

Harry stood. "I need to collect the egg."

He left the Great Hall without looking back.

For several minutes, neither girl spoke. Hermione watched the doors after Harry disappeared through them, then looked down at the fork he had left beside his plate. Around them, students continued eating, cutlery scraping against china. Winter clouds crossed the enchanted ceiling above the hundreds of candles burning beneath it.

Draconia touched one silver leaf on the corsage.

"Did he nearly ask you?"

Hermione looked up. There was no accusation in Draconia's face, which made dishonesty harder.

"Twice," she said.

Draconia's fingers stopped. "Twice."

"The first became a question about heating the egg. The second became an invitation to the kitchens."

"Impressive."

"And you?"

Draconia looked at the flowers. "He asked me to teach you how to dance."

Hermione's breath escaped in something dangerously close to laughter. Draconia's eyes narrowed.

"I apologise," Hermione said.

"You do not."

"Not entirely."

Against her will, Draconia's mouth moved. The humour lasted only a moment before Hermione glanced towards the doors again.

"He nearly asked you during the lesson, didn't he?"

"How do you know?"

"Because he began asking me before it. Afterwards, he could barely look at either of us."

Draconia considered that, turning the corsage box beneath her fingers.

"He said your name."

"When?"

"We were dancing. He nearly asked. Then he stepped away and asked whether I could teach Hermione."

The use of her name mattered. Hermione heard it as Draconia intended, with the awkward admission beneath it.

"He thought of me."

"Yes."

"While he was holding you."

"That is an unnecessarily specific way to describe it."

"Is it inaccurate?"

Draconia straightened the box. "No."

Jealousy moved through Hermione, swift and ugly. She pictured Harry's hand at Draconia's waist, their faces close beneath the moonlit trophy cases, and hated how easily she could imagine it. Then she remembered his thumb moving over the ink on her hand and the way he had said she was the first person he searched for.

Neither memory erased the other. Looking at Draconia across the table, Hermione began to suspect that this was precisely what had stopped him speaking.

"You wanted him to ask you," she said.

Draconia's composure returned. "So did you."

"I answered first."

"That does not grant strategic advantage."

"This is not a strategy."

Draconia gave her a look. Hermione conceded the point through silence, unwilling to examine how carefully she had noticed every invitation Harry had failed to finish.

"I am not withdrawing from the Ball," Draconia said. "Blaise asked respectfully. He does not deserve to be discarded because Potter requires a month to complete a sentence."

"Neither does Viktor."

"Then we agree."

Hermione folded her arms on the table. "I don't want this to become a competition."

Draconia looked surprised. "You dislike losing."

"So do you."

"I rarely experience it."

"Draco."

The surprise faded. Draconia looked down at her wrist, where the burn Harry had treated was diminishing beneath a pale coating of salve.

"Neither do I," she said eventually.

Hermione understood the answer beneath it. Neither wanted Harry to become something won at the other's expense. Imagining an evening with him was easy; imagining the third person left outside it was what made everything hurt. They did not yet have language for whatever possibility remained.

"This is going to become impossible," Hermione said.

Draconia closed the corsage box. "We have managed impossible before."

"Usually with more books."

"Then perhaps you should order some."

Hermione laughed. Draconia stood and lifted Harry's abandoned water glass from beside his plate.

"He has not drunk enough."

"I counted two glasses."

"That was his second."

"It is half full."

"Exactly."

They left the Great Hall together, carrying the water Harry had forgotten and an understanding neither intended to discuss where he could hear it.

The Ball entered every part of the castle. Dress catalogues appeared inside textbooks, invitations were negotiated between lessons, and music drifted from unused classrooms as older students practised. Most claimed to have entered for unrelated reasons. The suits of armour learned three new carols and performed all of them simultaneously.

Harry discovered that opportunities had become more numerous now that the two he wanted were unavailable. A Ravenclaw third-year asked him beside the Owlery. A fifth-year Hufflepuff sent a card that sang until he trapped it beneath a pillow. Two Gryffindor girls approached together, argued over which had intended to speak first and left without either completing the question.

He declined whenever refusal became necessary, trying to do it kindly. That made no one happier.

"You need a partner," Dean reminded him at breakfast.

Harry watched Krum place a book beside Hermione's plate. It was an early edition of Numerological Principles in Defensive Spellcraft, published in Bulgarian and English on facing pages. Hermione touched the worn leather cover as though it were fragile, and Krum said something that made her smile.

Harry looked down at his porridge. "I know."

At the Slytherin table, Blaise handed Draconia an envelope. She broke the dark green seal and read the enclosed list. Harry could not make out the words from here, but he had already heard enough about dress colours, arrangements and what somebody's mother insisted upon to imagine its contents. Blaise leaned closer to hear her answer.

Harry's spoon struck the bowl.

"You all right?" Dean asked.

"Porridge attacked me."

"Danger follows you."

Draconia glanced across the Hall, and their eyes met. Harry looked away first.

The trio continued working together, which somehow made the change worse. Hermione still waited outside Gryffindor Tower, Draconia still appeared after lessons, and they studied, argued and divided responsibility for Harry's healing dressings with the efficiency of a medical committee. But every mention of Christmas introduced a pause none of them knew how to cross.

Krum joined Hermione in the library twice. Blaise walked Draconia from dinner to the dungeon stairs. Harry disliked both developments and became excessively fair about them.

"He seems thoughtful," he said after Krum left.

Hermione looked disappointed by his approval.

"Blaise seems tolerable," he told Draconia.

She was offended for the rest of the afternoon. Harry began to suspect fairness was not helping.

Cedric found him on the covered bridge. Snow moved across the valley below, and the wooden roof creaked whenever wind entered through the open sides. Harry had chosen the spot because the cold discouraged conversation. Cedric ignored the discouragement.

"Hiding?"

"Enjoying the view."

"Of the wall?"

Harry turned from the stone pillar he had been studying. Cedric wore a yellow scarf and the expression of someone attempting not to smile.

"What?" Harry asked.

"I heard you still haven't asked anyone."

"Does the Tournament publish updates?"

"Hogwarts does."

Cedric leaned against the rail, brushing snow away before resting his elbows on it.

"You know you don't need to ask perfectly."

"I can't dance perfectly either. Apparently, standards are collapsing."

"I mean it. I asked Cho outside the greenhouses while carrying dragon-dung compost. She still said yes."

Harry looked at him. "You're taking Cho Chang?"

"That isn't the important part."

It felt moderately important. Cho was clever, kind and one of the best Seekers Harry had flown against. He remembered once thinking that her smile could make him forget whatever sentence he intended to say. Now the news produced only surprise. Apparently, his confusion had become highly specific.

"I tried to ask someone," he admitted.

"What happened?"

"I asked whether the egg should be heated."

Cedric stared. "Was that a metaphor?"

"No."

"Right."

Harry watched snow collect along the railing, filling the small grooves in the wood.

"Then I tried to ask someone else."

Cedric's eyebrows rose. "Someone else."

"I asked her to teach the first person how to dance."

Cedric turned fully towards him. "You fought a dragon."

"People keep saying that as though it establishes general competence."

"Harry, how many people do you want to take to the Ball?"

Harry rubbed both hands over his face, feeling the cold in his fingertips.

"I don't know."

"That sounded like two."

"They're going with other people now."

"Both of them?"

Harry gave him a look. Understanding arrived, and Cedric stopped trying not to smile.

"Oh."

"Helpful."

"I'm thinking."

"Everyone does that around me. It rarely improves things."

Cedric remained quiet for several seconds, looking out across the valley before speaking again.

"Did you want to ask both because you couldn't decide which one you liked?"

Harry followed his gaze. The castle grounds blurred beneath the falling snow, making it easier to answer without looking at him.

"I didn't want either of them to think I chose the other."

"That isn't quite the same thing."

"It feels the same."

"It probably doesn't to them."

That was the problem. Harry had been trying to avoid making either girl feel left behind, and both now seemed hurt by the care he had taken.

Cedric adjusted his scarf. "You could tell them."

"They already have partners."

"The Ball isn't a marriage contract."

"You have no idea what Draconia's mother considers binding."

Cedric laughed. Harry's mouth moved despite himself, and for a moment the conversation became easier.

"Ask someone you want to spend the evening with," Cedric said. "Not someone who makes the photograph look right. And perhaps don't discuss heated eggs first."

He left Harry on the bridge. The advice was sensible, which Harry distrusted immediately.

Sirius called through the mirror that night. Harry had not contacted him; the glass brightened while he lay behind closed curtains, listening to the other boys discuss Ball partners. Neville had asked Ginny Weasley and been gently refused because she had already accepted an invitation from a Ravenclaw. Dean intended to ask Parvati the following morning. Seamus claimed to have three possibilities and no fear.

Harry suspected one of those statements was true.

He opened the mirror. Sirius appeared beneath the light of the cottage kitchen, a bowl of something burned sitting behind him.

"Why are you miserable?"

"Good evening."

"It was until your face appeared."

"You called me."

"And discovered a crisis." Sirius leaned closer. "Did the burn worsen?"

"No."

"Contract?"

"Quiet."

"Nightmares?"

"Not last night."

Sirius's suspicion deepened. "What happened?"

Harry lowered his voice, turning the mirror away from the gap between the curtains.

"The Yule Ball."

Sirius stared. Then his entire face relaxed.

"Oh, thank God."

"Glad you're enjoying it."

"I thought someone had attempted to murder you."

"Not yet."

"What's the problem? Ask someone."

Harry considered closing the mirror. "I tried."

"And?"

"One thinks I wanted her to teach the other person how to dance. The other thinks I care more about warm food than taking her."

Sirius's expression emptied. "How?"

"It was complicated."

"It sounds astonishingly efficient."

"They both have dates now."

"Ah."

"Cedric said that."

"Diggory is perceptive."

Harry picked at a loose thread in his blanket. Saying the names made the situation sound more settled than he wanted it to be.

"Hermione is going with Krum. Draconia is going with Blaise Zabini."

Sirius's eyebrows rose. "Narcissa's Draconia?"

"How many do you know?"

"One was already plenty."

Harry waited. Sirius's amusement diminished as he read whatever remained visible on Harry's face.

"You wanted to ask both."

It was not a question. Harry's fingers stopped on the thread.

"I couldn't ask both."

"Why not?"

"Because that isn't how it works."

"According to whom?"

"Everyone."

"Everyone imprisoned me for twelve years. I find their judgement inconsistent."

Harry almost smiled.

"They would think I was taking advantage."

"Would Hermione and Draconia think that?"

"Probably."

"Did you ask?"

Harry looked away. Sirius sighed, the sound carrying clearly through the small mirror.

"You thought not choosing would preserve what you had."

Harry looked back. "Yes."

"But not choosing was still a choice."

The truth of it hurt. Harry had waited until Krum and Blaise provided the clarity he was afraid to create, then told both girls to go. He had tried to protect the trio from change and changed it through silence instead.

"They looked happy when they were asked," he said.

"That does not mean they would have refused you."

"It means they deserve to go with people who can finish a sentence."

Sirius gave him a long look. "You're allowed to be fourteen, Harry."

"I'm a champion when they want me to fight dragons."

"And a child when you're trying to understand love."

The word entered Harry's bed curtains and became too large for the enclosed space. He heard someone laugh beyond them, then the scrape of a trunk being shut, ordinary sounds carrying on around something that suddenly felt much less ordinary.

"I didn't say love."

"No." Sirius's voice gentled. "You rarely say the frightening part first."

Harry's scar prickled without pain. He turned the loose thread around one finger, then carefully unwound it.

"What am I supposed to do now?"

"Tell the truth when you can. Respect the choices they made. Ask someone whose company you actually want rather than treating another girl as consolation."

"That is annoyingly responsible."

"Remus helped."

"I did not," Remus called from somewhere beyond the mirror.

Sirius ignored him. "And Harry?"

"What?"

"One Ball does not decide the rest of your life."

Harry thought of Hermione laughing over the golden egg, Draconia's hand in his beneath the trophy-room moonlight, Krum's book and Blaise's white flowers.

"It feels like it changed something."

"Then find out what. Afterwards. Not by ruining their evening."

Harry nodded. Sirius remained until the dormitory fell silent, letting the conversation move away from the Ball without pretending it had been solved. Before ending the call, he asked Harry to promise he would not attend alone merely to punish himself.

Harry refused to promise and agreed to try. Sirius accepted the distinction.

The next evening, the trio returned to the egg. They occupied the Charms classroom with their usual books, notes and three mugs of tea. Hermione sat on Harry's left and Draconia on his right. The arrangement had not changed, although he could no longer settle into it without noticing everything they were careful not to say.

Harry opened the shell. The terrible wailing filled the room, setting the tea trembling inside their cups. Hermione raised the recording charm, Draconia adjusted the resonance spell, and Harry listened for language beneath the noise. Beyond the windows, snow moved silently through the dark.

After thirty seconds, he closed the egg. Silence rushed back, leaving the three of them looking at the same useless lines of notes.

"Nothing," Hermione said.

"Again tomorrow," Draconia replied.

They began collecting the books. Harry watched them move around each other: Hermione passing Draconia the notes she had missed, Draconia fastening Hermione's overfilled bag before the clasp could break. Their care continued beneath every complication, quiet and practised, and he wanted to tell them what he should have said before either invitation arrived.

He did not need them to change their plans or give him an answer. He only wanted to explain that he had not failed to ask because he wanted neither of them.

Harry opened his mouth. Hermione looked at him, and Draconia waited. He could hear Sirius's advice, remember how plainly it had been given, and still persuade himself that this was the wrong moment.

"We should try water tomorrow," he said.

Hermione considered the egg. "Sound does travel differently underwater."

Draconia added the idea to their list. The moment passed while her quill moved across the parchment.

At the second-floor landing, Hermione turned towards the library to return their borrowed books. Draconia took the staircase leading down to the dungeons. Each said goodnight with such careful normality that Harry almost asked them to stay, but their footsteps were already fading before he found the words.

He carried the golden egg back towards Gryffindor Tower alone. It was heavy beneath his arm, full of a message transformed into screaming because he was hearing it in the wrong place.

For the first time, Harry suspected the egg was not the only one.
