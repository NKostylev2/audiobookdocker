One arm of Harry's glasses had bent beneath the headstone. Draconia noticed it before the broken lens, before the green light passed over the graves, and fixed on it with the desperate certainty that something so small ought to be repairable.

The glasses lay half hidden in the grass, close enough for her to pick up. Harry was nowhere she could see. She knew he would not have left them there. He complained when they slid down his nose, cleaned them upon shirts her mother disapproved of and pushed them crooked whenever he wanted to make Hermione laugh. Without them, he would be almost helpless in the dark. She crouched to pick them up. The grass closed around her fingers.

Ink rose through it, black and warm, filling the lines of her palm. She pulled back, but the ground held her. Beyond the headstone, something scraped over stone. A shape passed between two monuments, too low and too long to be a person, and Draconia knew the Chamber was beneath the graveyard even before the earth gave way.

"Harry?"

Her voice travelled down into the opening. He answered from somewhere she could not see.

"Here."

She dragged one hand free and reached towards him. Wet stone replaced the grass. The pillars were there now, carved serpents watching her from above, and a school robe lay upon the floor where the passage narrowed. Pale hair spread across its collar. For a moment she recognised herself, and the shock made her turn away. When she looked back, the hair was black.

Harry lay upon his side, his arm extended towards her. Blood had dried beneath his nose. The branching marks she had seen in the hospital wing climbed beyond his torn collar and disappeared under his jaw. His glasses remained in her hand. She had them; she could put them back; there was something ordinary she could do before anything else became necessary.

She knelt beside him, the stone cold through her nightclothes. She wanted to turn his face towards her, to find the warmth of his breath against her fingers, but the glasses needed putting right first. If she could manage that, perhaps she could manage the rest.

"Potter. You are making this unnecessarily difficult."

His mouth moved, barely disturbing the dried blood beneath his nose. Draconia bent close enough to hear, holding her own breath so that she would not lose a word.

"There you are."

Relief loosened her grip upon the glasses. She reached for his shoulder, already preparing to complain, but her hand passed through his robes and struck the floor beneath them. Harry's body blurred. She could see the stone through his chest.

"No."

The diary opened between them with the soft scrape of a cover against stone. She had not seen it fall. One moment her hand had been reaching through Harry's shoulder; the next it rested upon a page she knew she should never touch again.

Its pages were blank until she tried to close it. Then narrow handwriting appeared beneath her fingers, forming words she had heard in no real room and nevertheless recognised.

You let him go alone.

"I didn't know."

The pages turned. The hospital wing vanished from her memory, taking the proof of his return with it. She could remember the maze, the crowd waiting, the empty place where the Cup ought to have appeared. Nothing followed. She had spent weeks inventing a reunion because she could not bear the alternative. Harry looked up at her. His eyes were red. Draconia woke before he spoke.

The sloping ceiling emerged from the darkness first, followed by the thin line of morning light at the curtains. Draconia tried to move her foot and found the sheets twisted around one ankle. The edge of the bedside table pressed painfully into her wrist because she had reached towards it in her sleep. She remained still until her breathing no longer sounded like someone running. Beyond the window, a peacock gave an indignant cry. Lucius answered it from the garden.

"If you would move, this would be considerably easier."

Draconia closed her eyes again, then opened them deliberately. The bedside table stood where the headstone had been, and there was no diary upon it. Her wand lay upon the table, solid wood beside a glass of water. The damaged bracelet rested in its box, its cloudy stone sealed beneath the clear covering Bill had added. She had spoken to Harry the previous evening. She remembered his voice, the lamp in his bedroom and the way he had tried to conceal a yawn while insisting he was listening.

Today was the thirty-first of July. Harry would be fifteen, and by the time the breakfast dishes were cleared he ought to be on his way to Hawthorn. Draconia made herself picture the front door opening, his rucksack catching on the umbrella stand, Sirius complaining about whoever had left it there. She needed something more definite than the promise that he would be here. A knock touched the door.

"Draconia?"

"Come in."

Narcissa entered carrying a folded blouse over one arm. She stopped when she saw the bedclothes, and Draconia wished she had taken the time to straighten them. Her mother put the blouse upon the chair.

"A bad dream?"

Draconia nodded. Narcissa sat beside her without asking what had happened. For a little while neither spoke. Her mother's sleeve felt cool against Draconia's bare arm; a faint trace of rose soap reached her when Narcissa turned towards the window. Downstairs, something metal struck the floor, followed by Sirius protesting that his motorbike had contained that part yesterday.

"Is he ready to leave?" Draconia asked.

"He has been ready since six. Remus is persuading him that the journey does not require every weapon he owns."

"A difficult argument."

"Apparently the sidecar is full."

Draconia's mouth moved, almost a smile.

Narcissa took her hand. "Harry is coming home."

"I know."

"You can know that and still be frightened."

Draconia looked down at their joined hands. The dream had left no ink beneath her skin, although she could still feel it when she thought too carefully about her fingers.

"I thought he hadn't come back," she said. "That all the time afterwards was something I'd made up."

Narcissa's grip tightened slightly.

"I saw him yesterday. So did you."

"I know that too."

Her mother did not repeat the reassurance. She stayed until the room had become an ordinary room again, then rose to open the curtains. Sunlight reached the little stars upon the wallpaper and the clothes Draconia had laid out with more care than she intended to acknowledge.

"Hermione is downstairs," Narcissa said. "She has brought the birthday card for you to sign."

"She brought it yesterday."

"You corrected its punctuation and returned it unsigned."

"It was necessary."

"This morning, try beginning with your name."

Draconia reached for the green blouse. She smoothed the sleeves across her knees before standing, then picked up her wand and the bracelet. Downstairs, Hermione would be waiting with the card, and there were still things she could do before Harry came through the door.

Sirius Black arrived at Privet Drive two minutes early, upon a motorbike loud enough to announce him to the whole street. Harry heard the engine before he saw it. He had been standing beside the hall window with his rucksack over one shoulder and Hedwig's cage in his hand, watching a neighbour wash a car which had been clean yesterday. Now the water hose went unattended. Curtains shifted along both sides of the road as the black motorbike came towards number four, followed by a dark blue car whose driver looked resigned even through the windscreen.

"Absolutely not," Uncle Vernon said.

Harry looked at the helmet in the sidecar.

"You could try telling him."

"There was to be no spectacle."

"He's brought transport. That was in the agreement."

Vernon's gaze moved towards the kitchen. The parchment on the refrigerator remained silent, its letters pale gold now that the final night was complete. Bill had confirmed the renewal shortly after midnight. Harry knew nothing more about it than he had before: he had finished the required stay, Hawthorn's warning link worked, and nobody could send him upstairs to wait for another summer.

He had packed before breakfast, checking the desk and wardrobe twice even though the room had already begun to look as though he had never lived there. The photograph was wrapped between two shirts. His medicines waited in the rucksack where he could reach them, and the mirror rested in his pocket.

Petunia had placed toast and an egg upon his plate without being prompted. Dudley had asked whether the motorbike could fly. Harry had said that was one of its better qualities, which did nothing to improve Vernon's temper. Nobody mentioned his birthday. By then, Harry had received three cards, a parcel from Hagrid and a mirror call in which Sirius sang until Remus intervened, so the omission had felt smaller than it once would have. Petunia stood behind him now, holding a folded sheet of paper.

"Your uncle has written down the arrangements."

Harry glanced at the list. "He's misspelt motorcycle."

"I have not," Vernon snapped.

"You've written motor sickle."

Dudley turned towards the sitting room. His shoulders shook once. Petunia folded the paper again and set it upon the hall table. Her eyes lingered upon Harry's grey shirt, the rucksack and the ordinary brass key hanging at his collar. Yesterday she had told him that Lily once came home from school without her eyebrows and considered it funny. She had admitted, too quietly and too late, that the child in the cupboard had been a child. Harry still did not know what to do with either fact.

He could carry those admissions away without deciding what they changed. Petunia had waited years to say them; Harry did not have to find an answer in the few minutes before the doorbell rang. Outside, Sirius stopped the motorbike. He removed his glasses, looked towards the house and smiled when he found Harry at the window. Harry's hand tightened upon Hedwig's cage. The doorbell rang at nine. Vernon drew himself up as though receiving a complaint from an important customer. Petunia moved out of the way. Harry reached the handle before either could speak.

For a moment he could see the cupboard door reflected in the narrow glass beside him. Its paint was newer than the surrounding wall. A little brass hook remained where the bolt had once been fixed beyond his reach. His name still lay beneath two layers of paint inside, scratched with a broken toy when he was six. He turned the handle.

Morning air entered the hall, carrying the smell of the motorbike's exhaust and the wet pavement where the neighbour had abandoned his hose. Sirius stood upon the step with his helmet tucked beneath one arm. At the sight of Harry, his smile faltered, and for a moment all the things he had been trying to disguise with jokes showed plainly upon his face.

For one suspended moment, neither seemed to know which version of the reunion they had prepared. Sirius's hand lifted as though to inspect Harry for injuries. Harry tightened his grip upon Hedwig's cage. Remus waited several feet behind, giving the choice room. Harry set the cage down, crossed the threshold and put both arms around Sirius. Sirius caught him hard enough to lift one of Harry's heels from the ground.

The leather jacket smelled of wind, engine oil and the smoke from Hawthorn's kitchen fire. Harry pressed his face against Sirius's shoulder. Something in his chest, held rigid since the platform, released without becoming tears.

"Got you," Sirius said into his hair.

Harry nodded against his shoulder.

"You're late," Harry muttered into his jacket.

Sirius glanced towards the clock in the hall. "By four seconds."

"Appalling."

Sirius did not let go. Harry did not ask him to. Behind them, Vernon cleared his throat. Sirius's arms tightened once before opening. He turned without moving away from Harry.

"Vernon."

"Black."

Vernon attempted to place several accusations inside the surname. Sirius looked delighted to have received them. Remus climbed the step. He wore a light shirt beneath a brown coat too warm for the day and carried a narrow wooden case in one hand. New lines marked the corners of his mouth, but his smile remained familiar.

"Good morning," he said.

"There is nothing good about that machine," Vernon replied.

"I did remove its tyres."

"He put them back," Harry said.

Remus sighed. "He often does."

Sirius glanced over one shoulder. "Are you two finished discussing me as though I lack hearing?"

"Not remotely," Remus said.

Harry hugged him next. Remus made a quiet sound of surprise before returning it. He held Harry differently from Sirius, one hand between Harry's shoulders and the other briefly against the back of his head. Chocolate, old wool and parchment replaced smoke.

"Welcome back," Remus said.

Harry closed his eyes against Remus's shoulder. He was still standing on the Dursleys' doorstep, but hearing welcome back made the distance to Hawthorn seem suddenly smaller. Remus meant him, wherever the hug happened.

"Thanks."

When he stepped away, Petunia stood in the hall with her arms folded. Dudley occupied the sitting-room entrance. Vernon remained between everyone and the furniture as though expecting the visitors to steal it. Remus opened the wooden case. Inside lay a silver instrument, a copy of the conduct agreement and one small crystal vial filled with gold light.

"Final boundary confirmation," he explained. "Then we leave."

"Finally," Vernon said.

Sirius looked at him. The air sharpened. Harry touched Sirius's sleeve before the look could become an argument. He wanted to leave; he did not want his last morning here spent waiting while Vernon shouted and Sirius gave him a reason to go on. Sirius glanced down at his hand and understood. He drew one slow breath and faced Harry instead.

"Ready?"

Harry looked inside number four. Morning light reached the polished hall table. Family photographs showed Dudley opening presents, riding a bicycle, wearing a school uniform and filling every available frame. The cupboard door remained closed beneath the stairs. At the far end of the hall, the agreement had begun to peel itself from the refrigerator. Petunia's eyes met Harry's.

He thought of Lily without eyebrows. A six-year-old scratching his name beneath paint. The clean glass of water waiting beside a lamp because external witnesses had made neglect dangerous.

"The number stays beside the telephone," Harry said.

Petunia nodded.

"Keep the curtains closed after dark," Remus added. "Mrs Figg will contact you if the risk changes."

Vernon puffed himself up. "We will not be taking instructions—"

"You will," Petunia said.

Everyone looked at her. She smoothed the front of her dress.

"If it concerns security."

Sirius studied her without gratitude. Harry was glad. Petunia did not deserve praise for becoming cautious after the cost had moved elsewhere. Remus raised the crystal vial. The gold light inside divided into two threads. One travelled towards Harry; the other passed through the open doorway and sank beneath the path.

"Cross when you're ready."

Harry lifted Hedwig's cage. Sirius took it from him before he could object. Remus collected the trunk. Harry kept only the rucksack and the key. He stepped through the door. The boundary recognised him.

Warmth travelled over his skin, gentle as sunlight. Gold appeared for an instant along the edges of the path and beneath the hedges. It crossed the brass key, passed through Harry's hand and continued towards a house miles away. Behind him, the conduct agreement released one clear note. The parchment folded itself into a white bird. It flew from the kitchen, crossed the hall above Vernon's head and passed through the doorway. Upon reaching Harry, it burst into colourless sparks.

Harry waited for a moment after the sparks faded. He had expected a pull beneath his ribs, or some last demand to turn around and go back inside. Nothing came. Number four stood behind him with its polished windows and clipped hedges, a house he had finally been allowed to walk away from.

"Harry," Dudley said.

Harry turned. His cousin had moved into the doorway beside Petunia. Vernon stood a pace behind them, purple with suppressed objection. Dudley lifted one hand. The gesture could have been a wave or an attempt abandoned halfway.

"Bye."

Harry adjusted the strap upon his shoulder.

"Bye, Dudley."

Petunia's lips parted. No words followed. Harry gave her the smallest nod. Then Sirius handed Hedwig to Remus, seized the motorbike by its handlebars and produced a helmet from the sidecar.

"You are not taking the boy on that," Vernon said.

Sirius tossed the helmet to Harry.

"What boy?"

Harry caught it.

"He means me."

"You look perfectly reckless to me."

"I've had professional instruction."

Remus placed Harry's trunk inside the motorcar.

"Every word of this conversation will be used against me," he said.

Harry put on the helmet. He climbed behind Sirius, fastened both hands around the leather jacket and looked back once. The Dursleys stood beneath the white doorframe of number four. Three people arranged inside a life which would continue without him.

An unexpected grief caught him as he looked back. He had no wish to climb down and return to the hall, but he remembered how long he had once waited for a birthday to be noticed or a school drawing to find its way onto the refrigerator. That child had believed that being good enough, or quiet enough, might eventually make him wanted. Leaving meant admitting how much of his childhood had gone into waiting. Sirius started the engine. He kept looking ahead as the motorbike moved, until the turn carried number four out of sight. They kept to Muggle roads until Little Whinging disappeared behind them.

Sirius obeyed the speed limit for nearly eleven minutes. Harry watched neat housing estates become shops, roundabouts and stretches of sunburnt grass. Wind pressed his shirt against the healing cuts along his chest. The motorbike's vibration travelled through his ribs, shaking loose the last quiet of Privet Drive. Remus followed in the blue car with Hedwig glaring through the rear window. At the edge of a long, empty road, Sirius tapped the fuel tank twice.

The mirrors folded inward with two small clicks. A shimmer passed across the handlebars, spread over Sirius's jacket and closed around Harry until he could see the road through the place where his knees ought to have been. He tightened his grip before remembering that the concealment changed what people could see, not what he could hold.

"Sirius."

"Yes?"

"Remus said no flying."

"This is not flying."

The front wheel left the road.

"It resembles flying."

"Aggressive levitation."

They rose above the hedges. Harry's stomach dropped with the road. The bike climbed into clear air, invisible except for the space it disturbed. Fields opened beneath them in green and gold squares. The blue motorcar diminished behind, its horn producing one long, furious note which the wind tore apart.

Sirius laughed, his shoulders shaking beneath Harry's hands, and the sound carried back through the rush of air. Harry began laughing too. For once he did not have to force it past fear or make a joke before anyone noticed that his hands were shaking. The air was warm, the road no longer touched them and a house with an ash tree waited ahead. Harry leaned with Sirius as the bike turned. His grip remained firm without being desperate.

For several miles, he watched the fields without searching the hedges for movement or wondering what waited out of sight. The wind brought water to his eyes, and he blinked it away, following the narrow lanes as they curved between farms. He had missed being able to look down at the world and simply enjoy how fast it passed. Sirius levelled the motorbike beneath the clouds.

"All right?" he shouted.

Harry looked across the fields.

"Faster!"

The engine answered. They reached Hawthorn well ahead of Remus, though Harry did not yet know how much explaining that would require. Sirius brought the motorbike down beyond the village, letting its tyres touch the lane before he eased them towards the cottage. The cottage appeared at the end of a narrow lane bordered by hedges which had never encountered a straight line.

Its roof sloped in three directions. Chimneys rose at uncertain angles above dark stone walls, and flowering vines had overtaken half the front windows despite Narcissa's attempts to identify them. The ash tree stood beyond the eastern corner, its branches spread wide enough to shadow the blue room upon the first floor. Two white peacocks occupied the front path.

Neither moved when Sirius approached. He stopped the motorbike short of the gate, and the larger bird opened its tail directly in front of them, its white feathers spreading until there was scarcely any path left to see. Sirius removed his glasses.

"I live here."

The peacock continued displaying.

"He knows," Harry said.

"Lucius trained them to do this."

"I don't think he did."

"Then they inherited it."

Harry climbed off the bike. The ground felt briefly unreliable after the flight. He removed the helmet and stood beside the gate. Hawthorn's wards reached him before he touched it.

Cool pressure passed over his face. The brass key pulled gently beneath his shirt. Runes hidden within the ironwork lit one after another, travelling from the hinge through tangled hawthorn branches worked into the metal. Harry drew the key. The gate possessed no keyhole, but it opened anyway. The peacocks stepped aside with evident reluctance. Sirius waited while Harry walked first.

The path curved towards the cottage between overgrown lavender and several holes Dobby insisted were part of a defensive garden. A blue mug waited upon the kitchen sill. Someone had hung washing between two trees: Sirius's black shirts, Remus's patched robes, Narcissa's pale linens and one pair of Lucius's socks positioned as far from the others as the line allowed. Voices moved behind the front door. Harry stopped upon the step.

The key had opened this door once before. During the previous summer, Sirius had placed it in Harry's palm and said the cottage would remain his whether he slept there for one night or every night thereafter. Harry had believed him as much as he knew how. The Dursleys had taught him that belonging could be revoked without notice. Hogwarts disappeared each summer. Safety always possessed an ending date. Returning to Privet Drive had tested the promise. The key turned in the lock without resistance, and Harry opened the door.

Harry had scarcely cleared the door before Hawthorn erupted around him. Someone called his name from the kitchen, a chair scraped across the floor, and Dobby appeared beneath the hall table before anyone else could reach him.

The elf launched from beneath the hall table and struck Harry around the knees with enough force to nearly return him to the path. A second figure in a knitted tea cosy collided with Dobby from the side. Winky seized Harry's left leg while declaring that young masters should not remain outside after arriving home.

"I was outside for four seconds."

"Four seconds is being four too many," Dobby said into his trousers.

Hermione stood beyond them, one hand over her mouth and tears already bright in her eyes. Draconia occupied the bottom stair. Narcissa waited behind her. Lucius stood near the sitting-room entrance with a cane in one hand, his expression composed except for the relief visible around it. The hallway held half the household and enough luggage to make movement theoretical. Harry looked at Hermione.

"You brought documentation, didn't you?"

She crossed the distance and threw her arms around him. The impact drove a laugh from his chest. Harry held her with one arm while Dobby maintained possession of his knees. Hermione's hair caught against his face. She smelled of parchment, soap and the familiar peppermint toothpaste from her parents' house.

"You're here," she said.

"I said I'd come."

"Yes, well." Her arms tightened before she let him go. "You're here now."

"Even Sirius turned up on time. I think he's afraid of you."

She pulled back only far enough to inspect him. Her fingers straightened his collar by instinct.

"You flew."

Harry looked towards Sirius.

"Aggressively levitated."

"Harry."

"I wore a helmet."

"That is not the issue."

"It is often considered one of them."

Hermione tried to remain angry, but her eyes kept returning to his face as though she needed to check he was really standing there. The corner of her mouth lifted despite her best efforts, and she gave his collar one final, unnecessary tug. Harry turned towards Draconia. She had not moved from the stair.

Her hair fell loose over one shoulder, held away from her face by a narrow silver clasp. The repaired bracelet circled her wrist. She wore dark trousers and a green blouse with the sleeves folded to her elbows. She looked exhausted. Harry noticed it as soon as her gaze met his, beneath all the care she had taken to look as though this was an ordinary morning.

"You wrote come home," Harry said.

Draconia lifted her chin. "You took an unreasonable amount of time."

"There was a magical requirement."

"Excuses."

Harry stepped around the house-elves. Draconia descended the final stair. For one second, they faced one another with too much already acknowledged and nothing decided. Harry remembered her hand against the mirror. Draconia looked towards the collar hiding his scars and then deliberately returned her gaze to his face.

Harry held out his hand rather than attempting another joke. Draconia took it, her fingers cool against his, and came down the last step when he drew her towards him. Her breath caught against his shoulder as his arm settled around her back.

Her arms closed around his back cautiously, avoiding the worst of the healing wounds without needing to ask where they lay. Harry rested his cheek against her hair. The house continued around them—Dobby arguing with Winky, Hermione telling Sirius that protective helmets did not justify airborne lawbreaking, one peacock tapping its beak against the open door—but the centre of the hall became quiet.

"There you are," Harry murmured.

Draconia held him more tightly. Her fingers caught the fabric at his back, loosened and caught it again. He had expected a remark about his lateness or the state of his hair. Instead, her forehead pressed against his shoulder, and her breath came unevenly through his shirt. Harry shifted enough to look at her.

"Draco?"

She shook her head without lifting it. He stayed still, resting his hand more securely between her shoulders while the argument continued behind them.

"I missed you," she said at last, so quietly that he almost lost it beneath Hermione's voice.

"I missed you too."

She remained against him for another moment before stepping back. Harry kept hold of her hand. Whatever had frightened her, she could tell him when she was ready.

"I am generally easy to locate," she said.

"Slytherin dungeons. Library. Wherever I'm doing something dangerous."

"A pattern created by your poor decisions."

When they separated, colour had risen faintly beneath her pale skin. Hermione noticed the colour in Draconia's face and looked down at the card she was holding. Narcissa noticed too, though she gave no sign beyond waiting a moment before approaching. Harry was grateful for the pause; he was still trying to work out what to do with his own hands.

She moved forward and placed both hands upon Harry's shoulders. Her eyes travelled over him with the efficiency of a healer and the concern of something less easily named.

"You have grown thinner."

"I was there for three weeks."

"Three weeks is quite long enough."

"I ate every meal. The contract became aggressive about toast."

Narcissa adjusted his collar, though Hermione had already corrected it.

"Breakfast is waiting."

"I just had breakfast."

Lucius approached. He leaned upon the cane more heavily than he wanted anyone to observe. The injuries from the graveyard had left one side stiff, and a faint silver scar crossed the base of his throat where Nott's curse struck. He stopped before Harry.

"Eat more," he said.

Harry glanced between him and Narcissa.

"You have rehearsed this."

"Once rarely seems sufficient with you."

"Hermione's influence is spreading."

Lucius looked alarmed enough to make Sirius laugh. Harry offered his hand. Lucius examined it for half a second before accepting. The formality might have felt cold from anyone else. Between them, it contained the graveyard: Lucius kneeling before Voldemort, the Portkey thrown across mud, a curse striking Nott before Harry could be stopped. Gratitude remained difficult terrain for both.

"Thank you," Harry said.

Lucius's grip tightened.

"You already have."

"Not for that. For staying here."

Lucius glanced around the cramped hall. A flowered umbrella stand held his cane collection. One of Sirius's boots occupied the bottom stair. Dobby had hung a protective charm from the chandelier beside three drying socks.

"I assure you, Potter, this arrangement contains very little sacrifice."

From the kitchen, something crashed. Sirius raised an eyebrow.

"The ceiling still unstable?"

"The ceiling lacks breeding," Lucius said.

Harry smiled. From behind him, Hermione lifted a cream envelope.

"Before anyone forgets what day it is."

Harry accepted it. Inside lay a card signed by everyone at Hawthorn. Sirius had drawn a motorbike beneath his name. Remus had added a helmet. Hermione wished him a happy fifteenth birthday in three careful lines; Draconia had written Fifteen is old enough to stop doing things solely because nobody has told you not to. He read that twice.

"A difficult promise to make," he said.

"I thought so," Draconia replied.

Narcissa touched his shoulder. "We will have cake after lunch. The larger celebration can wait until everyone can come."

"There's a larger celebration?"

Sirius became unusually interested in the umbrella stand.

"We'll discuss that," Harry said.

"After cake," Hermione advised.

Twenty-three minutes after the motorbike had reached the gate, the blue motorcar sounded outside. Remus entered several minutes later carrying Harry's trunk, Hedwig's cage and the expression of a man who had followed an invisible flying motorbike through three counties.

"You," he said to Sirius, "are insufferable."

"He wanted to go faster."

Remus looked at Harry. Harry removed his glasses and cleaned them upon his shirt.

"The wind was loud. Communication suffered."

Draconia stared at him. Hermione folded her arms. Sirius looked proud enough to become unmanageable.

"Breakfast," Narcissa announced before the alliance could strengthen.

Hawthorn's kitchen had not been designed for the entire household, two house-elves and a political crisis. The table occupied most of the room. Mismatched chairs had been collected around it from every other part of the cottage, including one with a velvet seat Lucius claimed no knowledge of transporting from the Manor. Parchment covered the dresser. A map of southern England had been pinned beside the pantry, its moving ink concealed beneath a tea towel whenever Muggle visitors approached the lane. Protective instruments turned upon the windowsills among pots of basil and rosemary. Breakfast waited beneath warming charms.

Eggs, mushrooms, tomatoes, toast, porridge, fruit and three varieties of pastry covered every remaining surface. Narcissa directed Harry into the chair between Draconia and Sirius. Hermione sat opposite with a ledger open beside her plate. Harry looked at it.

"Please tell me that isn't for my breakfast."

"It is the final Privet Drive record."

"Less frightening."

"I require your twenty-one reports."

"I left them there."

Hermione's spoon stopped halfway to the porridge.

"You what?"

"They belonged to the house."

"They are evidence."

"The wards kept copies."

Bill looked up from the far end of the table. "They did."

Harry had not noticed him among the crowd, partly because a tower of toast obscured the far end of the table and partly because Bill had been sitting very still, watching the steam rise from his mug. Bill's long hair was tied at his neck, and new burn marks crossed one forearm where the ward transfer had objected to its supervision. He held a mug in both hands and appeared not to have slept since June. Hermione redirected her concern.

"Are the records complete?"

"To the second."

"Can I see them?"

"After food," Narcissa and Harry said together.

The table became quiet. Narcissa looked at Harry.

Sirius placed a hand over his heart. "They grow so quickly."

Lucius reached for the teapot. "You have contributed nothing to this development."

"I taught him priorities."

"You taught him to fly illegally before breakfast."

"That was instinct."

"I heard that," Hermione said.

Harry served himself mushrooms. For several minutes, conversation divided around him. Bill explained the transfer to Hermione. Remus questioned Sirius about the motorbike's concealment charms. Winky informed Dobby that toast should not be charmed to monitor consumption because young masters deserved privacy. Dobby replied that privacy was not meaningfully endangered by bread. Draconia nudged a bowl of fruit towards Harry without looking at him. He took an apple.

Her knee touched his beneath the table. Harry paused with the apple in his hand, waiting for her to shift away, but she continued reaching for the teapot as though nothing had happened. After a moment he let his leg rest against hers. Neither mentioned it, and the silence made the small contact harder to mistake for an accident. Hermione's gaze dropped once. She turned a page in her ledger with particular care. Harry became interested in the apple.

Something warm and unfamiliar moved beneath his exhaustion. Not certainty. Nothing about Hermione and Draconia had become simple since the night by the lake. They had told him they liked him as more than a friend, and he had admitted that he felt the same about both of them. Saying it had not told him what they ought to do next, or given him a name for something that mattered to all three of them. The graveyard had taught him how easily love could be made into a weapon; the night by the lake had made it harder to hide the love itself. He had left Hogwarts with those conversations unfinished.

Now Draconia's knee rested against his. Hermione sat opposite with ink on one finger and a crease between her eyebrows. Harry did not know what came next. For the first time, uncertainty did not feel entirely like danger.

A sharp rattle at the kitchen window brought half the table's hands towards their wands. Outside, an owl flapped backwards from the glass, clutching a bundle of newspapers and looking thoroughly offended by the obstruction. Harry lowered his hand from his pocket while Bill leaned across to open the pane. Three copies of the Daily Prophet fell onto the table. Sirius seized the first. Lucius took the second. Hermione dragged the third between herself and Draconia. The same photograph of Harry and Cedric occupied the front page. Sirius read two paragraphs before blackening the edges of the paper beneath his hands.

"Interested adults."

"They mean you," Harry said.

"I gathered."

Lucius's expression did not change as he read. That was how Harry knew the article had wounded him.

"My interests are apparently the avoidance of prosecution and restoration of social standing."

"Is it working?" Sirius asked.

"Oh, splendidly. I have left my home to share a bathroom with you."

"You had the bathroom for forty minutes this morning."

Draconia read over Hermione's shoulder.

"They cite an unnamed healer."

"The Ministry sent one to Cedric yesterday," Harry said. "He asked whether Cedric had previous head injuries."

Hermione's mouth hardened.

"They're trying to make it sound as though he couldn't remember properly."

"They will suggest anything that leaves people uncertain," Lucius said. He folded the paper back along its crease. "They needn't prove Cedric wrong if they can persuade the public to stop listening."

Sirius pushed back his chair.

"Then we answer."

"After breakfast," Narcissa said.

"This concerns Harry."

"Harry is eating breakfast."

Everyone looked at him. Harry bit into the apple.

"Thrillingly."

Sirius sat again, though every line of his body objected. The article remained upon the table between the dishes. Harry's younger photograph smiled whenever the page moved. Words attempted to turn the graveyard into a debate whose participants could go home once the hour ended.

Harry waited for the anger he had felt at earlier headlines. Instead, he found himself reading the same sentence twice, with a cold, familiar heaviness settling beneath his ribs. The article made it sound as though remembering what had happened was itself something suspicious.

He had spent most of his life being told his own experience was inconvenient. The Dursleys called magic attention-seeking. Teachers at primary school believed Dudley's explanations because they required less paperwork. Fudge could not place Harry inside a cupboard or lock his bedroom door, but he understood the method.

Harry could imagine readers folding the paper and deciding they had heard enough. He could imagine Cedric being asked the same question until an exhausted answer differed from the first. Perhaps that was all the article needed to achieve: make speaking harder for the people who had been there, and listening less attractive to everyone else. Harry set down the apple.

"When is Cedric's statement?"

"Thursday," Bill said.

"Public?"

Lucius studied him. "Amelia is attempting to secure press access. Fudge is attempting to restrict the room."

"Then we find a larger room."

Sirius's anger paused. Harry reached for another piece of toast.

"After breakfast," he added.

Narcissa inclined her head. Narcissa passed the marmalade, and Remus resumed his complaint about the motorbike. The paper remained beside Sirius's plate, but nobody asked Harry to abandon his breakfast to answer it. For the moment, that was enough.

Upstairs, sunlight from the ash tree's side of the cottage lay across the landing. Harry followed Sirius to the blue room, brushing his hand along the familiar banister.

Sirius carried the trunk upstairs after breakfast despite Harry pointing out that magic existed. He claimed stairs built character. Remus claimed Sirius had spent three weeks refusing to let anyone else place the trunk inside because he wanted to carry it himself. The blue room waited at the back of the first floor.

Morning light entered through the broad window beneath the eaves, while the second window stood open a little to let the warm air through. The wallpaper was faded blue without a pattern, except where older flowers showed through near the skirting board. A narrow bed stood against one wall beneath a patched quilt. The desk faced the ash tree. Shelves occupied the space beside it, each leaning slightly in a different direction because Hawthorn had objected to straight lines before any of them arrived. Harry stopped in the doorway.

His possessions had found their way into every corner. Books had been moved to make room for newer ones, a shirt hung over the chair, and the rug beneath the desk had been straightened around its legs rather than tucked out of sight. Someone had taken care of the room without making it look as though he must never disturb it.

The Firebolt rested upon brackets Draconia had fixed above the desk. Books filled two shelves and waited in uneven piles upon a third. His blue mug sat beside the lamp. Clothes occupied the wardrobe, including four socks and one belonging to Dudley. The chess set from Sirius lay open upon a small table near the window, a match paused halfway through because Sirius had preserved the position from summer. Upon the wall above the bed hung no photographs. The frames waited face-down upon the desk. Sirius set the trunk at the foot of the bed.

"I didn't know which ones you'd want."

Harry crossed to the desk. He turned over the first frame. James and Lily waved from outside the Potter cottage. The second held Sirius and Remus arguing beside a much younger James. The print had been cropped tightly at one side; Harry recognised the space where Peter had stood in the original and was glad nobody expected him to make room for that face above his bed. The third showed Harry, Hermione and Draconia beneath the Hogwarts beech tree at the end of third year. Hermione looked towards the camera. Draconia looked towards Harry. Harry had been laughing at something beyond the frame. Colin's photograph would fit beside them.

"All of them," Harry said.

Sirius exhaled. Together, they placed the frames along the shelf, leaving enough space between them for the figures to move without disappearing behind another frame. Lily waved again when Harry stepped back. It was a small change, but the desk and bed no longer seemed to be waiting for someone to arrive; the people he wanted beside him were already there.

Harry unpacked Colin's photograph and set it nearest the bed. Sirius did not touch it. His own moving figure remained kneeling upon the pitch, one hand beneath Harry's head.

"Do you want the desk moved?" Sirius asked. "The light might be annoying in the morning. We can change the walls. The wardrobe bites if you close the left door first, but Dobby says that's because it dislikes Lucius, so it may behave for you. There's another rug downstairs. Not the red one. Something died on that."

Harry turned from the photographs. Sirius continued speaking.

"The bathroom is across the landing. Draconia's room is beside it, but the pipes knock, and we can move you farther down if—"

"Sirius."

He stopped. Harry sat upon the edge of the bed. The mattress yielded beneath him. New, but not too soft. Someone had tested it.

"The room is good."

"Good can mean tolerable."

"It means good."

Sirius looked towards the window. The ash tree moved beyond it, leaves flashing silver-green beneath the sun.

"I kept thinking you'd get here and realise it was only a cottage," he said. "That it was crowded and the roof leaks and half the people downstairs are planning a war in the pantry."

"The other half are arguing about curtains."

"Important work."

Harry smiled. Sirius did not.

"I told you that you could always come here," he said. "Then I took you back to that house. I can't seem to get past it."

"For twenty-one nights."

"That was twenty-one too many."

Harry looked around the room. His gaze moved from the photographs to the tilted shelves and the wardrobe Sirius had warned him about. The key rested in his palm, warm from being held, and nobody was waiting to take it back.

"Aunt Petunia said she kept me alive," he said.

Sirius went still.

"She did. Technically. There was food. A roof. Dumbledore's protection." Harry ran his thumb along the key. "I told her that wasn't the same as raising me."

Sirius sat beside him. He did not interrupt. Harry described the hallway conversation. Lily's injuries. The cupboard. The scratched name beneath the paint. Dudley learning that Harry had not enjoyed living there. He left out nothing because stopping would require deciding which parts mattered most. When he finished, the room had become quiet. Sirius stared at his hands.

"I want to destroy that house," he said.

"Remus would object."

"Remus could help."

"He would improve the plan while denying involvement."

A laugh escaped Sirius without moving the anger from his face. Harry leaned forward, elbows upon his knees.

"I don't want it destroyed."

"Why?"

"Because then everyone would talk about the house instead of what happened inside it."

Sirius considered him.

"That sounded disgustingly like Moony."

"Mrs Figg says I sound like you."

"Much better."

"She also warned me about recklessness."

"Slander."

Harry's smile faded.

"I left the reports there."

"Hermione informed us from downstairs. Several windows shook."

"I wanted something in the house to say what it was. Even if they throw them away."

Sirius rested his forearms upon his knees, matching Harry's posture.

"You don't have to decide today what leaving means."

"I know."

"You don't have to forgive them because one of them managed a sentence."

"I know."

"You don't have to feel grateful to me for giving you a room."

Harry turned his head. Sirius looked younger in sunlight and older around the eyes. Azkaban remained inside certain silences. It existed in the way Sirius kept doors open, avoided sitting with his back to a window and treated every offered affection as something which might be withdrawn if he held too tightly. Harry understood more of him after the graveyard. That was not a gift he would have chosen.

"It's not just the room," Harry said. He rubbed his thumb along the seam of the quilt, trying to get the rest out without making it sound smaller than it was. "You knew I needed one. I didn't have to ask you."

Sirius looked down at Harry's hand, then at the photographs they had placed together. After a moment, he placed a hand behind Harry's neck and pulled him sideways until their shoulders struck. Harry let himself remain there.

"You live here," Sirius said.

"I know."

"Say it convincingly."

"I live here."

"Again."

"You're becoming demanding."

"Again."

Harry looked at the open door.

"I live here."

The cottage answered with a crash downstairs.

Lucius shouted, "Black!"

Sirius sighed happily.

"Home."

Hermione and Draconia invaded the room before lunch. Hermione brought two stacks of books and a folder labelled SUMMER WORK: REVISED. Draconia carried one small wooden box and the expression of someone prepared to remove Hermione by force if the folder opened.

"No," Harry said as soon as he saw the label.

"You don't know what it contains."

"The threat has identified itself."

Hermione placed the folder upon his desk. "We have weeks before school."

"Exactly. Imagine what we could accomplish by not discussing essays."

"An impressive number of unfinished essays?"

"Rest."

Draconia set the wooden box beside the folder.

"I support Potter."

Hermione looked between them. "You both complained when I refused to help with the History of Magic assignment last year."

"We were younger," Harry said.

"It was three months ago."

"Time changes people."

Draconia opened the box. Inside lay three narrow metal discs upon chains. A serpent had been engraved upon one, an otter upon the second and a lion upon the third. Their surfaces were plain and new, without runes Harry recognised. He touched the lion.

"What are these?"

"Nothing yet," Draconia said. "Bill removed the tracking enchantments from our damaged bracelets. The stones cannot safely carry another connection. These will replace the emergency function once we agree upon its limits."

Hermione sat cross-legged upon the rug.

"An alarm," she said. "Nobody watching where we are all day, and none of us feeling what happens to the others. We agree when it can call for help before Bill puts anything into it."

"And no exploding," Draconia added.

Harry looked towards Hermione.

"Was exploding under consideration?"

"Sirius attended the first meeting."

"That explains it."

He lifted the lion by its chain. The unfinished metal reflected the room in a curved fragment.

"You made the animals."

Hermione glanced towards Draconia.

"She did."

Draconia's attention remained upon the box.

"The original design lacked distinction."

"It had our initials," Hermione said.

"Initials are unimaginative."

Harry turned the disc between his fingers. The lion's mane consisted of tiny lines. The face possessed an expression of patient irritation which looked suspiciously familiar.

"Mine looks annoyed."

"I know what you look like when you open a Potions textbook."

"And the serpent?"

"Elegant."

"Naturally."

Hermione handed him the otter. Its paws held an open book.

"You approved this?"

"She added the book after I objected to the teeth."

"The teeth were charming," Draconia said.

"They were enormous."

Harry held the three chains together. Metal touched metal with a faint clear note. No magic travelled between them. Harry could hold all three discs without feeling a pull towards either girl, and setting one down changed nothing in his wrist. He liked knowing there was still time to ask questions before the charms were added. This time they would all know what the connection could do before they agreed to wear it.

"I like them," Harry said.

Draconia looked up.

"Even the annoyed lion?"

"Especially him."

Her smile appeared slowly. Harry watched it too long. Draconia noticed. Instead of looking away, she held his gaze. Harry forgot the question he had been about to ask. Draconia's fingers rested beside the empty spaces in the box, and he became suddenly aware of how little distance separated them on the rug. Hermione cleared her throat.

"The shelves are crooked."

Draconia's eyes narrowed. "The floor is crooked."

"The upper shelf is lower on the left."

"Because the ceiling descends."

"That is not how levels work."

Harry leaned back upon his hands.

"I missed this."

Both girls turned towards him. The argument dissolved. Hermione's face softened. Draconia sat beside him upon the rug. Their shoulders touched. Hermione shifted to his other side with considerably less pretence than Draconia had used at breakfast. For a minute, none spoke.

The ash leaves moved beyond the window. Downstairs, an argument about security routes became audible and then disappeared when someone closed the kitchen door. Pipes knocked behind the wall in irregular pairs. Harry sat between the girls and allowed the quiet to remain uncomplicated.

"What did Petunia say?" Hermione asked eventually.

He told them. Not every detail. Sirius received the cupboard because he carried a different part of its history. Hermione and Draconia received Lily without eyebrows, the distinction between survival and care, and Dudley's impossible attempt at saying good for you. Hermione listened with her hands folded around her knees. Draconia's expression became colder whenever the Dursleys entered the account and unexpectedly gentle whenever Lily did.

"She did not apologise," Hermione said when Harry finished.

"No."

"That doesn't make what she did all right."

"I know."

"You keep saying that today," Draconia observed.

Harry looked at her. "People keep telling me things I know."

"You don't always act as though you do."

"Perhaps people enjoy speeches."

Hermione frowned. "We are trying to help."

Harry heard the defensive edge and understood he had placed it there.

"I know that too," he said, more softly. "I'm not angry with you. I just don't know what I'm meant to feel."

"Nothing in particular," Draconia said.

Hermione nodded. "You don't owe them grief or forgiveness."

"What if I have the grief anyway?"

The girls exchanged a glance. Draconia rested her repaired bracelet upon Harry's wrist, the clouded stones touching through leather and chain.

"Then be sad," she said. Her thumb moved once against his wrist. "You can be sad and still be glad you've left. We won't ask you to choose."

Harry looked down at their hands. The new discs waited inside the box, their chains coiled loosely beside one another. They were still unfinished, but he had been asked what he wanted from them. He could imagine wearing one without having to wonder what someone else had decided it should do.

"You sound like Sirius," he said.

Draconia recoiled. "Take that back."

Harry laughed. Hermione did too. Draconia attempted offence for several seconds before joining them. The sound travelled through the open door. A board creaked on the landing. Harry glanced towards the open door and saw Sirius standing there with a mug in his hand. His godfather smiled at them without interrupting, then continued towards the stairs. By evening, Hawthorn had remembered it was becoming headquarters for a war.

Moody arrived through the back door after circling the cottage twice beneath concealment. Kingsley followed by Floo and emerged from the sitting-room fireplace with ash upon otherwise immaculate robes. Amelia Bones came by Muggle car and parked beyond the wards, carrying a briefcase whose locks snapped at anyone except her. Cedric and Amos appeared inside the kitchen mirror for a discussion concerning Thursday's statement. The table disappeared beneath maps. Harry stood in the kitchen doorway. No one asked him to leave.

The difference mattered enough that he remained for the first half-hour, listening while Amelia explained the Ministry's strategy. Fudge could not deny the attack on the final task, Barty Crouch Junior's infiltration or the elder Nott's presence in the graveyard. Too many witnesses and foreign officials existed. Instead, the Ministry separated each fact from Voldemort until the remaining story became a conspiracy of damaged individuals.

"They will permit an inquiry," Amelia said. "Then ensure the inquiry asks whether Crouch and Nott staged the ritual to destabilise the Ministry."

"With what motive?" Hermione asked.

Amelia's mouth tightened. "They needn't explain that yet. If they can keep people arguing about what happened, they can delay deciding what to do."

Cedric's image shifted inside the mirror.

"I'll tell them what I saw."

"You will," his father said beside him. "In a room large enough to hear it."

Lucius tapped one finger against the map.

"Fudge will attempt to discredit me through the Manor searches. He will imply I assisted Nott before changing sides when the ritual failed."

"Did you?" Moody asked.

Draconia stiffened beside Harry. Lucius did not.

"I attended the graveyard after receiving a summons through a mark I acquired during the first war. I knelt. I delayed. Then I attacked Nott and assisted Potter's escape. Decide which portion of that constitutes assistance according to your preferred suspicion."

Moody fixed his natural eye upon Lucius while the replacement magical one turned towards the window. Harry looked away from its movement before the sight could become another memory of the man who had worn Moody's face at school.

"All of it requires watching."

"Alastor," Remus warned.

"No," Lucius said. "Let him watch. That is why he is useful."

The meeting continued. Harry understood perhaps half of the legal strategy and all of the fear beneath it. Hawthorn's kitchen felt smaller with every name added to the map. Safe houses. Suspected sympathisers. Ministry departments likely to cooperate and those already closing records. Sirius stood at Harry's shoulder but repeatedly looked towards him instead of the parchment.

Harry lasted until the kitchen clock had moved well past the half-hour. He had stopped following the routes on the map and was concentrating instead on keeping his hand steady against the doorframe. Then the voices began to overlap, and a name spoken at the table reached him with the sound of another place attached to it.

At the sound of Nott's name, Harry saw the man beside the cauldron rather than the ink upon the map. Moody's scarred face ought to have been reassuring; the real man was here, helping them. Yet every movement of his magical eye brought back the professor who had watched Harry all year, and knowing they were different people did not stop the memory. The map's red marks resembled blood soaking through graveyard soil. Harry pushed away from the doorframe. Sirius noticed before anyone else.

"Break," he said.

Moody scowled. "We're in the middle of—"

"My kitchen. My break."

"Sirius," Harry began.

"I need tea."

"You hate tea."

"War changes people."

Sirius swept the map beneath a concealment cloth. Narcissa began moving cups without comment. The interruption allowed Harry to leave without becoming the reason for it. He went upstairs while Narcissa asked who needed milk. Nobody hurried after him or called him back to explain, and he was grateful for the ordinary sound of cups being set out behind him. It gave him time to reach the landing without feeling everyone was watching him leave.

Harry entered the blue room and closed the door halfway. The house softened around him. Voices continued below, blurred by floorboards. Evening light filled the ash tree and threw moving leaves across his desk. He sat beside the window. The room belonged to him. The war existed downstairs. He had known both before, but tonight the distance between them felt smaller. Harry did not yet know how to protect one from the other. A quiet knock touched the open door. Sirius stood outside holding two mugs.

"Tea," he said.

"You really do hate tea."

"This one is mostly sugar."

Harry accepted it. Sirius sat upon the floor beneath the window. He did not ask whether Harry was all right. He looked towards the ash tree and drank from his own mug with visible regret.

"Terrible," he said.

"The tea?"

"Your influence. I used to have standards."

"You wore floral curtains yesterday."

"Formal occasion."

Harry smiled into the steam. They remained there until voices resumed downstairs and the red marks stopped moving behind Harry's eyes.

By the time the landing grew quiet, the sky beyond the ash tree had darkened to blue. A lamp still burned downstairs, and someone had left another lit beside the bathroom so that Harry would not have to find his way in the dark. Hermione occupied the other back room because Mrs Granger had agreed she could stay for several days. Draconia returned to Remus's room, which she had been using since the Malfoys moved in. Remus chose the sofa rather than remove Moody from the room he had claimed for security reasons. Lucius and Narcissa retired after midnight, still arguing quietly about whether the peacocks required additional wards. Harry prepared for bed beneath the open window.

The room smelled of summer leaves, old wood and the lavender Narcissa placed between the sheets. His medicine stood upon the desk, labelled but not hidden. His wand lay beneath the pillow because Sirius had not asked him to put it elsewhere. Harry placed the brass key upon the bedside table. Then he reconsidered and hung it around his neck. The house shifted.

Pipes knocked across the landing. A floorboard complained inside Draconia's room. Hermione sneezed twice through the wall, followed by the scrape of a book being moved despite her promise to sleep. Downstairs, Sirius and Remus spoke too quietly for Harry to distinguish words. Lucius objected to something in the corridor. Dobby told him the objection had been received and rejected.

The house sounded crowded even with most of its doors shut. Harry could hear where people were, could recognise them by the way they walked or objected to one another. At Privet Drive he had learned to keep track of those sounds so that he could stay out of the way. Here he found himself listening because he liked knowing they were close. Harry lay beneath the quilt. The door stood half open.

At Privet Drive, an open door had been evidence demanded by a contract. Here it was simply a choice. Harry watched the strip of warm corridor light cross the floor. Footsteps approached. Sirius appeared in the gap.

"Need anything?"

Harry considered the automatic answer.

No came to him first, as it usually did. He had learned to offer it before anyone could regret asking. Sirius waited in the doorway, one hand still upon the frame, and Harry realised he could try another answer without knowing exactly how it would sound.

"Stay until I fall asleep?"

Sirius's face changed so quickly Harry nearly withdrew the request. Then Sirius nodded.

"Move over."

He sat on top of the quilt with his back against the headboard. Harry lay down again. Sirius picked up the Defence book from the bedside table and opened to the page Harry had failed to read at Privet Drive.

"This author is an idiot," he said.

"You've read one sentence."

"It was an informative sentence."

"Read it anyway."

Sirius did. His voice filled the room, interrupting the text whenever an objection became necessary. Harry listened to explanations of defensive distance, insults directed towards the author and an unrelated story about James attempting a Shield Charm inside a greenhouse. The words lost shape gradually.

Harry's body remained alert after his mind began to sink. Each creak became a footstep near a grave. Each shift of the mattress suggested rope tightening around his chest. His fingers found the cloudy charm at his wrist. Sirius turned a page. The sound belonged to paper, not robes passing over wet grass. Hermione laughed through the wall at something Draconia said in the corridor. The sound belonged to living girls, not Voldemort's imitations. Harry loosened his grip upon the charm.

"Sirius?"

"Still here."

Harry closed his eyes. The front door stood downstairs, locked against the night and ready to open for him in the morning. The bedroom door remained half open because Harry had chosen it. For now, that was enough.
