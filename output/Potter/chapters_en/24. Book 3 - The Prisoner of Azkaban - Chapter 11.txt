11

Harry reached the end of the corridor before the memories changed owners again. One instant, he stood beneath an arched window with rainlight falling across his robes. The next, he could not move. His body lay beneath white hospital sheets. Voices surrounded him without shape. Harry tried to open his eyes and discovered they were already open, fixed upon a ceiling hidden behind darkness. Then paralysis released him.

His right hand lifted. Not his. A wand pointed towards the hospital bed. Red light gathered at its tip. Harry struck the wall.

Stone scraped his shoulder. The corridor returned around him, empty and colourless in the afternoon rain. He stared at his hand. No wand. His own lay inside his sleeve. The fingers were thinner than Draconia's. Ink marked neither skin nor nails. When Harry bent them, they obeyed.

He pushed away from the wall. The floor shifted. For several steps, he possessed Hermione's exhaustion: hours layered behind his eyes, every clock suspicious, every corridor possibly occupied by another version of himself. Then hunger twisted through him, small and old, and the walls narrowed into the cupboard beneath the stairs. Harry walked faster. He needed distance from the classroom. From the broken basin. From Hermione's tears and Draconia's face when he had used Riddle's possession to hurt her.

He also needed them close enough to prove they remained alive. The contradiction pulled him in opposite directions. Harry took the western staircase. It moved after he stepped onto it, carrying him upwards rather than down. The castle's motion drove a wave of panic through his chest. He seized the railing and closed his eyes.

Name.

Age.

Place.

The ritual belonged to Draconia. Harry used it anyway. Harry James Potter.

Thirteen.

Hogwarts.

The diary was destroyed. Hermione and Draconia were alive. His breathing steadied. The staircase reached the sixth floor. Harry stepped off.

A suit of armour stood near the landing. Its empty helmet turned towards him.

"Are you injured?" it asked.

Harry kept walking.

"Your nose appears structurally unsound."

"Thank you."

"Shall I summon assistance?"

"No."

The armour raised one gauntlet.

"Help!" it shouted.

Harry drew his wand. The shout ended abruptly. He had not cast anything. Professor Lupin stood at the far end of the corridor with his own wand raised. The armour saluted him.

"Distressed youth," it reported. "Bleeding."

"I can see that," Lupin said.

Harry touched beneath his nose. Fresh blood crossed his fingers. He wiped it against his sleeve. Lupin approached slowly.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

His gaze moved across Harry's face. The lie did not survive.

"Who cast the spell?"

"No one."

"Harry."

"I did."

Lupin stopped several feet away.

"You cast something upon yourself?"

"It wasn't meant to—"

Harry's voice became Hermione's inside his head.

It was meant to show one memory.

He pressed both hands to his ears. The movement did nothing. Lupin lowered his wand.

"May I come closer?"

Harry looked at him. The question reached through the noise. He nodded. Lupin closed half the distance and crouched, bringing them nearer eye level without touching.

"May I see your hand?"

"Why?"

"There are silver marks along your fingers."

Harry looked down. Thin lines crossed his skin where Hermione and Draconia had held him. They shone faintly, threads of moonlight beneath the surface. Lupin saw recognition in his face.

"What spell did you use?"

Harry said nothing.

"Was it Legilimency?"

"No."

"Memory extraction?"

"No."

"What, then?"

The corridor dissolved. Draconia's hand wrote across a diary page. Ink vanished. Hermione lay conscious inside a body which could not move. Petunia opened the cupboard and told Harry to apologise. He staggered. Lupin reached towards him and stopped before contact.

"Harry."

"They're still there."

"Who?"

"Hermione. Draco."

"Inside your mind?"

"I don't know."

Lupin's expression sharpened.

"Did you attempt a mnemonic link?"

Harry looked up. The recognition upon Lupin's face frightened him.

"It was meant to last a minute."

"Who anchored it?"

Harry laughed weakly.

"You already know."

Lupin closed his eyes. The anger appeared only briefly. It tightened his mouth and changed the angle of his shoulders. When he opened his eyes, it was controlled.

"We need to get you somewhere quiet."

"Not the hospital wing."

"Madam Pomfrey needs to check you."

"I'm not going."

"Then my office."

Harry hesitated. Lupin remained crouched.

"You may walk," he said. "I will not touch you unless you ask."

The distinction decided it. Harry nodded. They crossed the sixth floor together. Lupin kept one step behind him. He did not ask more questions. When students appeared ahead, he redirected them down another corridor before they came close enough to stare. By the time they reached his office, Harry's nose had stopped bleeding.

The borrowed memories had not.

Lupin darkened the room. He closed the curtains, reduced the fire and extinguished every lamp except the one upon his desk. Warm amber light settled over the worn furniture. The familiar wooden case remained locked inside the cupboard, and a fresh box of chocolate stood beside the tea tray. Harry sat in the broad-backed chair near the hearth. Lupin did not offer food immediately.

He knelt near the fireplace and drew a circle upon the stones with his wand. Pale gold magic moved around the room, sealing the walls.

"What is that?" Harry asked.

"A privacy charm."

"You trust me not to speak about you but not the paintings?"

"I trust the paintings to speak about everyone."

Harry looked towards the single framed landscape above Lupin's desk. The occupant—a shepherd sleeping beneath a tree—continued snoring. Lupin summoned a basin of clean water and placed it beside Harry.

"Wash your face."

Harry did. The water became pink. His reflection trembled across the surface. For an instant, red eyes looked back. Harry recoiled. Water struck the carpet.

Lupin vanished the basin.

"What did you see?"

"Nothing."

"You need to stop using that word."

Harry gripped the chair arms.

"Draco sees herself wrong in mirrors."

Understanding reached Lupin.

"Residual transference."

"It feels real."

"I know."

"You've done this spell?"

"No. I have experienced other forms of mental magic."

Harry looked at him. Lupin moved to the chair opposite.

"The link let you feel one another's memories," he said. "It should have ended cleanly. When it didn't, some of those feelings stayed with the wrong person. That can last for hours."

"It showed more than feelings."

"What did you see?"

Harry stared into the low fire. Hermione trapped beneath white sheets. Draconia writing to Riddle in the darkness. His own younger hands lifting a bowl of soup without a spoon.

"Everything."

"That is unlikely."

"Hermione said that too."

Lupin heard the anger. He corrected himself.

"I'm sorry. I don't know what you saw."

Harry loosened his hold upon the chair.

"We were inside each other."

"Reciprocal resonance."

"The book warned about it."

"Yes."

"They said it was rare."

"It is rare among trained healers."

"We aren't trained."

"No."

Lupin's anger returned beneath the final word. Harry stiffened.

"I agreed."

"Agreeing to it didn't make it safe."

"They explained it."

"All of it?"

"They said it could happen."

"Did they warn you that afterwards you might not be able to tell the memory from what was happening around you?"

"No."

"Did they know?"

Harry thought of Hermione's face after the basin shattered.

"No."

Lupin drew a slow breath.

"That matters."

"They were trying to help."

"I know."

"Don't punish them."

"This is not about punishment."

"That's what adults say before punishment."

Lupin studied him.

"No one will be punished tonight."

"Tonight?"

"Harry."

"You said no one."

"I won't bring anyone else in here tonight. We'll decide what to do when you've had some rest. But we will have to do something, Harry."

"I agreed," Harry repeated.

"Why?"

"So they would stop asking."

Lupin's face tightened.

"That's a poor reason to let someone inside your head."

"I could have left."

"Yes."

"I stayed."

"Yes."

"I cast the spell."

"Yes."

Lupin leaned forward, his forearms resting upon his knees.

"You agreed to try the spell," he said. "You didn't agree to everything it showed you. And they didn't mean for that to happen either. You can be angry with them without saying it was all their fault—or all yours."

Harry looked towards the curtains. Rain tapped gently against the glass behind them.

"They saw the cupboard."

The words entered the room quietly. Lupin stopped moving.

"And the food," Harry continued. "And my uncle."

Lupin's face lost colour.

"What did they see?"

"I don't know."

"Yes, you do."

Harry hated him briefly for saying it. He closed his eyes. The memories waited.

"They saw me locked in," he said. "When I was little."

"How little?"

"Four, maybe."

Lupin looked down.

"They saw Aunt Petunia make me apologise when magic happened. They saw Uncle Vernon—"

Harry's throat closed. He changed direction.

"They saw school. A teacher asked about my bedroom."

"What did you say?"

"That it was white."

Lupin covered his mouth with one hand. Harry laughed without humour.

"It was."

The fire shifted. A log split through its centre.

"Do you want me to stop asking?" Lupin said.

Harry considered the question.

"Yes."

Lupin nodded. He did not ask again. The silence which followed was not empty. It held the things Harry had said without trying to pull more from them. Harry's heartbeat gradually slowed. Lupin opened the chocolate box and placed it upon the small table between them.

"You don't have to eat," he said. "It may reduce the magical after-effects if you do."

Harry took one piece. Dark chocolate. Orange peel. He let it soften upon his tongue.

"Was it abuse?" he asked.

Lupin became very still. Harry looked at the fire rather than him.

"Hermione thought it was," he continued. "Draco did too."

"They were correct."

The answer came without hesitation. Harry's fingers tightened around the wrapper.

"It wasn't like that every day."

"It did not need to be."

"I got food."

"Not enough."

"You don't know that."

"No," Lupin said. "I wasn't there. I wish I had been. But I believe what you've told me, Harry. You should never have had to wait for someone to unlock a door before you could eat."

Harry felt himself shrinking inside the chair. Lupin saw. His voice gentled without becoming uncertain.

"What happened to you was abuse."

The word filled the office. Harry wanted to argue. He possessed every defence. The Dursleys had never broken a bone. They had given him clothes. They had allowed him to attend school. The cupboard had contained a mattress. Some children had nothing. Lupin seemed to hear the comparisons assembling.

"Another child suffering more would not make your suffering deserved," he said.

"I didn't say that."

"No."

"You thought it."

"I recognised it."

Harry looked at him.

"From what?"

Lupin's gaze moved towards the fire.

"I used to tell myself other people had it worse. It meant I didn't have to ask anyone for help."

"Did someone lock you in a cupboard?"

"No."

"Then it isn't the same."

"No," Lupin agreed. "It isn't."

Harry waited for him to explain. Lupin looked older in the firelight. The scars across his face showed more clearly, fine pale marks which crossed his skin at uneven angles.

"You asked why I did not come for you," he said.

Harry's chest tightened.

"I know why."

"Do you?"

"You thought the Dursleys were safe."

"That is the answer I gave myself."

"What's the real one?"

Lupin's hand closed around the edge of his sleeve.

"I was afraid of what I might do to you."

Harry frowned.

"You were poor."

"Yes."

"You didn't have a house."

"Not consistently."

"You were ill."

Lupin looked at him. Hermione's lunar charts returned through the borrowed memory. Professor Snape carrying a steaming goblet to Lupin's empty chair. Monthly absences. Cuts which appeared overnight. The enormous moon inside the Boggart lesson. The pieces had already belonged to Hermione. Now they belonged to Harry too.

"You're a werewolf," he said.

The shepherd in the painting stopped snoring. Lupin's face emptied. Harry heard the words after speaking them. He expected denial. None came.

"Yes."

The fire cracked.

"How long have you known?" Lupin asked.

"About six seconds."

"That is faster than most."

"Hermione knew."

Lupin closed his eyes.

"Of course she did."

"I saw it in her memory."

"And Miss Malfoy?"

"She suspected."

"How?"

"Snape."

Lupin's expression changed.

"Severus told her?"

"No. He made it obvious by being secretive."

"That sounds like Severus."

Harry examined the professor opposite him. Nothing visible had changed. Lupin still wore a patched cardigan. His hands remained steady. His voice sounded the same.

"Are you dangerous now?" Harry asked.

"No."

"When?"

"The full moon."

"How dangerous?"

"Completely."

Harry thought of the Boggart moon becoming a balloon.

"And the potion?"

"Wolfsbane. Professor Snape brews it each month. It allows me to retain my mind during the transformation, though I still isolate myself."

"That's where you go."

"Yes."

"Off the map?"

Lupin's eyes sharpened. Harry realised what he had revealed.

"What map?"

"Nothing."

"Harry."

"You told me to stop using that word."

Lupin almost smiled. It vanished quickly.

"I believed my condition made me unsuitable to care for a child," he said. "I still believe it would have made guardianship difficult."

"Difficult isn't the same as impossible."

"No."

"You could have visited."

"Yes."

"You could have checked."

"Yes."

"You could have written."

"Yes."

Each answer arrived without defence. Harry looked away. Lupin continued only after the silence had settled.

"I allowed shame to behave like a moral decision. I told myself staying away protected you from me. In truth, it protected me from discovering I had accepted a lie."

"What lie?"

"That you were safe."

Harry pressed the chocolate wrapper flat against his knee.

"Dumbledore said the blood protection required your aunt's home," Lupin said. "I did not ask what protection meant inside it. I did not ask whether surviving Voldemort justified everything which followed."

"Would you have taken me if you knew?"

Lupin's breath caught. The answer mattered too much to be given carelessly.

"I would have tried."

"That isn't yes."

"No." Lupin faced him. "Because I do not know whether the Ministry would have allowed it, whether Dumbledore would have opposed me or whether fear would have won. I will not make myself braver in the past to comfort you now."

Harry stared at him. Most adults altered old decisions until the person they described deserved forgiveness. Lupin left his failure unchanged.

"I would like to believe I would have taken you," he said. "I know I should have come."

Harry swallowed.

"You thought being a werewolf made you worse than them."

Lupin flinched.

"I thought it made me a danger."

"You're a werewolf for one night a month."

"The effects last longer than one night."

"The Dursleys are Dursleys all the time."

Silence.

Then Lupin laughed. The sound broke unexpectedly from him, rough and brief. He covered his eyes.

"That is not a recognised medical measurement," he said.

"It should be."

"I will suggest it to Madam Pomfrey."

The room eased around them. Only slightly. It was enough.

Madam Pomfrey arrived fifteen minutes later. Lupin warned Harry before summoning her.

"I need someone qualified to check the residual magic."

"Will you tell her?"

"That a mnemonic link exceeded its containment. Nothing more unless you choose."

"And Hermione and Draco?"

"I will not name them."

"You know it was them."

"Yes."

"So does everyone with a basic understanding of my life."

"Possibly."

Harry sat back.

"Fine."

Lupin's silver Patronus left through the office wall. It was a wolf. Harry watched it disappear. Lupin watched Harry.

"It suits you," Harry said.

"Most people find it unsubtle."

"Mine is becoming antlers."

"Yes."

"So yours could be worse."

Madam Pomfrey entered carrying a medical case and an expression which became more severe with every word of Lupin's explanation. She examined Harry's eyes, pulse and magical field. A pale spell passed over his head and revealed silver threads around his temples.

"Idiotic," she muttered.

Harry looked at Lupin.

"He said there would be no punishment."

"I am describing the decision."

"You don't know whose it was."

"I have several educated guesses."

She gave him a potion which tasted of sage and wet earth. The silver marks upon his fingers faded.

"Residual memories may surface until morning," she said. "Do not attempt Occlumency, memory alteration or further magic involving the mind."

"I wasn't planning to."

"After what you planned this time, I shall take no chances."

"Fair."

"You will not sleep alone tonight."

Harry stiffened.

"No hospital wing."

"I did not specify a location."

"He can remain here," Lupin said.

Madam Pomfrey looked at the narrow sofa.

"That is unsuitable."

"I possess Transfiguration skills."

"You possess a spine held together by stubbornness."

"An occupational condition."

Pomfrey enlarged the sofa with one movement. It became a narrow bed.

"You will wake him every two hours," she instructed. "If he fails to recognise you, summon me."

Harry looked at Lupin. "What happens if I recognise you and simply don't want to answer?"

"I will use the chocolate."

"That seems unethical."

"So was the memory spell," Madam Pomfrey said.

Harry stopped smiling. She noticed. Her voice softened.

"Rest, Mr Potter."

When she left, Lupin found a spare blanket inside the cupboard. Harry took off his glasses and lay upon the enlarged sofa. Lupin returned to his desk.

"You don't have to stay awake," Harry said.

"I have essays."

"Those are Neville's."

"How can you tell?"

"One corner is burnt."

Lupin lifted the parchment.

"Remarkable."

"He set the Grindylow diagram alight."

"Why?"

"It looked wet."

Lupin looked at him over the page. Harry pulled the blanket higher.

"Good night."

Sleep came before he expected it.

The memories followed. Harry was inside the cupboard when he heard his mother scream. The door would not open. Hermione lay on the other side, calling that she had solved it. Draconia's wand glowed red beneath the crack. Sirius laughed in the kitchen with Uncle Vernon.

Harry struck the wood.

"Let me out!"

The door opened. Lupin stood beyond it. Not young. Not the man from the wedding photograph. The professor Harry knew, scarred and tired and reaching towards him.

Harry woke with his wand in his hand. Lupin stood several feet from the sofa. His own wand remained lowered. The office fire had burned to embers. Darkness pressed against the windows.

"Harry."

Harry pointed the wand at his chest. Lupin did not move.

"What time is it?" he asked.

Harry looked around.

Office.

Fire.

Sofa.

"Two."

"Two what?"

"In the morning."

"Where are you?"

"Your office."

"Who am I?"

Harry's grip loosened.

"Professor Lupin."

"Good."

Lupin waited. Harry lowered the wand.

"I'm sorry."

"You were asleep."

"I could have cast."

"You didn't."

"Because I woke."

"Yes."

The distinction sounded like one Snape might give Draconia. Harry placed the wand upon the blanket.

"Did I say anything?"

"You asked to be let out."

Harry looked towards the dark window. Lupin sat in the chair beside the sofa.

"Would you like the fire lit?"

"No."

"Tea?"

"No."

"Chocolate?"

"It's two in the morning."

"That has never prevented chocolate."

Harry shook his head. Lupin stayed. The silence stretched.

"Did my dad know?" Harry asked.

"That I'm a werewolf?"

"Yes."

"James, Lily, Sirius and Peter all knew."

"Were they frightened?"

"At first."

"Then?"

Lupin looked towards the embers.

"They became offended on my behalf."

Harry could imagine James doing that.

"How did they find out?"

"I attempted to hide it."

"That works well."

"I was eleven. My technique lacked refinement."

"What did they do?"

"James asked directly. Sirius searched my trunk. Peter followed me to the hospital wing. Lily overheard all three arguing and solved it before they did."

Harry smiled faintly.

"Hermione would like her."

"Yes."

"What happened after?"

"I expected them to stop speaking to me."

"They didn't."

"No."

Lupin's expression changed beneath the dying fire.

"James spent two days researching werewolves. Most of what he found was inaccurate and offensive. He became so angry that he wrote a letter to the publisher."

"Did they answer?"

"They threatened legal action."

"Draco would like him."

"Sirius suggested burning the publishing office."

"Draco would like him too."

The name altered the room. Lupin folded his hands.

"He was good to you," Harry said.

"Yes."

"And then he wasn't."

Lupin's gaze lowered.

"That is what I believed."

"Do you still?"

A long silence answered first.

"I keep remembering my friend. Then I remember what they said he did. I don't know how to put those two things together."

"Lucius says the evidence is bad."

"Lucius is correct that Sirius never received a trial."

"He says that doesn't make him innocent."

"Also correct."

"What do you think he wanted inside Gryffindor Tower?"

"I don't know."

Harry sighed.

"You all need new answers."

"I have considered inventing one."

"Please don't."

"No."

Harry looked at Lupin's face. The professor appeared more tired whenever Sirius was discussed. Not frightened. Grieving.

"Did you see him after my parents died?"

"No."

"Not at all?"

"No."

"You never asked him why."

Lupin's jaw tightened.

"He was taken directly to Azkaban."

"You could have visited."

"Yes."

"Why didn't you?"

"I hated him."

The honesty struck. Lupin did not soften it.

"I believed he had murdered everyone I loved," he said. "I did not want an explanation. I wanted him to suffer."

Harry thought of the photograph beneath his curtains. He understood. That frightened him more than the admission.

"What changed?"

"Twelve years."

"That's all?"

"Time rarely changes only one thing."

Lupin reached towards the fire and turned one blackened log with his wand. A thin flame appeared.

"Sleep," he said. "We can continue being uncertain in the morning."

Harry put down his glasses. Lupin remained beside the sofa until he did.

Hermione and Draconia did not leave the classroom immediately. After Harry departed, the quiet became unbearable. Water spread across the floor between pieces of the basin. The moonstone powder had lost its light, leaving grey sediment upon the stones. Three chairs lay overturned.

Hermione stood beside the door. Draconia remained where Harry had left her.

"He flinched from me," she said.

Hermione closed her eyes.

"He was still experiencing the memory."

"He knew it was me."

"Yes."

"He still moved."

"Draco—"

"Do not explain it."

Hermione stopped. Draconia looked at her hands. Ink remained beneath her nails. It was not real. She knew that. The spell had created a sensation, not a substance. She scratched at it anyway.

Hermione crossed the room and caught her wrist. Draconia jerked away. The reaction mirrored Harry's. Both girls froze.

"I'm sorry," Hermione said.

Draconia closed her hands.

"We promised him we would stop."

"We tried."

"We failed."

"Yes."

"We knew it could happen."

"Yes."

Hermione did not defend the choice. Draconia looked towards the doorway.

"We should find him."

"He asked us not to follow."

"He was bleeding."

"He said he needed to go."

"He may become disoriented."

Hermione looked towards Harry's overturned chair. The Marauder's Map lay partly beneath it, where it must have slipped from his robes when the basin shattered. She picked it up. The parchment trembled in her hands. She opened it. Harry's name moved along the sixth floor. Another dot approached him.

Remus Lupin.

They watched the two names meet. Harry's stopped. Lupin's remained beside it.

"He has him," Hermione said.

Draconia did not relax. A silver wolf entered the classroom through the wall. Both girls drew their wands. The Patronus opened its mouth with Lupin's voice.

"Harry is safe. He needs space. Return to your common rooms. I will speak with you tomorrow."

The wolf dissolved. Draconia stared at the silver sparks.

"He knows."

"Yes."

"What will he do?"

"I don't know."

"He may tell Dumbledore."

"He should."

Draconia looked at her. Hermione folded the map.

"If the spell had harmed Harry magically, we would need help."

"And his memories?"

"No."

The answer came more firmly.

"They belong to him."

"We have already taken them."

Hermione's face crumpled.

"Yes."

They cleaned the classroom together. Hermione repaired the basin, though a silver crack remained around its centre. Draconia dried the floor. They returned the chairs to their positions and erased the three-circle diagram from the blackboard. No spell could remove what had entered them. When Hermione closed her eyes, she felt cupboard walls near her shoulders. When Draconia swallowed, she tasted soup from a bowl without a spoon.

They separated outside the library. Hermione climbed towards Gryffindor Tower. Draconia descended into the dungeons. Neither slept.

Lupin spoke to them separately the following morning.

Hermione went first. She entered his office at eight and emerged forty minutes later with red eyes and a written list of every magical risk they had failed to prepare for. Lupin had not deducted points. He had not raised his voice. That made the conversation worse. Draconia entered next. Lupin stood beside the window.

Harry was absent.

"Where is he?" she asked.

"Breakfast."

"Alone?"

"With Professor Snape."

Draconia winced.

"That is punishment."

"Severus insists it is nutrition."

"Father insists political blackmail is negotiation."

"Every family has its vocabulary."

Lupin gestured towards the chair. Draconia remained standing.

"Will you tell Dumbledore?"

"Do you believe I should?"

She disliked the question.

"I asked you."

"And I am asking whether you understand what happened."

"The containment failed."

"Before that."

Draconia looked away.

"Harry agreed."

"Did he want to?"

"He wanted us to stop asking."

"Is that the same?"

"No."

Lupin's face revealed none of his anger. Draconia wished he would shout. She knew how to defend herself from shouting.

"He could have left," she said.

"Yes."

"We explained reciprocal resonance."

"You explained what you understood."

"Yes."

"You did not understand enough."

"No."

Lupin moved towards his desk.

"Why did you use the spell?"

"He was disappearing."

"That is a description, not a reason."

Draconia thought of Harry falling from the sky. Harry lying in the stairwell. Harry asking whether she was alive while he held her wrist.

"I was afraid he would die while we respected his privacy."

Lupin nodded.

"I know you were frightened. That didn't make you ready to cast that spell."

Draconia's throat tightened.

"I know."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

"What would you do differently?"

"Ask an adult."

"Which one?"

The question stopped her.

"Professor Snape."

"Would Harry have agreed?"

"No."

"Then?"

"I don't know."

Lupin sat against the edge of his desk.

"Sometimes there isn't anything you can do to make it stop," he said. "You can listen. Keep him company, when he wants it. I know it doesn't feel like enough."

"He told us to go."

"Then go when he asks. You can still make sure he knows where to find you."

Draconia looked towards the empty sofa.

"Did he tell you what he saw?"

"No."

Relief and disappointment arrived together. Lupin noticed both.

"He did not need to," he said. "The question is what you will do with what you saw."

"Nothing."

"That is not possible."

Draconia looked at him.

"You will remember it," Lupin continued. "It will change how you understand him. You cannot undo that. You can choose not to use it as authority."

"I already did."

"In the classroom?"

"I told him he would hide in another cupboard."

Lupin closed his eyes briefly.

"He hurt me."

"Yes."

"So I hurt him."

"Yes."

Draconia waited for forgiveness to appear in the explanation. It did not.

"I am sorry," she said.

"Tell Harry."

"He will not want to hear it."

"Then write it. Apology is not a demand for an audience."

Lupin handed her a sheet of loose parchment. Draconia looked at it. Not bound. He had chosen it deliberately. Her eyes lifted.

He gave no indication of having noticed. She took the parchment.

Harry returned to Gryffindor Tower after lunch. No one stopped him in the corridors. No suits of armour shouted. The portrait hole opened before he finished giving Sir Cadogan the day's sixth password. The common room was empty except for Crookshanks.

The cat slept near the fire with one paw over his face. Two letters waited upon Harry's chair. One envelope was addressed in Hermione's familiar handwriting. The other bore a single word in sharp green ink.

Potter.

Harry sat. He opened Hermione's first.
