The storm withdrew before dawn. Rain continued dripping from the eaves and gargoyles, but sunlight had broken through the clouds by the time students entered the Great Hall. It spread across the long tables, flashed against silver plates and turned the enchanted ceiling into a pale blue September sky. The castle looked clean.

Nothing in the morning light suggested that Dementors waited beyond the gates. Harry sat at the Gryffindor table with a piece of toast cooling between his fingers. He had slept for less than an hour. The night remained inside him in fragments: the rain against the tower windows, the scrape of branches upon stone and Lily's voice entering every dream he managed to reach. Whenever Harry closed his eyes, he felt her body around his mind again. He remembered holding himself, loving himself and standing between a cot and death. No photograph had prepared him for that.

The people in Hagrid's album smiled, waved and laughed. They existed inside loops where nothing terrible was permitted to finish happening. James returned to the beginning of every broom flight. Lily turned towards the camera and smiled again. Harry had never known what their fear felt like. Now he knew too much.

"You haven't eaten that."

Hermione's voice pulled him back. Harry looked at the toast.

"I'm holding it."

"That is not eating."

"It's an early stage."

Hermione sat opposite him rather than beside him. Harry recognised the choice as deliberate. She had probably decided that proximity might feel like supervision after their conversation at the Leaky Cauldron. The distance did not prevent her from watching his plate. Harry took a bite.

"Progress."

"I didn't say anything."

"You didn't need to."

Hermione pushed a loose curl behind her ear. Her hair had not yet recovered from sleep, though the rest of her appeared fully prepared for the day. Books filled the bag beside her. Colour-coded tabs protruded from the uppermost volume, and three rolled timetables were secured beneath one strap. A fine gold chain disappeared beneath her collar. Harry noticed because it had not been there during summer.

"What's that?"

Hermione's hand went to her throat.

"What?"

"The chain."

She lowered her hand too quickly. "A necklace."

"I gathered."

"It was a present."

"From who?"

"Professor McGonagall."

Harry looked at her.

"That seems personal."

Hermione reached for her tea. "I can't talk about it."

"The box."

"Yes."

"What does it do?"

"I'm not allowed to discuss it."

"Does it help you attend three lessons at once?"

Her cup stopped before reaching her mouth. Harry sat straighter.

"It does."

"I did not say that."

"You made the face."

"What face?"

"The one I make when you catch me lying."

Hermione placed the cup down.

"I am not lying."

"You're avoiding."

"That is different."

"Draco's been teaching you."

Across the Great Hall, Draconia sat at the Slytherin table. Harry found her immediately. She occupied the place between Daphne Greengrass and an empty seat, pale hair distinct beneath the green-and-silver hangings. A bowl of porridge rested untouched before her. While Harry watched, Draconia looked up. Their eyes met across the room. Harry pointed subtly towards his toast.

Then towards her bowl. Draconia narrowed her eyes. She lifted her spoon. Harry took another bite. The exchange went unnoticed by most of the hall.

Hermione saw everything.

"You cannot monitor each other's breakfasts from opposite tables."

"We aren't monitoring."

"What would you call that?"

"Diplomacy."

Draconia ate one spoonful. Harry finished the toast. Both stopped. Hermione looked between them.

"That was ridiculous."

"You're only saying that because no one monitored your breakfast."

"I ate porridge."

"Did you?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"Before you arrived."

Harry looked at the bowls surrounding her. No empty porridge dish sat nearby. Hermione followed his gaze.

"Mine was taken."

"That sounds convenient."

"It was."

"Good."

Harry selected an apple and placed it in front of her. Hermione stared at it.

"What are you doing?"

"Diplomacy."

She opened her mouth. Then she closed it, picked up the apple and placed it inside her bag. Harry smiled. Hermione looked irritated. The expression reassured him more than gratitude would have.

Morning post arrived in a rush of wings. Owls descended through the enchanted ceiling carrying parcels, newspapers and letters. Students ducked beneath them while cups overturned and bacon disappeared from unattended plates. A tawny owl dropped the Daily Prophet beside Hermione. Sirius Black stared from the front page. Harry's appetite vanished. The headline occupied half the page.

BLACK SIGHTED IN NORTHERN VILLAGE

The photograph showed an empty road beneath rain. Every few seconds, a thin figure crossed the space behind a boarded shop and disappeared into an alleyway. The angle revealed little more than long dark hair and a torn coat.

Human.

Harry thought of the black dog upon the Leaky Cauldron roof. Hermione unfolded the paper.

"Same photograph as yesterday."

"The article is different."

Harry leaned closer. The Ministry had searched the village and found nothing. Two residents claimed Black asked for directions south. A third insisted he had not spoken but stood outside a closed post office for nearly an hour. The paper repeated that Black was armed, unstable and connected to the former Dark Lord. Nothing mentioned Harry.

"South," Hermione said.

"Hogwarts is north."

"He may have deliberately asked for the wrong direction."

"Or the witness lied."

"Or it wasn't him."

Harry looked towards the staff table. Professor Lupin sat beside Professor Flitwick. He held a cup in both hands and appeared to be listening while the Charms professor described something with energetic gestures. Lupin looked better than he had on the train. His robes remained patched, but his face had regained some colour. The scars across his cheek caught the morning light when he smiled. Professor Snape sat three places away.

He was not eating. His attention rested upon Lupin with cold, unbroken suspicion.

"Still specific," Harry murmured.

Hermione followed his gaze.

"Perhaps they know each other."

"He knew my parents."

"That doesn't mean Professor Snape knows him."

"Snape knew my mother."

Hermione lowered the paper. Harry had learned that much during first year. Snape's hatred for James Potter and complicated history with Lily existed somewhere beneath every conversation Harry had with him. If Snape and Lupin shared the same past, the school contained more of Harry's parents than anyone had told him. Professor Lupin looked up. His eyes found Harry.

For one heartbeat, Harry wondered if Lupin had sensed the attention. Then the professor gave him a small nod and returned to Flitwick. Snape saw the exchange. His mouth tightened.

"Definitely specific," Harry said.

The bell rang. Students rose. Hermione gathered her books with such urgency that one slipped from beneath her arm. Harry caught it.

An Introduction to Ancient Runes.

"First lesson is Transfiguration."

"I know."

"You don't need this."

"I might later."

"When?"

Hermione took the book. "Later."

Draconia met them in the Entrance Hall. Her porridge bowl had apparently failed to follow.

"You stopped eating," Harry said.

"You finished first."

"That was not the agreement."

"There was no agreement."

Hermione stepped between them. "Neither of you ate enough."

Draconia looked at the apple protruding from Hermione's bag.

"Is that yours?"

Hermione adjusted the flap to conceal it.

"No."

Harry smiled. Hermione walked towards the marble staircase without them. Draconia fell into step beside Harry.

"What did you do?"

"Diplomacy."

"That word does not mean what you think."

"I've had mixed instruction."

They followed Hermione upstairs. Professor McGonagall began third-year Transfiguration with animosity towards buttons. Twenty-four wooden ones had been arranged across the front desks. Some were round, others square, and one near Harry had been shaped like a flower. Their task was to turn each into a beetle, retain the original colour and then return it without damaging either form.

"The transformation must be complete," McGonagall said, pacing between the desks. "A beetle with buttonholes is not a beetle. A button capable of locomotion is not clothing."

Neville looked towards the object already crawling across his parchment. He raised one hand. Professor McGonagall closed her eyes briefly.

"Reverse it, Mr Longbottom."

Harry's first attempt produced six legs and no interest in movement. His second ran across the desk while remaining flat enough to fasten a shirt. Draconia watched from the neighbouring table.

"That is disturbing."

"It's progress."

"It's still a button."

"It moved."

"The castle moves. You do not wear it."

Harry touched his wand to the creature. The button-beetle sprang into the air and landed upon Hermione's notes. Hermione stopped writing. The creature crawled across a paragraph concerning partial transformation. Harry reached.

It darted beneath her hand. Draconia lifted her wand.

"Don't kill it," Hermione said.

"It is not properly alive."

"That does not mean—"

Draconia cast. The beetle became a button beneath Hermione's palm. Silence followed. Hermione lifted her hand. A blue button rested upon the parchment, perfectly round and entirely ordinary.

Professor McGonagall stopped beside them. Her gaze moved from the button to Draconia.

"Whose work?"

"Potter's," Draconia said.

Harry looked at her. McGonagall lifted one eyebrow.

"The transformation began as mine."

"And the reversal?"

Harry considered lying. Professor McGonagall's eyebrow rose farther.

"Draconia's."

"Three points to Slytherin."

Draconia inclined her head.

"And two from Gryffindor for allowing an incompletely transformed object to cross another student's desk."

Harry stared.

"It ran away."

"Then I suggest improving its obedience before releasing it."

McGonagall continued onwards. Draconia looked smug.

Harry lowered his voice. "Cheap victory."

"You created the opportunity."

Hermione smoothed the damaged section of her notes.

"Could both of you avoid turning my parchment into a battlefield?"

"No promises," Harry said.

By the end of the lesson, his beetle possessed a rounded shell, six legs and no buttonholes. It returned to its original form with only one minor complication: it retained antennae. McGonagall regarded them over the rim of her spectacles.

"Better."

"Can I leave those?"

"No."

"They're useful."

"For what?"

Harry looked at the button.

"I'll remove them."

Hermione completed the exercise first. Draconia completed it second and claimed first place on technical grounds because Hermione's beetle differed slightly in shade from the original wood. The bell rang while their argument was still developing. Students packed their bags. Harry moved slowly.

Professor McGonagall stood behind her desk sorting essays. The torn Hogsmeade form occupied the inner pocket of Harry's robes, folded so many times that the edges had softened. He had promised to ask. Hermione remained near the door. Draconia noticed Harry's hand touch the pocket and stopped beside her. Neither left.

McGonagall looked up.

"Something else?"

Harry approached the desk.

"My Hogsmeade form."

He removed both halves and placed them in front of her. McGonagall examined the tear. Her mouth tightened.

"I received Mr Malfoy's letter."

"Which one?" Draconia asked.

"Several."

"That does not narrow it."

"The first eight were about who could sign the form. The last three were about Dumbledore."

Harry looked towards Draconia.

"Your mother removed three."

"She did not review the later correspondence."

Professor McGonagall joined the pieces.

"Your uncle refused permission?"

"He agreed first."

McGonagall's eyes lifted.

"Then tore it after I made his sister float."

Hermione stepped forward. "He tore it before that."

"I know."

"That matters."

"Miss Granger," McGonagall said, "I am speaking with Mr Potter."

Hermione stopped.

"Yes, Professor."

Harry looked down at the parchment.

"Can someone else sign?"

"Only a parent or legal guardian."

"Lucius is a governor."

"He is not your guardian."

"The Grangers?"

"The same restriction applies."

"What about you?"

The question escaped. McGonagall's expression changed. Harry wished immediately that he had chosen someone less alarming.

"I mean—you're my Head of House."

"I understand what you meant."

Her voice had softened. That was not encouraging. Professor McGonagall folded the pieces carefully.

"I cannot authorise the visits without a legal signature."

"Even if you know why Vernon refused?"

"I do."

"And you know he lied to Aunt Marge about Hogwarts."

"Yes."

"And you know he locked my wand away when he wasn't supposed to."

A muscle moved in McGonagall's jaw.

"Yes."

"Then why does his decision count more than yours?"

The classroom quietened around the question. McGonagall removed her glasses. For the first time, Harry saw exhaustion beneath her sternness. Fine lines marked the skin around her eyes.

"Because the school requires guardian consent for students leaving its protective boundaries."

"He isn't trying to protect me."

"No."

"Then it's a bad rule."

"Yes."

Hermione inhaled. Draconia's attention sharpened. Harry had expected defence. A lecture about policy, responsibility or safety. McGonagall's agreement removed the argument and left only disappointment.

"I will continue speaking with the Headmaster," she said. "Until permission is secured, however, you may not visit Hogsmeade."

Harry nodded. McGonagall placed the form inside her desk.

"You're keeping it?"

"I am keeping it because your uncle initially agreed."

"For Lucius?"

"For anyone who needs to see it."

Draconia appeared satisfied. Harry did not. He put his hands into his pockets.

"All right."

"Mr Potter."

He looked up.

"I am sorry."

McGonagall rarely apologised. The words made the refusal heavier. Harry forced a smile.

"It's only a village."

Hermione looked at him. Draconia did too. Neither believed him. They left together. The first Care of Magical Creatures lesson took place beyond the greenhouses.

Hagrid had cleared a broad patch of grass near the forest and arranged buckets along one side. Each bucket contained soil, old coins and several glittering objects Harry suspected had been removed from the school's lost-property cupboard. Twenty wooden crates stood beneath a canvas awning. Something scratched inside them. Hagrid waited beside the crates wearing an enormous coat despite the mild weather. His beard had been combed. The attempt left one half smoother than the other. Students approached cautiously.

Slytherins gathered near the rear. Gryffindors occupied the front because Hermione walked there and everyone else failed to argue quickly enough. Hagrid lifted both hands.

"Right. Welcome."

No one spoke. He looked towards Harry. Harry gave an encouraging nod. Hagrid cleared his throat.

"Care of Magical Creatures is about respect. Yeh respect the creature, learn what it needs, don' assume it thinks like yeh do, and most things won't take a finger."

Draconia looked at the crates.

"Most?"

Hagrid ignored her.

"Thought we'd start with summat manageable."

He opened the nearest crate. A long black snout appeared. The creature climbed out with bright eyes, dense fur and broad front paws. It stood upon two legs, sniffed the air and immediately looked towards Draconia's hands.

"A Niffler," Hermione said.

Hagrid smiled.

"Exactly. Clever little things. Find anything shiny from half a field away."

The Niffler moved. It crossed the space between Hagrid and Draconia with alarming speed, climbed her robes and disappeared inside her cloak. Draconia froze. Everyone stared. A muffled rustling travelled around her waist.

"Remove it."

Hagrid approached. "Stay still."

"I am being robbed."

The Niffler emerged from her collar holding the silver fastening from one glove. Students laughed. Draconia's face became cold enough to freeze the lake. Harry caught the creature before it leapt away. It twisted in his arms, claws digging into his sleeves.

"That belongs to her."

The Niffler shoved the silver piece into a pouch across its stomach. Harry stared.

"It has a pocket."

"Pouch," Hermione corrected.

"This feels secondary."

Hagrid took the creature and pressed gently beneath its forelegs. The Niffler released the glove fastening with visible reluctance. Draconia held out one hand. Hagrid returned it.

"Good example," he said. "Always remove jewellery before handling 'em."

"You could have mentioned that before opening the crate."

"I thought it was obvious."

Draconia looked towards the class. Half the students still wore watches, rings or buckles.

"It was not."

The lesson improved once everyone removed anything valuable. Hagrid divided them into groups and released four Nifflers into separate patches of soft earth. The task was to identify which creature found the most buried objects without allowing it to steal from classmates. Harry, Hermione and Draconia shared one. The Niffler ignored the prepared soil and attempted to climb inside Draconia's bag.

"It has formed a preference," Harry said.

"I dislike it."

The Niffler placed both paws upon her shoe and gazed upward.

Hermione crouched. "He's probably detecting the buckles."

"They are beneath the leather."

"He can smell metal."

"That is invasive."

Harry held a brass token near the creature. The Niffler turned instantly.

"Here."

It followed Harry towards the patch. Hermione released it. The creature dug with astonishing speed. Soil flew behind it, coating Harry's shoes and the lower edge of Draconia's robes. Within seconds, it emerged holding a silver spoon. Hagrid marked one point upon the board. Their Niffler found two coins, a broken necklace, three buttons and a small brass key.

Then it discovered Pansy's bracelet. Pansy screamed. She chased the creature across the grass. The Niffler ran beneath Millicent Bulstrode, between Neville's legs and over a bucket. Coins scattered in every direction. Six other Nifflers abandoned their assigned patches and joined the pursuit. The lesson became chaos. Hagrid shouted instructions.

Hermione attempted a Summoning Charm before remembering magic was not permitted around unsecured creatures. Draconia cornered one against a tree. Harry tackled another before it entered the forest. The Niffler rolled in his arms, pouch jingling.

"Give it back."

It bit his cuff.

"I'm beginning to understand Draco."

By the time every creature returned to its crate, the grass looked excavated. Pansy recovered her bracelet, though the clasp had been chewed. Neville found three Sickles no one remembered burying. Hagrid stood before the class, hair full of soil.

"Well."

Harry waited. Hagrid looked towards the damaged field.

"Good first lesson."

Draconia brushed dirt from her robes. "By what standard?"

"No one lost a finger."

Hagrid sounded genuinely proud. Harry laughed. The class returned towards the castle discussing the Nifflers. Even the Slytherins admitted the lesson had been interesting, though Pansy intended to send the bracelet repair bill to the school. Hagrid called after the trio.

"Stay a minute?"

They waited while the others crossed the grounds. The sun had warmed the grass. Beyond the outer boundary, dark figures stood at intervals along the distant road.

Dementors.

Harry avoided looking directly at them. Hagrid shut the final crate.

"Heard what happened on the train."

Harry's shoulders tightened. Hermione glanced at him.

"Hagrid—"

"Not askin' fer details," Hagrid said quickly. "Only wanted ter see yeh."

"You saw us at the station."

"In the rain. Couldn' see me own boots."

Hagrid's eyes moved over all three. He lingered upon Harry, then Draconia.

"Yeh all right?"

"Yes," Harry said.

Draconia answered at the same time. "Perfectly."

Hermione looked towards the castle. Hagrid folded his arms.

"Good."

He did not believe them. Harry waited for questions. None came. Hagrid kicked soil over one of the holes.

"Tea after lessons?"

Harry looked towards Hermione. She had three classes scheduled.

"When?"

"Four?"

Hermione's expression shifted. Harry saw calculation begin.

"Can't," she said. "Arithmancy."

"Your timetable says Muggle Studies," Draconia replied.

Hermione looked at her.

"I changed it."

"When?"

"This morning."

"Why?"

"Professor Vector altered the introductory schedule."

Draconia's eyes narrowed. Harry intervened.

"Five?"

Hermione hesitated again.

"Library."

"You scheduled that?" Harry asked.

"We have homework."

"We've attended three lessons."

"Which produced homework."

Hagrid looked between them.

"What about six?"

"Dinner," Draconia said.

"Seven?"

"Potions reading," Hermione answered.

Hagrid scratched his beard.

"Tomorrow?"

The question created another round of calculations. Harry looked at him.

"We'll come tonight at seven."

Hermione opened her mouth.

"We'll bring the reading."

"You won't get much done."

"We'll manage."

Draconia inclined her head. "A compelling slogan."

Hagrid smiled.

"Seven."

They left him beside the Niffler crates. Halfway to the castle, Harry looked back. Hagrid was still watching. Hermione's first journey through time occurred on Tuesday at nine fourteen in the morning. She attended Ancient Runes in a classroom on the sixth floor.

Professor Babbling spent the first fifteen minutes explaining the development of Elder Futhark and the dangers of treating translation as direct substitution. Hermione sat in the front row, took three pages of notes and watched the classroom clock approach quarter past nine. At nine thirteen, she packed silently. Professor Babbling paused.

"Miss Granger?"

"I'm sorry. Professor McGonagall approved my leaving at this time."

The professor looked towards a note upon her desk.

"Of course."

Every student watched Hermione walk out. She closed the door. The corridor stood empty. Her heart had begun beating too quickly. Professor McGonagall's instructions required a secure location. Hermione crossed to an unused classroom, checked beneath the desks and locked the door.

The hourglass rested beneath her robes. She pulled the chain free. One turn. Only one. Hermione rotated the hourglass.

The world moved. Not backwards exactly. It folded. Light stretched across the room. Dust rose from the floor and returned to the rafters. Sounds entered her ears in reverse: footsteps unfalling, voices collapsing into breath and a bell pulling its final note back into metal. Her stomach lurched.

Then everything stopped. Hermione stood inside the same classroom. The clock above the door read eight fourteen. For several seconds, she could not breathe. Her hands gripped the desk. The room leaned beneath her.

She had lived the hour. It still existed ahead. Somewhere in Gryffindor Tower, an earlier Hermione was preparing to leave for Ancient Runes. Within minutes, that girl would walk through the same corridors carrying the same books and believing herself singular. Hermione felt suddenly divided from her own body. The sensation frightened her.

She pushed it aside. Divination began at nine. Hermione remained inside the classroom until eight thirty, then slipped into a side passage and climbed towards the North Tower by a route she had not used earlier. At eight forty-seven, she saw Harry. He walked two floors below beside Draconia. Both were heading towards Transfiguration, unaware Hermione watched through the gap in the stairs.

Harry said something. Draconia laughed. Hermione could not hear the words. An unexpected loneliness caught her. They were speaking to a version of Hermione who still sat in Gryffindor Tower.

No.

That was wrong. Hermione had not been with them during that walk. She remembered leaving earlier for Runes. Harry and Draconia had been alone. The knowledge should not have mattered. It did.

Hermione stepped back before they looked up. At nine, she entered Divination. Professor Trelawney's classroom pressed heat, perfume and incense around her. Curtains covered every window. Round tables filled the space, each surrounded by soft chairs which made note-taking unnecessarily difficult. Harry and Draconia arrived together. Harry saw Hermione already seated.

He stopped.

"How did you get here?"

Hermione's mouth went dry.

"I walked."

"You were going to Runes."

"No."

Draconia looked at her.

"You told Hagrid Arithmancy."

"I changed it."

"Again?"

Professor Trelawney entered through a beaded curtain before the questions continued. The lesson began. Hermione spent it studying tea leaves while the previous hour occupied part of her mind. She remembered Runes. She remembered Professor Babbling's lecture, leaving at nine fourteen and turning the hourglass. At the same time, the castle had contained an earlier version of her walking towards that lecture. Two Hermiones had occupied Hogwarts.

One still did. During the final fifteen minutes of Divination, the other Hermione would be leaving Runes and turning the hourglass. The circle had already happened. Hermione understood the theory. Understanding did not make it feel natural.

At ten, she left Divination with Harry and Draconia. The day continued. She had gained an hour. By lunch, Hermione felt as if she had lost several. Potions began badly.

Professor Snape entered the dungeon classroom with the silent speed of an approaching shadow. Conversations died before he reached his desk.

"Third year," he said, looking across the Gryffindor and Slytherin students. "Against all expectation, some of you have survived long enough to reach it."

His gaze stopped upon Harry. Harry folded his arms. Snape's eyes moved on.

"Today, you will prepare a Befuddlement Draught. If brewed correctly, it encourages recklessness, confusion and emotional volatility."

Ron lifted one hand.

"Does it make people drunk?"

Snape looked at him.

"No, Mr Weasley. Unlike alcohol, the potion's effects eventually end."

Several Slytherins laughed. Snape turned towards the board. The instructions appeared in white chalk. The potion required exact timing. Ingredients had to be added at intervals of thirty, forty-five and ninety seconds. If stirred too early, the steam caused temporary memory loss. Too late, and the draught became combustible. Draconia read the instructions once.

Her face lost colour. Harry noticed. She removed a small notebook from her bag and placed it beside the cauldron.

"What's that?" he whispered.

"Timing."

"There's a clock."

"I prefer a written record."

Hermione arrived three minutes late. Harry stared. Hermione was never late. She entered carrying books for Ancient Runes, Divination and Transfiguration. Her hair clung damply to her forehead despite the cold corridor. Snape looked towards her.

"Miss Granger."

"I'm sorry, Professor."

"Did another classroom refuse to release you?"

A few students laughed. Hermione's cheeks reddened.

"No."

"Five points from Gryffindor."

She took the place beside Harry. Draconia examined the books.

"You attended both."

Hermione opened her potion kit. "Not now."

"How?"

"Draco."

"Miss Malfoy," Snape said from across the room, "if Miss Granger's tardiness fascinates you, I suggest preserving the questions until your own potion is less likely to explode."

Draconia turned back.

"Yes, Professor."

Their cauldrons heated. For twelve minutes, everything proceeded correctly. Harry crushed sneezewort. Hermione measured scurvy-grass. Draconia recorded each addition and crossed it from her list. At the ninety-second stage, the potion darkened. Not black.

Deep purple. Draconia froze. The surface moved. Steam formed narrow lines above it, curling through the air like writing. Harry saw the change before Hermione did.

"Draco."

She did not answer. Her eyes fixed upon the steam. The quill in her hand pressed against the notebook, leaving a spreading blot.

"Twenty seconds," Hermione said.

Draconia remained still.

"Draco."

Harry touched her wrist. She recoiled so violently that the quill flew across the table. The cauldron rocked. Hermione caught the handle.

"Ten seconds."

Harry seized the next ingredient.

"How much?"

"Two pinches."

He dropped it into the potion. Hermione stirred. The draught flashed orange, hissed and settled into the required blue. Draconia stared at it. Snape stopped beside their table.

His gaze travelled from the finished potion to the ink spreading across Draconia's notebook.

"Acceptable," he said.

No praise entered the word. He moved on. Draconia's breathing remained shallow. Hermione lowered her voice.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

"You missed the—"

"I know."

Draconia closed the notebook. Ink had soaked through four pages. Harry picked up the fallen quill. The feather was green. The nib carried ordinary black ink.

"It looked like writing," he said.

Draconia's eyes flashed.

"Do not."

Hermione looked between them. Understanding arrived. She said nothing. The rest of the lesson passed under strained control. Draconia completed every stage herself. She refused assistance and checked each time three times before making an addition. Their finished draught earned ten points for Slytherin.

When the bell rang, Snape spoke without looking up.

"Miss Malfoy. Remain."

Draconia stopped. Harry and Hermione did too. Snape lifted his head.

"I did not request an audience."

"We're waiting outside," Harry said.

"You may wait in the Great Hall."

"The corridor is closer."

Snape's eyes narrowed. Harry packed his bag. Hermione dragged him through the door before points were deducted. They stood in the dungeon corridor. Torches burned blue along the walls. Students disappeared towards lunch, their voices fading up the stairs.

Ten minutes passed. Hermione leaned against the stone.

"She attended two lessons," Draconia said from memory.

Harry looked at her.

"Hermione."

"I know."

"How?"

"I don't know."

"You think the chain does it."

"Probably."

Hermione touched the place beneath her collar.

"She looked ill."

"So does Draco."

The dungeon door remained shut. Harry shifted his bag.

"Do we ask?"

Hermione's expression tightened.

"We're practising waiting."

"Whose terrible idea was that?"

"Hers."

"That explains it."

The door opened. Draconia emerged. Her face revealed nothing. Snape stood behind her. His attention moved towards Harry and Hermione.

"Predictable."

Draconia shut the door. They began walking.

"What did he say?" Harry asked.

She continued straight ahead.

"You lasted three seconds," Hermione murmured.

"I waited ten minutes."

"That was physical waiting."

"There are categories?"

Draconia turned at the stairway.

"Professor Snape said exhaustion damages precision."

Harry looked at the shadows beneath her eyes.

"He's not wrong."

"I did not ask."

"What else?"

Draconia glanced towards Hermione.

"He asked whether I had experienced missing time."

Hermione's hand went to the gold chain again.

"What did you say?"

"No."

"Is that true?" Harry asked.

"Yes."

Draconia's response was immediate. Then she looked away.

"I remember every part of last night."

Harry heard the distinction. Hermione did too. Draconia continued upstairs. Harry and Hermione followed. At the Great Hall entrance, she stopped.

"He said control is not recovered by pretending it never failed."

"That sounds almost useful," Harry said.

Draconia's mouth tightened. "It was delivered unpleasantly."

"Snape would consider that important."

Lupin's office occupied a corner near the Defence classroom. It had not yet settled into belonging to him. Books remained stacked inside open crates. A kettle sat upon the windowsill beside three unmatched cups. The walls were bare except for a calendar, a map of Scotland and one small photograph turned face-down upon the desk. A glass tank stood near the window. Inside it, a grindylow pressed webbed fingers against the glass and watched Harry enter.

"Friendly?" Harry asked.

"No."

"Good."

Lupin closed the door. He placed the kettle upon a small iron stand and lit the flame with his wand. Then he opened a tin.

Biscuits.

Harry sat.

"I'm not—"

"Hungry," Lupin finished. "I remember."

He placed three biscuits upon a plate and left them between the cups. No instruction followed. Harry looked at them. Lupin organised the books while water heated.

"You said there may be a way to fight Dementors."

"There is."

"The Patronus Charm."

Hermione had found the name in one of her books. Harry had not yet understood the description.

"It conjures a protective force," Lupin explained. "At its strongest, it takes an animal form. The Patronus stands between a caster and the Dementor, providing something the creature cannot consume."

"What?"

"Whatever it is that makes you want to keep going. The Dementor tries to take that from you."

Harry thought of Draconia's boggart.

"Can everyone cast one?"

"No."

"Can you?"

"Yes."

"What shape?"

Lupin poured hot water into the teapot.

"That is generally considered personal."

"Why?"

"The form may reveal attachments, character or change."

"That sounds useful."

"To intrusive thirteen-year-olds?"

"Especially."

Lupin sat opposite him. His cup had a crack near the handle repaired with silver.

"The spell is advanced," he said. "Many qualified wizards cannot produce more than light."

"But you'll teach me."

"If Professor Dumbledore agrees."

Harry's face fell. Lupin lifted one hand.

"I asked this morning."

"And?"

"He agreed."

Hope rose quickly.

"When do we start?"

"Next Thursday."

"Why not today?"

"Because you nearly collapsed in a corridor."

"I sat down."

"With limited choice."

Harry reached for one biscuit. He broke it in half.

"What do I need?"

"A memory capable of sustaining genuine happiness."

Harry stopped. Lupin watched.

"That is where most students encounter difficulty."

"I have happy memories."

"I'm sure."

"I do."

The defensiveness surprised him. Lupin did not challenge it. Harry thought of flying. The trio laughing upon the train. Hermione and Draconia beneath the Christmas tree during first year. Hagrid's hut. Narcissa's shortbread. Mrs Granger saying hello, love. Each memory contained warmth. Each also contained the fear that the person inside it could disappear.

"What if it changes?" Harry asked.

"The memory?"

"When you think about it."

Lupin folded his hands around the cup.

"Then we find another."

"What if they all do?"

The question escaped more quietly than Harry intended. Lupin's gaze settled upon him.

"The memory doesn't have to be perfect," he said. "It has to be strong enough to hold on to when the Dementor comes near. We'll try a few. You don't have to find the right one tonight."

Harry looked at the biscuit.

"That sounds worse."

"It is certainly less convenient."

"Lucius says truth is like that."

"Lucius Malfoy and I have now agreed twice in one conversation. I may need Madam Pomfrey."

Harry smiled. He ate the first half of the biscuit. Lupin looked towards the window.

"You asked why the Dementor affected you more strongly than the others."

Harry nodded.

"It did not react to weakness," Lupin said. "Dementors seek wounds. People who have lived through terrible things may give them more to find."

"That sounds like a polite version of weakness."

"No."

Lupin's answer was immediate.

"Surviving harm teaches the mind and body to recognise danger. Sometimes they continue recognising it after the danger has passed. That is what happened when you heard the bench."

Harry looked towards the office door.

"You think I'm imagining things."

"I think your body remembered before you could tell it where you were."

The explanation settled differently from everything Harry had told himself. Not cowardice. Not loss of control.

Memory.

"Can it stop?"

"Yes."

Lupin's voice carried certainty. Harry looked at him.

"How?"

"Slowly."

"That isn't very magical."

"No."

Lupin selected a biscuit.

"Magic is often better at repairing bones than convincing someone they are safe."

Harry thought of his unscarred arm.

"What if they aren't safe?"

"Then we teach the difference between present danger and remembered danger."

"And Sirius Black?"

"Present."

The word ended the comfort. Harry's fingers tightened. Lupin saw.

"He will not reach you easily inside Hogwarts."

"You said he was your friend."

"Yes."

"Did you believe he betrayed them?"

Lupin looked into his tea.

"For twelve years."

"Do you still?"

A pause. Harry heard the grindylow move inside its tank.

"I do not know what I believe," Lupin said.

The honesty surprised him.

"Everyone else seems sure."

"Certainty can become habit."

"What was he like?"

Lupin's gaze moved towards the face-down photograph.

"Loud."

Harry smiled faintly. "Lucius said that."

"He would."

"Reckless."

"Frequently."

"Arrogant."

"Often."

"Deliberately offensive."

A real smile touched Lupin's face.

"He would have considered that praise."

Harry looked at him carefully.

"You miss him."

Lupin's smile vanished.

"I miss who I believed he was."

The sentence carried twelve years inside it. Harry understood enough not to press. Lupin pushed the plate closer. Harry ate the second half of the biscuit.

"Thursday," Lupin said.

"What time?"

"Seven."

"I'm meant to meet Hagrid."

"Eight?"

"Hermione scheduled library."

"Miss Granger schedules your evenings?"

"She tries."

Lupin considered.

"Half past seven. We'll begin with theory."

Harry nodded. He stood. His legs held. At the door, he looked back.

"Professor?"

"Yes?"

"My mother's first word before the curse was my name."

Lupin's face changed. Harry did not know why he had said it. Perhaps because Lupin had known her. Perhaps because the memory felt too large to keep only between Harry, Hermione and Draconia.

"She chose me," Harry said.

Lupin's eyes shone.

"Yes."

One word. No hesitation. Harry looked at him.

Lupin continued, "Lily always chose people. Especially when choosing them cost something."

Harry's throat tightened. He opened the door.

"Thursday."

"Thursday," Lupin agreed.

Harry left. Hermione and Draconia were waiting outside the History of Magic classroom. The lesson had ended twenty minutes earlier. Hermione held Harry's bag. Draconia held the assignment Binns apparently issued without noticing Harry's absence.

Both looked angry. The effect should have been frightening. Harry found it oddly reassuring.

"Where were you?" Hermione demanded.

"With Lupin."

"Why?"

"He found me."

"Where?"

"Sitting down."

Draconia's expression sharpened. "Where?"

"On a floor."

"You collapsed."

"I sat."

"You left class," Hermione said.

"Binns didn't mind."

"Binns didn't notice."

"Exactly."

Draconia stepped closer.

"Why were you sitting on the floor?"

Harry looked between them. Waiting had lasted less than a week. Their technique remained poor. He could lie.

Instead, he said, "Someone moved a bench. It sounded like the Chamber."

Hermione's anger vanished. Draconia's face became still. Harry took his bag from Hermione.

"Lupin said my body remembered before I did."

Neither spoke.

"He's going to teach me the Patronus Charm."

"What?" Hermione asked.

"To fight Dementors."

"That is advanced magic."

"I've been told."

"When?"

"Thursday."

"We should come," Draconia said.

Harry shook his head. "First lessons are individual."

"How convenient."

"He knew my parents."

"That does not make him trustworthy," Draconia replied.

Hermione frowned. "He protected us on the train."

"So did Lockhart during first year, accidentally."

"He apologised after the boggart," Harry said.

Draconia stopped. Harry looked at her.

"You don't have to trust him. I think I do."

The statement surprised all three of them. Draconia studied his face. At last, she nodded.

"If he attempts murder, shout."

"Useful."

"We will be nearby," Hermione said.

Harry looked towards her.

"Nearby where?"

"The library."

"During my private lesson?"

"We already planned to study."

"At half past seven on Thursday?"

"Yes."

Draconia looked at Hermione. "You had not."

"I have now."

Harry started walking. They followed.

"Are you going to watch the corridor?" he asked.

"No," Hermione said.

"Yes," Draconia answered.

Harry smiled. For the first time since the train, Thursday felt like something worth reaching.
