8

On the morning the other schools arrived Hogwarts had been transformed.

The suits of armour received their polish before breakfast, filling the corridors with the sharp smell of metal cleaner and a chorus of indignant creaks. Portraits were instructed to remain inside their frames and stop offering directions which led visitors through walls. Filch spent forty minutes removing a stain from the Entrance Hall, only for Peeves to replace it with three more and a verse concerning international cooperation. The verse followed him up two staircases.

House-elves replaced faded tapestries. Brass door handles shone brightly enough to return distorted faces. Someone persuaded the suits of armour outside the Great Hall to stand in matching poses, though the one at the end continued lifting its visor to complain that nobody had polished behind its ears.

The castle submitted to the attention with poor grace. Staircases changed direction immediately after being labelled. Two freshly washed windows developed handprints on the outside of the seventh floor. By midmorning, a portrait of a seventeenth-century headmaster had begun directing every foreign-looking student towards a cupboard because he considered it amusing.

Even the weather appeared to have been warned. The rain stopped shortly after dawn, and by lunch the clouds had lifted from the mountains, leaving a pale, cold sky above grounds turned bronze by late October. Sunlight flashed from every newly washed window; below them, the lake lay dark and still, with only the narrowest fringe of ripples disturbing its banks.

Wind gathered fallen leaves against the courtyard walls and carried the smell of wet earth through open doors. Far beyond the greenhouses, the mountains had acquired their first white edges of snow. Hogwarts looked polished, ancient and faintly startled by its own reflection.

Nobody concentrated in class. Quills paused above unfinished sentences whenever footsteps passed a door, and even the students usually sensible enough to hide their distraction had begun watching the windows. Professor McGonagall abandoned the Transfiguration lecture after Dean turned a guinea fowl into an object resembling the Bulgarian Minister.

"You will use the remaining twenty minutes," she said, vanishing the result, "to ensure your robes are clean, your behaviour is civilised and your knowledge of this school's staircases sufficient to prevent directing foreign students into the third-floor lavatory."

Seamus looked offended. "That happened once."

"It happened this morning."

Released at last by the bell, Harry and Hermione met Draconia in the courtyard with an hour remaining before assembly. She wore a new winter cloak clasped at the throat with silver, its folds falling neatly even in the wind. Hermione had attempted to flatten her hair and produced greater volume. Harry's own robes contained a crease which neither girl seemed able to remove without transfiguring part of him, and he was beginning to suspect they considered that an acceptable price.

They had spent the previous weeks practising beside Cedric whenever homework and weather allowed. Most sessions ended with Cedric and Harry racing; the rest ended with Hermione explaining why their definition of defensive training was inadequate while Draconia improved the spells neither boy had realised she was watching. The Tournament still belonged to other people then—an interesting danger contained behind an age line and Cedric's decision to meet it.

Today, the castle seemed to understand that containment was ending.

"Stand still," Hermione said.

"You've tried three spells."

"The fabric is resisting."

"I respect its principles."

Draconia caught the front of his robe, and Harry stopped moving rather more readily than he had for Hermione's spells. With a few careful movements, she smoothed the crease beneath her palm, adjusted his collar and picked away a thread near his shoulder. Her knuckles brushed the hollow beneath his throat. Harry became aware of the courtyard in inconvenient detail: cold stone beneath his shoes, Hermione's wand still raised and Draconia standing close enough that the silver clasp of her cloak reflected green in his glasses.

"There."

Hermione stared. "You didn't use magic."

"A revolutionary technique."

Harry's gaze dropped to her hand, which rested against his chest for a moment after the crease had gone. She withdrew it without the haste which might have suggested embarrassment, yet soon enough to leave him wondering whether he had imagined the pause. He could think of no sensible reason to ask.

"You're both strange," Hermione said.

"You attacked me with steam," Harry replied.

"For presentation."

The bell rang across the grounds. Students began gathering before the castle.

They assembled by House in four uneven lines stretching from the oak doors towards the lawn. First-years occupied the front and seventh-years the rear, which placed Harry and Hermione several rows from Draconia. Professor McGonagall moved between them straightening hats, silencing arguments and removing a Fanged Frisbee from Fred Weasley's sleeve without looking.

Dumbledore stood at the top of the steps beside the staff, while Moody kept several feet apart from everyone else. His magical eye surveyed the road, sky and lake in rotation, never resting long enough for Harry to decide which he considered the greatest threat. Once, it stopped upon Harry himself. He had grown accustomed to that, and disliked the fact more each time he noticed it.

At five past five, nothing happened. By ten past, the first-years were shifting from foot to foot, pressing closer to one another as the evening cold found its way through their formal robes. Breath showed white above the lines, briefly obscuring the faces of students craning to see the road.

Harry flexed his fingers inside his sleeves. Beside him, Hermione's teeth caught briefly upon her lower lip whenever McGonagall turned away. Across the gap between House lines, Draconia stood beside Daphne with her chin lifted against the cold. She met Harry's eyes once and gave the smallest shrug, as though international magical education had already failed by arriving late.

"Perhaps Beauxbatons rejected our staircase standards," Hermione whispered.

"International incident," Harry replied.

Then Lavender Brown pointed towards the sky, and Harry followed her hand to something enormous crossing the mountains. At first he thought it was a dragon: wings moved against the sunset, too broad and slow for any bird, while gold flashed beneath the descending shape. Around him, the restless shifting stopped.

A shadow passed over the lawn. Hundreds of faces tilted upwards together. The first heavy beat of wings reached them several seconds after Harry saw it, a low concussion which disturbed the bare branches beside the drive and sent the remaining leaves spiralling into the air.

The object resolved into a pale blue carriage the size of a house, drawn through the air by twelve winged horses. Each horse possessed a white-gold coat and stood larger than Hagrid's hut door. Their manes streamed behind them. Hooves struck nothing and produced thunder.

Students ducked as the carriage swept overhead, the rush of displaced air lifting hat brims and tugging at cloaks. The horses landed first, sending a shudder through the earth beneath Harry's feet. Behind them, the carriage wheels struck the ground and carved two deep tracks across the grass before the whole magnificent contraption stopped thirty feet from the entrance.

The nearest horse tossed its head. Its breath streamed silver through jewelled harness, and one dinner-plate hoof sank several inches into the lawn. Hagrid made a sound of pure admiration from the staff line. Professor Sprout looked at the torn grass and made a very different one.

A golden crest shone upon the carriage door: two crossed wands above three stars. When it opened, the headmistress of Beauxbatons emerged ahead of her delegation. Madame Maxime stood nearly as tall as Hagrid, with black satin robes falling from her shoulders to the ground. Her dark hair had been drawn into a knot at the base of her neck, and opals flashed at her ears as she inclined her head towards Dumbledore. The students nearest the steps leaned back to look at her properly.

Hagrid forgot to close his mouth. Harry saw Hermione notice.

"Don't," he whispered.

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"I hate that everyone has named those."

The students followed Madame Maxime in pairs. They wore pale blue silk despite the cold. Several wrapped cloaks tightly around themselves and looked at the Scottish castle as though weather represented a personal discourtesy. Their shoes barely marked the wet lawn. A faint fragrance followed them—orange blossom, woodsmoke and something Harry could not identify—and for a moment the Hogwarts students in heavy black robes appeared badly dressed for their own welcome. Fleur Delacour stepped down near the middle of the group.

Harry recognised something before knowing what.

Silver-blonde hair fell to her waist, catching the last of the evening light even beneath the carriage's shadow. Her face carried a beauty too precise to seem accidental, and the nearest boys became quiet with a suddenness which made the silence noticeable. Harry heard someone behind him forget the end of a sentence.

It was not the same as the Veela at the World Cup. No warmth pressed against Harry's thoughts; no sudden urge demanded he climb anything tall or humiliating. Instead, attention simply arranged itself around Fleur. People looked once, then again, and seemed annoyed to discover their eyes had made the decision without them.

Across the lines, Draconia looked towards Harry. He kept both feet firmly on the ground, which seemed a perfectly respectable improvement upon the World Cup, but her eyebrow rose all the same. He lifted his hands in innocence. Beside him, Hermione watched the exchange with an expression which suggested that his defence had failed before he offered it.

"You lasted nearly four seconds," she murmured.

"I was identifying magic."

"With your mouth open."

Harry closed it. No music accompanied Fleur, and no Veela stood nearby. Still, Harry had to make a small, deliberate effort to return his attention to the steps, where Dumbledore was beginning his welcome.

"Part Veela," Hermione murmured.

"How can you tell?"

She looked at him.

"Academic interest," Harry added.

"Naturally."

Dumbledore welcomed Madame Maxime, and the Beauxbatons students entered the castle beneath stares they pretended not to notice. The Hogwarts lines immediately began to break, students turning towards the warmth of the open doors, until McGonagall restored them with one look.

"Durmstrang has not arrived."

Everyone turned towards the road, but nothing moved between the gates. Minutes passed while the light faded from the mountains and the cold deepened. Hagrid continued watching the carriage, apparently unaware of either, as its enormous horses tore divots from the lawn and breathed steam above their handlers. Harry drew his hands farther into his sleeves and began to wonder how much diplomatic damage a second late arrival permitted.

Then the lake moved.

Near the centre of the lake, a whirlpool began to form. Black water turned slowly at first, gathering leaves and the last scraps of reflected light, then widened until waves reached the shore and struck the bank beneath the students' feet. Harry could feel the faint vibration through his soles.

The carriage's enormous horses became restless. Heads lifted. Harnesses chimed. Along the Hogwarts lines, conversation disappeared until Harry could hear water dragging against stone far below.

A mast broke the surface. Ropes followed it into the air, then furled sails and the pointed bow of a ship covered in black weed. Water poured through its railings in silver streams, yet the lanterns along the deck burned steadily with green fire, emerging from the depths as though the lake had been no more troublesome than mist.

The vessel looked less as though it had risen from another school than another century. Barnacles covered the lower hull. Carved beasts opened their mouths beneath the prow, streaming lake water from wooden teeth. For a few seconds the whole ship stood nearly upright, its black sails hanging like folded wings; then the stern cleared the surface and crashed down in a sheet of spray.

Several first-years screamed delightedly. The giant squid released one offended tentacle near the opposite bank and disappeared. Once its hull had cleared the surface, the ship drifted towards the shore without wind, trailing weed and a broad wake through water which was only beginning to settle. An anchor fell with a heavy splash, followed by the clatter of chain. The Durmstrang students came ashore wearing heavy crimson robes beneath fur cloaks. They moved with greater order than Beauxbatons and considerably more warmth.

Their headmaster led them. Igor Karkaroff possessed narrow features, silvering hair and a smile which appeared before the feeling required to support it. He opened both arms when he saw Dumbledore.

"My dear old friend!"

Moody's ordinary eye became cold, and the magical one stopped moving. Harry noticed because it had not been still once since Moody arrived. Now it fixed upon Karkaroff with a precision more threatening than any wand. The Durmstrang headmaster noticed; his open arms faltered, then his smile failed for a fraction of a second before he recovered it. Harry wondered what stood between them.

Then Viktor Krum stepped from behind the other students.

Recognition swept through the Hogwarts lines, turning whispers into shouts as students who had watched the World Cup discovered its most famous player standing on their lawn. Several abandoned dignity entirely. Colin Creevey lifted his camera, and a Ravenclaw seventh-year dropped her gloves without noticing that they had landed in the wet grass.

Krum walked with his shoulders curved, exactly as he had before the World Cup. Away from a broom, he appeared less like the greatest Seeker of his generation and more like someone wishing corridors contained fewer people. Harry understood the posture. Fame looked different when it belonged to somebody else: less like light and more like a hundred hands reaching for pieces the person had not offered. Draconia's miniature Krum climbed from her bag onto her shoulder. It folded its arms.

Harry stared.

"You brought it?"

"It packed itself."

The real Krum looked towards them, his eyes stopping upon the miniature version perched on Draconia's shoulder. She went very still. Untroubled by the occasion, miniature Krum waved, and the real one's mouth moved in something which was not quite a smile. Draconia looked determined not to blink first.

"You may never recover," Harry said.

"Silence."

Hermione's laugh escaped as a cough. Draconia turned the look upon her instead, which only made Harry's restraint more difficult.

The delegations entered Hogwarts together.

The Great Hall had been transformed.

The banners no longer displayed the four Houses. Behind the staff table hung the Hogwarts crest beside the crossed wands of Beauxbatons and Durmstrang's double-headed eagle. Hundreds of candles burned with white flame. The golden plates reflected them until each table appeared lit from below.

Beauxbatons joined Ravenclaw. Durmstrang chose Slytherin. Krum sat two places from Draconia.

The arrangement divided the Great Hall into smaller performances. Ravenclaws attempted conversational French with varying success. Slytherins discovered that asking a professional Quidditch player the same question twelve times did not improve his answer. At Gryffindor, Fred and George began taking bets on which visiting student would be the first to assault a host.

Krum became the immediate favourite after George noticed the miniature version still perched upon Draconia's shoulder. She discovered the wager within seconds. Her head turned by degrees towards the Gryffindor table, and her expression communicated a problem of national significance. Harry smiled supportively across the Hall. Draconia answered by lifting a butter knife just far enough to make its intended purpose clear, while the miniature Seeker remained serenely unaware of the difficulty he had caused. Hermione watched the exchange.

"You're enjoying this too much."

"She brought the figurine."

"It packed itself," Hermione repeated.

Harry turned. "How did you hear that?"

"Your whisper carries."

Food appeared. Conversation required more effort than eating. Hogwarts students asked questions too quickly. Beauxbatons students answered in careful English until tired of correcting pronunciation. Durmstrang students regarded the pumpkin juice with suspicion and consumed most of the roast instead.

New dishes occupied the spaces between familiar ones. A pale shellfish stew steamed beside the potatoes. Dark bread arrived warm enough to soften butter on contact. Hermione identified bouillabaisse before Seamus tasted it, then refused to tell him what he had eaten until after he admitted liking it.

Across the Hall, Draconia spoke to Krum in the precise, distant manner she used for adults she had not yet decided to respect. Miniature Krum copied the real one's hunched shoulders. When Draconia noticed, she pressed the figure into her pocket. Harry smiled into his goblet.

Hermione caught him watching.

"You could simply speak to her later."

"I'm appreciating international relations."

"You have been appreciating the same table for six minutes."

Harry turned towards her. "Were you counting?"

Hermione chose the shellfish stew with great concentration. Fleur sat near the centre of Ravenclaw. She criticised the decorations in French. Hermione translated under her breath.

"You speak French?" Harry asked.

"Enough."

"Since when?"

"Before I met you."

Harry found the idea of Hermione existing before he met her unexpectedly strange. He had grown so used to finding her beside him that those earlier years were suddenly difficult to picture.

Across the room, Cedric left Hufflepuff to welcome several Beauxbatons students who had been left without seats, bringing two chairs himself rather than waiting for anyone else to notice. Fleur watched him, and something in her expression revised itself. Cho noticed both the kindness and the attention it received. Harry decided the Tournament had already become dangerous.

Near the staff table, Moody drank from his flask. His blue eye watched Karkaroff. Karkaroff avoided looking back.

After dessert, Dumbledore stood. The Hall quieted with visible reluctance.

"Now that our guests have arrived, the moment has come to explain the selection of our champions."

Filch carried an old wooden chest towards the staff table. Its tarnished metal corners and scratched lid seemed too ordinary for the attention surrounding it, and Harry might have passed it in a storeroom without looking twice. When Dumbledore opened the chest, however, blue-white light spilled over his hands. He lifted out a large cup carved from rough wood, with fire burning steadily inside it.

The Goblet of Fire.

Every candle in the Hall dimmed. The flame moved without wind, casting light across Dumbledore's beard and the faces leaning towards it.

Harry felt no scar pain, and no voice entered his mind, yet the magic made the hairs along his arms rise. Beside him, Hermione had gone very still. Old, she would call it; Harry thought the word would be right, though it did not explain why an object could seem so thoroughly awake.

The distinction mattered. The diary had felt hungry. The Dementors had felt empty. The Goblet offered no malice Harry could recognise, yet its flame leaned towards every sound in the Hall as if listening. He wondered how many names it had consumed, how many children had mistaken being chosen for being safe.

Dumbledore placed the Goblet upon a stone pedestal near the doors, where its light reached the ends of all four tables. Students leaned into the aisles for a better view.

"Those wishing to submit themselves for consideration must write their name and school clearly upon a piece of parchment and place it within the flame. The Goblet will accept entries until six o'clock tomorrow evening. The champions will be named during the Halloween feast."

His gaze moved across Hogwarts.

"Once selected, a champion enters a binding magical contract. No name should be submitted lightly."

Hermione's attention sharpened. Harry felt rather than saw her become suspicious. Her shoulders squared. Her eyes moved from Dumbledore to Crouch, then to the Goblet itself, searching the announcement for the clause everyone else had accepted because it sounded ancient.

"To ensure the age restriction is observed, I will draw an Age Line around the Goblet. No student below seventeen will cross it."

Fred and George exchanged a look.

"Do not," Hermione said from several seats away.

They smiled. Dumbledore raised his wand, drawing a thin gold circle across the floor around the pedestal. The line pulsed once. Moody's magical eye rotated towards it, the blue iris briefly shining with reflected fire, before turning towards Harry. The look lasted no longer than the others. Harry could still feel it after the feast ended.

After dinner, the pedestal was moved into the Entrance Hall, where the Goblet would remain overnight. Its gold boundary travelled with it, settling around the stone as neatly as though it had always been drawn there. Students leaving dinner passed it in a widening circle. Older students drew close enough to feel the heat. Younger ones tested the Age Line with shoes, quills and one unfortunate apple which returned to its owner as warm sauce.

The Hall had never seemed designed for a single object. Torchlight climbed the marble walls, but every shadow leaned towards the pedestal. Blue reflected along the staircase rails and turned faces ghostly whenever students approached. Filch had positioned himself beside the doors with Mrs Norris and a list of infractions; neither discouraged anyone.

Hermione stood beyond the gold ring with both arms folded.

"A binding magical contract," she said.

Harry joined her. "You've said that twice."

"Dumbledore said it once."

"He has a beard. Words sound more important near it."

"The phrase is imprecise."

"It means the magic binds you," Harry said.

"Where? To what? Through which act?"

"You're making the cup sound under-researched."

"It is seven hundred years old. That is not the same thing as peer reviewed."

Draconia emerged from the Great Hall with the Durmstrang delegation behind her. She crossed directly towards them.

"Krum spoke to me."

Harry glanced over her shoulder. The Bulgarian Seeker walked beside Karkaroff near the marble staircase. He did not look back.

"Did you survive?"

"Barely."

Hermione's concern arrived before she recognised the subject. "What did he say?"

Draconia retrieved the miniature figure from her pocket and held it between two fingers. It appeared pleased to have been admitted to the conversation.

"He asked where I obtained this."

"And?" Harry prompted.

"I told him it was a gift."

"Technically."

"Then it bowed to him."

Miniature Krum demonstrated. Hermione pressed her lips together.

"You may laugh," Draconia said coldly.

Hermione did. Harry lasted another second. Draconia returned the figure to her bag.

"I regret confiding in either of you."

"First mistake," Harry said.

"One of many," Hermione added.

They turned towards the Goblet. Up close, the wooden surface appeared blackened by centuries of flame. No name or crest marked it. The fire rising from the bowl produced no smoke and very little warmth beyond the Age Line.

Harry watched a seventh-year Ravenclaw approach with a folded piece of parchment. The gold circle admitted her without a flicker, and she paused beside the Goblet to read her name once before dropping it into the fire. Blue became red; the parchment vanished, and the flame settled back into its original colour. Applause followed from the Ravenclaws waiting nearby, already treating the submission as something worth celebrating.

"That's all?" Harry asked.

"Were you expecting blood?" Draconia replied.

"Old magic usually likes presentation."

Hermione crouched beyond the line. "The Goblet was used to arbitrate magical disputes before the Tournament existed. It chooses according to the terms presented to it."

"Which terms?"

She looked irritated.

"Exactly."

Harry understood the concern.

"Dumbledore only said name and school."

"He said an impartial judge would select the champion best suited to represent each institution. How does it define suitability? Does it measure power? Character? Probability of success? And what precisely creates the contract—the person submitting the name or the Goblet selecting it?"

Draconia studied the flame.

"You researched this before the other schools arrived."

"Obviously."

"And found nothing."

Hermione stood.

"Nothing consistent."

The admission offended her personally. Harry had seen Hermione confront monsters with less resentment than an incomplete citation.

Draconia knelt beside the line without allowing her robes to touch the floor. "Can the terms be examined?"

"While the Goblet is active, possibly. Most surviving records describe the result rather than the mechanism."

"Because everyone was too impressed by fire to ask sensible questions."

"That appears to be a recurring problem in magical history."

The blue flames leaned towards Harry, their tips stretching briefly in his direction before folding back into the bowl.

"I feel judged."

"You should," both girls said.

A Durmstrang student approached, passed through the Age Line and submitted his name. As the flame accepted it, Harry noticed Karkaroff watching from the stairs, with Moody watching Karkaroff in turn. He had barely begun studying the professor when the magical eye rotated towards him without Moody turning his head. Harry broke the contact first.

He disliked that more than he would have disliked losing a staring contest to an ordinary eye. An ordinary eye required a person's attention. The blue one seemed capable of watching him while Moody thought about something else entirely.

"We should go," Hermione said.

"Because of Moody?" Draconia asked.

"Because curfew was six minutes ago."

They separated at the staircase. Harry glanced back once. Moody stood near the Great Hall doors. The staff had begun leaving. The Goblet burned within its gold circle. His ordinary eye followed Karkaroff up the stairs. The magical one remained upon the flame.

Fred and George attempted to cross the Age Line at seven twenty the following morning.

Having announced the event in advance, they ensured that half the school occupied the Entrance Hall by seven fifteen. Students leaned over banisters while first-years claimed the marble stairs, and Lee Jordan moved among them collecting bets with the air of someone providing a public service. Near the Great Hall doors, Professor McGonagall pretended the crowd had not already explained itself.

The twins entered together, each having consumed enough Ageing Potion to add six months. White streaks showed at their temples, and Fred had developed a single wrinkle which George inspected with envy. They appeared to regard this uneven distribution of maturity as the only flaw in the plan.

"Gentlemen," Fred called, holding up his parchment.

"Ladies," George added.

"Future international admirers."

"Observe."

They stepped across the line, and the magic threw them backwards before either could lower his parchment. Harry moved aside as Fred slid across the floor. George struck a suit of armour, which applauded the collision with a delighted clatter of gauntlets. Both twins sat up wearing long white beards, their expressions briefly lost beneath the unexpected growth.

The Entrance Hall exploded with laughter. Draconia examined them from beside Harry.

"An improvement."

George stroked his beard.

"I feel distinguished."

"You look flammable," Hermione said.

Dumbledore appeared at the top of the stairs, his eyes twinkling as he surveyed the results. He did not seem to require an explanation.

"I suggest you visit Madam Pomfrey. She has prepared something for the occasion."

"You knew?" Fred asked.

"I hoped."

The twins left beneath applause, lifting their beards clear of their shoes as they went. The crowd remained, unwilling to surrender a useful view of the Goblet simply because its first entertainment had concluded. More candidates arrived before breakfast. Angelina Johnson submitted her name to loud support from Gryffindor. Warrington entered for Slytherin. Two Ravenclaws crossed separately and argued over which received greater applause. Fleur Delacour descended the staircase with the Beauxbatons students, her pale blue robes uncreased and the parchment bearing her name held unfolded in one hand. The crowd opened before she reached it, leaving a clear path to the Goblet.

Fleur crossed the Age Line without hesitation. Fire turned red around her parchment and climbed twice its usual height. The Beauxbatons delegation applauded with restrained confidence. Several Hogwarts boys joined them, applauding rather more enthusiastically than the occasion required, until Fleur looked in their direction. They stopped. Krum arrived moments later. Durmstrang students surrounded him closely enough to keep everyone else at a distance. He passed through the line, dropped his parchment and watched until the flame consumed it.

When Krum turned, his gaze crossed the hall and found Hermione. She had been explaining to Harry why the intensity of the fire did not necessarily indicate the strength of a candidacy, but stopped halfway through her argument when she noticed him looking. Krum inclined his head. Hermione glanced behind herself, finding only a thoroughly unoccupied stretch of marble floor.

"He was acknowledging Draco," she said.

Draconia stood on Harry's other side.

"I am not located through your spine."

"Perhaps he recognised Harry."

Harry waved. Krum appeared confused.

"Helpful," Hermione said.

The Durmstrang party entered the Great Hall, leaving Hermione to adjust her bag with unnecessary force. On Harry's other side, Draconia's expression became thoughtful in a way he had learned to distrust. He recognised danger before she opened her mouth.

"No," he told her.

"I said nothing."

"Your face did."

"I hate that everyone has named those," Hermione muttered.

Cedric arrived at eight. The Hufflepuffs did not surround him as Durmstrang surrounded Krum. They came beside him. Cho walked on his right. Professor Sprout stood near the Great Hall and attempted not to appear more invested than a Head of House ought to be.

Harry had spent three wet afternoons discovering that Cedric's politeness disappeared completely during a duel. He was patient before the first spell, apologetic after the last and ruthlessly inventive between them. He could redirect a Stunner off a conjured shield, cast while rolling and exploit Harry's instinct to protect anyone who appeared to stumble.

The Goblet could choose other qualities. Harry no longer doubted Cedric possessed the necessary ones.

Amos was not there, and Harry thought that mattered. Cedric had come without his father's voice filling the space around the decision, without anyone telling the crowd what it ought to mean before he had made it. Whatever the Goblet answered, the question belonged to him.

Carrying a single piece of parchment, Cedric stopped before the Age Line. The Hall quieted around him, its attention settling more gently than it had upon Krum. Harry moved through the crowd until Cedric saw him, then halted just beyond the gold circle.

"Still think I should?"

"Do you?"

Cedric looked at the Goblet.

"Yes."

"Then yes."

The response felt heavier now than it had beside the pitch. Harry was not encouraging an idea. He was standing beside a decision Cedric had tested against weeks of work, cold rain and Hermione's increasingly detailed list of ways historical champions had died.

Cho took Cedric's hand and squeezed it once before releasing him to cross the gold line. Harry watched without envy as he stood straight before the flame, reading what he had written. His lips moved around the words, giving them a final moment in which they still belonged entirely to him.

Cedric Diggory. Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

He let the parchment fall, and red fire took it. The Hufflepuffs cheered, with Harry joining them as Cedric emerged from the circle. His smile was too uncertain to be pride and too pleased to be anything else; the empty hand at his side opened and closed once, as though still expecting to hold the paper.

"Now what?" Harry asked.

"I regret every decision until tonight."

"Good strategy."

Cho reclaimed his hand, and Cedric allowed the Hufflepuffs to carry him into breakfast, answering their congratulations with a smile which grew a little steadier each time. Before the crowd swallowed him, he looked back and pointed two fingers towards Harry, then the practice yard—a promise that being entered did not excuse him from Sunday training. Harry returned the gesture.

Moody stood in the doorway, his magical eye following Cedric into the Hall while his ordinary one rested upon Harry. It was difficult to tell when a conversation with him had begun; sometimes Harry seemed to enter one which Moody had already been conducting silently.

"Not tempted?" he asked.

His attention settled upon the gold line.

"I promised several dangerous people I wouldn't be."

"Promises make poor barriers."

"That one comes with Hermione."

Moody's scarred mouth shifted.

"Stronger, then."

He entered the Great Hall. Draconia joined Harry.

"What did he want?"

"To discuss the structural integrity of Hermione."

"Reckless."

Hermione reached them.

"I heard my name."

"International diplomacy," Harry said.

She did not believe him. They went to breakfast.

The Goblet occupied Hermione's thoughts for the remainder of the day. She took six books to the library before lunch and found four more afterwards, carrying the second stack with her chin pressed to the topmost volume. By three, their table had disappeared beneath histories of magical arbitration, Tournament records and contract theory so dense that the footnotes required their own index.

Late sunlight crossed the high windows and moved slowly up the shelves. Elsewhere, the library maintained its usual afternoon murmur, but their table had developed the atmosphere of an inquiry. Hermione arranged contradictory sources into separate piles. Draconia kept a list of authors whose family interests made them unreliable. Harry was placed at the remaining corner with his essay and strict instructions not to disturb the chronology.

Harry completed half his Herbology essay while Draconia finished all of hers, then corrected three statements in his introduction without invitation. She turned the parchment towards him with the calm certainty of someone who considered the improvements self-evident.

"That sentence is hostile," she said.

"It describes Bubotuber pus."

"The grammar is hostile."

"The plant understands."

Hermione shut one book.

"The Goblet cannot compel participation by anyone whose name was submitted without consent."

Harry put down his quill.

"Good."

"According to this."

"Less good."

She opened another.

"This account says selection itself establishes consent because the Goblet cannot choose an unwilling champion."

Draconia frowned. "How would a cup determine willingness?"

"It does not explain."

"Poor scholarship."

"This one describes a champion attempting to withdraw in 1742. His magic became unstable until he returned to the task."

Hermione pushed the book towards them. The account occupied less than half a page. The champion's name appeared once; the officials responsible for compelling his return appeared seven times.

"It does not say how unstable," Draconia said.

"Or whether returning cured it," Hermione replied. "The next record begins with a different Tournament."

"Convenient."

Harry read the final line twice, rubbing a fingertip along the worn edge of the page. The parchment felt colder than the room justified. He glanced towards the library doors, suddenly aware that the Goblet was still burning several floors below while they tried to discover what a person actually promised by placing a name inside it.

"Why are you researching withdrawal before anyone is chosen?"

Hermione hesitated.

"Preparation."

"For Cedric?"

"For anyone."

Draconia closed Harry's essay.

"You dislike the contract."

"I dislike magic which treats participation as consent."

Harry saw the sentence reach Draconia in the way her hand moved towards the green satchel. The diary no longer occupied it, but the habit remained: a movement learned during months when something inside that bag had seemed to offer answers. Hermione noticed too. Her anger found a clearer direction, and she closed the book more carefully than before, leaving her palm against its cover.

"There should always be a way to say no," she said.

Draconia met her eyes.

"Yes."

The agreement held more history than either explained.

"Dumbledore would not use something dangerous without understanding it," Harry said.

Hermione stared.

"What?"

"I wanted to hear whether it sounded convincing."

"Did it?"

"No."

They returned to the books.

Near four, Krum entered the library alone. Without the Durmstrang delegation around him, he seemed less conspicuous until students began looking up from their books. He kept his attention upon the shelves.

Madam Pince looked up from her desk, recognised him and became even less impressed than usual. Krum moved between the shelves with the careful uncertainty of someone who could locate a Snitch across a stadium and not A Guide to Advanced Transfiguration from three feet away.

Hermione noticed after his third pass and immediately attempted to look as though she had not. Draconia noticed Hermione noticing. Harry, watching this exchange from behind his Herbology essay, considered leaving before the situation acquired his involvement. Krum approached their table, slowing beside the nearest stack of books. His eyes moved over Harry, settled briefly upon Draconia and returned to Hermione, who had been reading the same line for rather longer than usual.

"You know where is human transfiguration?"

Hermione pointed. "Second aisle, left side, upper shelves. Theory before practical applications."

She tucked a section of hair behind one ear while she answered. The movement left a streak of ink near her temple. Harry nearly mentioned it. Krum's attention remained upon Hermione long enough for him to decide the streak was probably not urgent. Krum nodded.

"Thank you."

He left. Hermione opened her book. Draconia waited.

"Do not," Hermione said.

"I remain silent."

"Aggressively."

Harry's gaze moved between them. He could not see why either considered the encounter remarkable enough to require a warning.

"He needed a book."

Both girls turned towards him.

"What?"

Draconia sighed. "Nothing."

Hermione's face had become pink. Harry returned to Bubotubers, satisfied that the matter had been explained. Silence gathered across the table until he looked up again. Both girls were staring at him.

"What?"

"Nothing," they said together.

The answer sounded considerably less reassuring in unison.

Sirius answered the mirror from beneath the cottage sink. Only his hand appeared at first.

"Why are you on the floor?" Harry asked.

Sirius's face rose into view. A pipe behind him sprayed water across one shoulder.

"Domestic mastery."

"The kitchen is flooding."

"Temporary disagreement."

Winky's voice came from beyond the frame.

"Master Sirius is not touching pipe again!"

"I live here!"

"Then Master Sirius is wanting floor to stay below him!"

The pipe stopped spraying, and Sirius crawled from beneath the sink with a dignity considerably diminished by the water dripping from his hair. Somewhere beyond the mirror, Winky continued expressing her opinion of his workmanship. Behind him, the cottage kitchen looked thoroughly inhabited. Towels covered two chairs. Remus's mug sat beside an open book. A pan steamed on the stove while Winky muttered from somewhere below the mirror's edge about wizards who believed ownership created competence. Harry loved it with a suddenness which hurt. Nothing matched. Nothing remained orderly for long. The room carried evidence of people expecting to be there tomorrow.

"Tyranny," Sirius muttered, glancing towards the voice.

Harry smiled into the mirror.

"Cedric entered."

"Tournament?"

"This morning. Krum and Fleur Delacour too."

Sirius dried his hair with one hand.

"And you?"

"Still fourteen."

"Excellent work."

The answer was immediate, but Sirius's shoulders lowered afterwards. He had asked lightly and waited seriously. Harry told him about the Age Line, the twins' beards and Hermione's contract research. Sirius listened until he described Moody watching the Goblet.

"He watches everything," Sirius said.

"You keep saying that."

"Because you keep describing it."

Harry moved towards the dormitory window. The grounds had darkened beneath an early evening. The Beauxbatons carriage stood beyond Hagrid's hut. Durmstrang's ship floated upon the lake with green lights along its deck.

"Hermione found accounts where champions couldn't withdraw."

"That is the Tournament's favourite defence. Binding contract."

"You sound like you don't believe it."

"I believe old magic is often a phrase adults use when they do not want children asking who designed the rules."

Harry turned back to the mirror.

"Did you ever want to enter?"

"At fourteen? Desperately."

"Really?"

"Glory, danger and an audience. I was an idiot with excellent hair."

"What changed?"

Sirius considered, his hand slowing against the towel. For a moment the kitchen noises seemed louder in the pause between them.

"The danger stopped being imaginary."

He wiped water from the mirror's edge with his sleeve.

"Your father and I used to think volunteering made danger noble. Remus usually pointed out that it merely made danger scheduled."

"Was he right?"

"Frequently. We found it exhausting."

The answer remained with Harry.

"You don't want to enter," Sirius said.

"I know."

"Wanting to know if you could win is not the same thing."

Harry touched the brass key beneath his robes.

"Moody said that."

"I resent him stealing my wisdom before I deliver it."

"You were fixing a pipe."

"Heroically."

Winky shouted something about towels. Harry ended the call laughing. The sound stayed with him after the glass cleared. He returned to the window and watched green lanterns move upon Durmstrang's ship, feeling the distance to the cottage without feeling abandoned by it.

By then, Halloween had been consumed by speculation. Breakfast, lunch and the hours between had become versions of the same discussion conducted at different volumes. Someone claimed the Goblet had whispered Krum's name before noon. By four, Beauxbatons students were insisting it had rejected three Hogwarts entries as illegible. Nobody could identify who had heard the whisper or seen the rejected parchment, which did little to slow either story.

Students continued submitting names as the deadline approached. At six precisely, the flame rose to the ceiling and the gold Age Line vanished.

Entries had closed.

Shortly afterwards, the Goblet itself disappeared from the Entrance Hall. Only a black mark remained upon the floor where the pedestal had stood. Harry stepped over it on the way to the courtyard, his shoes crossing the place where the gold boundary had shone. Nothing happened, though Hermione looked relieved anyway.

They spent the final hour before dinner beside the lake. Draconia sat upon the low wall with her cloak beneath her and watched Durmstrang's ship rise and fall against the shore. Hermione carried no books. Harry had checked twice.

The afternoon had surrendered its colour early. Bare branches scratched together above the bank, and the castle windows began lighting one by one behind them. From the ship came the occasional groan of timber and a language Harry could not understand. Its green lanterns drew narrow paths across the black water.

For once, the three of them allowed the silence to remain ordinary. Hermione's shoulder rested against Harry's where they sat upon the grass. Draconia's shoe touched his knee from the wall. Neither contact appeared significant enough to mention and both made the cold easier to ignore.

Cedric found them there. He wore formal robes beneath his coat and carried more nervous energy than he displayed. Cho walked with him but left after wishing him luck, joining several Ravenclaws near the castle.

"Any last advice?" Cedric asked.

"From us?" Harry replied.

"Hermione has read every account of the Tournament."

"Not every account."

"That means most," Draconia said.

Hermione ignored her. "If selected, do not agree verbally to any additional conditions until they are explained in writing."

Cedric nodded with great seriousness.

"Do I sign anything?"

"Not without reading it."

"Do I accept mysterious objects?"

"No."

"Follow voices into forbidden areas?"

Harry answered that one. "Only if they sound trustworthy."

"Harry."

"No voices."

Cedric smiled. Then the smile faded.

"I don't think it will choose me."

"Why?" Harry asked.

"Krum is a professional athlete. Fleur looks as though she has won things none of us know exist. Angelina has played three years of Quidditch and can cast a Shield Charm while flying."

"The Goblet chooses one champion from each school," Hermione said. "Krum and Fleur are not competing against you yet."

"Comforting word."

Draconia looked towards the castle.

"You entered because you wanted to know whether it would choose you."

"Yes."

"Then allow it to answer before objecting."

Cedric absorbed that.

"You sound like my mother."

Draconia's face became appalled. Harry laughed.

Cedric tried to apologise, failed because Harry continued laughing and finally joined him. The tension left his shoulders for several seconds; when it returned, it no longer seemed capable of carrying him away. The bell interrupted his next attempt at an apology, and Draconia allowed him to survive it by turning towards the castle.

They crossed the lawn together. At the Entrance Hall, House lines separated them once more. Cedric went towards Hufflepuff. Draconia joined the Slytherins. Harry and Hermione entered beside Gryffindor. Harry glanced back. Cedric stood beneath the torchlight while Hufflepuff students gathered around him. Draconia caught his eye from across the space and lifted one hand. Three fingers. Harry raised his own. Three of them. Whatever happened.

The promise had begun as reassurance during smaller dangers. Standing beneath the Halloween torches, with students pressing past them and Cedric waiting for his answer, Harry felt how much it had come to hold. Whatever the year brought, whatever doors closed between them, there would still be three. No boundary drawn by an official could decide where their concern for one another ended.

The Great Hall glowed with Halloween. Hundreds of carved pumpkins floated near the walls. Orange light filled their mouths and eyes. Black candles burned above the tables. The enchanted ceiling showed a clear night divided by fast-moving clouds. The Goblet stood before the staff table. Its flame had become blue again.

The sight transported Harry to second year with such speed that the Hall shifted. Draconia's empty seat. A voice inside the walls. Water upon the corridor floor and red words moving down stone. By the time dessert appeared, Harry had scarcely noticed what he was eating. Hermione placed a treacle tart upon his plate, bringing his attention back to the table with the scrape of her serving spoon.

"You haven't eaten dessert."

Harry blinked at her.

"It just appeared."

"Preparation."

Her hand lingered beside the plate until Harry picked up his fork. Across the Hall, Draconia watched long enough to confirm he had taken a bite before turning back to Daphne.

The feast continued around him, plates filling and emptying while conversation rose over competing predictions. Cutlery rang against gold, and every movement at the staff table caused a hundred heads to turn. Harry ate another bite of tart, keeping his attention upon its sweetness until the Hall felt properly settled around him again.

Moody ate with his ordinary eye upon the plate. The magical one faced the Goblet. Karkaroff whispered to Krum. Madame Maxime spoke quietly with Fleur. Professor Sprout sat behind Cedric's line of sight and smiled whenever he glanced back.

At the Slytherin table, Draconia ignored Warrington's claims that his family's defensive magic ensured selection. Daphne listened with the expression of someone examining an insect, while Pansy watched Krum. Beside Draconia's plate, the miniature Seeker had escaped her bag once more and was making his determined way towards her shoulder.

The plates cleared, leaving the gold surfaces bare beneath the candles. Dumbledore rose, and silence spread before he requested it, students leaning forward along every bench.

"The Goblet is ready."

He drew his wand and extinguished most of the candles. As the Hall darkened, pumpkin light remained along the walls, its warm orange scarcely reaching the tables. At the front, the Goblet burned blue-white and restless, painting every waiting face in cold colour.

Harry felt Hermione's hand close around his beneath the table. He did not think she had done it deliberately; her eyes remained upon the Goblet, and her fingers had found his with the blind certainty of habit. He turned his palm upwards, allowing their hands to settle more comfortably together. Her fingers stayed. Warmth gathered where they met, a small, familiar certainty in a Hall which suddenly felt enormous.

Dumbledore moved behind the Goblet.

"When each champion's name is called, they will proceed through the door beside the staff table and await further instruction."

The flame reddened, and something moved inside it. A piece of parchment shot into the air, turning once before Dumbledore caught it. The fire returned to blue as he unfolded the paper, its light catching the rims of his spectacles.

"The champion for Durmstrang Institute is Viktor Krum."

The Hall erupted. Durmstrang students struck their fists against the table, and Hogwarts joined them, many students standing to applaud. Krum rose without smiling and endured Karkaroff's embrace before being allowed to leave. As he crossed the Hall past Slytherin, the miniature version on Draconia's shoulder stood and applauded with impressive solemnity. The real Krum saw it and, this time, smiled before disappearing through the side door.

The flame turned red again, lifting a second parchment above the bowl while the last cheers for Krum were still fading.

"The champion for Beauxbatons Academy is Fleur Delacour."

Beauxbatons applauded with silver sparks dancing above their hands. Fleur stood, pride changing her beauty into something sharper, and Madame Maxime kissed both her cheeks before sending her towards the chamber. Her eyes passed briefly over Cedric on the way. Cho noticed, and Harry decided again that the Tournament contained unlisted risks.

The third parchment emerged more slowly, turning once above the flame before Dumbledore caught it. Hogwarts seemed to stop breathing. Harry watched Cedric rather than the paper; he was staring at the Goblet, his face emptied of every expectation except the one he would not permit himself to keep. At the staff table, Dumbledore read the name, and his smile widened.

"The Hogwarts champion is Cedric Diggory."

The Hufflepuff table exploded. Students climbed onto benches. Professor Sprout abandoned restraint and clapped both hands above her head. Amos was not present to shout, but the pride he would feel appeared upon every face surrounding his son.

Cedric remained seated for a moment, looking as though he needed to hear the announcement again before he could believe it. Cho pulled him upright. Harry did not remember releasing Hermione's hand; he was already standing, applauding until his palms hurt and grinning at Cedric's bewildered expression.

For one clean minute, the Tournament was exactly what it had promised to be. Cedric had chosen to enter. The Goblet had answered. Hufflepuff's joy filled the Hall without taking anything from anyone else.

Cedric looked across the Hall until he found Harry. At Harry's nod, his smile became real, and he crossed towards the side chamber while Hogwarts cheered his name. Students reached out to clap his shoulder as he passed, sharing the triumph without slowing him for long.

Draconia stood at Slytherin despite Warrington's expression. Daphne joined her. Several others followed slowly.

Cedric passed through the door, but the cheering continued. Dumbledore raised both hands and waited nearly a minute for silence to return. During it, the Goblet's flame lowered, paling from blue to white before dimming into the bowl. Harry remained standing, his attention still following Cedric.

"Excellent," Dumbledore said. "Our three champions have been chosen. I am certain you will join me in offering them every support as they—"

The Goblet turned red.

Dumbledore stopped speaking as the fire rose higher than before, twisting above the rim in a narrow column. Sparks struck the enchanted ceiling and vanished among the stars. Confusion moved through the Hall, students looking from the unexpected flame to the Headmaster's unfinished gesture. The cheers did not stop together. They broke apart table by table, voice by voice, until the last few students realised everyone else had fallen silent. Cedric's name still echoed faintly from the stone as the new flame climbed above Dumbledore's head.

Moody stood, his magical eye fixed upon the flame. Beside him, Karkaroff's chair scraped against the stone, the sound carrying through the newly quiet Hall.

"What is this?"

The Goblet released another parchment. It flew rather than floated, spinning hard enough to scatter sparks behind it. Dumbledore caught the scrap against his chest with both hands, and for a moment the blue-white fire illuminated the underside of his fingers through the paper. Then the flame collapsed into blue, leaving the Hall completely silent.

Harry could hear the soft sputter of the remaining candles, the movement of fabric as the staff rose one by one. Beside him, Hermione drew a breath. Rain had begun unexpectedly against the high windows, its scattered taps audible beneath the silence. His body recognised the silence before his mind supplied a reason. Cold passed through his stomach. The scar beneath his fringe gave one dull pulse—not pain, only memory—and every instinct which had kept him alive demanded he find the door before anyone spoke.

Dumbledore unfolded the parchment, and his face changed. It was no dramatic reaction: his eyes lowered, and the lines around his mouth became still. For one instant, Harry saw an old man confronted by something he had not prepared to understand. Then Dumbledore looked towards Gryffindor, and Harry's stomach dropped before the name was spoken.

"Harry Potter."

For a moment, nothing happened. The words had entered the Hall without finding anywhere they belonged, and Harry waited for someone to correct them.

He had heard his name shouted by crowds before. He had heard it whispered through corridors, printed by newspapers and used by strangers who believed knowing it meant knowing him. This was different. Dumbledore spoke it as the Goblet had written it: not a person, but an answer to a question Harry had refused to ask.

Harry remained standing because he had not sat down after Cedric's departure. Hermione's hand closed around his wrist, its pressure the first thing he properly felt.

"No," she whispered.

Across the Hall, Draconia went white. Dumbledore read the parchment again.

"Harry Potter."

The room returned through sound. Whispers struck from every table as benches shifted beneath students turning to look at him. Someone laughed once, believing a joke had begun, then stopped when nobody joined. At the front of the Hall, the Goblet burned on, indifferent to what it had done.

"I didn't."

His voice disappeared beneath the first returning whispers. He said it again, louder.

"I didn't enter."

Hermione stood beside him.

"I know."

There was no hesitation in her answer, no question tucked behind it for him to resolve. Hermione believed him before he could begin explaining.

"Harry," Dumbledore said.

The Headmaster's voice remained calm, and that made the command real. Harry's legs moved before he had decided what he wanted to do. As he stepped around the bench, Gryffindor students leaned away to let him pass, their faces carrying disbelief, excitement, suspicion and envy in combinations too quick to read. Hermione's hand slipped from his wrist when the gap between them grew too wide.

The aisle lengthened. Harry felt every stare find the back of his neck. Someone at Ravenclaw whispered that the Age Line had been impossible to cross. A Hufflepuff voice said Cedric's name as though Harry had already stolen part of it. The enchanted ceiling darkened overhead, clouds sealing the stars one by one.

Across the aisle, Draconia moved too, pushing back from Slytherin's table. Professor Snape's hand closed around her shoulder before she could leave, and she turned towards him with a look Harry could read even from that distance.

"Not yet," he said.

Harry saw the words rather than heard them. Draconia's eyes returned to him, and she lifted one hand, showing three fingers. Harry did not feel like one of three. With the aisle open before him and every table watching, he felt completely alone. He raised his hand anyway. The gesture steadied nothing yet, but reminded him that steadiness might return. Then he walked towards the door, keeping his attention upon its dark wooden panels until he reached them. Moody's magical eye followed every step, while his ordinary one remained upon the Goblet.

The chamber beyond the Great Hall was small and overheated.

A fire burned beneath the mantel. Portraits of former headmasters crowded the walls, their painted faces pretending sleep while Harry entered. Fleur stood nearest the hearth. Krum occupied the shadow beside a bookcase. Cedric had been examining a silver tray upon which four untouched glasses waited.

The door closed behind Harry and removed the Great Hall's whispers without removing their pressure. Resin cracked inside the fire. A clock ticked above the mantel with the patient certainty of a room expecting the correct three children.

Four.

Harry stopped at the detail. The room had been prepared before the selection, and three champions should have required three glasses. The fourth belonged to whichever official briefed them, of course; there was nothing remarkable about someone expecting a drink. The correction arrived too late to prevent the unease, and he looked away from the tray.

Cedric turned from the tray, relief crossing his face before he noticed Harry's expression. He was still holding the parchment bearing his own name.

"Harry. Did Dumbledore send you?"

"Not exactly."

Fleur looked towards the door. "They are wanting us back?"

Harry's mouth had gone dry.

"The Goblet chose another name."

Krum stepped from the shadow.

"Whose?"

Harry looked towards Cedric.

"Mine."

The fire cracked in the pause which followed, and the clock continued its patient ticking. Fleur laughed once, waiting for Harry to show her where the joke ended. When he did not, her expression became offended.

"You are too young."

"I noticed."

"You entered?" Krum asked.

"No."

Cedric crossed the room.

"What happened?"

"Dumbledore read my name. Twice."

"But you didn't put it in."

Harry waited for the doubt. It had become a familiar interval: the pause after he told someone something impossible and before they decided whether Harry Potter was more likely to be endangered or dishonest.

Cedric met his eyes, and Harry remembered the World Cup firelight, Eleanor struggling to breathe and the choice he had made to stay with her rather than follow invisible attackers. Cedric had been there. He had read Harry's letter afterwards and spent a month talking with him about his own entry, while Harry never once circled the Goblet for himself. Whatever Cedric saw now, it belonged to someone he already knew.

"All right," Cedric said.

The belief arrived as simply as Hermione's, without an examination Harry had to pass before Cedric would offer it. His throat tightened, making the next words more difficult than they ought to have been.

"That's it?"

"You said you didn't."

Cedric's answer contained no generosity. He did not appear to believe Harry as an act of kindness. He believed him because they had flown together, trained together and stood at the Goblet together when Cedric submitted the only name either of them intended to enter.

Fleur stared between them. "He is a Hogwarts student. Now Hogwarts has two champions."

"I'm not a champion," Harry said.

The door opened, and adults filled the room, bringing the argument with them. Cold air entered beneath their robes. Karkaroff's protest collided with Madame Maxime's before either reached Harry, while Bagman attempted to speak over both with the strained brightness of a commentator whose match had departed from the rules. The little chamber seemed to lose half its space at once.

Dumbledore entered first. Professor McGonagall followed close enough that her robes struck his heels. Karkaroff and Madame Maxime came behind them, both speaking before crossing the threshold. Ludo Bagman appeared almost excited. Barty Crouch entered beside him with his face arranged into official neutrality. Professor Snape and Moody remained near the door.

Neither Hermione nor Draconia had followed them inside. Harry searched the doorway before it closed, already feeling farther from the Hall than the short walk justified. He had travelled less than fifty feet without them, yet it felt like returning to years in which adults decided what happened to him while he waited outside the conversation about his own life.

"This is outrageous," Karkaroff said. "Hogwarts cannot be permitted two champions."

"Eet is obvious what has occurred," Madame Maxime replied. "You wish to double your chances."

"Nobody at Hogwarts entered Harry's name with my knowledge," Dumbledore said.

"Then your knowledge is conveniently limited."

McGonagall moved between Harry and the visiting headmasters.

"Mr Potter is fourteen."

"A fact which makes his presence proof of deception," Karkaroff said.

"Not his deception."

Snape's voice crossed the argument quietly enough that everyone had to stop to hear it. Harry turned towards him, finding his face unreadable. His eyes rested upon the parchment in Dumbledore's hand rather than upon Harry, as though the evidence was being held in quite the wrong place for the questions they were asking.

"The Age Line could not have been crossed by any underage student," he continued. "Either an older person submitted the name or the Goblet's enchantments were altered."

"Potter has demonstrated unusual success circumventing rules," Karkaroff said.

"Usually less sophisticated ones," Snape replied.

It was nearly a defence. Harry accepted it all the same. Dumbledore approached, and the noise diminished around him. He stopped several feet from Harry and lowered himself slightly so their eyes met without Harry having to look upwards. Harry wished the gesture did not make him feel younger.

"Harry," he said, "did you place your name in the Goblet of Fire?"

"No."

"Did you ask another student or adult to place it there for you?"

"No."

"Did you tell anyone you wished to be entered?"

Harry thought of admitting curiosity to Moody. Wanting to know if he could do it. Not the same as wanting to enter. Moody had asked the question twice. He had listened closely to the difference. Harry became aware of the professor near the door, one eye on him and the other hidden somewhere behind its spinning lid.

"No."

Moody's blue eye fixed upon him while Dumbledore held his gaze. There was no accusation in the Headmaster's face, only concern, but concern did not feel sufficiently different while Harry stood surrounded by adults discussing him as a problem. He could still feel the empty place at each side of him.

"I believe you," Dumbledore said.

Relief should have followed. Instead, Harry wondered why being believed still left him standing among people debating whether he had earned the danger. Karkaroff made an incredulous sound, then the chamber door shook beneath an impact. Everyone turned as it opened several inches and stopped against something invisible. Hermione's voice entered through the gap, crisp with a patience which had already expired.

"The ward is unnecessary."

"It is a private conference," Bagman called.

"Then stop shouting loudly enough for the Entrance Hall to hear."

Draconia spoke after her.

"Move."

The ward flashed, allowing the door to open another inch before it resisted again. Moody laughed. McGonagall closed her eyes with the weary recognition of someone who had encountered this particular interpretation of an instruction before.

"They followed him," she said.

"They were instructed to wait," Snape added.

"We did," Hermione called. "For four minutes."

Dumbledore lifted one hand, and the ward disappeared. Freed from its resistance, the door flew open. Hermione entered first with her wand drawn, followed by Draconia holding the small mirror she had apparently been prepared to use as a blunt instrument. Hermione's hair had begun escaping whatever effort she made before the feast. Draconia's silver clasp sat crooked where someone had caught her cloak outside. They looked furious, frightened and entirely incapable of obeying a ward merely because an adult had placed it there.

Both found Harry immediately. Whatever they had intended to say on entering became secondary as Hermione crossed to his left and Draconia took his right, closing the empty spaces beside him.

Draconia's fingers closed around the fabric at his wrist—not checking for injury or attempting to restrain him, only confirming that she had reached him. Of all the explanations being thrown across the room, Harry understood that one first: she had crossed a ward because he was on the other side.

Harry drew his first proper breath since entering the room, discovering only as his chest loosened how carefully he had been keeping himself still. Neither asked whether he had entered. Their presence answered the question before anyone else could repeat it.

"You cannot simply admit additional students," Madame Maxime said.

"We are not champions," Draconia replied.

"Neither is he," Hermione added.

Bagman attempted a conciliatory smile. "The Goblet seems to disagree."

"Objects can be deceived," Hermione said.

Moody's ordinary eye sharpened.

"Explain."

She looked at the fourth parchment still held in Dumbledore's hand.

"It selects one champion for each participating school. If Harry's name competed against every Hogwarts entry, it would have selected either him or Cedric—not both."

Crouch became very still beside Bagman. Hermione continued, her attention upon the parchment rather than the official faces around it.

"Someone made the Goblet recognise a fourth school, then submitted Harry as its only candidate."

Silence followed her conclusion. Moody struck his staff once against the floor, its blunt knock startlingly loud in the crowded chamber.

"Would require a powerful Confundus Charm."

The conclusion arrived too readily. Harry studied him, but Moody's scarred face offered only professional interest. The magical eye focused upon the parchment as though it might reveal the wand which cast the spell.

"Exactly."

"Or prior access to the Goblet's arbitration enchantments," Draconia said. "It chooses according to terms presented. Someone changed the terms."

Harry's attention shifted to her. The words came from the library research. They had been preparing for a problem without knowing whose shape it would take.

Karkaroff pointed towards Hermione. "And a fourth-year student determines this in seconds?"

"She began yesterday," Harry said.

"Naturally," Draconia added.

Hermione ignored them.

"Professor Dumbledore, examine the Goblet."

Dumbledore turned towards the small table where he had placed Harry's parchment. Beneath the lamplight, the paper appeared harmless: black ink, a torn lower edge and no school written beneath the name. Harry could see the uneven fibres where it had been ripped from a larger sheet. Dumbledore raised his wand.

"Revelio."

Silver light crossed the parchment, drawing letters into view around Harry's name. They belonged to no alphabet he recognised. Symbols formed a border along all four edges, each line writing itself from opposite corners until they met. Three sections shone gold; the fourth remained the deep blue of the Goblet's flame, its colour troublingly familiar.

Hermione stepped closer.

"Four categories."

"Four schools," Draconia said.

Karkaroff's protest died before reaching his mouth. Dumbledore touched his wand to the first gold section, and the crest of Durmstrang rose above the parchment. The second produced Beauxbatons' crossed wands; the third became the Hogwarts shield. Above the final section, an empty outline hovered: a crest without an image, belonging to a school which did not exist beyond the terms someone had forced the Goblet to accept.

Harry stared at the empty crest, finding it worse than the others. He had been entered alone because no one else could represent nothing. There was no school behind him in that blue outline, no other candidate whose name the Goblet might have chosen instead.

The cruelty of it possessed an administrative neatness. Someone had not broken every protection. They had altered one definition, constructed an empty category and allowed the Goblet to perform the rest.

"Can you remove it?" he asked.

Dumbledore did not answer. He drew the tip of his wand beneath Harry's name, and the ink lifted. For one hopeful second, the letters separated from the parchment, rising like black smoke to gather around the wand. Harry's fingers loosened.

Pain closed around Harry's right wrist, tearing a gasp from him. It felt like a wire tightening beneath the skin, winding through tendon and bone towards the centre of his palm. His fingers seized before he could pull his hand away, and blue light flashed along the veins beneath the skin.

Hermione caught his arm as Draconia drew her wand. The ink snapped back onto the page, but Harry's own wand tore halfway from its holster, wrenching against his side despite the fastened leather strap. Blue fire ran across the handle and vanished beneath his sleeve.

For one sick instant, Harry felt the magic searching deeper. It tugged at something behind his ribs—something instinctive and enormous which answered threat before thought. The fire surged in the grate. Every glass upon the tray rang at once.

Dumbledore ended the spell, and the pain stopped so abruptly that Harry staggered. Hermione caught his elbow while Draconia placed herself between him and the parchment, her wand levelled at an object which had already proved it could hurt him through anyone who touched it. Harry's hand remained cramped, his fingers slow to obey when he tried to straighten them.

"What was that?" McGonagall demanded.

Harry opened his fingers. A thin blue line circled his wrist, fading as they watched until only the remembered pressure remained. Dumbledore looked older than he had beside the Goblet, the wand in his hand lowered well away from the paper.

"The contract is anchored to his magic."

"Then sever the anchor," Karkaroff said.

"And risk severing what it has mistaken for the source?" Snape asked.

Harry looked from one professor to the other.

"The source of what?"

Nobody answered immediately. Hermione did.

"Your magic."

Her voice was barely audible.

Draconia seized the parchment, and the symbols flashed red beneath her hand. Harry's wrist burned again. She released the page at once, ending the pain, then stared at her empty fingers as though it had bitten her. Her eyes went immediately to Harry's hand, checking that the blue light had gone.

"It punishes interference through him."

Draconia's voice remained even. The hand holding her wand did not.

"A protection against tampering with another champion's entry," Dumbledore said. "Misapplied because the Goblet recognises the submission as valid."

"It is not valid," Hermione said.

"No." Dumbledore lowered his wand. "But the contract cannot distinguish deception from consent once selection has occurred."

"Then the magic is defective."

Bagman appeared scandalised. "The Goblet has judged champions for centuries."

"That means the defect is old," Hermione replied. "Not acceptable."

Her face had gone pale. Anger kept her voice steady where fear could not. Moody's magical eye fixed upon the disappearing line around Harry's wrist.

"Whoever did this knew removal would hurt him."

The observation settled over the room. Whoever had done this had understood the Goblet well enough to exploit its protections deliberately. They had understood the contract too, knowing exactly which rules would become weapons once Harry's name emerged. Even trying to help him had brought the trap tighter.

Harry looked from Crouch, who helped design those rules, to Karkaroff, who feared Moody, and finally to Moody himself. The magical eye had not left Harry's wrist. The blue outline of the imaginary crest faded above the parchment. Harry rubbed his wrist, turning it under the lamplight in search of a mark. The skin looked unchanged, yet something beneath it no longer felt entirely his.

"It has extinguished," Crouch said.

His voice sounded more exhausted than authoritative.

"The enchantments withdraw after selection. They cannot be reconstructed until the Tournament ends."

Harry turned towards him. Crouch avoided Moody's eye.

"How do you know?" Harry asked.

"I assisted in drafting the regulations."

"Did you test what happens if someone else submits a name?" Hermione demanded.

"The Goblet does not accept unwilling candidates."

"According to whom?"

Crouch's jaw tightened.

"Miss Granger—" McGonagall began.

"No."

Hermione's voice broke. She stepped closer to Harry.

"Everyone keeps saying the contract is binding, but nobody agrees when it begins or how consent is determined. Harry did not enter. He did not consent. The Goblet was deceived. Why should its mistake be allowed to compel him?"

The question entered every silence the adults maintained. Dumbledore's face had become grave.

"It should not," he said.

Hope arrived painfully, lifting Harry's attention towards the Headmaster. Then Crouch spoke.

"The contract is already formed."

Harry felt the hope close before he had properly allowed himself to hold it.

"The rules are explicit," he continued. "A selected champion must compete."

"Or what?" Harry asked.

Crouch looked at him.

"The consequences are not known."

"That is not an answer."

"The Tournament has no recorded refusal carried through after selection."

"Then let me be the first."

Dumbledore raised his wand again, this time without touching the parchment. Fine threads of silver spread around Harry's wrist and travelled several inches into empty air before vanishing.

"The bond is active," he said quietly. "It is not compelling Harry's movements, but it is waiting for conditions which have not yet been declared. Refusal may allow the magic to interpret his life or magical ability as the unpaid forfeit."

McGonagall's expression hardened. "May?"

"I will not experiment with Harry's magic while death remains one possible result."

Harry heard the care inside the decision. He also heard the shape of the cage: nobody knew enough to release him, and that ignorance was being used as the reason he must remain. Bagman's smile vanished. Karkaroff and Madame Maxime stopped arguing long enough to stare, the question of an advantage seeming briefly smaller than the possible cost of removing it. Draconia's shoulder pressed against Harry's, and he leaned into the contact without looking away from Dumbledore. Crouch's voice became formal.

"Withdrawal may destabilise the champion's magic. It may be interpreted as forfeiture. It may affect the other contracts. Without the active Goblet, no official possesses authority to release you."

"Convenient," Draconia said.

"This is not convenience."

"No. It is a fourteen-year-old being ordered to risk his life because an object was tricked."

"The Ministry did not enter him."

"The Ministry merely insists he obey."

Crouch flinched, and Harry saw it before the official expression returned. Something in Draconia's words had struck beyond the Tournament, though Harry could not tell which part. Moody moved from the door, his staff sounding against the floor as the other adults made room for him. Harry watched him approach with the uneasy attention the magical eye always seemed to demand.

"Someone entered Potter knowing the contract would hold him."

"We have established that," Karkaroff snapped.

"No. You've established cheating. Cheating wants victory." Moody's magical eye turned towards Harry. "This wants the boy inside three dangerous tasks where accidents are expected."

The room seemed to chill despite the fire. McGonagall's face lost colour, and Harry heard the logs shift in the grate behind the sudden pause.

"You believe someone is attempting to kill him."

"Wouldn't be original."

Harry's laugh escaped without humour.

"He's right."

Draconia turned towards him. He looked at Moody.

"Do you know how they did it?"

"Not yet."

"Do you know who?"

"Not yet."

"But I still have to compete."

Moody did not soften the answer.

"Unless Dumbledore finds a way to break older magic before the first task."

Everyone looked at the Headmaster. Dumbledore held the fourth parchment carefully by its edge, avoiding the symbols, and for the first time Harry could remember he appeared uncertain. There was no answer waiting behind his silence.

"I will examine every possibility," he said.

"And until then?" Sirius was not present to ask it, so Harry did.

Dumbledore's eyes did not leave his.

"Until then, we proceed as though the contract will hold. Not because the Tournament's rules deserve obedience, Harry, but because your life does not deserve to become the experiment which proves them wrong."

It was the most honest answer any adult had given. Harry hated it anyway.

"Before when?" Harry asked.

Bagman looked at the champions.

"The first task is scheduled for the twenty-fourth of November."

Three and a half weeks.

Harry tried to imagine fitting an answer into that space: finding the person responsible, breaking a contract centuries old, learning whatever he needed to survive if neither succeeded. The days ahead seemed to draw closer rather than stretch away.

"Champions will receive no advance information concerning the task," Bagman continued, recovering some of his official cheer. "It is intended to test courage in the face of the unknown. Wands will be examined on the eighteenth, and—"

"He is not a champion," Hermione said.

Bagman stopped.

Cedric moved beside Harry, joining a line the officials had not intended: Hermione, Harry, Draconia and Cedric. Fleur and Krum remained near the fire, watching the arrangement form. Harry had forgotten, during the argument, that Cedric still had a triumph of his own waiting outside.

"If he has to compete," Cedric said, "Hogwarts still has one champion."

Karkaroff laughed sharply. "You surrender your advantage before the first task?"

"He didn't choose to be our advantage."

Harry met his eyes. Cedric lifted one shoulder.

"You said you didn't enter."

Simple belief again. Fleur stepped forward.

"Beauxbatons will not accept Hogwarts scoring twice."

"Nor Durmstrang," Krum said.

Krum's voice remained quiet, without the sharper accusation which had filled the room earlier. Harry understood them both. Believing his innocence did not make the arrangement fair to anyone else, and neither Fleur nor Krum had agreed to compete against a second Hogwarts student. Dumbledore nodded.

"Harry will be scored separately from Hogwarts until we determine the nature of the fourth entry."

"Under which school?" Hermione asked.

No one answered.

"The parchment carries only his name," Dumbledore said.

He turned the parchment. Black ink crossed its centre:

HARRY POTTER

No school appeared beneath it. Harry stared at the handwriting, at the shape of letters someone else had made while deciding what should happen to him. It was not his. That should have mattered more than it did.

The meeting dissolved without resolving anything. Fleur, Krum and Cedric received written instructions for press photographs the following morning, as though the officials could recover some order by returning to their schedule. Bagman attempted to hand Harry the same sheet twice before leaving it on the table beside him. Karkaroff left threatening formal protest, with Madame Maxime following to make colder promises.

Crouch departed alone. As he passed Moody, the magical eye rotated towards the back of his head, and his hand shook once at his side. Harry saw the movement. Then the eye turned towards Harry, catching him watching before Crouch disappeared through the door.

The Great Hall had emptied while they argued. Harry discovered this when McGonagall led them from the champions' chamber, her footsteps echoing beneath the candles still burning above four abandoned tables. Half-finished cups stood between the benches. Across the far wall, a Hufflepuff banner conjured after Cedric's selection hung with its gold letters still rearranging themselves into congratulations.

No banner carried Harry's name. He found he preferred the silence. Fleur and Krum departed with their headmasters. Cedric remained near the staff table while Professor Sprout spoke to him. Bagman disappeared in search of the photographers he had scheduled. Snape crossed to the Slytherin doors without addressing Draconia. Moody stayed in the side chamber, but Harry knew the wall could not prevent his eye from watching. He resisted the urge to look back towards a face he would not be able to see.

"Mr Potter," McGonagall said.

He looked at her. The severity in her face had become something else. Fear did not suit Professor McGonagall. It made the control around it appear carved rather than natural.

"The Headmaster will contact your guardian immediately."

"I can tell him."

"Professor Dumbledore is required to provide a formal account."

"Sirius won't want a formal account."

McGonagall understood. Her eyes softened for a moment, though her voice retained enough firmness to carry the suggestion as an instruction.

"Then I suggest you reach him first."

She looked at Hermione and Draconia.

"You have twenty minutes before I expect each of you inside your proper common room."

Draconia lifted her chin. "Professor Snape—"

"Will be informed."

"Twenty minutes is insufficient."

McGonagall's nostrils narrowed.

"Thirty."

"Thank you."

"Do not negotiate further."

They left through the Entrance Hall.

Harry walked between the girls without choosing a destination. The castle had become unfamiliar around him. Corridors joined staircases he knew, yet every turn seemed to carry the possibility of students waiting beyond it. Portraits whispered as they passed. News had travelled through painted frames faster than footsteps.

"Harry Potter," a knight repeated from inside a battle scene.

His opponent lowered a sword. "Bit young."

Harry kept walking.

Hermione selected an empty Arithmancy classroom on the third floor. Draconia locked the door while Harry drew the curtains, though night already covered the windows. Their footsteps died away, leaving the soft patter of rain and the classroom's smell of chalk and old parchment. With the corridor shut out, there was nowhere else to go.

Harry stopped near the teacher's desk. Hermione placed her bag down, and Draconia remained against the door, both watching him with a patience he suddenly found difficult to bear.

"I didn't enter," Harry said.

"We know," Hermione replied.

"I didn't ask anyone."

"We know."

"I promised."

Draconia crossed the room.

"And you kept it."

"My name came out."

"Someone else broke the terms," she said. "Not you."

Harry looked down at his hands. They had begun shaking somewhere between the champions' room and the stairs, though he had not noticed until there was nothing left to do with them. He closed them, pressing his fingers into his palms.

Accusation had always possessed a strange power over him. At Privet Drive, being blamed often became evidence by itself. If Vernon repeated a charge loudly enough—food missing, Dudley upset, something broken—Harry eventually began searching his own behaviour for the moment which justified punishment. Defence only proved insolence. Silence proved guilt.

The Goblet had announced him before hundreds of witnesses. Some old, celebrated magic had placed his name in Dumbledore's hand. A smaller part of Harry, trained long before Hogwarts, immediately began asking what he must have done to deserve it.

"Part of me wanted to know."

Neither girl answered immediately. In the pause, Harry heard how the admission might sound and felt it growing worse, as though he had finally supplied the explanation everyone had been waiting to hear.

"When Cedric entered, I wondered whether I could do it. When Dumbledore described the tasks, I thought about winning. What if the Goblet—"

"No," Hermione said.

Harry looked up.

"Curiosity is not consent."

Her voice had become fierce, stopping the thought before he could finish making it into an accusation against himself.

"Wanting to know whether you are capable of something does not give anyone permission to force you into it. The Goblet did not reach inside your mind and uncover a secret wish. Someone wrote your name."

Hermione stepped close enough that he could see the ink still caught beneath one thumbnail from her afternoon notes.

"You do not have to confess to wanting a thing you refused."

Draconia stopped before him.

"Someone changed the rules until your refusal stopped mattering."

Harry could see the Chamber in her face: the diary, the commands hidden inside thoughts, a child made into an instrument and blamed by the person using her. She knew what it cost to untangle a stolen choice from the things which had happened afterwards.

"They took your choice," she said. "Do not make it easier for them by calling that something you wanted."

Draconia's composure cracked around the final word. Not visibly enough for anyone who did not know her; completely enough for Harry. She was not only defending him. She was speaking to the girl who had opened a diary, answered a lonely voice and spent months believing that first mistake made every stolen action afterwards her own.

Harry's hands opened. Draconia took one and Hermione the other, neither waiting for him to explain what he needed. For several seconds they stood without speaking while he concentrated upon their fingers. Draconia's palm remained cooler than his; ink marked Hermione's thumb. Their grips were real, and neither tightened when he began to shake again.

"Where are you?" Hermione asked quietly.

"Hogwarts."

"What can you feel?"

"You. Both of you."

Hermione's thumb moved over his knuckle. Draconia shifted nearer until her shoulder touched his. Harry held on while the room returned in pieces: chalk upon the board, the locked door, rain against the windows and two people refusing to let an enchanted object define what he had chosen.

Draconia's grip tightened.

"What do you know?"

Harry looked at them.

"I didn't enter."

"Again," Draconia said.

"You believe me."

"Again."

He breathed.

"We'll find out who did."

Hermione nodded.

"Better."

Reluctantly, Harry released their hands and removed Sirius's mirror. His fingers missed the corner on the first tap, and Draconia steadied the glass while he tried again. The surface remained dark. He tapped a fourth time, then a fifth, watching his own pale reflection and trying not to measure how long each unanswered moment lasted.

"He may be speaking to Dumbledore," Hermione said.

"Or outside the wards," Draconia added.

The mirror brightened, replacing Harry's reflection with Sirius, his hair loose and his wand drawn. Beyond him, the familiar kitchen lamp was still alight. Harry held tightly to that ordinary glimpse.

"Harry?"

"I'm here."

Sirius's gaze moved across his face.

"Dumbledore's Patronus arrived. What happened?"

Harry had rehearsed no version.

"The Goblet picked Cedric."

"Good."

"Then it picked me."

Sirius stopped moving. Even the wand in his hand remained fixed, and the mirror became unnaturally still.

"Say that again."

"Someone entered my name as a fourth school. Crouch says the contract means I have to compete."

Behind Sirius, a chair fell. Remus entered the frame.

"He is fourteen," Sirius said.

The words were directed at no one in the classroom.

"I know."

"The Age Line—"

"Someone older did it. Hermione thinks the Goblet was Confunded."

Sirius's eyes reached Hermione.

"Can it be broken?"

"Nobody knows," she said. "The Goblet extinguished after selection. Crouch claims Harry's magic may become unstable if he refuses."

"Claims."

"We asked for evidence."

"And?"

"There isn't any."

Sirius swore, and the mirror distorted around the force of it. Harry flinched before he could prevent the movement. Sirius saw, his anger changing direction immediately as he leaned closer to the glass.

"Not at you."

Harry looked down.

"I promised I wouldn't enter."

"You didn't."

"My name—"

"Harry." Sirius's voice cut through him. "Did you write it?"

"No."

"Did you give it to anyone?"

"No."

"Then you kept your promise."

The same words Draconia had used. Harry's vision blurred, and he took off his glasses to clean them against his robe, though they were not dirty. It gave him something to do while the assurance reached the part of him still waiting to be blamed.

"Are you coming?"

Something in Sirius's face gave way. Until then he had looked ready to tear apart the people responsible; now he looked as though the distance between them had hurt him.

"You could not keep me away with an Age Line."

"Professor Dumbledore may try," Remus said.

"Then he can enjoy failure."

Remus leaned towards the mirror.

"We will be there tomorrow. Tonight, stay with Hermione and Draconia as long as the professors permit. Do not eat or drink anything not taken directly from the tables. Keep your wand attached."

"Moody said Nott might use someone trusted."

"Moody is correct."

Draconia's hand found Harry's shoulder. Sirius saw it.

"Draconia."

"I'm here."

"Do your parents know?"

"Not yet."

"Tell them."

"I will."

"Hermione?"

"Already making a list," she said.

Sirius closed his eyes briefly.

"Good."

Relief softened the word. Harry could hear how much it mattered that the girls were there. Harry put his glasses back on.

"What do I do?"

Sirius opened his eyes.

"Tonight? Breathe. Eat something if you can. Let your friends remain irritating. Tomorrow, we make every adult involved explain exactly why their rules matter more than your refusal."

"And if they still say I have to compete?"

Sirius looked towards Remus. Neither lied.

"Then we prepare you," Sirius said. "And we find the person who did this before they decide what should happen inside those tasks."

Harry nodded. The answer frightened him, but it also gave the fear edges. There were things to prepare for, questions to investigate, someone to find. He could begin with one of those.

"Call when you reach your dormitory," Sirius said.

"That will be after curfew."

"Tragic."

Harry almost smiled.

"All right."

"I love you."

Sirius said it quickly, as if speed could carry the words through before embarrassment built a wall. Harry stared at the mirror. Remus looked away, offering what privacy he could, while Hermione's fingers closed around the edge of the desk and Draconia stood very still. Nobody moved to fill the silence before Harry found an answer.

"Love you too," Harry whispered.

The words felt frightening for reasons the Tournament did not. They also felt true. Harry let them remain in the room without covering them with a joke. When the connection ended, Harry's reflection returned to the glass. He placed the mirror face-down upon the desk and left his hand resting over it for a moment. Nobody commented. Hermione looked down at her parchment as though allowing him privacy. Draconia's fingers tightened once upon his shoulder. Neither pretended not to have heard.

Hermione began extracting parchment from her bag.

"We need a timeline. Everyone who approached the Goblet, every member of staff with access before it was placed and every reference to fourth-school deceptions in the Tournament archive."

"Crouch," Draconia said.

Harry looked at her.

"He knew immediately the Goblet could not be examined after it extinguished."

"He helped write the rules," Hermione replied.

"And Winky referred to a young master who took his wand."

"You think he entered me?"

Draconia shook her head. "I think he is afraid of something connected to this."

Hermione wrote his name at the top of the page. Below it, she added Nott. Then Moody. Harry saw.

"He warned us."

"He watches you," Hermione said. "Warning does not remove him from consideration."

Harry thought of the magical eye fixed upon the Goblet, of Moody asking whether he would enter. He remembered the advice concerning trusted people, missing time and gifts without origins. Good advice; he had repeated it to Sirius. Now, with Moody's name beneath Crouch's and Nott's, Harry wondered what the professor might have drawn his attention away from.

Draconia added Karkaroff.

"Former Death Eater," she said. "Father disliked him even before changing sides."

"That may not be evidence," Hermione replied.

"It is a promising foundation."

The list lengthened. Twenty minutes passed, then thirty, while Hermione drew lines between names and Draconia disputed which connections deserved them. Harry remained close to the desk, answering questions about who had spoken to him and when. McGonagall knocked at forty-two and entered without waiting, surveying the books, the names and three untouched chairs pulled close together.

"I see my instruction was interpreted generously."

"We lost track of time," Hermione said.

"You constructed a colour-coded suspect list."

"Efficiently."

McGonagall's mouth tightened. Harry thought she might remove points. Instead, she placed a plate of sandwiches upon the desk, moving a stack of parchment aside to make room. Their smell reminded him that dinner had been hours ago, though he could not imagine being hungry.

"Eat. Then return to your common rooms."

She looked at Harry.

"Professor Dumbledore has secured the Goblet's remains. Nobody will approach them tonight."

"Can he break the contract?"

"He is trying."

"That wasn't the question."

Pain crossed her face.

"No. Not yet."

Harry picked up a sandwich because Sirius had told him to eat. The bread was soft, the filling still faintly cool; he could recognise both without tasting either.

Cedric waited outside the classroom, the parchment bearing his name still folded in one hand. Hufflepuff cheers echoed faintly from several floors below. He glanced towards them when the door opened, then returned his attention to Harry.

"I thought you'd be with your House," Harry said.

"They're celebrating."

"You should be there."

"I will." Cedric looked at Hermione and Draconia. "Can I speak to Harry?"

Neither girl moved.

"Alone," he added.

Draconia folded her arms. Harry touched her sleeve.

"It's Cedric."

"Trusted people remain capable of poor decisions."

Cedric accepted the accusation. "End of the corridor. You can watch."

The arrangement satisfied nobody, but Harry and Cedric made their way to a rain-darkened window at the end of the corridor. Hermione and Draconia remained close enough that privacy became mostly symbolic. Rain traced crooked paths down the glass between reflections of the two boys, while the distant celebration continued below.

"The Hufflepuffs think you cheated," Cedric said.

Harry looked at the glass.

"Right."

"Not all of them. Enough."

"Do you?"

"No."

Cedric answered before Harry finished asking, firmly enough to distinguish himself from the voices Harry had heard in the Hall.

"Then why tell me?"

"Because you'll hear it tomorrow, and I don't want you thinking everyone agrees."

The honesty hurt less than discovering it in whispers would have. Harry appreciated Cedric for understanding that kindness was not the same as keeping unpleasant things secret. Harry pressed his fingers against the stone sill.

"They waited years for something to be theirs," Cedric continued. "Hufflepuff gets described as loyal when people mean unimportant. Tonight, the Goblet chose one of us, and before the celebration finished, your name came out."

"I didn't want that."

"I know. They don't yet."

"I'm sorry."

Cedric's expression sharpened. "For what?"

Harry did not have an answer which survived the question. He had meant being selected, interrupting the celebration, existing in the particular way which caused danger to arrive and rearrange everyone else's life around it. Spoken aloud, none of those would explain what he should have done differently. He looked at Cedric's folded parchment instead.

"Exactly," Cedric said.

Cedric unfolded the parchment, smoothing its crease with one thumb. His name appeared in his own careful writing, with Hogwarts beneath it. Harry could remember him reading those words before he let them fall into the flame.

"I entered because I wanted to be chosen. You didn't. That makes the difference simple to me."

Harry looked at him.

"What happens when the scoring starts?"

"We compete."

"Against each other."

"Probably."

"And then?"

Cedric folded the parchment again.

"Then I try to win without becoming someone who thinks you deserved this."

The words settled something Harry had not realised was moving. He had been waiting for competition to make belief conditional. Cedric had made room for both.

"All right."

"And you do the same."

Harry frowned. "Try to win?"

"Try not to decide my competing means I abandoned you."

The possibility had already existed somewhere Harry did not wish to examine. Cedric would train to defeat him. Hufflepuff would cheer when Harry failed. None of it had to erase the boy who believed him in the champions' chamber.

"Deal," Harry said.

Cedric held out his hand. Harry took it.

"For what it's worth," Cedric said, "if someone entered you because they expect the tasks to kill you, they have misunderstood how irritating you are."

Harry smiled faintly.

"That's what my friends say."

"They look ready to prove it."

Harry followed his gaze. Hermione held a rolled parchment like a weapon. Draconia's arms remained folded, but the toe of one shoe tapped against the floor—a quiet betrayal of how badly she wanted the conversation finished and Harry returned. Their nearly identical expressions of suspicion made Harry's smile linger. He released Cedric's hand, his fingers steadier than they had been inside the classroom.

"Congratulations."

Cedric's smile returned.

"Thank you."

Cedric left for Hufflepuff, carrying his folded parchment back towards the celebration he had chosen. McGonagall escorted the other three towards Gryffindor Tower first; afterwards, she would see Draconia safely to the dungeons. The castle had gone quiet, but portraits woke as they passed. Their eyes followed Harry. Whispers moved between frames ahead of him. At the Fat Lady's corridor, Draconia stopped before continuing with McGonagall.

"Harry."

He turned. She raised three fingers. Hermione lifted hers beside him, and Harry raised his own, holding the gesture long enough for Draconia to see it clearly.

Three. Whatever happened.

The Fat Lady opened, and noise struck the corridor. Gryffindor waited inside with banners, food and Harry's name written in gold across the wall. Someone near the fire saw him first, and the shout travelled through the room before he had stepped inside.

For one disorienting instant, the common room resembled every fantasy Harry might once have formed about belonging there. Red and gold covered the walls. People he knew shouted his name. A space had been cleared for him beside the fire, and hands reached to pull him into it.

Then the blue line around his wrist seemed to tighten in memory.

The cheering swelled as gold sparks crossed the room. Someone released a flock of miniature dragons beneath the ceiling, where they circled the banner bearing Harry's name and breathed harmless fire into the curtains. The room smelled of butterbeer and warm food, prepared for a triumph he had not wanted.

Fred and George reached him first, slipping through the crowd with the practised ease of people who usually caused it. Their beards had disappeared. Their excitement had not.

"Brilliant," Fred said.

"Completely illegal," George added.

"Possibly fatal."

"We're devastated you didn't share the method."

Harry remained beside the portrait hole, with the last of the corridor's cool air against his back. He could not make himself take the place they had cleared for him by the fire.

"I didn't enter."

The sentence felt too small for the room, striking the cheering and vanishing beneath it. The twins waited for the rest, their smiles holding while they expected him to explain the trick.

"Who did?" Fred asked.

"I don't know."

The celebration continued around them, but its centre became uncertain. Students nearest Harry began listening, while those farther away kept calling for him to come and tell them how he had managed it. Seamus approached carrying two bottles of butterbeer.

"Come on, Harry. We won't tell Dumbledore how you crossed the line."

"I didn't cross it."

"Someone put your name in?" Dean asked.

"Yes."

"Who?"

"If I knew, I wouldn't have spent the last hour being questioned."

Seamus lowered one bottle.

"But you're competing."

"They say I have to."

"Then you're champion."

"No."

The word emerged harder than Harry intended. Conversations near them faded. Seamus held the butterbeer without offering it, and someone behind Dean lowered a handful of unused gold sparks. Harry heard his own breathing. Every face which had been delighted to see him now required a different emotion, and not everyone changed quickly enough. The delay hurt more than open disbelief might have done. Hermione stepped through the portrait hole and closed it behind her.

"Someone Confunded the Goblet into accepting a fourth school and submitted Harry as its only candidate. The contract attached itself to his magic. Attempting to remove his name caused him pain."

Her explanation was precise enough to become a weapon. Hermione looked from face to face as she spoke, making sure the students closest to them heard every part.

"This is not a celebration."

The final sentence silenced the room. Overhead, one of the miniature dragons struck the canopy and vanished in a burst of gold, the little celebration finishing itself while nobody watched. Fred looked at Harry properly, taking in the way he stood beside the exit and the hand Hermione kept near his elbow. The humour left his face.

"You really didn't do it."

"No."

"Right." Fred turned towards the banner. "George."

They removed it together. George extinguished the floating dragons with a sweep of his wand while Fred took down the banner, folding it inward so Harry's name disappeared first. Neither made a performance of the correction.

"We'll keep the food," George said quietly. "Under revised circumstances."

It was an attempt at normality rather than humour. Harry appreciated it enough not to answer. Not everyone appeared convinced. Harry saw it in the glances passing behind shoulders, the disappointment of people asked to surrender a story more exciting than the truth. Seamus looked at the butterbeer. Dean studied Harry as though trying to find which version fit the person he knew.

"I believe you," Dean said eventually.

Seamus rubbed the neck of one bottle. "I just don't understand how anyone could do it."

"Neither do we," Hermione replied. "That is why disbelief should be directed at the mechanism, not Harry."

Seamus's face coloured. "I didn't say he lied."

"You asked him three times."

"Hermione," Harry said.

She stopped because he asked, not because her anger had finished. Neville crossed from the fire.

"He said he didn't."

Seamus looked at him. Neville's voice shook. Harry could see the effort it cost him to stand in front of the others, but he continued anyway.

"That should be enough until there's a reason it isn't."

The words did not restore the party. They gave Harry somewhere inside it to stand, with someone who required no demonstration before believing what he had said. One by one, the remaining decorations came down. Nobody resumed cheering. A few students approached Harry to say they believed him; others went upstairs without meeting his eyes. The room became what it should have been from the beginning—not certain, not celebratory, but concerned.

"Thank you," he said.

Neville nodded.

Hermione touched Harry's elbow. "Upstairs."

Nobody stopped them. The fourth-year dormitory remained empty, and Harry welcomed the familiar untidiness of trunks and shoes beside the beds. He closed the door, drew every curtain around his own bed and took out the mirror.

The celebration food remained untouched downstairs. He had eaten half of McGonagall's sandwich because Sirius asked and could not imagine swallowing anything else. The canopy reduced Hogwarts to a square of red fabric, his trunk, the faint outline of Hermione beyond the curtain and the mirror shaking slightly in his hand.

Sirius answered immediately.

"You're in the dormitory?"

"Yes."

"Hermione?"

Harry turned the mirror. She waved from beside the bed, then paused at the doorway before leaving for her own stairs. Harry watched until she had gone, grateful she had stayed long enough for him to make the call.

"Draconia?" Sirius asked.

"McGonagall took her to Slytherin."

"Call her."

"She has the other mirror."

"Good." Sirius's face remained tense. "Lock the curtains."

"They're fabric."

"Symbolically lock them."

Harry tied the cords.

"Done."

"Sleep if you can. I'll be there before breakfast."

"You don't know the password."

"I am Sirius Black. Doors have historically failed to discourage me."

Harry sat on the bed, feeling the familiar dip of the mattress beneath him. Noise resumed gradually below: discussion and argument, his name moving between other people. He could no longer make out individual words, which made it easier to keep his attention upon the mirror.

"Sirius?"

"I'm here."

Harry believed him. The words did not make the castle safe, but for one minute he could rest without deciding how to protect himself from everything beyond the curtains. Sirius was watching, and Harry could let that be enough.

They remained connected until the mirror he shared with Draconia began glowing beside his pillow. Sirius saw the second light spill over the red bed curtains and waited while Harry reached for it.

"Answer her," he said.

"Will you stay?"

"Try getting rid of me."

Harry opened Draconia's mirror with one hand while keeping Sirius's propped against the pillow. Draconia appeared beneath green curtains, pale hair loose around her face and wandlight trembling across the canopy. Relief crossed her features when she saw him.

"You reached the dormitory."

"Apparently."

"Did Gryffindor behave?"

Harry looked towards the curtain through which the last discussion downstairs still travelled.

"Eventually."

Draconia understood the part he omitted. Her mouth tightened, and her eyes moved over his face as though checking what the delay had cost him.

"I will hex them tomorrow."

"Take a number," Sirius said from the other mirror.

Harry laughed once. It was exhausted and not entirely happy, but it belonged to him.

He had kept his promise.

Someone had found a way to make the promise irrelevant.

They had not made him face what followed alone.
