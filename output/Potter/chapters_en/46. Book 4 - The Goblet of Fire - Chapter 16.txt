16

The Map changed while they ran.

Harry held it open between both hands, the parchment snapping in the wind made by their own speed. He trusted Hermione to keep him from colliding with the walls and Draconia to choose the shortest route; looking anywhere except the ink would mean losing the dot among the hundreds still roaming Hogwarts after curfew. Corridors shifted beneath his thumbs, and staircases swung away from their drawn positions. The name labelled BARTY CROUCH left the Defence Against the Dark Arts office, crossed the third-floor landing and moved towards the central staircase.

Towards them.

Professor McGonagall remained high above them in Dumbledore's office. Dumbledore himself was nowhere on the parchment.

"He's heading down," Harry said.

"Then we intercept," Draconia replied.

Hermione caught Harry's sleeve before the second-floor turn. "We find McGonagall."

"He'll reach the Entrance Hall first."

"Harry—"

"We don't confront him. We keep him in sight."

"That is how confrontation begins."

The staircase ahead began turning away, its lower steps grinding free of the landing just as they reached it. Draconia pointed her wand.

"Subsisto."

Stone groaned. The staircase stopped halfway between landings, leaving a gap of nearly three feet above the dark shaft.

"That spell is not meant for architecture," Hermione said.

"Neither is the staircase."

Draconia jumped. For one impossible second she hung between the landings with her robes spread behind her, then her shoes struck the opposite edge. She caught the banister, spun and reached back. Harry jumped after her. Her fingers closed around his forearm and dragged him forwards before his heel could slip. Hermione landed behind them with less grace and considerably more disapproval, while the staircase shuddered against the holding charm.

They reached the third floor. Draconia released the spell, and the stone structure swept away with offended force, carrying a portrait of three monks into an argument none of them had been awake for moments earlier. The map-dot approached the corner. Harry stopped, and both girls stopped with him.

Footsteps sounded beyond it: uneven, one hard wooden strike after another. Moody came around the corner.

For nearly nine months, Harry had learned that rhythm. Staff, wooden leg, boot: a hard, uneven procession which announced the professor before his scarred face appeared. It had followed Harry through corridors, paced behind him during Defence lessons and approached his desk whenever Crouch—whenever Moody—thought he had done something worth praising. Now each strike carried the man towards them through a corridor gone terribly quiet.

His staff met the flagstones beside the carved leg. The magical eye rotated towards them at once, passing over Harry, Hermione and Draconia before fixing on the open parchment. On the Map, BARTY CROUCH occupied the exact place Moody stood. No second dot appeared.

Harry folded the parchment. Too late. The magical eye had seen through it.

"Out after curfew," Moody said.

His voice sounded the same. The scar pulling one side of his mouth remained the same; the damaged nose, grey hair and weathered hand around the staff belonged to the professor who had taught Harry to fight the Imperius Curse. The man who had given him a path through the First Task, whose approval had felt dangerous and useful in equal measure.

The Map did not care what a person looked like.

"We found Crouch," Harry said.

Moody's ordinary eye narrowed. "Did you?"

"Inside your office."

Silence reached along the corridor. Hermione's wand remained hidden inside her sleeve. Draconia's satchel shifted as one hand entered it. Harry could feel both girls deciding whether motion would begin the fight, and kept his own wand still against his leg.

Moody's magical eye turned backwards into his skull, looking through walls for approaching staff.

"Clever map," he said.

Harry's mouth went dry. No denial.

"Where is Professor Moody?" Hermione asked.

The man smiled. Harry had seen Moody laugh with half his mouth and bare his teeth at carelessness, danger and bureaucratic rules. This was none of those expressions. The smile sat strangely within the old scars, too smooth at the corners, as though it belonged on a younger mouth and had been waiting all year for the disguise to stop requiring restraint.

"Closer than you think."

Draconia moved first, drawing a silver coin from her satchel and crushing it inside her fist. Green light burst between her fingers. The Malfoy family alarm crossed every ward connected to Lucius, Narcissa and Snape.

Barty Crouch Junior attacked.

His wand left the staff so quickly that Harry never saw the hidden compartment open. One instant his right hand gripped only dark wood; the next, a wand lay along his forearm and red light tore down the corridor.

"Down!"

Harry caught Hermione around the waist and pulled her with him. They hit the floor together. The curse passed close enough to warm his hair before striking a portrait of a moonlit orchard, where painted apples burst from their branches and the occupants fled screaming into neighbouring frames.

Draconia rolled behind a suit of armour. The alarm coin had cut her palm when she crushed it; blood ran between her fingers, making her wand slick.

"Expelliarmus!"

Her spell hit Crouch's shield and sprayed sparks across the ceiling. Harry rose onto one knee.

"Stupefy!"

Crouch deflected it with his staff. Hermione's Body-Bind came from the opposite side, but he turned through both attacks, his carved leg anchoring him with unnatural strength. He answered with a curse aimed at the torch bracket above her head.

Iron tore loose from stone. Hermione rolled, and the bracket shattered where her shoulder had been, spilling fire across the floor. Harry stamped on the nearest flames and sent another Stunner from one knee. Crouch turned his staff, sending the spell into the ceiling.

"Three against one," he said. "He told me friendship would make you predictable."

The words chilled Harry more than the curse had. Crouch knew who he would pull out of danger first. He knew Hermione preferred binding magic in enclosed spaces, knew Draconia carried the alarm coins in the left pocket of her satchel and drew her wand with her right. He knew because he had watched them all year.

Harry sent a Blasting Curse at the floor before him. Stone exploded, peppering the walls with fragments. Crouch shielded his face, and Draconia used the half-second to close the distance, her boots cutting through the dust before Harry understood she had moved. Her curse struck his shoulder.

Black robes tore. The skin beneath rippled. For an instant, scar tissue loosened like wax held too near a flame, the shoulder narrowed, and a smooth, pale arm appeared. It was wasted from years indoors and branded by the faded lines of a Dark Mark. Then Moody's stolen flesh dragged itself back over the bone.

Hermione saw. "Polyjuice."

"More than that," Crouch replied.

He drove his staff into the ground. The flagstones rolled, and the corridor buckled in a single travelling wave. Harry lost his footing, striking the floor hard enough to jar his teeth. Hermione hit the wall. The suit of armour lurched from its plinth and collapsed over Draconia with a crash of metal, burying her beneath a breastplate, shield and several centuries of dust.

Crouch crossed the moving floor towards Harry, his magical eye staring without blinking.

"You were supposed to remain uncertain."

Harry pushed himself backwards. "I disappoint people."

"Not him."

Crouch raised the wand.

"Imperio."

The curse struck Harry in the chest. Warmth entered his mind, bringing relief so complete that the corridor softened around its edges. Stone beneath his palms became unimportant. Pain vanished from his knee, fear loosened inside his ribs, and the noise of Hermione dragging herself upright receded until it might have come from another floor. Crouch's scarred face filled the world with certainty.

Give him the Map.

Simple.

Stand. Follow.

There would be no more decisions, no fear for Hermione or Draconia. No maze, no graveyard dream, no impossible answer waiting between two girls. No need to decide which danger belonged to him and which person would be hurt if he chose wrongly. Obedience spread through him like sleep, and his fingers closed around the parchment.

No.

The thought was small. Crouch smiled.

"Good. Bring it here."

Harry began to stand. His bruised knee protested, but the curse turned even that pain distant, something happening to a body he no longer needed to manage. He remembered Crouch Senior striking his own head beside the Forest.

Obey. Work. Sleep. Obey.

Years inside the same warmth. A life narrowed until command felt like choice. Harry straightened and took one step. Crouch extended his hand.

Harry punched him.

The blow caught Moody's damaged nose. Bone shifted beneath Harry's knuckles, and Crouch staggered into the wall, more startled than injured. The warm command splintered into a burst of white pain behind Harry's eyes.

"You really," Harry said through the pressure splitting his skull, "should stop teaching me how that curse works."

He drove the Map beneath his shirt and raised his wand. Crouch's expression filled with delighted fury, and their spells met. Crimson and blue-white light collided between them, neither curse lethal but both violent enough to bleach every colour from the corridor. The joined magic screamed. Portrait glass fractured on both walls, and the flames Hermione had avoided flattened towards the windows.

Harry's wand shook. The Imperius Curse had left his muscles slow and badly connected, but he braced one shoe against a broken flagstone and held. Crouch leaned into the connection with the strength of an adult wizard inside a stolen body, confident that he knew precisely how long Harry could maintain the defence.

Then he glanced towards Hermione. Harry understood the feint because Crouch had taught it to him. He broke the connection first and threw himself sideways; Crouch's second curse missed him and cut a smoking trench through the wall.

Hermione's voice cut through the light.

"Deprimo!"

The ceiling above Crouch collapsed. He turned to shield, and Harry's next spell struck his chest, throwing him backwards through the dust. Draconia emerged from the fallen armour bleeding near one temple and pointed her wand at the corridor behind him.

"Colloportus!"

Every door sealed.

"No exits," she said.

Blood had reached her chin, and one sleeve hung torn from shoulder to cuff. She stood between Crouch and the nearest staircase anyway. He rose inside the settling dust, looking at her with a familiarity Harry wished he had not earned.

"Your father had the same confidence."

Draconia froze. "You were at the World Cup."

"I watched Lucius decide he possessed principles. It was very moving."

"You cast the Mark."

"With my father's wand." Crouch touched the stolen magical eye. "I wanted them to remember what loyalty looked like."

"They remembered cowardice in masks," Draconia said. "I can understand how you confused the two."

The smile vanished from Crouch's borrowed face. Hermione moved beside Harry.

"Where is Crouch Senior?"

"Beyond caring what his children became."

The answer struck even Crouch. His mouth tightened around something which might once have been grief. Portraits shouted from both ends of the corridor, and bells began ringing behind sealed classroom doors as Draconia's alarm reached the school wards.

Professors were coming. Crouch heard them, and the magical eye spun.

"We are not finished," he told Harry.

"You seem surrounded."

"I did not mean us."

His left hand opened. A black coin lay on his palm, marked with a serpent winding around a standing stone. Draconia recognised the crest.

"Nott."

Harry cast. The Disarming Charm reached Crouch as his fingers closed, and the corridor folded. Dark magic compressed the air into a single point above the coin. Sound disappeared. Dust, flame and torn scraps of canvas streamed towards Crouch as his stolen body stretched after them.

Dumbledore appeared at the far staircase with Snape and McGonagall behind him. Harry had never seen the Headmaster move so quickly: one moment the landing was empty, the next blue robes snapped through the archway and his wand was already raised. It moved once. A band of white fire wrapped around Crouch's chest and both arms.

The Portkey pulled. For a second, the two forces held him suspended, and Moody's face came apart.

The transformation peeled backwards in pieces. Cheekbones narrowed beneath sagging flesh; grey hair darkened at the roots, then shortened in uneven clumps. Scars travelled across skin becoming younger beneath them, and the ruined nose straightened with a wet crack. The false magical eye fell from its socket and hung on the leather strap, still spinning.

Barty Crouch Junior looked at Harry through one blue eye and one empty hollow. He was older than the courtroom photograph and thinner than any healthy man, with damp dark hair across his forehead. His mouth trembled with effort, terror and something horribly close to joy. The expression belonged to someone who had endured captivity and mistaken devotion for freedom.

"June," he gasped.

The Portkey won. He vanished, and white fire closed on empty air.

The false eye struck the floor. It continued moving, its blue iris rolling once towards Dumbledore, once towards the sealed doors and finally towards Harry. The pupil contracted as though it recognised him even without the man who had worn it. Then it stopped.

Harry looked down at his own wand. His hand had begun to shake only after the danger was gone.

No one was permitted to enter Moody's office immediately. Dumbledore sealed both ends of the corridor while Snape tested the handle, hinges and keyhole for curses. McGonagall raised a stone barrier from the floor and sent the trio behind it, ignoring their objections until Harry pointed out that the Map might identify anyone hidden inside.

The office showed one name: ALASTOR MOODY. It occupied the centre of the room and did not move.

Dumbledore opened the door without touching it, and the smell escaped first—stale potion, unwashed cloth and damp wood, with something medicinal attempting and failing to conceal the sourness of a body kept too long without sunlight or clean air. Harry followed before McGonagall could stop him.

The room looked almost as it had whenever he had visited for advice. Dark detectors crowded the shelves, and the Foe-Glass upon one wall showed shadowy figures moving near its surface. A Sneakoscope spun so quickly that its whistle had become one continuous note. Nothing else appeared disturbed; even the chair stood at the familiar angle beside the desk.

That familiarity was the worst part. Harry knew which chair Crouch had offered him and recognised the desk across which the impostor had slid books, warnings and praise. A chipped mug still stood beside a stack of homework, its bottom stained by the bitter tea Harry had smelled on the man's breath during lessons. He had sat within arm's reach of that mug, listening carefully, while another person lived beneath their feet.

Dozens of empty flasks filled the desk drawers. Most bore dates and careful measurements; several held a single grey hair suspended in residue. Polyjuice clung to every bottle. The preparation had been administered like medicine, with the same patient attention Harry had mistaken for a teacher's care.

Draconia entered behind him, with Hermione beside her. Snape stood near a large wooden trunk with seven locks along its front. On the Map, Alastor Moody's name remained inside it.

Dumbledore opened the first lock. The lid revealed folded robes that smelled of camphor; beneath them, a second lock opened onto spellbooks, then a third onto disabled Dark detectors. The fourth compartment contained bottles of stolen hair. The fifth held food, potion ingredients and a narrow shaving blade, and Harry looked away before he could imagine its use. The sixth was filled with copied notes in Moody's handwriting, hundreds of lines repeating the same phrases until one person's script became another's.

The seventh lid opened onto darkness far deeper than the trunk should have contained. A ladder descended into it.

Harry lit his wand, but the glow reached only a few feet before the darkness swallowed it. Dumbledore sent a sphere of white light down ahead of them, illuminating rung after rung until it reached the stone below.

At the bottom lay a man.

The real Alastor Moody had been reduced to bone and anger. Grey hair hung in uneven patches where it had been cut repeatedly, and one leg ended above the knee. An empty socket scarred half his face. Chains bound both wrists to iron rings in the floor, surrounded by scratches where metal had worried against stone during months of useless resistance.

A narrow mattress lay beneath him, with water standing in a bowl beyond the reach of one hand. Harry could see no blood or disorder to explain away as a sudden fit of cruelty. The cell had been maintained. Someone had calculated the minimum required to keep a human being alive and supplied exactly that.

Moody's remaining eye opened beneath the light and found Dumbledore.

"Took you long enough," he whispered.

Then it moved to Harry, and his face changed.

"Potter?"

Harry could not answer immediately. The voice was the same one that had instructed him, challenged him and urged him towards every task, yet its suspicion fell differently from the real man—unpolished by performance, stripped of the private amusement Crouch had never quite managed to hide. There was recognition in that exhausted eye, but none of the familiarity Harry had come to expect from his Defence professor. Moody knew him from the business with Pettigrew the previous year. Every lesson since September belonged to someone else.

Dumbledore descended the ladder. Only when Moody's remaining eye left him did Harry remember how to breathe.

Madam Pomfrey turned the hospital wing into a fortress. Moody occupied the bed nearest her office, with no curtains surrounding it; she refused to place anything between herself and a patient who had survived nine months inside a trunk. Warming stones lined his body, and three potions entered his arm through silver tubes. His missing leg had been bandaged, but the empty eye socket remained uncovered because every salve applied to the damaged skin disappeared beneath old protective magic.

He slept without becoming peaceful. Even unconscious, his jaw remained set, and his fingers curled whenever someone came too close to the bed.

Harry watched from the neighbouring mattress. Madam Pomfrey had placed him there after discovering the bruise spread across his chest by the Imperius Curse and the tremors passing through his arms whenever he tried to hold them still. Hermione occupied a chair beside him, one shoulder turning purple beneath a torn sleeve. Draconia sat upon the edge of his bed despite being told twice that beds were for patients; the cut across her palm had been sealed, and a white bandage circled her temple where the armour had struck.

Sirius paced between them and the door. He had arrived through Dumbledore's Floo seven minutes after the family alarm, with Remus behind him. Lucius and Narcissa arrived almost simultaneously from the Manor, proving either that the Malfoy wards operated faster or that Sirius had stopped to acquire a weapon. The knife remained inside his boot. Harry had seen the handle when he sat down.

"He taught you for nine months," Sirius said.

"Not him." Harry looked towards the sleeping Moody. "Crouch."

"Wearing that face."

"Yes."

Sirius resumed pacing, each turn ending several feet from Moody's bed. The resemblance made anger choose the wrong body, and Harry understood; he had flinched when the real man opened his remaining eye. Draconia had noticed. She noticed everything concerning fear and pretended not to, shifting just enough to sit between Harry and the other bed without appearing to do so.

"Crouch used his magical eye," Hermione said. "How?"

Dumbledore stood at the foot of Moody's bed with the false eye wrapped inside three layers of warded cloth. Snape examined the wooden leg upon a separate table, while McGonagall catalogued objects removed from the office. Her list had already reached four pages.

"Alastor's replacements were made from his own bone, blood and magic," Dumbledore said. "They are not ordinary prostheses. Each carries enough of his identity to answer certain protections as though the man himself were present."

Harry remembered the false eye travelling through Crouch's transformation while the rest of Moody's face came apart.

"The checks recognised the eye."

"The eye, the leg and Alastor's wand," Dumbledore said. "Nott bound them to Crouch through a ritual layered beneath the Polyjuice transformation. Sophisticated, cruel and dependent upon keeping Alastor alive."

Sirius stopped pacing. "You said you examined him."

"I did."

"You trusted the result."

"Yes."

The answer offered no defence, though Dumbledore's regret filled the space anyway. Harry had seen adults turn mistakes into explanations so often that the absence of one felt almost startling. It did not make the failure smaller; it made it possible to look at directly.

"Crouch possessed Alastor's memories?" Remus asked.

"Not directly. We found a Pensieve inside the office and instruments designed to extract memories without cooperation. He studied them. Combined with information supplied by Nott and his own observation, it appears to have been enough."

"Enough to fool you," Sirius said.

"Enough to fool all of us."

Sirius looked towards Harry. "Not all."

Harry disliked the pride because it arrived too close to responsibility. "The Map found him."

"You believed it."

Hermione's hand closed around Harry's uninjured one. Draconia looked at Sirius, her expression composed despite the bandage at her temple.

"Harry also resisted the Imperius Curse and struck him."

"With my fist," Harry said.

"A technique of questionable elegance."

"It worked."

"That is not elegance."

Sirius's mouth moved despite himself, and Madam Pomfrey crossed from her office before the almost-smile could become anything louder.

"If this becomes a celebration, leave."

No one moved. She checked Moody's pulse and adjusted one of the silver tubes. "He requires quiet."

"Has he spoken?" Dumbledore asked.

"He demanded his eye, his leg, his wand and the immediate arrest of everyone responsible. Then he asked where he was and lost consciousness."

"Improvement," McGonagall said faintly.

Madam Pomfrey turned to Harry. "You may leave after drinking this."

The potion she offered was purple and smoking. Harry accepted it with suspicion, watching a thin coil of vapour drift over the rim.

"What does it do?"

"Prevents the residual curse from turning your muscles against each other while you sleep."

He drank at once. It tasted of liquorice and metal, and the warmth left behind in his throat bore very little resemblance to comfort.

"Disgusting."

"Alive," Madam Pomfrey replied. "A useful exchange."

A crack sounded near the linen cupboard. Every wand in the room rose.

Winky stood beneath the high windows, wearing the green dress Narcissa had given her during summer. It hung more loosely now, and one corner of an old tea towel remained tucked inside the front pocket. Her enormous eyes found the real Moody's bed, then the false eye wrapped at Dumbledore's feet. She began to shake.

"Young master is gone."

Hermione lowered her wand first. "Winky."

The elf looked at her, hands twisting together.

"We aren't going to make you answer anything."

"Winky is answering."

Narcissa approached slowly and knelt several feet away, leaving the space between them untouched. "Only what you choose."

Winky's ears folded against her head. "Winky chose wrong."

Harry got off the bed. When Madam Pomfrey protested, he sat upon the floor instead, bringing himself closer to the elf's height without approaching her. The stone was cold through his robes, but it steadied him better than the mattress had.

"The gillyweed?" he asked.

Winky nodded, tears gathering in her eyes.

"Young master found Winky in kitchens. Young master is wearing bad wizard's face. Winky is not knowing. He says Master Crouch is alive. He says Master Crouch is suffering because Winky left."

"Did he threaten him?" Hermione asked.

Winky struck both hands over her mouth, and the old binding caught. Narcissa reached towards her, then stopped before touching.

"You do not have to say his command."

Winky's knees gave way. She sat upon the stone with the green dress gathered around her, looking suddenly smaller than the room could bear.

"Young master says Harry Potter must reach final task. Says if Harry Potter fails, Master Crouch dies before Winky can help him. Winky is stealing plant. Winky is carrying it. Winky is bad elf."

"You kept me alive," Harry said.

She stared at him.

"The gillyweed worked."

"Young master is wanting Harry Potter alive for bad reasons."

"That isn't the choice you made. You were trying to protect your family and me."

Winky's face crumpled. "Winky does not know how to choose both."

Harry looked at Hermione and Draconia. He remembered Dobby in the hospital wing two years earlier, punishing himself for failing one impossible duty while trying to fulfil another. House-elves were taught that obedience and love meant the same thing, then blamed when those commands tore them in opposite directions. Human beings were less honest about doing it to one another.

"Neither do I," he said.

The elf made one broken sound. Narcissa opened her arms, and Winky went to her, burying her face against pale robes and sobbing while Narcissa held her without ownership, order or condition. Lucius turned away; his hand tightened upon the silver head of his cane.

"Where is Crouch?" Sirius asked quietly.

Winky heard, and her crying changed. "Young master says father is finished."

The hospital wing became still.

"Finished how?" McGonagall asked.

"Winky is not knowing. Young master is laughing. Says there will be no more orders after tonight."

Crouch Senior had escaped the Imperius Curse and crossed miles of Forest to confess. His son had reached him first. Dumbledore closed his eyes for a moment before asking, "Did he tell you where he would go?"

Winky shook her head against Narcissa. "Home."

"The Crouch residence?"

"Young master says Dark Lord is home."

Harry's scar burned. This time the pain carried recognition rather than memory, opening above his right eye with enough force to drop him onto one hand. Hermione caught his shoulder. Draconia was beside him at once, her newly bandaged hand closing around his wrist hard enough for him to feel her pulse through the cloth.

Somewhere beyond the hospital walls, laughter entered Harry's mind, high, cold and pleased. For one hideous instant he felt the satisfaction behind it: a loyal servant recovered, an enemy silenced and a plan bruised without being broken.

Then it disappeared.

The real Moody woke at dawn. Harry had fallen asleep in the neighbouring bed despite Madam Pomfrey's promise that he could leave. Sirius occupied the chair beside him with his head tilted against the wall; Hermione slept curled beneath a borrowed blanket. Draconia remained upright against the footboard, wand across her lap and eyes closed without looking rested.

A glass bottle shattered, and Harry woke with his wand raised.

Moody sat halfway out of bed. He had torn one silver tube from his arm and thrown the nearest potion at a shadow; its contents ran down the wall beneath the broken glass. His remaining eye swept the room, finding the windows, doors and every visible wand before it settled anywhere else.

"Hospital wing," Harry said.

Moody turned. His face was the face Crouch had worn, but without the magical eye the scars appeared incomplete. Without the wooden leg, his body ended beneath the blanket in a shape Harry's mind kept refusing to accept. His unevenly cut hair showed where the disguise had been supplied. The anger belonged to him.

Harry lowered his wand. "Hogwarts," he added. "Crouch escaped."

Moody's gaze moved over him. "Potter."

"You remember me."

"Of course I remember you. Pettigrew, last year." Moody gripped the edge of the blanket. "That was me, Potter. I was taken before term began. Haven't been outside that damned trunk since."

Sirius woke so abruptly that his chair struck the floor behind him. For one instant, godfather and Auror pointed wands at each other.

"Sirius," Harry said.

Neither lowered his. Moody's eye narrowed. "Black." Recognition did not settle the question; after Crouch, a familiar face proved nothing.

"Prove it's you."

"You attempted to steal my enchanted boot during an Order meeting and spent three days hopping because it glued itself to your foot."

Sirius's wand lowered by one inch. "That could be in the memories Crouch stole."

"You cried when James married Lily."

"Everyone knows that."

"You blamed pollen. In December."

Remus, awake in a chair near the door, made a quiet sound which might have been agreement. Sirius lowered the wand.

"Fine."

Moody looked at Harry again. "He taught you."

Harry nodded.

"Imperius?"

"Yes."

The man's expression closed. "Demonstrated Unforgivables upon children while wearing my face."

"He used spiders."

"Then used the curse on you."

It was not a question. Harry looked towards the bruise beneath his nightshirt, suddenly conscious of its ache against the sheets.

"Last night. I resisted."

"Because he trained you."

"Partly."

Moody leaned back against the pillows. For the first time, anger failed to organise his face, leaving something exhausted and difficult beneath it.

"Anything he taught you," he said, "verify it."

"Most of it worked."

"That's how useful lies survive."

Harry thought of dragons, gillyweed and the praise Crouch had offered whenever he became easier to guide.

"I know."

Moody's gaze sharpened. "Do you?"

Harry almost answered automatically. Hermione stirred beneath her blanket, and he remembered the distinction he had been trying to learn: good kind or bad kind. Knowing that something had happened was easier than understanding what it had left behind.

"Not yet," he said.

Moody studied him, then nodded. "Better."

The Ministry arrived before breakfast. Cornelius Fudge entered Hogwarts with twelve Aurors, two legal clerks and the expression of a man offended that disaster had occurred before he finished dressing. A lime-green bowler hat sat crooked upon his head. Amelia Bones walked beside him in severe black robes, her monocle already fixed over one eye.

They sealed the Defence corridor and questioned every person who had spoken to the false Moody since September. By noon, the line reached the marble staircase, where frightened students kept asking each other whether lessons had been cancelled. No one seemed certain who could answer.

Harry gave his statement inside Dumbledore's office beneath the silver instruments whose purposes no one had explained to him. Too many bodies crowded the circular room. The windows had been opened, yet the air still smelled of Floo smoke, wet wool and the sharp preserving charms surrounding the evidence upon Dumbledore's desk.

Hermione and Draconia sat beside him despite a clerk's attempt to describe them as unnecessary witnesses. Sirius stood behind Harry's chair. Lucius occupied the wall near the door with Narcissa, Remus and a solicitor whose narrow case contained more paper than appeared physically possible.

Fudge sat opposite Dumbledore, with Bagman and the foreign headmasters completing the circle. Karkaroff looked ill. Madame Maxime looked capable of placing the Minister inside a drawer.

"Let us establish what we know," Amelia Bones said. She possessed the only voice in the room which did not attempt to control its mood.

"Barty Crouch Junior survived Azkaban through an illegal substitution arranged by his parents. He remained concealed within the Crouch residence under the Imperius Curse until the World Cup. After the Cup, Theodore Nott's father removed him from the residence and helped abduct Alastor Moody before the start of term. Crouch then entered Hogwarts beneath a magically reinforced disguise."

"Allegedly," Fudge said.

Amelia's monocle turned towards him. "We recovered Moody from a trunk."

"I refer to Nott's involvement."

Draconia placed a black coin bearing her family's copy of the Nott seal upon the table. Her bandaged fingers did not shake until she withdrew them. Beneath a crystal evidence dome, the serpent-and-stone crest could be compared with the description of the Portkey that had carried Crouch away.

"That is my family's formal copy of the Nott seal," she said. "The original appears upon eleven generations of Ministry property records. The standing stone belongs to the older ritual device. His signet uses the serpent across an eight-pointed star; both are recorded under the same family."

Lucius's solicitor opened one narrow file, and Fudge adjusted his hat.

"Old families exchange objects."

"Not emergency Portkeys keyed to their blood," Lucius said.

Karkaroff shifted in his chair. The Dark Mark remained hidden beneath his sleeve, but Harry saw him feel it; his hand closed over his forearm for a moment before returning to his lap.

Amelia continued. "Crouch Junior escaped. Nott is not present at any registered residence. Barty Crouch Senior remains missing and, according to testimony provided by Winky, may be dead."

"The elf's testimony is compromised," Fudge said. "Her attachment to the family—"

Hermione rose halfway from her chair. Harry caught her sleeve, and she sat because Winky had asked not to be made the centre of another room. Her expression promised the Minister would regret surviving this one.

"Crouch named Voldemort," Harry said.

Fudge flinched. "He named a dead man while delirious."

"His son told Winky the Dark Lord was home."

"His son is a convicted fanatic."

"Who lived inside Hogwarts for nine months without you noticing."

The Minister's face reddened. "That failure belongs to the school."

Sirius's hand settled upon the back of Harry's chair. Dumbledore remained calm.

"It belongs to everyone who found certainty easier than investigation."

Fudge looked away first. Madame Maxime placed both broad hands upon the desk, making a small silver instrument tremble beside them.

"The Tournament is finished."

"Agreed," Karkaroff said immediately. "My champion was attacked upon school grounds by a Ministry fugitive. Durmstrang withdraws."

"You cannot," Bagman said.

Every face turned towards him. He had lost the brightness usually surrounding the Tournament; his yellow robes were creased, and the dark crescents beneath his eyes made him appear older and less harmless.

"The Goblet's contract remains active until the final task concludes."

"Then conclude it," Maxime said.

"The Cup has not been claimed."

"Declare no victor."

Bagman rubbed both hands over his face. "We attempted that after Potter's selection. The contract does not recognise administrative withdrawal. If a champion refuses a task, binding pressure accumulates until participation becomes physically necessary."

Harry remembered the pain that followed every attempt to reject his place: magic drawing tight around his ribs, his heartbeat turning uneven, the Goblet demanding movement towards danger without caring whether consent existed. His hand went to his chest before he stopped it.

"What if all four refuse?" Hermione asked.

"The pressure applies to all four."

Fleur stood beside Madame Maxime's chair. "You are saying we complete this spectacle or the Goblet punishes us."

Bagman did not answer. That was an answer.

"The final task can be stripped of danger," Dumbledore said. "No unknown creatures. No imported enchantments. Every hedge and object examined by independent teams."

"The Cup," Harry said.

Bagman's attention shifted towards him.

"Where is it?"

"Ministry vault."

"Who arranged its transport?"

Silence settled over the desk. Behind the clerks, Percy Weasley's face lost colour.

"Mr Crouch," he said. "The instruction arrived in January. The Cup remains sealed until the morning of the task. It is to be brought directly to the centre by two Department officials."

"Change the Cup," Sirius said.

"The object is named in the contract," Bagman replied.

"Then open it now."

"Breaking the Ministry seal before the scheduled date may be interpreted as tampering."

Sirius leaned over Harry's chair. "Someone entered his name beneath a fourth school, and you remain worried about procedural tampering?"

"I'm worried about triggering magic none of us controls."

Lucius looked at Percy. "Did any instruction concerning the Cup arrive after Crouch disappeared?"

Percy swallowed. "Three. Each reaffirmed the original transport and prohibited substitution."

Harry looked from Percy to the evidence upon the desk. They had found nothing wrong with the Cup itself, but every instruction concerning it led back to a man whose authority his son had been using. Somewhere in the Ministry, those orders had been received, filed and obeyed while Crouch drank Polyjuice behind a locked classroom door. Harry wanted someone to explain how a seal could make that history reassuring.

Amelia Bones removed her monocle. "The Cup will be guarded continuously by Aurors who had no connection to Crouch's department. It will be examined without breaking the seal. The officials transporting it will be selected the morning of the task."

"And every person entering the maze will be searched," Dumbledore said.

"Including staff," Harry added.

Fudge's head snapped towards him. "A fourteen-year-old does not establish Ministry procedure."

"A fourteen-year-old found your fugitive."

Draconia's shoe touched his beneath the table. The slight pressure carried approval, and Harry found it easier to keep looking at Fudge.

The Minister stood. "There remains no evidence that He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named is involved. Crouch Junior and Nott are dangerous escaped criminals. Treating their delusions as proof of resurrection would cause irresponsible panic."

Harry's scar pulsed. "Panic won't make him more alive."

The room went quiet. Fudge placed the bowler hat properly upon his head, tugging the brim as though that restored something the conversation had taken from him.

"The Tournament continues under strengthened security. The Ministry will handle the fugitives."

"As it handled Sirius?" Harry asked.

The Minister's mouth hardened. He left without answering, and for a moment everyone listened to the sound of his shoes upon the stairs.

Amelia remained. "Mr Potter," she said, "do you wish to continue?"

The question carried more honesty than the contract allowed. Harry surveyed the other champions: Cedric beside Professor Sprout, jaw set; Fleur holding Gabrielle's hand; Krum behind Karkaroff with the bruise from Crouch's attack still dark near his temple. Then he looked back at Hermione and Draconia. Neither told him what courage required.

"Do I have a safe way to refuse?"

"No," Dumbledore said.

"Then wanting doesn't matter."

"It matters," Hermione said. "Even when the answer cannot change the action."

Harry held her gaze. Saying it felt harder than agreeing to another dangerous thing, which made him suspect he needed to say it.

"I don't want to continue."

The admission entered the room without shame. Draconia's expression softened, and Cedric leaned forward.

"Neither do I. Not under these conditions."

"Nor I," Fleur said, tightening her fingers around Gabrielle's hand. "Continuing because refusal is punished is not agreement."

Krum ignored Karkaroff's sudden warning glare. "I continue because magic forces it. Record that also."

One of the clerks hesitated above his parchment. Amelia Bones held out her hand, and he surrendered the quill.

"But I will," Harry said. "And I want everyone to stop pretending those are the same decision."

Amelia Bones wrote it into the official record.

Hogwarts acquired checkpoints. Aurors guarded every entrance, while portraits received lists of authorised faces and became insufferably enthusiastic about enforcement. Students had to state their names before entering common rooms even when the portrait had known them for years.

Professor Flitwick placed revealing charms upon every classroom door. Snape brewed enough identity potion to test the entire school, and McGonagall personally inspected the staff at breakfast. On the first morning, Professor Binns failed the test because he no longer possessed blood. He continued teaching while the Ministry debated whether ghosts could be impostors.

No one laughed as much as they should have. Security made danger visible without making it smaller; every guarded doorway reminded Harry how many unguarded ones Crouch had walked through. Crouch Junior and Nott remained free, Crouch Senior's body had not been found, and the Cup remained inside a Ministry vault bound by a contract written before any living judge was born.

Thirty days remained until the maze. Preparation became the only answer anyone could offer which did not sound like waiting.

Hermione divided it into six categories. Draconia added a seventh entitled HUMAN INTERFERENCE, and Cedric read the list twice.

"You think someone may enter the maze to attack us?"

They occupied an unused classroom upon the fourth floor. Desks had been stacked against the walls, leaving pale rectangles in the dust where they had stood for years. Chalk lines divided the open space into corridors wide enough for two people to move beside each other. A dozen borrowed suits of armour stood along the edges, and cushions hovered near the ceiling, ready to drop without warning. Late sunlight entered through tall western windows and turned every disturbed grain of chalk to gold; beyond the locked door, an Auror's boots passed at regular intervals.

Harry stood in the centre wearing padded duelling robes. "Someone entered the school wearing a professor," he said. "Entering a hedge seems easier."

Cedric considered this. "Fair."

Hermione tapped the board. The categories read NAVIGATION, CREATURES, CURSES, ILLUSIONS, PHYSICAL OBSTACLES, EMERGENCY SIGNALS and HUMAN INTERFERENCE. Below them, Draconia had written DO NOT TRUST CONVENIENT PATHS in letters large enough to read from the corridor.

"We train five evenings each week," Hermione said. "Two hours on weekdays, four on Saturday. Sunday is rest."

Harry lifted one hand. "Why does rest require scheduling?"

"Because you interpret unassigned time as an opportunity to add practice."

"Cedric might."

Cedric leaned against the wall. "I value my life."

"Coward."

"Living coward."

Draconia handed each boy a copy of the schedule. "Meals are included."

"This is a dictatorship," Harry said.

"You requested our help," Hermione reminded him.

"I misunderstood the terms."

"There were nine pages."

"Exactly."

Cedric turned to the final page. "There are signatures."

"Hermione has learned from Father," Draconia said grimly.

"Efficiency should not be restricted by ideology."

Harry signed. Cedric watched him, apparently reconsidering which of them possessed the stronger instinct for survival.

"You didn't read it."

"I once signed a Hogwarts permission form allowing Madam Pomfrey to regrow all my bones. Standards change."

Hermione took the parchment before he reconsidered, and training began with navigation.

The Four-Point Spell appeared simple: hold the wand flat, say Point Me and allow the tip to indicate north. Harry mastered it in four attempts, while Cedric managed it in two. Draconia then charmed every suit of armour in the room to produce conflicting magnetic fields. Both wands spun.

"The maze may interfere," she said.

"The maze may not own twelve magnetised suits of armour," Harry replied.

"Then this will be pleasantly excessive."

Hermione released the ceiling cushions. Harry dodged the first; the second struck his shoulder. Cedric shielded against three and walked into a chalk wall Draconia had made invisible. His nose met stone with a sound that made Harry wince in sympathy.

"Navigation," Hermione said, writing upon her clipboard.

"Cruelty," Cedric corrected.

By the end of the first hour, Harry could use the spell beneath interference if he touched the wand to bare ground before casting. Cedric learned to identify invisible barriers by sending dust ahead. Hermione recorded both solutions, and Draconia changed the conditions each time they adapted. Any satisfaction at mastering an exercise lasted roughly as long as it took her to lift her wand.

On Friday, the cushions began behaving like Bludgers. On Saturday, they learned to scream.

"Why?" Harry shouted while deflecting one.

"Distraction."

"They sound like the egg."

"Then you possess relevant experience."

Cedric's Shield Charm caught two cushions and sent them into a cupboard. "Does she like you?" he asked Harry.

"Apparently."

Draconia struck them both with a stinging hex.

The real Moody sent Harry a package from the hospital wing. It contained one battered field manual, a list of nineteen spells and a note written in block capitals.

CROUCH KNEW WHAT I KNEW. THAT DOES NOT MAKE IT WRONG. VERIFY EVERYTHING. KEEP WHAT WORKS. BURN THE REST.

Beneath it, in smaller letters:

RETURN THE BOOK OR I WILL FIND YOU.

Harry showed Sirius through the mirror.

"Definitely him," Sirius said.

"The threat?"

"The lack of a deadline."

Harry verified every spell. Flitwick confirmed defensive applications, while Snape identified side effects with visible pleasure. McGonagall corrected three wand movements and objected to a curse which temporarily reversed a target's joints. Hagrid recommended keeping that one for acromantulas. Hermione crossed it out; Draconia copied it onto a separate page.

The field manual proved more useful than the spells. Moody had written through every margin during years as an Auror, his notes contradicting the printed advice, one another and occasionally themselves. NEVER USE THE CENTRE OF A CORRIDOR appeared beside one passage. Six pages later, he had written WALLS ARE WHERE AMBUSHES WAIT.

Harry found a line beneath both in different ink: IF SOMEONE TELLS YOU THERE IS ONE SAFE PLACE TO STAND, THEY WANT YOU STANDING THERE.

That survived verification.

He visited Moody on Sunday. The Auror had acquired a temporary wooden leg and an ordinary glass eye which stared in the same direction as his remaining one. His hair had been cut evenly, while the missing magical eye remained sealed inside Dumbledore's office as evidence. Moody hated all three improvements.

He sat in a chair beside the hospital window, sharpening a knife against Madam Pomfrey's orders. Harry stopped inside the doorway, his body remembering before reason could intervene: one scarred hand, one wooden leg, a face associated with curses, praise and the feeling of being observed through walls.

Moody saw him hesitate. "Door stays open."

Harry looked at him. "Wasn't going to close it."

"Good."

There was no offence in the answer and no performance of trust. Harry entered and chose the chair nearest the exit. Moody returned the knife to its sheath without being asked.

"Book useful?"

"Contradictory."

"So is surviving."

"Sirius liked it."

"Black owes me a boot."

"He says you stole it first."

"Then his memory remains defective."

They sat with the window open between them and every ward visible upon the door. The breeze stirred the edge of the blanket over Moody's knees, carrying the distant sound of students crossing the grounds.

"Did Crouch hurt you?" Moody asked.

Harry considered the question. "Yes."

"Using my face."

"Yes."

Moody's jaw shifted. "You can hate the face."

"I don't."

"Not yet."

Harry looked towards the grounds. The maze hedges had reached ten feet; from the hospital window, their paths appeared simple. Inside them, every turn would conceal the next.

"He helped me," Harry said.

"He positioned you."

"Both can be true."

Moody's remaining eye moved back to him. "Yes."

"He told me to use the broom against the dragon. Pointed me towards gillyweed. Taught me to resist Imperius."

"And every time his advice saved you, trusting him became easier."

Harry touched the bruise fading beneath his shirt. "That was the point."

"Part of it." Moody leaned back. "He also admired you."

The possibility felt worse. Harry could bear being useful to an enemy more easily than he could bear being understood by one.

"You don't know that."

"I know obsession. Crouch talked through the trunk. Practised my voice. Described your lessons afterwards to see whether I reacted."

Harry's skin crawled. "What did he say?"

"That you fought the Imperius Curse faster than grown Death Eaters. That you understood danger once people stopped protecting you from it. That the Dark Lord would be pleased."

The hospital sounds receded. "Pleased with what?"

"You alive. Competent. Willing to reach the end."

The maze waited below. Harry stared at its centre, where the empty paths seemed to converge upon something he could not yet see.

"Why train someone you plan to kill?"

"Maybe death isn't the first thing planned."

Harry thought of Voldemort living without a body, of hands inside his mind and visions which arrived like memories. A voice saying eventually before Frank Bryce died.

"June," he said.

Moody nodded.

"He said it when he escaped."

"Then treat every easy path as his."

Harry stood. At the door, he looked back. "Thank you for the book."

Moody grunted.

"Professor," Harry added.

The real man's expression changed. Not much, but enough.

Cedric joined the trio's training without attempting to understand the trio. This became a survival strategy.

When Hermione corrected Harry's stance by placing one hand against his back, Cedric examined the ceiling. When Draconia fastened Harry's wrist guard herself and Hermione became unnecessarily interested in a spellbook, he practised silently. When both girls argued over which of them understood Harry's limits, he left to obtain water and returned after the shouting stopped.

"You are a coward," Harry told him.

"Living coward," Cedric replied.

Cho attended twice. She proved excellent at producing moving targets and terrible at pretending not to distract Cedric. Luna came once, released every practice beetle Hermione had collected and apologised after learning none was Rita.

The sessions became the most ordinary part of Harry's week, and he liked them for that. There were rules he could learn, mistakes he could correct and people who complained when he failed to take a break. For a few hours, being prepared meant something he could actually do.

They trained Stunning Spells until Harry could hit three targets moving in different directions. Cedric taught him a Hufflepuff binding charm which turned grass into ropes. Draconia introduced non-verbal feints, and Hermione designed logic puzzles whose incorrect answers released paint, feathers or, once, a flock of furious paper birds.

Harry spent an afternoon bright orange.

"Visibility can prevent accidents," Hermione said.

"I am visible from France."

Fleur, passing the open door, confirmed this.

Hagrid brought them creatures. The least objectionable was a hinkypunk which led Cedric into a cupboard. The most objectionable was a young Blast-Ended Skrewt Hagrid claimed had become calmer with age. It set Harry's sleeve on fire.

"Recognition," Hagrid said.

"From what?" Draconia demanded while extinguishing Harry. "It was not alive when we were thirteen."

"Might've heard stories."

The Skrewt exploded through a desk. Hermione banned live creatures after that, and the blackened fragments remained beside the door as a reminder of why.

Harry's reflexes sharpened, Cedric's shields strengthened, and they learned one another's movements well enough to fight back-to-back without collision.

"Inside the maze, we separate," Cedric reminded him.

"Inside the maze, we compete," Harry said. "That doesn't mean we ignore screaming."

"Agreed."

They sealed it with a handshake. Draconia added a written version to Hermione's schedule. Neither boy signed it; she kept it anyway.

As the sessions continued, the difficulty stopped being learning more magic. It was deciding which of the magic Harry already knew still belonged to him.

Crouch had taught him to throw off the Imperius Curse. He had shown him how to watch an opponent's shoulders instead of their wand, how to keep moving after a missed spell and how to recognise the silence that fell half a second before an ambush. He had praised Harry's instincts, forced him to sharpen them and, on the night his deception was uncovered, used every lesson against him.

Afterwards, Harry discovered doubt in the smallest movements. His wand hesitated before a feint Crouch had demonstrated; he abandoned a perfectly good defensive stance because the false Moody had once called it efficient. During a practice duel with Cedric, he recognised the opening for a low Stunning Spell and failed to take it.

Cedric's curse struck him squarely in the chest. Harry landed upon his back, breathless, staring at the high stone ceiling while the classroom fell still around him.

Cedric lowered his wand. "You saw that coming."

"I was enjoying the view."

"The ceiling?"

"Very historic."

Hermione cancelled the cushioning charm beneath him and allowed the floor to make its opinion known. Harry grunted.

"Again," she said.

He pushed himself upright. "You could pretend to be concerned."

"I am concerned. That is why we're doing it again."

Draconia sat upon the edge of a desk with Moody's field manual open across her knees. Nearly every page now carried annotations in three colours: green for information verified by a professor, blue for techniques they had tested themselves, and red for claims which were misleading, incomplete or designed to make the reader behave predictably.

She looked from the book to Harry. "Why did you stop?"

"I didn't."

"Your wand did."

"Perhaps it has developed caution."

"Your wand has more sense than you do, but not enough to act independently."

Cedric moved to retrieve the practice target he had knocked aside. He did it slowly, granting Harry the illusion that the conversation was private. Harry rubbed the place where the spell had caught him.

"He taught me that move."

No one needed to ask who he was. Hermione's severity softened.

"The move still works."

"That's the problem."

Draconia closed the manual. "No," she said. "That is the solution."

Harry gave her a tired look.

"Crouch wanted access to your trust," she continued. "He obtained it by giving you useful things. If you discard all of them merely because they passed through his hands, he keeps deciding what you may use."

"That's almost exactly what Moody said."

"Then one of us has been reading better books."

Hermione stepped closer. "You resisted the Imperius Curse because you resisted it. He may have created the circumstances, but he didn't put that part inside you."

Harry looked at the wand in his hand: eleven inches, holly and phoenix feather, the first thing in the magical world which had chosen him without being told to. Crouch had borrowed Moody's face, his voice, his knowledge and his history. Perhaps that was why Harry could not stop feeling as though everything learned in that classroom had been borrowed too.

But the wand remained his. So did the hand holding it.

"Again," Harry said.

Cedric returned to his mark. This time, when his shoulder turned, Harry dropped beneath the first curse, let the second skim his hair and sent the low Stunner across the floor. Cedric blocked it. Harry followed with the feint, then changed its ending.

The shield Cedric raised caught empty air. Harry's real spell struck the flagstone beside his foot and twisted the grass ropes hidden there around both ankles. Cedric toppled backwards with considerably less dignity than Harry had managed.

Draconia made a neat blue mark beside the relevant paragraph, and Hermione helped Cedric sit up.

"Better," she said.

"To whom?" Cedric asked.

Harry offered him a hand. "Definitely me."

That evening, he copied the feint into his own notes. He did not use Crouch's wording.

Soon, the precautions acquired personalities of their own. Peeves stole the new passwords and shouted them from the rafters. The Fat Lady refused to admit Professor McGonagall until she answered three personal questions, one of which caused her lips to compress into a line capable of cutting glass. Filch began demanding signed travel slips between lessons and lost the entire first day's collection when Mrs Norris slept upon them beside a leaking window.

The precautions were inconvenient, excessive and impossible to resent. Harry resented them anyway.

Every violet flare reminded him that a face could lie. Every challenge at a doorway recalled the enchanted eye rolling in its socket while Crouch watched him learn. When professors used phrases the false Moody had used, Harry's attention snagged upon them. If someone approached from behind, his hand closed around his wand before he recognised their footsteps, and releasing it required an effort he hoped they would not notice.

He began waking at precisely three seventeen in the morning. There was no reason for the time: no historic murder Hermione could locate, no significance Draconia recognised, and no pattern to the nightmares except that each ended with a familiar face becoming unfamiliar.

Sometimes Moody's scars melted into the younger Crouch's pale skin. Sometimes Sirius opened the door of Hawthorn Cottage with Nott's eyes. Once, Hermione smiled at him across the library and spoke in Voldemort's voice.

Harry woke with his wand aimed at Neville's curtains.

For several nights, he lay awake afterwards and watched darkness collect around the bedposts. That was the old method: remain silent, become smaller than the fear and wait for morning to make it embarrassing. By breakfast, he could usually tell himself that speaking about it would have been unnecessary. At three seventeen, the argument felt less convincing.

On the fourth night, he reached beneath his pillow for the mirror Sirius had given him. Its surface showed only his own exhausted face. Harry whispered Sirius's name, but nothing happened.

He almost put it away. Three in the morning was not a civilised hour. Sirius needed sleep, and explaining that a dream had frightened him sounded considerably worse when the dream was already fading. His fingers had begun to close around the mirror's edge when the glass brightened.

Sirius appeared with his hair flattened on one side and a wand held ready beneath his chin.

"Harry?"

"Sorry."

"Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Is anyone else hurt?"

"Not unless Neville wakes up and sees where I'm pointing my wand."

Sirius lowered his own, though he did not put it away. The room behind him came slowly into focus: crowded shelves, a banked fire and the corner of the green sofa in Hawthorn Cottage's sitting room. A blanket lay tangled across his lap. On the table beside him stood two mugs, one empty and one abandoned halfway through.

"What happened?" Sirius asked.

Harry looked at the mirror's silver edge. "Nothing."

"Excellent. We can rule out nothing. What happened after it?"

Despite himself, Harry smiled. "I had a dream."

Sirius waited. He did not ask what sort or point out that Crouch had fled. He simply waited as though the answer could take whatever time it required, and Harry gradually stopped searching for a way to make it sound smaller.

"You were in it," he said at last. "Except you weren't."

"A tragic improvement?"

"Nott's eyes."

The humour left Sirius's face without hardening it. "Did dream-me at least have my hair?"

"Unfortunately."

"Then your mind retains some standards."

Harry settled against the headboard. The joke did not erase the dream, but it returned the dormitory to its proper size. He could hear Neville breathing beyond the curtains and the wind pressing faintly against the window.

"I keep thinking about all the things he taught me," he admitted. "Every time I use one, it feels like he planned that too."

Sirius considered this. "When I was sixteen," he said, "your father taught me a charm for opening a locked window from the outside."

"Why?"

"There was a curfew and an unreasonable quantity of pudding involved."

"That explains nothing."

"It explains the important part. Years later, I used the same charm to get away from an Auror who wanted to return me to Azkaban. James didn't plan that. He wasn't responsible for where I carried what he gave me."

Harry traced his thumb along a crack in the mirror's backing. "Crouch isn't my dad."

"Thank every available power." Sirius leaned closer to the glass. "But the rule remains. A person can put a tool in your hand. They cannot own every choice you make with it afterwards."

"Draco said something similar."

"She's disturbingly sensible."

"Don't tell her that."

"I would rather face another trial."

The quiet returned, gentler this time. Harry could hear the faint pop of the fire through the mirror; somewhere beyond Sirius's room, old pipes knocked within the walls. He listened until another question escaped before he had quite decided to ask it.

"Are you disappointed?"

Sirius frowned. "In Draconia?"

"In me."

Harry wished immediately that he had not said it, though he could not have explained which answer frightened him more. Sirius sat fully upright, the blanket slipping from one shoulder.

"You uncovered a Death Eater who fooled Dumbledore."

"After he lived here for nine months."

"You survived nine months beside a man employed to steer you into a trap, recognised the moment the evidence changed and fought him when he tried to escape."

"He did escape."

"That is not the same as you failing."

"It feels close."

"Then the feeling is wrong." Sirius's voice remained quiet. "Harry, listen to me. You do not have to earn my pride by catching every monster before it reaches you."

Harry did not speak. His thumb had stopped moving over the crack in the mirror, and he held it still between both hands.

"You don't have to win the Tournament," Sirius continued. "You don't have to be calm about any of this. You do not have to become impossible to deceive, injure or frighten. That isn't strength. That's a gravestone with good publicity."

A laugh caught painfully in Harry's throat. Sirius smiled, but his eyes did not.

"Call me when you're frightened," he said. "Call me when nothing has happened. Especially then."

"At three in the morning?"

"I spent twelve years surrounded by Dementors. My sleeping habits are already beyond repair."

The fire behind him flared. A shape moved at the edge of the mirror, and Remus appeared with a fresh cup of tea. He wore a dressing gown and the expression of a man who had been awake throughout the entire conversation but had chosen not to intrude.

"Tell him the part about eating," Remus said.

Sirius accepted the tea. "Have you eaten?"

"Yesterday."

"Harry."

"There was dinner."

"How much of it?"

"I didn't measure."

"Hermione will know," Remus observed.

Harry narrowed his eyes. "You two are alarmingly organised."

"We have a shared objective," Sirius said. "Keeping you alive long enough to become truly unbearable."

"I'm making excellent progress."

"That's my godson."

When the mirror finally darkened, it was nearly four. Harry still did not sleep at once, but he placed it upon the pillow beside him instead of hiding it underneath. When the dream returned, Sirius's face remained his own.

Hermione knew about the call by breakfast. Harry never discovered how, though he suspected Remus, who believed privacy was important until it interfered with nutrition. Draconia arrived halfway through the meal carrying a letter from Narcissa and the expression of someone who had already received a full report.

Neither girl demanded an explanation. Hermione pushed a plate of toast across the table, and Draconia sat beside him rather than opposite, pouring tea into his cup. Harry glanced between them.

"This is subtle."

"I don't know what you mean," Hermione said.

"You have moved breakfast into defensive formation."

"Eat."

"There it is."

Draconia added milk to his tea, remembering how much without asking. "Did the mirror help?" she said.

Harry considered lying, mostly from habit. "Yes."

Hermione's shoulders lowered by an amount most people would not have noticed. Draconia did. So did Harry.

"Good," Draconia said, placing one hand briefly over his where it rested beside the cup. She withdrew it before the gesture could become a subject.

Hermione buttered a second piece of toast with unnecessary concentration. Harry watched the pink rise at the edge of her ears and decided, with rare wisdom, not to mention it. Instead, he broke the toast in half and placed one piece upon each girl's plate.

"Shared objective," he said.

Hermione looked puzzled, while Draconia's mouth curved. "Keeping you alive?"

"Apparently until I become unbearable."

"Too late," Hermione said.

Harry smiled into his tea. For the first time since the corridor, the violet light around the Great Hall doors did not feel like a warning. It felt like proof that people had noticed the danger and stayed.

They found Bartemius Crouch Senior six days later—or what remained of him.

The Aurors began at the place where Harry and Krum had last seen him, widening the search through the trees one measured circle at a time. They found fragments of spellwork, Krum's blood upon a root and a scorched patch where a Portkey had activated. Beyond that, the Forest gave them nothing.

It was Fang who led Hagrid to a stand of ash trees half a mile from the path. Beneath newly disturbed earth lay a single white bone no longer than Harry's hand.

Professor McGonagall told them after supper. She did not soften the facts: an expert from St Mungo's had confirmed human transfiguration, and Dumbledore reversed it in the presence of Amelia Bones and two Aurors. Crouch had been dead before Harry returned with help. His son had killed him while still concealed on the grounds behind Moody's face, before the confrontation that exposed him.

Harry sat very still in McGonagall's office. Rain pressed against the windows, blurring the Quidditch pitch beyond them. Hermione occupied the chair on his right. Draconia stood at the mantel because she had refused to sit after hearing the first sentence.

"Krum was watching him," Harry said.

"Mr Krum was attacked from behind," McGonagall replied. "He could not have prevented it."

"I left them."

"You went for assistance."

"I left them."

The words were arithmetic, each repetition returning the same answer. Harry had chosen the sensible action, and Crouch had died during the minutes it required. He could still picture the path beneath his feet, the moment he had turned away, the distance he had believed he could cross quickly enough.

Draconia turned from the fire. "Had you remained, Crouch Junior would have attacked all three of you."

"Maybe we could have stopped him."

"Or he would have killed you first."

"You don't know that."

"Neither do you."

Her voice was sharper than the room required. Harry looked at her and saw the memory of the first task: a dragon's claw falling, Harry disappearing beneath it and Draconia unable to reach him from the stands. Her fingers had curled against the mantel, pale at the knuckles.

Hermione reached for his clenched hand. "He came to you because he thought you would help," she said. "And you did."

"He died."

"Those things can both be true."

Harry hated how often the year required two terrible truths to occupy the same space. There ought to have been a right thing that led to a right ending. He was tired of being told he had done it and then hearing who had suffered anyway.

McGonagall removed her spectacles and folded them upon the desk. "Mr Crouch committed grave wrongs," she said. "He imprisoned his son unlawfully. He controlled him, concealed him and allowed fear of scandal to govern choices which endangered others. He also fought through an Imperius Curse and crossed miles of hostile ground to deliver a warning. Neither truth cancels the other."

"Winky?" Hermione asked.

McGonagall's gaze lowered. "Your mother is with her," she told Draconia.

Draconia looked towards the rain-dark window.

"Is there going to be a funeral?" Harry asked.

"A private one. The Ministry has not yet decided what may be disclosed."

"He was a Ministry official for half his life."

"That does not make the Ministry courageous."

It was the closest Harry had ever heard McGonagall come to insulting the government. He almost appreciated it.

At the funeral two days later, fewer than twenty people stood beneath a grey sky. Amelia Bones came in plain black robes. Percy Weasley represented the Department of International Magical Co-operation and spent the entire service with his jaw held rigid. Dumbledore spoke briefly about choices, consequence and the final courage required to resist one's own mistakes. No newspaper attended.

Winky stood between Narcissa and Draconia. When the coffin disappeared into the earth, she made no sound; her whole body folded around the grief as if silence might keep it from becoming real. Draconia knelt in the wet grass and held her, with Narcissa's hand resting upon them both.

Harry remained several paces away with Hermione and Sirius. He did not know whether Winky wanted him near. She had spent years serving a family who mistook ownership for loyalty, and he would not turn comfort into another command. He waited until the mourners began to leave, when she approached him herself.

Her eyes were swollen. She wore a black tea towel bordered with the smallest line of Malfoy green.

"Harry Potter came when Master called," she said.

"I should have stayed."

Winky shook her head so violently that her ears struck her cheeks. "Bad son would kill Harry Potter too. Master knowing. Master sending Harry Potter away for help."

"He didn't send me."

"Master knowing you goes."

Harry's gaze moved to the fresh earth behind her. Perhaps Crouch had known. Perhaps he had reached Harry because the boy who ran towards danger could also be trusted to run towards help. It did not make the ending fair, but it made it bearable by a fraction.

Winky drew something from beneath the edge of her tea towel: a brass key, old and darkened around its teeth. She held it carefully, as though giving it away might disturb the last thing she still knew how to do for him.

"Master keeps this since young master is boy," she said. "Key to old house study. Winky finds in master's clothes. Master says papers there must never be found by bad son."

Sirius and Amelia exchanged a glance.

"May I?" Amelia asked.

Winky held the key more tightly. "Winky gives Harry Potter."

Harry crouched so they were nearer eye level. "Can we give it to Madam Bones together?"

Winky looked from him to the Head of Magical Law Enforcement. Then she placed the key across both their palms.

The search of Crouch's study uncovered twelve years of concealed records. Most concerned his son: food, medicines, strengthening draughts, the repeated renewal of the Imperius Curse and payments routed through false departmental accounts. One ledger recorded visits by Theodore Nott's father beginning eighteen months earlier. The earliest entry contained no explanation, only a line in Crouch's compressed handwriting:

He knows.

The final relevant page had been torn away. Its absence told them almost as much as any confession. Nott had discovered the secret and cultivated it, waiting until it could be made useful to someone else.

June settled over the castle beneath an illusion of summer. Sunlight warmed the castle walls, wildflowers spread beside the lake, and students revised under trees with their shoes abandoned in the grass. From the open windows came the sounds of exams, exploding ink bottles and Professor Sprout shouting after a mobile hedge which had escaped its pot. Beyond the glass, the school looked capable of an ordinary end to the year.

The maze rose upon the Quidditch pitch. Each morning its hedges stood taller; at first Harry could see over them from the stands, but a week later their upper branches reached above the goal hoops, dense and dark despite the brilliant weather. Ministry warders walked the perimeter in pairs. Professor Flitwick tested the boundaries daily, while a Gringotts curse-breaker inspected every root for foreign magic.

The Triwizard Cup remained in a sealed Ministry vault. Everyone knew where it was, and that should have made Harry feel safer. Instead, certainty gathered around the object like a target.

The next vision struck nine days before the final task, in the middle of Potions.

One moment Harry was slicing asphodel root beneath Snape's supervision. The next, the knife fell from his fingers and struck the table. Pain opened behind his scar, and the dungeon vanished.

Cold stone pressed beneath hands that were not his. He saw by firelight from close to the ground, with the sensation that his body occupied too little space. A room breathed around him—small, ruined and damp, with roots pushing between the floor slabs. Rain tapped upon a broken window. Beyond it stood a weathered stone angel, one wing split down the middle. Farther back, rows of pale markers disappeared beneath the spreading branches of a yew.

Nott stood beside a table. His robes were clean, but mud darkened the hems; one hand rested upon the back of a chair as though he required its support and resented the fact. Across from him, Barty Crouch Junior paced with Moody's old flask clenched in his fist. The younger face looked wrong after so many months beneath the older one, too smooth and too hungry. His pale eyes moved constantly, searching even here for threats only he could see.

"You exposed me too soon," he said.

The answering voice came from below Harry's line of sight, high, cold and almost bodiless.

"You exposed yourself."

Crouch stopped pacing. "The boy had an artefact we did not predict."

"And still he proceeds."

"Dumbledore has changed the officials. Bones is examining every order my father approved. They search the staff twice a day."

Nott's fingers tightened upon the chair. "They have searched my house three times. I am sure the fourth will improve their spirits."

Neither laughed. Something shifted close to the floor. Harry felt the movement rather than saw it: a frail shape within cloth, small enough to be held and powerful enough to make both grown men afraid. His stomach turned, though he could no longer tell whether the feeling belonged to his own body or the thing through which he watched.

"Flitwick intends to ward the maze himself," Crouch said. "The boy will carry half the school's protections."

"Will he enter?"

"The contract holds."

"Then concern yourself with what I asked of you."

The fire bent although there was no wind. Harry tried to turn towards the table, to see what lay beneath Nott's hand, but the body through which he watched would not obey him. The broken window slipped from view. Damp wool brushed his cheek; somewhere close, something scraped against stone.

"He is more prepared now," Nott said.

"Good."

Pleasure entered the thin voice, and Harry's scar split wider.

"Let him believe preparation will save him."

The room turned. Red eyes opened in the dark and looked directly at Harry, holding him as surely as a hand closing around his throat.

"I have waited thirteen years."

The voice no longer addressed the men.

"I can wait nine days more."

Harry came back screaming. Glass shattered somewhere nearby, and his cheek struck cold stone. The smell of burnt herbs flooded his lungs, thick enough to choke him, while hands reached towards him from every direction.

He rolled before he recognised them. His wand was in his grip, and a Shield Charm burst outward, overturning stools and throwing three cauldrons from the nearest bench. Students shouted as Snape's counter-curse struck the shield and dissolved it.

"Potter."

Harry pressed one hand to his scar. Blood ran warm beneath his palm as the dungeon swam into focus: the spilled potion, the broken glass, the faces that had been replaced a moment earlier by darkness. Hermione stood on the far side of the room, white-faced, her wand already drawn. Draconia had left the Slytherin benches and was approaching slowly, both hands visible.

Neither touched him. They had learned.

"It was him," Harry managed.

Snape went utterly still. "Who?"

"Voldemort."

Several students flinched at the name. Draconia stopped within reach and waited, her gaze remaining upon Harry's face. He lowered his wand. Only then did she take his wrist, and Hermione reached them a second later.

"What did you see?" she asked.

Harry looked at the two of them, at Snape and at the overturned room beyond. The old instinct rose first: say nothing until he understood it, protect them from knowledge by carrying it alone, wait until certainty made the fear respectable. He could feel the familiar answer forming, ready to explain away what everyone had just witnessed.

He let the instinct pass.

"Nott," he said. "Crouch. Voldemort. They know about the new precautions. They are still planning something during the final task."

Snape swept towards the door. "Granger, fetch the Headmaster. Malfoy, the hospital wing."

"I'm coming to Dumbledore," Harry said.

"You are bleeding onto my floor."

"It's stone. It'll recover."

Snape's gaze dropped to Draconia's hand around Harry's wrist, then moved to Hermione, who had not taken a single step towards the door.

"All three of you," he said with lethal resignation. "Now."

Dumbledore listened without interruption. Sirius arrived through the Floo before Harry finished, with Remus close behind, fastening his robes as he entered. Amelia Bones appeared ten minutes later with two curse-breakers and the expression of a woman who had begun to regard sleep as a political conspiracy.

Harry repeated every word he could remember. He described the broken window, the fire, the glimpse of pale stone and the yew tree. Hermione recorded the details, while Draconia questioned him about angles, distances and the colour of the mud upon Nott's robes. Sirius stood with one hand against the back of Harry's chair, never quite touching him and never moving away.

When Harry reached the final words, the office became silent. The scratch of Hermione's quill stopped, and even the silver instruments seemed unusually quiet.

"Nine days," Amelia said.

"The final task," Hermione replied.

Dumbledore gazed into the dark surface of his Pensieve. "He expects the final task to give him an opportunity."

"The Cup," Sirius said. "Those transport orders came from Crouch."

"That makes it a priority for examination," Amelia replied. "It does not tell us what has been done to it, or whether anything has."

"Then destroy it," Sirius said.

"The Department tried," Amelia told him. "Three controlled severing spells. The contract redirected the damage into the champions' bindings. Diggory collapsed during the second."

Harry turned sharply. "Cedric was hurt?"

"Briefly. He recovered before he understood the cause."

Sirius's hand closed around the chair. "Use another Cup."

"The object is named by provenance and enchantment," Hermione said quietly. "Substitution would count as a failure to provide the contracted goal."

"Then don't place it in the maze."

"The same result," Amelia replied. "The task must be capable of completion."

"A beautifully constructed prison," Lucius said from beside the window.

He had arrived with Narcissa after Draconia sent the family coin. Summer light caught the silver at his cuffs, but his face held none of its usual composure. Narcissa stood close beside him, listening with her hands clasped so tightly that the rings pressed into her skin.

"One designed by someone with access to Ministry procedure," he continued. "Crouch Senior authenticated the instructions before he escaped his son's control. Every safety objection leads back to an obligation already approved."

"Has anyone found transport magic on it?" Harry asked.

"No," Flitwick said from beside the Pensieve cabinet. "The examinations through the seal show its original Tournament enchantments. No Portkey spell, no foreign destination and no trace of Nott's ritual signature. We shall repeat them when the seal is lawfully opened."

"Could someone add it afterwards?"

"That is why I intend to supervise its placement myself. The Cup will be watched from the moment it leaves the vault."

Harry looked at the Headmaster. "You think that makes it safe?"

"I think it addresses the danger we have identified," Dumbledore said. "Your vision tells us that Voldemort expects to attack despite our precautions. It does not tell us which precaution he means to defeat."

"So we examine the contract as well," Hermione said. "Every instruction Crouch approved, and everything the final task is meant to do."

Amelia drew a fresh sheet towards herself. "The originals, not the departmental summaries."

Harry leaned back. They could guard the Cup, examine the maze and search every person who entered it. He appreciated the difference between those precautions and doing nothing; what frightened him was having to enter before anyone knew which danger they were looking for. Beyond the office windows, sunlight lay across the grounds where the hedges were still growing.

"Then we know when he means to try," Harry said.

Sirius looked down at him. "That is not reassuring."

"It wasn't meant to be."

"Harry—"

"We know the day. We know he is somewhere with graves and a yew tree. And he knows I have to enter the maze." He heard how calm his own voice sounded and disliked it. "That's more than we had this morning."

"It still leaves us guessing," Draconia said. She moved closer to his chair. "If you reach the Cup and anything is wrong, you wait."

"I will."

"You do not decide the quickest way out is worth the risk."

"If touching it is required to complete the task—"

"You do not."

"Draco."

"No." The word cracked across the office. "We have spent an entire year allowing a magical contract to decide what may be done to you. It does not get this."

Her control had thinned. Harry saw the fear beneath it, raw and furious, the same fear which had driven her from the stands during the first task and left half-moons in his wrist after the second. She was looking at him as though he had already begun to disappear, and he could not make himself offer the reassurance he did not believe.

Hermione closed her notebook. "There has to be another completion condition," she said. "The task ends when a champion reaches the Cup, not necessarily when they take it. We can examine the wording."

Amelia nodded. "I will obtain the original."

"And if the wording requires contact?" Harry asked.

"Then we establish exactly what it requires before you go in," Sirius said. "And we give you a way to stop when something is wrong. I am not sending you in with orders to ignore a warning."

Harry almost argued. Then he looked at the hand Sirius had finally placed upon his shoulder, at Hermione already turning to a fresh page, and at Draconia, whose fear had made no effort to disguise itself again. For once, he allowed their certainty to stand beside his doubt.

"All right," he said.

The answer required trust, and giving it felt harder than surrender ever had.

The meeting continued until sunset. Amelia ordered the Cup moved into a warded chamber beneath the Department of Mysteries. Flitwick and Bill Weasley would inspect it together. Dumbledore would place an emergency Portkey upon Harry which answered only to his blood and Sirius's magic. Snape began developing an antidote kit for potions likely to be used in forced transport. Every entrance to the maze would be watched by an Auror who had undergone identity verification within the previous hour.

The protections grew until the parchment could barely contain them. Harry listened, appreciating each measure and wishing that appreciation could become complete belief. He could picture the men in the ruined room too clearly, speaking about those same precautions without fear.

When they finally left the office, evening had painted the castle windows gold. Hermione walked on his right and Draconia on his left, their shoulders touching his occasionally as the spiral staircase carried them down.

"You told us immediately," Hermione said.

Harry glanced at her. "Snape's floor may disagree."

"You know what I mean."

He did. Draconia linked her smallest finger around his, a gesture so slight he might have pretended not to notice. He curled his finger in return.

Hermione saw. Her expression shifted, an old uncertainty searching for a place to settle. Harry reached for her hand too, and for three steps they descended in an awkward line, joined in a manner which made walking considerably more difficult.

"This is impractical," Draconia observed.

"You started it," Hermione said.

"I had one hand."

"Harry has two."

"A design flaw," Harry said. "I'll raise it with someone."

Hermione laughed, and the sound followed them into the corridor. Harry held on until the stairs released them onto level stone, where walking together became a little easier.
