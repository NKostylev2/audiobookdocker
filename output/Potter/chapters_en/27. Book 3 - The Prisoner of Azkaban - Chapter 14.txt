14

The Whomping Willow's branches tore at the night, striking the ground hard enough to throw clods of frozen earth into the air. The hidden entrance lay somewhere beneath its roots, lost behind a confusion of shadows and lashing wood. On the Map, Peter Pettigrew and Sirius Black moved steadily through the tunnel below it.

Overhead, the first Dementors crossed the boundary. More followed until their cloaks obscured pieces of the moon and the sky itself appeared to be descending. Cold travelled ahead of them. Grass stiffened beneath Harry's shoes, and his breath caught pale before his face. A woman screamed within his memory.

Not Harry—

He shut his eyes. Not here. Not yet. Hermione gripped his elbow. Her fingers were nearly as cold as the air.

"Harry."

"I'm fine."

"You aren't."

"No, but I'm standing."

Draconia stepped closer, the silver cage pressed beneath one arm and her wand held in the other. Mud darkened her robes to the knees. A shallow cut marked her cheek where a piece of the shattered garden wall had caught her. Behind them, Professor McGonagall fought to keep the werewolf from reaching the castle. She had transformed the fallen stones into thick iron chains. They wound around Lupin's chest and legs, dragging furrows through the ground as he strained against them. His jaws snapped. Foam shone around his teeth. Every wrench of his body twisted the metal and drove McGonagall's shoes deeper into the mud.

Professor Snape was on one knee near Hagrid's hut, one hand clamped over his shoulder. Blood had soaked through his fingers and spread across the side of his robes.

"Potter!" McGonagall shouted. "Take the girls inside!"

The werewolf threw itself sideways. One chain broke. McGonagall turned, and the ground rose at her command. A wall of earth struck Lupin across the chest and carried him several yards from them. He landed heavily, rolled and regained his feet with frightening speed.

"Now!"

Harry looked towards the castle. Between them and its nearest doors lay a stretch of open ground rapidly filling with Dementors. The first entered the boundary of the Quidditch pitch. Frost spread across the grass beneath it in branching white lines.

Draconia followed Harry's gaze. "That route is becoming less sensible by the second."

"We go to Hagrid's," Hermione said. "We can use the Floo."

"His fireplace isn't connected," Harry replied.

"How do you know?"

"He complains about it every winter."

"Greenhouses," Draconia suggested.

"Glass walls," Hermione said. "Excellent protection."

"Better than no walls."

McGonagall cast another chain. Lupin twisted beneath it, wrenching his foreleg free before the metal settled. Harry opened the map. Sirius and Peter were farther along the tunnel now. Their names moved beneath the grounds towards Hogsmeade. Away from the professors. Away from every chance to prove what had happened.

Peter had confessed. Not formally and not cleanly, but Harry had heard him. McGonagall, Snape, Lupin, Hermione and Draconia had all heard enough. It should have been over. Instead Pettigrew was escaping for the second time, and Sirius was following with twelve years of anger and a stolen wand. If Sirius caught him alone, only one of them would return. Perhaps neither.

"Harry," Hermione said, recognising the expression on his face. "Don't."

"I haven't said anything."

"You don't need to."

Draconia glanced down at the map.

"Peter is heading for Hogsmeade."

"Sirius is behind him," Harry said.

"We know."

"If Peter gets out of the tunnel, he can disappear anywhere."

"We know."

"If Sirius catches him first—"

"We know, Harry."

Hermione's voice sharpened on his name. She looked frightened, breathless and furious with him for making the conclusion obvious. He folded the map.

"I'm going after them."

"No," she said.

Draconia spoke at the same time. "That is a terrible plan."

"I didn't call it a plan."

"That does not improve it."

The werewolf howled. The sound rolled across the grounds, deeper and more anguished than any ordinary animal's cry. Harry's skin tightened. Somewhere inside that shape was Professor Lupin, though nothing in the yellow eyes or bared teeth recognised them. McGonagall forced him back again.

"Go!" she shouted.

Harry looked from the castle to the Willow.

"I'm not going alone."

Hermione stared at him. The previous year, she had been Petrified and Draconia had been taken into the Chamber. Harry still remembered how helpless he had felt, and how quickly the fear of losing them had made every other consideration disappear. He had nearly died. Draconia had nearly remained there. He knew what fear did to his judgement. He could feel it attempting the same work now, reducing the world to Peter, Sirius and the tunnel between them.

This time, Hermione stood on one side of him and Draconia on the other. He made himself ask.

"Will you come with me?"

Hermione closed her eyes.

"That is not fair."

"I know."

"You cannot offer me the choice between following you into danger and letting you run into it without us."

"I know."

"You were supposed to be learning."

"I am. I asked."

Draconia looked at him with an expression balanced between exasperation and reluctant approval.

"He has technically improved."

"Do not encourage him."

"I said technically."

Hermione glanced towards McGonagall. The professor's wand carved a burning line through the air. Flames erupted between the werewolf and Hagrid's hut, forcing Lupin towards the edge of the forest. Snape struggled upright behind her.

"There are Dementors between us and the school," Harry said. "The tunnel is protected."

"By a homicidal tree," Hermione replied.

"I said protected, not welcoming."

The cold deepened. A thin, broken voice drifted across the grounds.

Harry—please—

The voice belonged to no single memory. The Dementors had reached inside him, found the shape of helplessness and taught it how to speak. Knowledge did not stop his body from responding. His knees weakened. Draconia caught his wrist. The grip was firm, familiar and warm despite the cold.

"We decide now," she said.

Hermione drew one hard breath.

"Beforehand."

Harry frowned.

"You promised to inform a professor before doing anything dangerous," she reminded him.

"I don't think shouting across the grounds counts."

"It is going to have to."

Harry turned.

"Professor McGonagall!"

She looked towards him while maintaining the chains with both hands.

"We're going after Pettigrew!"

Her face transformed with horror.

"You most certainly are not!"

"We've informed her," Harry said.

Hermione stared at him. "That is an appalling interpretation of the agreement."

"Discuss it while moving."

They ran. McGonagall shouted their names. A spell struck the ground ahead of them, but she could not maintain it while controlling Lupin. Harry jumped the smoking furrow. Hermione landed beside him. Draconia cleared it without breaking stride. The Willow turned towards them. Branches swept outward.

"Down!" Hermione cried.

They threw themselves into the mud. Wood roared over Harry's head, close enough to stir his hair. A thinner branch snapped towards them from the side. Draconia raised a shield. The impact drove her backwards on one knee. Cracks of silver light spread across the charm. Harry seized her arm and pulled her up.

"Still a terrible plan?" he asked.

"Getting worse."

They reached the outer spread of roots and could go no farther. Every branch seemed to possess a separate intention. One struck the ground to Harry's left. Another gouged a channel through the mud behind Hermione. The trunk groaned, twisting to bring more of its reach towards them.

"The knot!" Harry shouted. "There's a knot that stops it!"

"Where?"

"On the trunk!"

Hermione stared through the moving branches. "That is a very large trunk!"

A thick limb descended. Harry pushed both girls aside and rolled. The branch struck where they had stood. Earth leapt into the air. Pain travelled through Harry's ribs even without contact. Draconia cast a Severing Charm. The spell left a black mark across the bark and made the tree angrier.

"Excellent," Hermione shouted. "Now it knows which one of us to hate!"

"It attacked first!"

"Trees do not understand revenge!"

"This one understands violence!"

The silver cage slipped from Draconia's grip. Harry caught it before a root crushed it. A shape flew past his shoulder. Crookshanks landed lightly upon a root, his ginger fur lifted by the wind. He slipped beneath one descending branch, jumped over another and darted towards the trunk.

"Crookshanks!" Hermione screamed.

The cat did not look back. A branch swept towards him. Harry fired a Blasting Curse. It struck the wood and altered its path by inches. The branch smashed into the ground beside Crookshanks, showering him with dirt. He continued. His small body vanished briefly behind a root. Then he rose upon his hind legs and pressed one paw against a place low on the trunk.

The Willow stopped. Silence arrived so suddenly that Harry heard frozen mud sliding from the nearest branch. Crookshanks sat beside the knot and began washing one shoulder. Hermione's mouth remained open.

Draconia retrieved the cage and turned it over. The lower locking plate still bore the split created when Lupin's transformation overloaded its wards. One of the three linked catches hung loose.

"It will not survive another transformation," she said.

Hermione knelt beside her. "Hold it still."

She traced the damaged silver with her wand. Metal flowed together beneath a precise Reparo, sealing the fracture and drawing the three runes back into a continuous line. When Draconia tested the door, every lock answered, although the repaired corner glowed less brightly than the rest.

"It will hold?" Harry asked.

"One contained transformation," Draconia said. "Provided nothing else interferes with the field."

She looked towards Crookshanks. "I owe that cat several reassessments."

"Come on."

They scrambled over the roots. The entrance waited beyond the trunk, a dark opening where earth had drawn back from the tree. Cold air breathed from it. The tunnel sloped steeply beneath the grounds, too low to walk upright. Harry hesitated at the edge. The map in his hand showed Sirius and Peter approaching the far end. McGonagall called again behind them.

Harry glanced back. The professor stood near the garden wall, both arms raised. Iron bands encircled the werewolf, but Lupin's claws tore at the earth as he fought them. Snape had regained his feet and was limping towards her. Beyond them, the Dementors crossed the Quidditch pitch. There would be no returning across the grounds now. Draconia lowered herself into the tunnel first.

Hermione looked offended. "Why are you leading?"

"Because Potter will chase the rat without checking for traps."

"I check for traps."

"You discover them."

"There's a difference," Harry said.

"That is the difference."

Draconia disappeared down the slope. Harry followed. Hermione entered behind him, and Crookshanks slipped inside before the Willow awakened. The moment his paw left the knot, the branches began striking the earth again. The entrance vanished behind them. The tunnel smelled of wet soil, rotting roots and animal fur.

Harry crawled upon his hands and knees. The ceiling pressed so low that his back scraped it whenever the passage narrowed. Draconia's wand lit the way ahead, throwing her shadow over the curved walls. Hermione's light followed behind them. Above, the Willow's roots trembled with each blow against the ground. Soil sifted into Harry's hair.

"How long is this?" Hermione whispered.

"A long way," Harry said.

"Useful."

"The map doesn't include measurements."

"It includes several hundred moving names but not a scale?"

"You can complain to the makers."

"I intend to."

Draconia stopped. Harry nearly crawled into her.

"What?"

She held up one hand. They listened. The passage ahead carried the scrape of movement. It was faint, separated from them by turns and distance, but undeniably present.

Sirius.

Or Peter.

Draconia lowered her voice. "Extinguish one light."

Hermione put out her wand. Darkness closed behind Harry. They continued more slowly. Roots twisted through the walls like bones. Several had been clawed smooth, bearing long scratches from years of Lupin's transformations. Harry tried not to imagine a werewolf crawling through the narrow space around them. The tunnel turned sharply and began to climb. A stone pressed into Harry's palm. He shifted his weight, and pain shot through the hand Peter had bitten.

Hermione noticed his breath catch.

"Let me see."

"Later."

"Harry."

"We don't have time."

She lit her wand only long enough to examine his fingers. Blood had dried along the side of his hand, and two punctures marked the skin below his thumb.

"It needs cleaning."

"It was a rat bite."

"It was a grown wizard pretending to be a rat for twelve years. I am not certain that improves matters."

Draconia glanced back. "Can you hold your wand?"

Harry closed his fingers around it.

"Yes."

"Properly."

He tightened his grip despite the pain.

"Yes."

She nodded and continued. Hermione remained close enough that her shoulder occasionally brushed Harry's shoe. The contact might have been accidental at first. After the third time, he understood that it was not. They travelled another hundred feet. The walls drew nearer. Harry's breathing changed before he recognised why.

The Chamber tunnel had been wider. He knew that. It had been built from stone and carved with serpents, not packed through earth beneath tree roots. Yet darkness confused detail. Each bend appeared to descend rather than rise. The cold air tasted damp. His knees remembered the slick floor beneath the school. He could almost see Draconia lying motionless in the darkness ahead, beyond his reach.

Her body will lie in the Chamber forever.

Harry stopped. Hermione collided with him.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Draconia looked over her shoulder. The light from her wand caught Harry's face. Her expression altered.

"Harry."

He could not draw enough air. The ceiling rested against his back, heavier with each breath. Soil pressed on every side. The tunnel stretched behind them, the entrance sealed by a violent tree, while somewhere ahead waited a house everyone believed haunted. It was not the Chamber. Knowing that should have mattered. His body had never been particularly interested in logic.

"I need a second," he whispered.

Draconia crawled backwards until she could turn. There was barely space for the three of them to kneel without touching.

"We should keep moving," Harry said.

Neither girl did. Hermione's wand brightened.

"What can you see?" she asked.

"The tunnel."

"Be specific."

Harry shut his eyes.

"That generally works better with them open," Draconia said.

He opened them and found her inches away. She was filthy. Mud streaked one cheek. Several strands of pale hair had escaped her braid and clung to her forehead.

"You," he said.

"Unfortunate, but accurate."

"Hermione."

"I'm here."

"Roots. Dirt. Your wand."

Hermione placed her hand over his uninjured one.

"What can you hear?"

"The tree."

Draconia tilted her head.

"Crookshanks purring," Harry added.

The cat had squeezed into the space beside Hermione. The sound was absurdly loud in the confined tunnel.

"And us," Hermione said.

"And you."

"Where are we?"

"Under the grounds."

"Not the Chamber."

Harry swallowed.

"No."

Draconia's face remained steady, but her eyes had changed. Harry knew what she was remembering too: waking in the hospital wing with Riddle's voice still threaded through her thoughts, Harry unconscious in the next bed and dried blood beneath his fingernails. She shifted nearer until their foreheads almost touched.

"I am not at the end of the tunnel," she said.

Harry looked at her.

"I am here."

The pressure around his lungs loosened by a fraction.

"Thank you," he managed.

"Do not make me repeat it. The wording was sentimental."

Hermione's thumb moved over his knuckles.

"Better?"

"No."

"Can you continue?"

Harry took one deliberate breath, then another.

"Yes."

That was not the same as being better. It was enough. Draconia turned and resumed crawling. This time, she kept a slower pace. Harry followed. He did not remove his hand from Hermione's until the passage widened.

Crookshanks stopped behind them. His purr ended, and both ears turned towards the distant entrance. A low growl travelled through the earth—too deep to belong to the tree.

"Crookshanks?" Hermione whispered.

The cat wheeled around and raced back the way they had come. Hermione reached for him, but her fingers closed upon empty air.

"He heard Lupin," Draconia said.

Harry checked the Map. Remus Lupin's name moved near the Willow above them. He followed the tunnel to its far end and opened a small flap he had mistaken for part of the border. Beneath it lay a separate plan of the Shack and the ground immediately around it, linked to Hogwarts only by the line of the tunnel. Peter Pettigrew had stopped inside. Sirius Black's name occupied the same room.

They had reached the Shack.

Outside, Minerva McGonagall saw the Whomping Willow begin moving again. The children had disappeared beneath its roots; she could no longer see even the light from their wands.

The werewolf threw itself against the restraints at the same instant. One iron band snapped. Another tore free from the ground and whipped through the air. Minerva transformed it into rope before it struck Severus, but the interruption cost her the chains around Lupin's hind legs. He broke free.

"Run!" Severus shouted.

Minerva did not. She drove her wand into the earth. Stone rose in a ring around Lupin, folding inward until only his head remained above it. He bit through the first layer. Cracks travelled down the structure. The Dementors were halfway across the grounds. Cold spread through Minerva's robes. The night lost colour. She heard her brother laughing beside a river, a memory from childhood she had not summoned in years—and then heard the laughter stop. She cut the thought away.

"Expecto Patronum!"

A silver tabby sprang from her wand. It raced towards the Dementors, growing brighter as it crossed the frozen grass. The leading creatures recoiled, but the Patronus could not turn so many. They separated around it and continued towards the hut. Severus reached Minerva's side. Blood ran freely from his shoulder.

"The students?" he demanded.

She looked towards the Willow.

"Gone."

His face became murderous.

"I told Potter to return to the castle."

"Perhaps you lacked specificity."

"I said inside!"

Severus swept his wand across the grounds. "The tunnel."

"Yes."

"They have followed Black."

"And Pettigrew."

"That distinction will comfort their parents."

The stone around Lupin exploded. Minerva raised a shield. Claws struck it, sending fractures of light across its surface. Severus cast over her shoulder, wrapping Lupin in conjured cords. The werewolf twisted and dragged him several feet through the mud.

"Your potion," Minerva said. "Why is he still aggressive?"

"He missed tonight's dose."

"He told us he had taken it."

"He took yesterday's. The potion reduces danger only when the full course is completed."

Lupin lunged. Severus's injured arm failed. Minerva caught his robes before he fell. A silver phoenix burst above them. It circled once, showering the ground in sparks. Albus Dumbledore's voice issued from its open beak.

Minerva, I am returning. Keep the Dementors beyond the Willow. Alastor Moody and Lucius Malfoy are approaching the Hogsmeade boundary. Do not permit Ministry forces onto the grounds.

The Patronus vanished. Severus stared at the empty air.

"Lucius Malfoy?"

"Not the most urgent part of that message."

"The man possesses a talent for becoming the most urgent part of any room."

The werewolf turned away from them. Its muzzle lifted. A scent had caught its attention. The tunnel. Minerva saw the instant animal instinct overcame the confusion produced by spells and pain. Lupin bounded towards the Willow.

"Stop him!"

She transfigured the earth beneath his front paws into a snare. He stumbled, recovered and leapt over the next spell. Severus cast a jet of fire. It struck a branch above Lupin, forcing the werewolf aside. The Willow responded. One of its limbs swept down. Minerva ducked. Severus did not. The branch caught him across the chest and threw him through the air. He landed near the garden wall, his wand disappearing into the mud.

Lupin reached the roots. Crookshanks shot from the tunnel entrance with his fur standing on end. A branch descended towards him. The cat sprang to the knot and flattened one paw against it. The Willow stilled.

Crookshanks turned to flee. Lupin seized the opening first and bounded past him into the passage.

"Remus!" Minerva shouted.

The werewolf vanished beneath the ground. Crookshanks hissed, struck uselessly at one grey hind leg, then removed his paw and disappeared after him. The tree awakened. Minerva ran to Severus. He was conscious, which she determined by the extraordinary profanity with which he greeted her.

"Can you stand?"

"I can kill Lupin."

"I asked whether you could stand."

She pulled him upright. A second wave of cold passed over them. The Dementors had reached Hagrid's garden. Minerva turned. More than forty floated beyond her Patronus. Their hoods tilted towards the Willow. They sensed Sirius below it, hidden by earth and old enchantments.

The creatures did not need the tunnel. The Shack possessed a broken entrance of its own.

"Severus," she said, "find your wand."

He wiped blood from his mouth.

"And then?"

"We keep them out of that tunnel."

The passage ended beneath a trapdoor. Draconia pushed it carefully. The wood lifted half an inch and caught against something heavy. She lowered it again.

"Blocked," she whispered.

Harry unfolded the map across the dirt. Sirius's name moved through the room directly above them. Peter remained near the far wall.

Hermione pointed. "There may be another entrance."

"There isn't," Harry said. "The map only shows this tunnel."

"Then we move whatever is blocking it."

"With magic?" Draconia asked.

"If Sirius sees the trapdoor opening itself, we lose any advantage."

Harry looked at the low ceiling.

"We don't have much advantage."

"We possess surprise."

"And a cage."

"You mocked the cage twenty minutes ago."

"I've grown."

Draconia placed her ear against the trapdoor. A man's voice travelled through it.

"Come out, Peter."

Sirius.

The voice was harsher than it had been in Lupin's office, stripped raw by exhaustion. A piece of furniture scraped across the floor.

"You wanted to live," Sirius continued. "Here's your opportunity. Come out and convince me."

No answer followed. Hermione mouthed, Where is Peter? Harry examined the map. Pettigrew's dot occupied the outer edge of the room, almost overlapping the wall.

"Inside it," he whispered.

Draconia understood. "A rat hole."

A crash shook dust from the trapdoor. Sirius swore. Peter's name darted along the wall, stopped, then doubled back.

"He's searching for another exit," Hermione said.

"There won't be one," Harry replied. "He lived here. Lupin, I mean. Dumbledore would've sealed it."

Draconia drew her wand.

"We open the door, identify both targets and maintain distance."

"Sirius isn't a target."

"He is armed, unstable and attempting murder."

"Fine. One and a half targets."

"Hermione takes Sirius," Draconia continued. "I contain Peter. Harry—"

"Why does Hermione get Sirius?"

"Because she is less emotionally compromised."

Hermione gave Draconia a disbelieving look.

"Compared with Harry," Draconia amended.

"What do I do?" Harry asked.

"Try not to be kidnapped."

"An essential contribution."

"Can you manage it?"

"Probably not."

Another impact struck the floor above. Harry slipped the map inside his robes and positioned himself beneath the trapdoor.

"On three."

"You have not been assigned to open it," Draconia said.

"I'm the strongest."

"That is plainly false."

"In this position."

"Still debatable."

"One," Hermione whispered.

They braced their shoulders beneath the wood.

"Two."

The room above went quiet. Harry met Draconia's eyes.

"Three."

They pushed. The trapdoor rose. A piece of furniture slid across the floorboards above them. Harry's arms shook beneath the weight. Hermione added a Levitation Charm. The obstruction scraped away, and stale air rushed into the tunnel. Draconia climbed through first. Harry followed before she could object.

He emerged beneath a four-poster bed. The mattress had collapsed through its frame, and one curtain hung in strips to the floor. Dust coated every surface. Wallpaper peeled from the walls in long grey curls, exposing damp plaster beneath. Moonlight entered through boarded windows, dividing the room into narrow silver bars. The air smelled of mould, old blood and dog. Draconia crouched beside the bed, wand raised. Hermione pulled herself through the trapdoor and lowered it gently behind her.

No one attacked. Harry crawled from beneath the bed. The room appeared empty.

"Map," Hermione whispered.

Harry opened it. Sirius Black stood directly behind them. Harry turned. A hand seized his robes and dragged him backwards. His wand flew from his fingers.

Hermione shouted. Draconia cast. Sirius twisted Harry between himself and the curse, forcing her to break it against the ceiling. Plaster rained down. The stolen wand pressed beneath Harry's jaw.

"Stop."

Sirius's breath struck his ear. His arm was locked across Harry's chest, but the pressure was controlled. He could have crushed Harry's throat. He did not. Hermione aimed between Sirius's eyes.

"Let him go."

"Where's Lupin?"

"Outside," Harry said.

Sirius's grip tightened. "Transformed?"

"Yes."

"Did he take the potion?"

"Most of it."

"That sounds like Moony."

Draconia moved two steps to the left. Sirius followed her with the wand.

"Do not," he warned.

"You are holding my friend hostage."

"I'm keeping your friend alive."

"Those activities should look less similar."

A scratching sound came from behind the wall. Sirius's head snapped towards it. Peter's name moved on the map. Harry drove his heel down on Sirius's foot. The arm loosened. Harry ducked and tore free. Hermione fired. Sirius deflected the spell, but Draconia's second curse caught his wrist.

The stolen wand spun away, skittered through the open bedroom doorway and clattered down the stairs. Harry dived for his own. Sirius reached the wall first. He struck a section of peeling wallpaper with his bare hand. The plaster broke. A brown rat burst from the opening, flashed between his feet and raced towards the trapdoor. Draconia threw the cage.

It expanded in the air, struck the floor around Peter and sealed with a metallic clang. The rat hit the bars. Silver runes flared. His body began to grow. Peter screamed.

The cage expanded with him, pushing aside the broken bed. Fur sank into flesh. Hind legs lengthened. His narrow skull widened, and ragged clothing unfolded around a human body until Peter Pettigrew crouched inside the silver bars. He seized them. Magic burned his palms.

"Harry!"

Sirius lunged. Hermione stunned him. The spell struck his chest and threw him into the wardrobe. Rotten wood collapsed around him. Draconia stared at Hermione.

"You might have warned me."

"You were busy."

"Fair."

Peter pressed himself into one corner of the cage. His small eyes moved rapidly between the three of them.

"Thank goodness," he gasped. "Thank goodness you came."

Harry retrieved his wand.

"That isn't the response I expected."

"Sirius was going to kill me."

Sirius lay motionless beneath the shattered wardrobe. Harry kept his wand trained on him while Hermione pointed hers at his chest.

"Rennervate."

A groan came from beneath the broken wood.

"He still may," Draconia said.

Hermione kept her wand trained upon Peter. "Stay away from the locks."

"I'm the victim."

"We'll see."

Peter looked at Harry. The resemblance affected him immediately. His face crumpled, and tears gathered with such speed that Harry wondered whether Peter could produce them on command.

"Harry," he whispered. "You look exactly like James."

"So everyone says."

"The hair. Your face. Even the way you hold your wand."

"Did my father point his at you often?"

Peter's mouth opened. Behind them, Sirius forced the wardrobe door from his chest. Hermione turned her wand.

"Stay there."

"Peter's in a cage."

"We noticed."

"You don't understand that cage won't hold him."

"It is specifically designed to hold an Animagus," Draconia said.

Sirius stared at her.

"Of course it is."

"My family believes in appropriate luggage."

Harry moved between Sirius and Peter.

"Sit down."

Sirius looked at him.

"You stunned me."

"Hermione did."

"Good spell."

"Thank you," Hermione said automatically.

Harry pointed his wand towards the remains of a chair.

"Sit."

"I'm not a dog."

"No, you're an escaped prisoner with no wand and several broken ribs."

Sirius's expression shifted.

"How do you know about the ribs?"

"You stop between breaths whenever you think no one is looking."

Draconia's attention shifted towards Sirius's ribs.

Harry pointed at her. "Whatever diagnosis you are preparing, keep it to yourself."

Sirius gave a short, surprised laugh. Pain cut it off. He lowered himself onto the piece of chair. Harry remained standing. The room settled around them. Wind passed through gaps between the boards across the windows, stirring loose wallpaper. Pipes knocked within the walls. From below came the distant groan of the house shifting upon its foundations. Peter watched Sirius.

Sirius watched Peter. Hatred occupied the distance between them with such density that Harry could almost feel it. Hermione moved the cage farther from the wall.

"No one casts anything unless we agree," she said.

Sirius lifted his brows. "Is that how this works?"

"Yes."

"James would be devastated."

"I'm not my father," Harry said.

Sirius's gaze found him.

"No," he said quietly. "You're not."

Peter wrapped both hands around his knees.

"Harry, please. You must listen to me before he fills your head with lies."

Sirius surged halfway out of the chair. Harry aimed at him.

"Sit."

"He has spent twelve years—"

"I said sit."

For one stretched second, Sirius looked ready to challenge him. Then he sat. Peter's relief appeared too quickly. Harry saw Draconia notice it.

"We're going to hear both accounts," Hermione said. "Separately where possible."

"This isn't a Wizengamot hearing," Sirius replied.

"No. A hearing would have occurred before you were imprisoned."

The bitterness vanished from his expression. Hermione drew a roll of parchment from her bag. Sirius stared at it.

"Did you bring notes into a pursuit?"

"She brings notes everywhere," Harry said.

"I brought a containment procedure."

"There are headings," Draconia observed.

"It's easier to read with headings."

"We are in a haunted house with a transformed werewolf somewhere outside."

"Then clarity is especially important."

Harry lowered himself to the floor where he could see both men. Draconia remained near the cage. Hermione stood beside him, quill poised above the parchment.

"Start with the Secret Keeper," she said. "Who suggested changing from Sirius to Peter?"

"I did," Sirius answered.

"At whose request?"

"No one's."

"Why?"

"Voldemort knew James would choose me. Everyone knew. Peter was meant to be the last person he would suspect."

Peter's fingers tightened around his knees.

"He forced me."

Sirius's face turned towards him. Harry raised his wand before either moved.

"How?" Hermione asked Peter.

"He said they would all die if I refused. James believed everything Sirius told him."

"Were you placed under Imperius?" Draconia asked.

"No."

"Threatened with a wand?"

"Not directly."

"Physically restrained?"

"No, but—"

"Then he persuaded you," Hermione said.

"I was terrified!"

"You could have said no," Draconia replied. "And none of that made you tell Voldemort where they were."

Peter turned towards Harry.

"You don't understand. Sirius suspected Professor Lupin. He said one of us was the spy. He made everyone afraid."

"Remus suspected me," Sirius said. "We were all afraid."

"Not James," Peter whispered.

Sirius looked away.

"James trusted all of you," Harry said.

The words tasted bitter.

Sirius nodded once. "That was his mistake."

Peter leaned against the bars.

"He trusted me too."

"Did you tell Voldemort where my parents were?"

Peter's lips trembled.

"He found me."

"That isn't what I asked."

"I tried to resist."

Sirius made a disgusted sound. Peter flinched.

"He knew," Peter said urgently. "He already knew I had been chosen. Someone told him."

"Who?"

Peter looked at Sirius. Sirius laughed.

"You're going to blame me for telling Voldemort that you were the Keeper?"

"You were his spy!"

"If I were the spy, I would have remained Keeper and handed over the secret myself."

Peter's mouth closed. Hermione's quill moved.

"That's not what you said before," she said. "First Sirius forced you. Now he told Voldemort. You still haven't told us why you gave away the address."

"I was tortured."

"The Fidelius secret cannot be extracted through torture," Hermione said.

"You don't know that."

"Professor Lupin confirmed it. So did Foundations of Concealment Magic, Protective Charms and Their Ethical Limitations and three separate legal decisions."

Peter stared at her.

Hermione added, "I checked."

Sirius almost smiled. Peter looked towards Draconia instead.

"Your father would understand."

The temperature of the room appeared to fall.

"What would he understand?" she asked.

"Survival. The Dark Lord gave people no choice. Men with families did whatever they had to do."

"My father has used that defence."

Peter saw agreement where none had been offered.

"Exactly."

"He has also stopped using it."

"Because the Dark Lord is gone."

Draconia's expression became completely still.

"And if he returns?" Peter asked. "Do you believe Lucius Malfoy will die for Harry Potter?"

Sirius rose. Draconia stopped him with one raised hand. She approached the cage. Peter shifted backwards.

"My father's courage is not on trial," she said. "Yours is."

"I was younger than he was."

"You were trusted by people who loved you."

"They would have died regardless!"

Harry stood so quickly that the floorboard cracked beneath his shoe. Peter recoiled. The screaming inside Harry's head no longer belonged to the Dementors.

"They died because Voldemort found them."

"He was winning. No one could stop him."

"My mother did."

Peter looked at Harry's scar.

"Yes," Harry said. "That's why I'm here."

Silence closed around the cage. Peter began crying again.

"You have her eyes," he whispered.

"Stop saying things you think will matter to me."

"But I knew them. I knew you. I held you when you were a baby."

Harry hated the image. Peter continued before he could stop him.

"You used to grab people's hair. James said it proved you would be a Seeker. Lily said it proved you had hands."

Sirius's eyes closed. The detail was too ordinary to have been invented easily. Harry felt it land within him: a piece of his life, held by the wrong person.

"Your mother made a cake for your first birthday," Peter said. "It collapsed in the middle, and Sirius filled the hole with sweets when she wasn't looking. You were sick on his robes."

"You told Voldemort where that house was."

Peter's breath hitched.

"You knew what was inside it," Harry continued. "You knew there was a cot upstairs. You knew where my mother baked cakes and where my father left his wand."

"I didn't know he would go that night."

"When did you think he would go?"

Peter had no answer. Harry's hand shook. He lowered his wand before the movement became visible. Sirius watched him with an expression Harry could not read. Grief, perhaps. Or recognition.

"Tell me what happened after," Harry said.

Peter wiped his face. "After what?"

"After they died."

Sirius answered.

"I went to Peter's hiding place."

His voice had changed. The anger remained, but it no longer controlled every word.

"I had arranged it. A cottage in Bristol under a false name. He was supposed to stay there until I could move him after the charm was cast. When I arrived, the wards were broken and the place was empty."

"When?" Hermione asked.

"The night of the thirty-first. I had been checking Order safe houses. I heard rumours that Voldemort had disappeared, but nobody knew why. I went to Peter first because he was the only person who could confirm whether the Fidelius had broken."

Sirius rubbed one hand across his face.

"I found his robes in the bedroom and blood on the floor. Not much. Enough to make it look like someone had taken him."

"He staged an attack," Draconia said.

"Yes."

"Why go to him before my parents?" Harry asked.

"Because I still believed the house was hidden. If Peter was alive and had said nothing, James and Lily were safe."

Sirius looked at him.

"And because I was an idiot."

"That seems to be common in stories about my parents."

"It was a competitive field."

Harry nearly smiled. Sirius continued.

"When I reached Godric's Hollow, the house was visible."

His gaze dropped to his hands.

"I knew before I went inside."

Harry's lungs tightened. Hermione lowered her quill.

"You don't have to—" she began.

"Yes," Harry said. "He does."

Sirius looked up. Harry held his gaze.

"Tell me."

"The front door was gone. James was in the hall."

The room around Harry retreated.

"He hadn't reached his wand," Sirius said. "It was on the sofa. He must have left it there when Voldemort arrived."

Harry had seen that memory through his mother's eyes: James rising, calling for Lily to take Harry and run. Empty hands. Footsteps in the hall.

"What did you do?" he asked.

"I checked him."

"Even though you knew?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because knowing is not the same as accepting."

Harry understood that too well.

"I heard you upstairs," Sirius continued. "Hagrid arrived before I reached you. Dumbledore had sent him."

"Hagrid took me."

"He said he had orders."

"You let him?"

Pain moved over Sirius's face.

"I tried not to."

Harry imagined Hagrid standing among the ruins with a baby in his arms, Sirius wild with grief before him.

"I told him I was your godfather. I told him James and Lily had appointed me guardian. He refused. Dumbledore's instructions were to take you to your aunt."

"You knew about the Dursleys?"

"I knew Lily had a sister. I had met her once."

Harry blinked. "You met Aunt Petunia?"

"Briefly."

"What happened?"

"She asked whether I lived in a squat."

Draconia's mouth twitched.

"What did you say?"

"That the Black family owned more houses than she owned matching plates."

Despite everything, Harry laughed. It emerged sharply and vanished almost at once, but Sirius stared at him as if the sound had opened a door.

"She disliked me," he said.

"She dislikes everyone."

"Lily said that too."

Harry folded his arms around himself.

"You gave Hagrid your motorbike."

"I wasn't thinking clearly. I told him I wouldn't need it."

"Because you were going after Peter."

"Yes."

"To kill him."

"Yes."

Peter made a wounded noise. Sirius turned upon him.

"You knew what I would find in that house."

"I never wanted them dead."

"You sold them to Voldemort!"

"I wanted protection!"

"You had us!"

The words struck the walls. Peter shrank against the cage. Sirius's chest rose and fell. Harry could see how little separated him from violence. Hunger and grief had stripped away every softer layer.

"What happened in the street?" Hermione asked.

Sirius did not take his eyes from Peter.

"I found him the following morning. He chose a Muggle street because he knew there would be witnesses. The moment he saw me, he began shouting that I had betrayed James and Lily."

"He was crying," Peter whispered. "Laughing."

"After you blew apart twelve people."

"I didn't!"

Sirius crossed the room in two strides. Harry blocked him. Sirius looked down at him. Up close, the damage of Azkaban was impossible to ignore. His face was gaunt enough that every emotion moved across bone. Grey threaded hair that should still have been black. Scars circled one wrist where iron had worn through skin repeatedly.

"He raised his wand," Sirius told Harry. "The street exploded. I saw him cut off his finger before he transformed."

Peter thrust his left hand through the bars. Only the smallest finger was missing.

"Proof," he said. "Proof he tried to kill me."

"You cut it off yourself," Harry replied.

"Would anyone do that?"

Draconia's gaze moved to the missing finger.

"To avoid Azkaban?"

Peter became quiet.

"That was how you escaped," Hermione said. "You cast the spell, then transformed. A rat could disappear through the damaged street or the sewers."

"I was injured."

"But alive."

"I hid because Sirius was hunting me."

"You lived with the Weasleys," Harry said.

"A good family. They took care of me."

"You belonged to Percy first."

Peter nodded eagerly. "I watched the family. Protected them where I could."

"Then Ron."

"Yes."

"Whose father worked at the Ministry."

Peter's expression shifted. Draconia followed Harry's reasoning.

"And who travelled regularly through places where you could hear political news without exposing yourself."

"I was a pet."

"You were waiting," she said.

"For what?"

"The outcome."

Peter looked at her.

"If Voldemort returned, you needed to know before the public did. If his enemies remained powerful, the Weasleys gave you protection. If his supporters began reorganising, you could leave."

"That is absurd."

"It kept you safe," Draconia said. "Whatever happened to anyone else."

Peter's eyes hardened for the first time. It happened only briefly. The wet fear withdrew, revealing calculation beneath it. Harry saw. So did Sirius.

"There you are," Sirius whispered.

Peter immediately crumpled again.

"Harry, please. James would not want you to listen to him. Sirius hated your father at the end. They argued constantly."

"About what?" Harry asked.

Peter hesitated.

"The Secret Keeper. Your mother. Sirius thought James was becoming weak."

Sirius went very still. Peter pressed closer to the bars.

"He resented that James chose his family over their friendship. Everyone knew. He was reckless during missions because he wanted the war to continue. He wanted James beside him."

"Is that true?" Harry asked.

Sirius's jaw tightened.

"We argued."

Peter seized upon it. "You see?"

"Be quiet."

"Why?" Harry asked.

Sirius looked at him.

"Why did you argue?"

"Because James stopped taking missions after you were born."

The answer was reluctant.

"I thought he was frightened."

"Was he?"

"Of course he was. He had a son."

Sirius sank back onto the broken chair.

"I was twenty-one and believed fear was a character defect. James wanted to stay with Lily. I accused him of hiding. He told me there were things worth being frightened for."

Harry thought of the man who had faced Voldemort without a wand.

"Were you angry?"

"Yes."

"Enough to betray him?"

"No."

"How do I know?"

Sirius looked towards Peter, then at the cage, the map and Hermione's notes.

"You don't."

Peter smiled faintly. Sirius saw it.

"I can give you reasons," he continued. "Memories. Evidence if Moody finds the original records. But you don't know me. You shouldn't trust me because I knew your parents or because they chose me once."

Harry had expected pleading. The absence of it mattered more.

"What should I trust?" he asked.

"What I do now."

Lupin's words. Trust will depend upon what I do next. Harry lowered his wand. Sirius looked towards the movement but did not stand.

"You sent the Firebolt," Harry said.

Hermione stared. "Now?"

"We're checking details."

Sirius blinked.

"That's what you want to ask me?"

"It's an expensive broom."

"Yes."

"How?"

"Crookshanks took the order to the owl office. I told the shop to take payment from my vault."

Hermione's suspicion sharpened. "Gringotts allowed that?"

"Goblins care more about correct authorisation than Ministry warrants."

"Why didn't you include your name?"

"I thought you might object to receiving post from an escaped mass murderer."

"You could've written 'not actually a mass murderer.'"

"In retrospect, that would have resolved everything."

Harry glanced at Hermione.

"That was sarcasm," she said.

"I recognised it."

Sirius's mouth curved. Peter looked between them.

"You are letting him charm you."

"No," Draconia said. "Sirius Black is many things. Charming is not currently among them."

"I've had a difficult decade."

"That explains the smell more than the personality."

Sirius stared at her.

"You are definitely related to Narcissa."

"She has better manners."

"She had tutors."

The exchange loosened the room by a fraction. Peter watched it happen. His hand moved behind his leg. Harry saw the shoulder shift.

"What are you doing?"

Peter froze.

"Nothing."

Draconia struck the cage with her wand. The silver bars contracted. Peter cried out as they forced him towards the centre. A narrow metal pin fell from his hand—the inner catch from the locking plate Hermione had repaired beside the Willow. Hermione snatched it through the bars with a Summoning Charm.

"You were dismantling the lock," she said.

"No, I found it on the floor."

"Inside your sleeve?"

Draconia touched her wand to the damaged plate. Two of the runes answered at once. The third flickered.

"The door will remain shut," she said, "but the containment circuit is weakened. Do not touch the lower corner again."

Peter's fear returned in full.

"You don't understand what he'll do to me."

"I do," Harry said.

Everyone looked at him. Sirius's face hardened.

"He deserves it."

"Probably."

Peter began shaking. Harry faced Sirius.

"But you're not killing him."

The house creaked around them. Sirius rose slowly.

"He murdered your parents."

"I know."

"He left you with those Muggles."

Harry's throat tightened. Sirius noticed.

"What did they do?"

"Not now."

"What did they do to you?"

"Not now."

The force of Harry's answer stopped him. Sirius looked at Harry's clothes, the thinness Lupin and the girls had spent months teaching Harry not to hide, and the fading scar upon his wrist where Vernon's hand had closed too hard. Understanding arrived by degrees.

"I should have been there."

"You were in Azkaban."

"Because I went after him."

"Yes."

"I left you."

"You didn't choose the Dursleys."

"I chose revenge."

The sentence stripped every excuse from it. Harry did not know how to comfort an adult for failing him. He did not want to. The hurt was too new, attached to a version of his childhood in which somewhere else a godfather should have been searching for him.

"You can't change that," he said.

"No."

"You can decide what happens next."

Sirius's gaze moved to Peter.

"That is what I'm trying to do."

"Killing him won't clear you."

"I don't care about being cleared."

"I do."

Sirius looked back at Harry. The words surprised them both. Harry stepped nearer.

"If he dies, everyone keeps believing you did it. The Dementors take you back, or they Kiss you. Peter becomes a dead hero again."

"He won't be a hero to us."

"That doesn't help if you're gone."

Sirius's breathing stopped. Hermione lowered her parchment. Draconia watched Harry rather than either prisoner. Harry forced himself to continue.

"I don't know if I trust you. I don't know if you're safe. You broke into my dormitory with a knife, which was a very stupid way to introduce yourself."

"I had imagined it differently."

"But if you're telling the truth, then you lost twelve years because of him."

"Yes."

"Don't give him the rest."

Peter's eyes darted between them. Sirius seemed to fold around the words. Anger remained in his face, but Harry could see effort being applied to it now—every muscle resisting the direction in which it had travelled for years.

"What do you want me to do?" he asked.

"Wait for Dumbledore."

"There are Dementors outside."

"McGonagall sent for him."

"And Remus?"

"Transformed."

Sirius looked towards the floor.

"He'll come here."

"The tunnel is closed by the Willow."

"He knows how to stop it."

"So does Crookshanks."

Sirius swore.

Hermione gathered the map. "Then we leave before he arrives."

"With Peter?" Harry asked.

"In the cage."

"The tunnel is too narrow."

Draconia examined the runes around its base. "It can contract while containing a transformed Animagus, but he must become a rat first."

Peter pressed himself against the bars. "I won't."

"You transformed to escape thirty minutes ago," she said.

"I will not let you carry me to the Dementors."

"No one is giving you to them," Harry replied.

"You cannot promise that."

"He can't," Hermione said. "Dumbledore can prevent it."

Peter laughed weakly.

"You think Dumbledore controls the Ministry? They will kill me before I can speak. They cannot admit what they did to Sirius."

"Then perhaps you should have surrendered before escaping twice," Draconia said.

"I was afraid."

"Yes," she replied. "You've told us. And everyone else paid for it."

A howl travelled through the floor. Everyone stopped. It came from below them. The tunnel. Sirius transformed.

The change moved through him with a series of violent shifts. His hands struck the floor and became paws. Black fur covered his wasted frame. Within seconds, the man was gone and the enormous dog stood between Harry and the trapdoor. The Animagus form did not conceal Azkaban's damage. His ribs showed through matted fur, and one hind leg carried his weight unevenly. He growled towards the floor. Another howl answered.

Closer.

Hermione backed towards Harry.

"We need to move."

"Where?" Draconia asked.

The bedroom door stood open upon a dark landing. A staircase descended to the ground floor, but the Shack's doors and windows had been sealed for years. Even if they found a way outside, Hogsmeade lay beyond it and the Dementors might already be surrounding the building. The trapdoor shook. Dust leapt from its edges. Sirius barked.

"Can he hold Lupin?" Harry asked.

"James and Sirius controlled him together," Hermione said. "Sirius is injured."

Draconia looked at the cage.

"We use Pettigrew."

Peter's face whitened. "What?"

"Werewolves prefer human prey."

"Draco," Harry said.

"I am creating incentive."

The trapdoor lifted. Sirius threw his weight onto it. Wood slammed shut. Claws scraped beneath them. The dog's paws slid across the floorboards as the werewolf pushed from below.

Harry ran to help. He braced both hands upon the trapdoor beside Sirius. Hermione joined him, then Draconia. The wood jumped beneath their weight. A claw broke through. Hermione screamed and jerked her hand away. Blood welled across her palm. Harry cast through the gap.

"Stupefy!"

Red light illuminated fur and teeth. The werewolf recoiled. The trapdoor dropped flat. Sirius remained pressed over it, growling. Hermione wrapped her injured hand in the edge of her sleeve.

"Show me," Harry said.

"It's a scratch."

"Hermione."

"No bite. No saliva. It barely broke the skin."

Draconia inspected it. "She's right."

A crash sounded downstairs. Everyone turned. Peter smiled. It was small and involuntary. Harry followed his gaze.

The cage door remained shut, but the lowest rune had gone dark. Peter had worked a section of silver wire from the weakened corner while their attention remained upon the trapdoor. Blood streaked both of his palms.

"Draco—" Harry began.

Peter moved. His body collapsed inward. The break in the runic circuit extinguished the containment ward, and although the cage shrank automatically, one bar failed where he had weakened it. Rat-Peter squeezed through the gap, leaving a strip of fur behind. A jagged sliver of the broken bar scraped after him, clenched between his teeth. Draconia cast. Peter dodged beneath the broken bed. Sirius abandoned the trapdoor. The werewolf struck from below at the same instant.

Wood exploded into the room. Harry was thrown against the wall. His head struck plaster, filling his vision with white light. Hermione landed beside the wardrobe. Draconia caught the bedpost and remained upright. The werewolf pulled itself through the shattered floor. Its shoulders barely cleared the opening. Claws tore at the boards. Yellow eyes found movement beneath the bed.

Peter.

The rat bolted. Sirius intercepted the werewolf before it could follow. Black dog and wolf collided. The impact broke the bed frame. Sirius locked his jaws into Lupin's shoulder. The werewolf twisted and threw him across the room. Sirius struck the wall.

Harry heard bone crack.

"No!"

He cast a Shield Charm between them. The werewolf hit it and sent Harry sliding backwards across the floor. Hermione fired a binding spell. Ropes caught Lupin's hind legs, then snapped when he kicked. Draconia summoned the collapsed cage, caught it by the handle and pursued Peter. The rat vanished through the bedroom door.

"Peter's getting away!"

Harry looked from the staircase to Sirius. The dog tried to stand. One front leg folded beneath him. The werewolf advanced.

"Go!" Harry shouted at Draconia.

She hesitated.

"Get Peter!"

Draconia ran downstairs. Hermione moved beside Harry. Together they cast.

"Impedimenta!"

The combined spell struck Lupin's chest. It did not stop him, but every movement slowed, his limbs forcing themselves through thick invisible pressure. Harry reached Sirius.

"Can you change back?"

The dog's lips drew away from his teeth.

"I'm taking that as no."

Harry hooked one arm beneath his chest. Sirius was lighter than a dog his size should have been. Hermione seized his other side.

"We cannot carry him down the stairs."

"We don't have to."

Harry looked towards the wardrobe.

"Help me move it."

They dragged Sirius behind the wreckage just as the Impediment Jinx failed. The werewolf shook itself. Its head turned towards them. Harry aimed his wand, but Hermione caught his wrist.

"Don't attack unless it moves."

"It's looking directly at us."

"Lupin may recognise Sirius."

"He just threw him through a wall."

"He is injured and frightened."

"So are we."

The werewolf sniffed. Sirius growled from behind the wardrobe. Lupin's ears turned towards the sound. His head lowered. Harry felt the shift in the room.

"Professor," he said.

The werewolf's gaze snapped to him. Hermione's grip tightened.

"It's Harry."

The yellow eyes showed nothing human. Harry remembered Lupin's lessons. Fear took shape, but it was not authority. A body could react before the mind understood. Panic could fill every available space and still not deserve obedience. Lupin had taught him that. Harry lowered his wand by an inch.

"You know me."

The werewolf stepped forward. Hermione raised her wand. Harry did not move.

"You taught me the Patronus Charm," he continued. "You gave me chocolate on the train. You told me my father was an idiot."

The wolf's lips drew back.

"That last one was mostly implied."

It lunged. Sirius exploded from behind the wardrobe. He struck Lupin from the side, and both animals crashed through the bedroom door. The banister splintered. They fell down the stairs in a storm of fur, wood and snarling.

Harry ran after them. Hermione caught the back of his robes.

"Wait!"

"We can't leave him."

"We also can't climb over a werewolf."

The sounds below stopped. Then a door slammed. Silence followed. Harry and Hermione reached the landing. The staircase curved down into the ruined sitting room. Chairs had been overturned. Dust revolved through moonlight entering between boards. The front door stood open, hanging crooked from one hinge.

Cold air entered the house. Sirius and Lupin were gone. So was Peter. Draconia stood near the centre of the room, wand directed towards a narrow hole in the skirting board. The broken cage lay beside her boots.

"He went through there."

"Can you get him out?" Harry asked.

"Not without removing the wall."

"Do it."

Hermione reached them.

"The werewolf is outside."

"So are the Dementors."

Harry opened the map and unfolded the little plan of the Shack. Near the tunnel entrance, Sirius Black and Remus Lupin circled one another across the snow. Peter Pettigrew remained inside the house. Beyond the narrow strip of ground around the building, the parchment showed nothing of Hogsmeade; if Peter escaped into the village, they would lose him.

"There," Harry said. "He hasn't left."

His name occupied the wall behind the fireplace.

Draconia followed the map. "The chimney."

A faint scraping descended through the bricks. Harry ran to the hearth. It contained no fire, only years of ash and broken plaster. The opening above narrowed too quickly for a grown man but offered ample space to a rat.

"He's climbing to the roof," Hermione said.

Draconia pointed her wand into the chimney.

"If I collapse it—"

"You'll crush him," Harry said.

Her jaw tightened. "I am aware."

"We need him alive."

"Then suggest another option."

Harry looked around. On the floor near the shattered door lay Sirius's stolen wand. He summoned it. The wood was dark, chipped near the handle and slightly bent. It did not belong to Peter, but Harry remembered Ollivander explaining how allegiance could change when a wand was won.

"Can Prior Incantato show what Sirius cast?" Hermione asked.

"Yes, but how does that help?"

"It proves whether he caused the explosion."

"Wrong wand."

"I know. I'm thinking."

The scraping above them stopped. Peter's name reached the upper chimney. Then it began moving down again.

"Why is he coming back?" Draconia whispered.

A shadow passed across the boarded windows. Cold entered the room. The remaining glass whitened with frost. Hermione's face lost colour.

"The Dementors."

A hooded shape drifted past the opening. Another followed. They had reached the Shack. Harry heard his mother inhale. The memory came gently this time, which was worse.

Lily stood beside his cot, one hand passing over his hair. Footsteps climbed the stairs. She knew no one was coming to help. She knew James had fallen.

Please.

Harry gripped the mantel. The room dimmed.

"Harry." Hermione's voice sounded distant. "Stay here."

"I am here."

"Look at me."

He tried. The screaming began.

Draconia moved into his line of sight. "Not the tunnel. Not the Chamber. This room."

Frost spread across her hair. A Dementor entered through the broken front doorway. Its robes dragged across the splintered threshold without sound. Tall and decayed, it drifted into the sitting room and turned its hood towards them. Hermione cast.

"Expecto Patronum!"

Silver mist poured from her wand. It formed the outline of an animal—small, low and quick—then dissolved before Harry could see it clearly. The Dementor slowed. A second entered behind it. Draconia stepped in front of Harry.

"Expecto Patronum!"

A ribbon of pale light snapped from her wand and coiled through the air. It did not take shape, but the nearest Dementor recoiled. Peter dropped from the chimney. He landed in the ash, transformed before his feet touched the floor and seized Draconia from behind. One arm closed around her throat. The other held a length of sharpened silver against her side—the missing piece of cage bar.

"Lower your wands!"

Harry turned. Peter dragged Draconia backwards towards the broken door. Ash covered his hair and robes. His face no longer carried tears.

"Let her go."

"Tell them to lower their wands."

The Dementors drifted closer. Their presence stole warmth from Peter too. His body shook, but desperation sharpened rather than weakened him. Draconia remained unnaturally still within his grip. Harry knew that stillness. She was thinking. Hermione did too.

"We lower them," Harry said.

Hermione looked at him.

"Now."

They placed their wands on the floor. Peter tightened his arm around Draconia.

"Kick them away."

Harry obeyed. His wand slid beneath an overturned chair. Hermione's stopped near the foot of the stairs.

"Open the passage," Peter said.

"What passage?"

"The one to Honeydukes."

"There isn't one."

"Every old house in Hogsmeade has smuggling routes."

"You lived here," Harry said. "You tell us."

"I know it is below the cellar. Open it."

"The map only shows the Willow tunnel."

"Then the map is incomplete."

A Dementor extended one scabbed hand. Harry heard a body strike the floor in another room; then he saw Draconia lying pale and motionless on the Chamber floor and Hermione Petrified behind the hospital curtains. The moments collided without sequence. His mind supplied every person he had nearly failed, then changed nearly into had before reality could object.

Peter pulled Draconia nearer to the door. The Dementor followed.

"Call them off!" Peter screamed.

Harry stared at him. "Do you think I control them?"

"You control everything else!"

"Clearly."

Draconia's fingers moved against Peter's sleeve.

Once.

Twice.

A signal. Hermione saw. Harry kept Peter's attention.

"If you leave the house, they'll Kiss you."

"Not if I have her."

"They don't care who they hurt."

"Her father does. The Ministry won't risk Lucius Malfoy's daughter."

"The Ministry sent Dementors to a school."

Peter's confidence faltered.

"Look at them," Harry said. "Do they seem concerned about her surname?"

The nearest Dementor drifted towards Peter. He turned his head. Draconia moved. She drove her heel into his foot, caught the wrist holding the silver and twisted. Peter shouted. The weapon fell. Hermione dived for her wand.

Harry reached his beneath the chair. Peter shoved Draconia towards the Dementor and transformed. Harry caught her robes before she crossed its path. The rat struck the floor.

"Crookshanks!" Hermione shouted.

The ginger cat dropped from the staircase. He landed directly upon Peter. They rolled across the ash. Peter squealed and twisted beneath him, but Crookshanks closed his teeth around the rat's back and held on. Draconia snatched up the broken cage.

"Put him inside!"

Crookshanks dragged Peter towards her. A shadow covered them. The Dementor lowered its hood. Harry saw the mouth. It was a glistening hole within decayed skin, opening upon hunger without end.

Draconia froze. Awareness of the room vanished from her face. Harry knew without being told what she heard: Riddle's voice inside her thoughts, praising each secret she had surrendered. The basilisk moving through darkness. Harry calling her name from very far away. The Dementor bent towards her. Harry stepped between them.

His wand felt numb in his hand. He searched for a memory. Flying upon the Firebolt. Hermione's first letter beneath his mattress. Draconia talking to him in Madam Malkin's as though he were simply another boy buying school robes. Mrs Granger hugging him at King's Cross. Narcissa adjusting his collar. Lupin's patient voice during their lessons. The Dementor continued. None of the memories held.

They were real, but fear entered each one. The broom had fallen from him. The letters had stopped. Draconia had vanished into the Chamber. Every happiness carried proof it could be lost. Harry understood suddenly why the Patronus had failed him so often. He had been trying to remember a moment no darkness could touch. There was no such moment. Happiness was not safe because nothing could take it.

It mattered because he had possessed it anyway. Hermione's hand closed around his. Draconia rose behind him. He did not stand alone. Harry raised his wand.

"Expecto Patronum!"

Silver light burst across the room. It struck the Dementor hard enough to drive it backwards through the doorway. Antlers formed first, wide and brilliant. A long muzzle lifted. Hooves struck the floor without sound. The stag emerged completely. Harry forgot to breathe. It stood taller than he did, built from light yet solid enough to disturb the ash. Its head turned towards him.

Prongs.

Not James. Harry knew that too. The Patronus was his. It charged. Dementors fled through the broken doorway and the gaps between the window boards as the stag drove them from the sitting room. Its antlers swept through darkness, scattering it into scraps of shadow. Hermione's wand released another stream of silver. This time, the light gathered into a small animal with a long tail and rounded ears.

An otter. It raced after the stag, twisting beneath its legs before leaping at the nearest Dementor. Hermione stared. Draconia lifted her wand. Her silver light coiled again. It lengthened, scales forming along a narrow body. A serpent rose from the floor, bright and elegant, and struck at the hands reaching around the ruined doorframe.

Stag.

Otter.

Serpent.

Harry might have laughed if his lungs had remembered how. Together, the Patronuses cleared the house. Warmth returned gradually. Frost melted along the walls and dropped from the ceiling like rain. The boards across the windows creaked as ice released them. Crookshanks still held Peter. Draconia opened the damaged cage.

Hermione repaired the broken bar with a sharp movement of her wand. Draconia resealed the interrupted rune, forcing silver light through the entire base until the circuit held. Crookshanks dropped the rat inside. The door slammed. Every lock engaged. Peter transformed, but the cage expanded with him. The silver bars burned red where his hands struck them.

"No!" he shouted. "You don't know what you're doing!"

Harry walked towards him. The stag remained beside Harry's shoulder. Peter stared into its light. Harry had wondered whether producing his father's Animagus form would feel like finding James. It did not. The Patronus carried no borrowed memory, no voice from the dead. Its shape belonged to love Harry had inherited but never known, and to the choices he had made afterward.

James had been Prongs. Harry was not. The stag lowered its antlers towards Peter.

"Please," Peter whispered.

"You keep saying that word," Harry replied.

"Your father—"

"Is dead."

Peter recoiled.

"And I am finished letting you tell me what he would want."

A spell cracked against the floor between Harry and the cage. The stag wheeled towards the sound. Harry's concentration broke, and the Patronus dissolved into sparks. Professor Snape stood in the broken doorway. Blood covered one side of his robes. Mud streaked his face, and his left arm hung at an unnatural angle. He held his wand in his right hand.

Its tip pointed at Harry. Behind him, the night was crowded with Dementors.

"Step away from Pettigrew," Snape said.

Hermione lifted her wand. Draconia moved beside Harry.

"Professor," Harry began, "he confessed."

"I heard enough."

"Then you know Sirius is innocent."

Snape's eyes travelled towards the ruined doorway. Outside, the black dog and werewolf fought in the snow.

"I know Black brought a werewolf onto grounds occupied by children."

"Lupin came after us."

"Because Black led you here."

"We followed Pettigrew."

"After being explicitly ordered not to."

"That's separate."

"It rarely is with you."

Snape entered the room. The Dementors gathered beyond the doorway but did not cross. The fading light of the Patronuses lingered in the walls, holding them back for seconds at most. Hermione placed herself between Snape and the cage.

"Sir, Peter Pettigrew was the Secret Keeper. The map proves he is an Animagus, multiple people heard his admission and Mr Malfoy has obtained irregularities from the original investigation."

"Move, Miss Granger."

"No."

Snape's face tightened.

"You are exhausted, injured and incapable of evaluating the danger."

"I can evaluate a cage."

"Ten points from Gryffindor."

Hermione looked almost insulted by the smallness of it.

Draconia raised her chin. "Will you remove points from Slytherin if I also refuse?"

"Fifty."

"So you don't care what we've found?"

"My intended action is to return three students to the castle."

"And Pettigrew?"

"To the Headmaster."

Harry lowered his wand by an inch.

"That's what we want."

Snape looked at him.

"Black?"

"He's protecting us from Lupin."

"He is attempting to kill Lupin."

A crash sounded outside. The werewolf struck the Shack's outer wall. The room shuddered. Dust fell from the ceiling. Snape's wand did not waver.

"If Sirius changes back," Harry said, "you listen to him."

"No."

The answer came before Harry finished. Anger rose.

"Then you don't care what happened."

"I care that you leave this building alive."

"You care about hating Sirius."

Snape's expression became still. Harry knew he had found the wound.

"He tried to kill me when we were sixteen," Snape said.

Hermione's breath caught.

"What?" Harry asked.

"He sent me into this tunnel during a full moon."

The sounds outside seemed to retreat.

"Professor Lupin was transformed," Snape continued. "Black told me how to pass the Willow. Your father pulled me back before Lupin reached me."

Harry looked through the broken doorway. Sirius's dog form rolled across the snow with the werewolf, jaws locked around Lupin's throat without closing hard enough to kill.

"He was sixteen."

"You were twelve when you entered the Chamber. You understood what a basilisk could do. Black had four more years to understand a werewolf."

"That isn't the same."

"No. Miss Malfoy did not invite you there for amusement."

Draconia flinched. Snape saw and regretted the comparison, but the damage remained. Harry stepped in front of her.

"You still have to listen."

"To the man who attempted to feed me to a werewolf?"

"To the evidence."

Snape crossed the remaining distance. His wand rose until it touched Harry's chest.

"You believe resemblance makes you invulnerable to his cruelty."

"I told you I'm not James."

"No," Snape said. "You are a thirteen-year-old boy standing between an armed professor and a traitor because an escaped prisoner bought you a broom."

Harry's face burned.

"That isn't why."

"Then move."

"No."

Snape's gaze shifted beyond him. Peter stood inside the cage with both hands raised.

"Severus," he said.

Snape's wand moved. Peter shrank.

"You know me," he pleaded. "You know what Sirius was like."

"I know what all of you were like."

"Then help me."

"Be silent."

Peter obeyed. Snape returned his attention to Harry. The Dementors pressed closer to the threshold. Their cold entered the room again.

"Take the cage," Snape ordered Hermione. "Miss Malfoy, watch the rear passage. Potter, remain where I can see you."

"We need Sirius," Harry said.

"We need Pettigrew alive."

"To clear Sirius."

"To establish the truth."

"That is the same thing."

"Not until it is proven."

It was the most Snape would give him. Harry heard it. So did Hermione. She levitated the cage.

"Fine," Harry said. "We bring Peter to Dumbledore. Then we come back for Sirius."

"If Black survives."

"He will."

"You cannot know that."

Harry looked through the doorway. Sirius lay in the snow. The werewolf stood over him. The dog's black body did not move. Harry forgot Snape's wand.

He ran.

"Potter!"

The night struck him with cold. He crossed the broken threshold, boots sliding upon snow and mud. The Dementors turned towards him. Behind their robes, darkness deepened. Harry raised his wand.

"Expecto Patronum!"

The stag returned. It charged through the gathering creatures, opening a path between Harry and Sirius. The otter followed a second later, then the silver serpent. Their combined light washed across the snow. The werewolf lifted its head. Sirius remained beneath it. Harry ran directly towards them.

"Harry!" Hermione screamed.

Lupin stepped away from Sirius's body. For one impossible instant, Harry believed the professor recognised him. Then the werewolf lunged. A red spell struck it from the side. Lupin flew through the air and vanished between the trees.

Harry turned. Snape stood in the doorway, wand extended.

"Five hundred points from Gryffindor," he said.

Then Peter Pettigrew's cage exploded behind him.
