6

The Daily Prophet arrived before anyone in the cottage had slept long enough to call it morning.

Its owl struck the eastern window at half past seven. Hermione jerked upright on the sitting-room sofa, Mr Granger dropped the blanket he had been folding, and Sirius came through the kitchen doorway with his wand raised. Remus appeared at the top of the stairs a second later, barefoot and still wearing the shirt he had fought in. His first spell searched the window for curses; his second examined the bird.

The owl struck the glass again. It carried six copies of the same newspaper and seemed deeply offended that attempted murder had become a more plausible explanation for its arrival than delivery.

"That one is mine," Lucius said from the armchair beside the fire.

He had refused the bedroom Narcissa prepared for him. The Healer's potion had left him colourless, his injured side bound beneath layers of linen, but he sat fully dressed with his cane resting against one knee. Only the blanket over his legs acknowledged that anything was wrong.

Sirius lowered his wand.

"Your owl has the manners of a Bludger."

"It is not my owl."

"Then I can dislike it without creating a diplomatic incident."

Harry watched from the bottom stair. He had slept for forty-three minutes, a fact the brass clock in his room had recorded with unnecessary precision. Whenever he closed his eyes, the Dark Mark returned above the stream. Sometimes it remained a skull; sometimes it lowered its head and became the enormous stone face in the Chamber. Once, the serpent emerged from its mouth with Nott's voice and Lucius's bleeding hands.

He had stopped trying shortly before dawn and lain beneath his blankets, listening to the cottage breathe around its unexpected guests. Pipes shifted, floorboards settled, and someone crossed the passage twice, pausing outside his door without entering. Sirius, probably. Possibly Draconia. Harry had been unable to bring himself to call out and discover which.

He entered the sitting room as Sirius released the window wards. Cold air slipped through the gap, carrying wet grass and woodsmoke. The owl swept inside, dropped all six papers on the carpet and landed on Lucius's chair. When Lucius offered it a coin, it bit him before accepting payment.

"Excellent judge of character," Sirius observed.

Narcissa entered behind Harry, her hair loose around her shoulders. The sleeve of her travelling dress had been repaired where three curses had torn it. Draconia followed in yesterday's clothes, holding her green satchel against her side. Hermione's gaze moved over her pale face and settled on the bag.

"Did you sleep?"

"Briefly."

"That was not a number."

"It was not intended as one."

Harry said nothing. Draconia looked at him, and the same silence passed between them.

Lucius unfolded the first newspaper. Its headline occupied half the front page.

TERROR AT THE WORLD CUP

Beneath it, smaller print announced:

DARK MARK RETURNS — FORMER DEATH EATER AT CENTRE OF ATTACK

The photograph showed Lucius beneath the green skull. It had been taken while Draconia held him, her face turned towards the camera above his shoulder at the instant the shutter moved. His torn sleeve exposed the faded mark on his arm, and blood covered one hand. In the moving picture, Draconia crossed the clearing, Lucius caught her, and the Mark burned above them both. Then the scene began again.

Narcissa took the paper from his hands. She did not tear it, though Harry thought that degree of restraint deserved recognition.

"Rita Skeeter," Hermione said.

The name appeared beneath the photograph.

"Is she the woman who described Sirius as Azka-ban's most eligible escapee?" Harry asked.

"In the same article that suggested I maintained my innocence through dangerous personal charm," Sirius replied.

"Did it work?"

"On the important people."

Remus took another copy before Sirius could demonstrate. They read in silence.

The article began with the fires. It described masked figures marching through the campsite, the Roberts family suspended overhead, and the Dark Mark appearing almost an hour later. Fourteen serious injuries, hundreds of damaged tents and an international investigation followed, with Minister Fudge promising a response that would be "swift, transparent and proportionate."

Nott's name appeared once, attached to a figure allegedly identified by several distressed minors. Harry received an entire paragraph.

Harry Potter, fourteen, claimed that his famous scar reacted painfully to the appearance of the Mark and that an unidentified voice had spoken inside his mind. Ministry experts caution that Mr Potter has previously reported unusual experiences during periods of extreme emotional distress.

"I did not say a voice spoke inside my mind," Harry said.

"You told Fudge you could feel him," Hermione replied.

"That is not the same thing."

"It becomes the same thing when someone requires you to sound unreliable."

Sirius held out his hand. Remus passed him the paper, and he read the paragraph once.

"Can I set her desk on fire?"

"No," Remus said.

"Her chair?"

"Also no."

"You discourage civic participation."

Lucius reached for another copy. Narcissa kept hold of the first.

"Rest."

"I am sitting."

"Less argumentatively."

Harry picked up a paper from the carpet and read the section beneath Lucius's photograph. Skeeter had carefully avoided saying he had joined the procession. Instead, questions occupied every place an accusation might have stood. Why had a former member of Voldemort's inner circle been present when the Mark appeared? Why had the attackers addressed him by name? Why had he concealed his own mark with a glamour, and why had his family been standing with Harry Potter when the Aurors arrived?

The rescue of the Roberts family received two sentences. Lucius had apparently intervened after the disturbance attracted Ministry attention.

Harry read the phrase again. He remembered the little boy turning in the air, one ankle caught while the rest of him fell, and Lucius walking towards the masks with his wand raised. There had been a moment when Harry had misunderstood that choice. He still felt ashamed of it. On the page, the same moment had been arranged so that thousands of strangers might misunderstand it forever. The paper crackled beneath his fingers.

"You were already bleeding before the Aurors arrived," Harry said.

"The journalist was not."

Lucius's voice remained calm, but his fingers tightened once on the blanket. Draconia saw the movement.

"They know what happened."

"Knowing and printing have never enjoyed a reliable relationship."

"Then make them correct it."

"A correction appears on page fourteen beneath advertisements for wart removal."

"Buy page fourteen."

Despite everything, Lucius almost smiled.

The Grangers came from the kitchen with tea. Mr Granger had changed into clothes transfigured from an old set of Sirius's, though the trousers remained too short and the jumper declared THE WEIRD SISTERS: LEGALLY UNDEAD across his chest. He set a cup beside Hermione.

"How bad?"

She gave him the paper. His expression changed as he read.

"They've made the person who helped us sound responsible."

"Ambiguity protects them from legal response," Lucius said. "A direct allegation can be challenged. Questions merely invite readers to accuse me on the writer's behalf."

"That's cowardly."

"It is journalism."

Hermione lowered her own copy.

"Those should not be synonyms."

Another owl struck the western window, and everyone turned. This one carried an official Ministry envelope tied to its leg. The wards held it outside until Remus had examined the seal; only then did he admit it. Lucius slid a finger beneath the fold and read the letter.

"Well?" Narcissa asked.

"The Department of Magical Law Enforcement requests my voluntary attendance at eleven."

"Requests?" Sirius repeated.

"The word provides comfort."

"You are not going."

Lucius looked at him. Sirius folded his arms.

"You can barely cross the room."

"A condition which may persuade them I did not spend the morning escaping internationally."

"Or they arrest you while you cannot defend yourself."

"They will not."

"Your confidence in the Ministry is touching."

"My confidence is in their fear of appearing incorrect twice in the same week."

He folded the letter. Draconia stepped between the chairs.

"I'm coming."

"No."

"I identified Nott."

"You are a minor recovering from an attack."

"So is Harry, and they printed his name before breakfast."

"That does not improve the argument."

"I saw Nott's ring. I heard what he said about the diary. If they question you without—"

"Draconia."

Lucius softened his voice, and she stopped.

"You will remain here."

"Father—"

"That is not negotiable."

The old authority entered the room with his words. Draconia straightened instinctively, lifting her chin as her expression emptied. Harry had seen her do the same whenever Madam Pomfrey discussed the Chamber within her parents' hearing. Her manners grew so perfect that no emotion could pass through them.

Lucius seemed to recognise it too. Regret crossed his face, but he held to his decision.

"Kingsley will attend," he added. "I will provide testimony. You will speak when the Ministry is prepared to treat your account as evidence rather than the distress of a child."

"They may never do that."

"Then I will make the alternative inconvenient."

She gave a precise nod.

"Of course."

Draconia returned to the window. Harry wanted to follow, but Narcissa reached her first. She made no attempt to touch her daughter; she simply stood beside her, facing the orchard, close enough that their sleeves met.

The third owl arrived at eight. By nine, there had been eleven. Two carried Ministry notices, three brought requests for newspaper statements, and others came from the Malfoy solicitor and the board of governors. The remaining envelopes had no return marks, and Remus placed them in a warded basin.

The first unfolded itself and screamed TRAITOR until Sirius set it alight. The second released a black liquid that tried to write Draconia's name across the table. The third held a small silver serpent with its head broken off.

Lucius studied it for a long time.

"Meaning?" Sirius asked.

"An old warning."

"I gathered that."

"The head is removed when a family is believed to have placed affection above obligation."

Harry glanced towards Draconia, who had not moved from the window.

"They make that sound like a bad thing."

Lucius met his eyes.

"They generally do."

Narcissa picked up the broken serpent and dropped it into the fire.

Winky woke shortly before Lucius left for the Ministry. The cottage had made her a small guest room between the pantry and the stairs, with a narrow bed, a washstand and a window overlooking the kitchen garden. A folded nightdress lay on the chair. Winky had chosen the floor beneath it, and Hermione found her there.

Harry heard them while carrying plates into the kitchen.

"The bed is clean," Hermione said.

"Winky is not permitted."

"Mr Crouch does not decide that here."

The elf made a frightened sound. Harry stopped at the doorway. Hermione sat cross-legged on the rug with her back against the bed, her hair escaping whatever attempt she had made to contain it. Winky had retreated beneath the washstand, Narcissa's cloak wrapped around her drawn-up knees.

"Winky is dismissed," she whispered.

"Yes."

"Winky has no family."

Anger flickered across Hermione's face, but she kept it out of her voice.

"That does not mean you have to sleep on the floor."

"Good elves is not using beds."

"Dobby used Harry's."

Winky stared at her. Harry entered before Hermione could develop the example.

"He jumped on it. I don't know whether that counts."

Hermione looked up.

"You were listening."

"The corridor was listening. I happened to be in it."

He set a plate within Winky's reach. It held toast, sliced apple and a boiled egg. Sirius had proposed bacon before Hermione explained that offering an exhausted creature food she considered improper might do little to encourage trust.

Winky looked at the breakfast.

"Is Master Crouch here?"

"No," Harry said.

Her face crumpled. She turned away before he could see the full force of her grief.

"Winky should not have let young master take the wand."

The words came quickly, and then the old secrecy binding caught her. Her hands flew to her throat. Hermione moved towards her, but Winky struck the underside of the washstand trying to retreat.

"Young master?" Hermione asked.

Winky shook her head violently.

"Mr Crouch's son died. He was sent to Azkaban after the war."

The elf closed her eyes, pain crossing her face so plainly that Hermione's next question never came.

Harry knew little of Crouch's family. He remembered a paragraph in one of Sirius's old newspapers: a celebrated official, a disgraced son, and a death that had allowed the father to continue his career alone. The story offered this as proof that Crouch placed justice above blood. Whoever had written it had never watched Winky grieve.

Harry crouched several feet away.

"You don't have to tell us."

Her eyes watered.

"Winky must not."

"All right."

"Winky is bad."

"Dobby said that a lot."

She became very still.

"Harry Potter knows Dobby?"

"He tried to keep me out of Hogwarts by sealing a barrier, stealing my letters and enchanting a Bludger."

Hermione glanced at him.

"You make friendship sound unusual."

"It often is."

Winky tightened her fingers around the cloak.

"Dobby is free elf."

"We don't know where he is."

"Dobby is always wanting freedom." Her mouth trembled. "Winky is not."

Hermione began to answer, but Harry caught her eye. For once, she stopped.

Narcissa appeared carrying folded fabric. She had spent part of the morning examining the old enchantments with Remus. Crouch's glove had ended his claim upon Winky's service; the separate oaths concealing his household's secrets remained. They were working through those carefully, Narcissa had told Hermione, because forcing them apart could hurt the person they bound. Winky shrank further beneath the washstand as she approached, but Narcissa lowered herself to the rug, her dress spreading pale around her. She moved less easily than Hermione, though she took equal care to remain at Winky's level.

"You need not decide anything today."

Winky peered at her.

"Mistress is offering work."

"I am."

"Winky cannot take wages."

Hermione's shoulders tightened. Narcissa continued before she could object.

"Then they will be placed into an account until you decide otherwise. You will receive clothing because this household does not require servants to announce their status upon their bodies. You may sleep wherever you choose, though I would prefer the bed because the floor is draughty."

She placed three simple dresses beside the breakfast: blue, brown and deep green. None resembled a tea towel.

"Winky is not knowing old Malfoy wards," the elf said.

"Neither do I, according to the number Nott has bypassed."

Winky blinked.

"We can learn them together."

Harry saw the offer change. Narcissa had left something unfinished, something Winky might eventually help shape, and she was waiting without demanding an answer. The elf crawled from beneath the washstand and reached for the green dress.

Hermione looked at Narcissa.

"An account?"

"You wished her to be paid."

"Yes."

"Then allow me to accomplish the objective without requiring her to become comfortable at your preferred speed."

Hermione absorbed the correction.

"All right."

Narcissa inclined her head. The agreement seemed to surprise them both, and Harry left before either found a reason to ruin it.

Hermione followed him into the passage and drew the door almost closed behind them.

"Young master."

Harry stopped.

"I heard."

"She said he took the wand. Not that he had taken it years ago. She was speaking about last night."

The care Hermione took to keep her voice quiet made the words more alarming.

"Crouch's son died in Azkaban," Harry said.

"That is what the papers reported."

Beyond the door, Winky had begun speaking softly to Narcissa, the binding turning every third word into a breath.

"We aren't asking her again."

Hermione's expression tightened.

"I know."

Harry could see what it cost her to leave the question unanswered.

They found Sirius in the kitchen, sorting Ministry messages into three piles labelled USEFUL, FUDGE, and PEOPLE PRETENDING NOT TO BE FUDGE. The last pile was the largest. He looked up when they entered.

"That expression usually precedes property damage."

Hermione repeated Winky's exact words. The humour left Sirius's face before she had finished.

"You're certain she said young master?"

"Yes."

"And that he took Crouch's wand?"

"The binding stopped her immediately afterwards."

Sirius pushed the letters aside.

"Bartemius Crouch Junior was sent to Azkaban at nineteen. They announced his death within the year."

"Did you see him die?" Harry asked.

"No. Different block." His gaze grew distant. "I heard him for months. Everyone did. He screamed for his mother until he no longer had the strength. Then one week his cell went quiet, and the guards said he was dead."

Hermione sat opposite him.

"Who identified the body?"

"The prison recorded it."

"That wasn't my question."

"No. It wasn't."

Sirius took the emergency mirror from his coat and called Kingsley. The Auror answered amid such noise that shouted orders broke through every pause. Sirius gave him the phrase without adding any interpretation: young master took the wand. Kingsley repeated it once, then fell silent.

"I'll retrieve the Azkaban death record," he said. "Do not question the elf again. If the binding belongs to Crouch, pressure may force her to injure herself."

"We know," Hermione said.

His eyes shifted towards her voice.

"Good."

"Will you question Crouch?" Harry asked.

"I will ask him to clarify whom Winky serves as young master."

"That isn't the same thing."

"It is what I can ask without showing him every conclusion before I have evidence."

The mirror darkened. Harry looked at his own reflection where Kingsley's face had been.

"Do you think he's alive?"

Sirius took a moment to answer.

"I think a frightened elf referred to someone as young master. And I think the Ministry has buried living men before."

He returned the mirror to his pocket.

"We tell no one else until Kingsley answers."

Hermione nodded. Harry thought of the empty seat beside Winky, the invisible footprints in the stream, and Crouch's fear when he saw his wand. The facts were drawing closer together, though they had yet to form an answer he could trust.

Lucius was ready to leave through the Floo at ten thirty, with Narcissa accompanying him. Sirius continued arguing until Lucius agreed that Remus could come as an independent witness. The compromise satisfied nobody and delayed their departure by sixteen minutes.

When the green fire vanished, Harry stayed by the empty grate. Draconia stood beside him with her arms folded.

"He'll come back."

"You cannot know that."

"No."

She glanced at him, perhaps expecting reassurance he could not honestly give. Harry kept looking at the coals.

"I can know they'll have to get through your mother, Remus and Sirius if they try anything."

"Sirius is here."

Behind them, the pantry door slammed. Their host's voice followed an owl through the kitchen, using language Mrs Granger pretended not to hear.

"Mostly here," Harry amended.

Draconia's mouth moved without quite becoming a smile.

"Father should not have gone."

"He would have looked guilty if he stayed."

"He may look guilty regardless."

"Then he went because he chose to."

Something in the words reached her, though Harry could not tell what. She tightened her folded arms and walked away.

The cottage spent the next few hours attempting normality. Shortly after noon, the Grangers returned home, with two Aurors sent ahead to examine their street and Kingsley arranging a temporary ward around the house. Hermione packed slowly, finding reasons to pass from room to room until her mother promised she could come back to the cottage before the Hogwarts Express.

She hugged Harry by the front door.

"Write if anything changes."

"You'll be back before the train."

"That was not the instruction."

"Yes, Hermione."

She looked towards Draconia.

"Both of you."

"I am capable of understanding plural directions."

"Your behaviour suggests otherwise."

Hermione hugged her too. Draconia stiffened briefly, then allowed it. Harry watched the Grangers leave through the gate, and the house grew quieter around their absence.

Messages kept arriving. Sirius heard from Ministry contacts and people who had once belonged to the Order. Kingsley reported that the Manor's wards were compromised in four places. Amelia Bones requested Harry's and Draconia's statements without the language Fudge had added, and Cedric wrote that Eleanor remained stable. Amos had apparently begun telling every Healer within reach that Harry had performed emergency magic while under attack.

Harry read Cedric's letter twice. Draconia read none of the messages. She sat at the kitchen table with the World Cup article folded beneath one hand, her fingers closing on it whenever the wards announced another owl.

At four, Lucius returned. The Floo threw him into the room; Narcissa caught one arm and Remus the other. Blood had spread through the fresh bandage beneath his robes. Draconia rose so quickly that her chair fell backwards.

"What happened?"

"He attempted to remain upright for three hours," Narcissa said.

"An aggressive administrative procedure," Lucius added.

His knees failed. Harry and Sirius cleared the sofa while Remus lowered him onto it. Narcissa opened his robes and swore with a precision Harry had previously associated only with her husband.

Draconia stopped several feet away. Sirius had summoned the medical case, Remus was cutting the bandage, and Narcissa pressed a potion to Lucius's mouth with an order to swallow. There was no task left for Draconia. Harry's attention caught on her stillness as she watched blood soak into the sofa. Her face became perfectly calm, and he found that harder to bear than a scream.

The wound had reopened beneath a restraint ward intended to prevent Apparition. It had pressed against every trace of foreign magic around Lucius, including the remnants of the curse that had struck him in the campsite. For three hours, it had worried at the damaged tissue while Ministry officials asked the same questions in different orders. He had told them what was happening. They had offered to pause once the statement was complete.

"Who questioned you?" Sirius asked.

Lucius lay beneath two blankets with his eyes closed.

"Dawlish. Proudfoot. A clerk who introduced himself three times and Fudge for approximately seven minutes."

"No Healer?"

"One examined me before the interview."

"And left you beneath the ward?" Narcissa asked.

Lucius opened his eyes.

"He advised against it."

"Name."

"Cissa."

"His name."

"The Healer or the person who ignored him?"

"Both."

Sirius pushed the medical case towards her.

"I like you."

"The feeling remains under review."

Remus completed the final sealing charm. Pale light travelled along Lucius's side and sank beneath the skin; the bleeding stopped.

"Did they believe you?" Harry asked.

"Belief was not the purpose."

"Then what was?"

Lucius turned his gaze towards him.

"To determine which version of my involvement created the least difficulty."

"And?" Sirius asked.

"I am presently a cooperative witness with a regrettable history."

"That sounds almost flattering."

"It means they may use my testimony while condemning my character."

Draconia stood beyond the rug's edge.

"Did you tell them about Nott?"

"Everything."

"The diary?"

"Everything relevant."

"What did they say?"

"That your identification will require independent confirmation."

"Harry saw him."

"Harry is also fourteen."

"Cedric saw his face."

"Briefly, at night, while duelling."

Her calm acquired a sharpness.

"So they intend to do nothing."

"They intend to search properties he abandoned months ago, circulate a description which omits his name and wait for him to attempt something sufficiently public that acknowledging him becomes less embarrassing."

Harry heard the answer beneath all those words.

"Nothing."

Lucius looked towards the ceiling.

"Yes."

Draconia picked up her chair, placed it beneath the table with exact care, and left through the kitchen door. Narcissa watched her go.

"I'll speak to her."

"No." Lucius tried to sit up, but pain stopped him.

Narcissa pressed a hand to his shoulder.

"You will remain horizontal until a qualified Healer says otherwise."

"She believes I dismissed her evidence."

"You did not."

"She heard the result."

"Which you cannot repair by bleeding through another sofa."

Sirius glanced at the stain beneath the blankets.

"It was an ugly sofa."

"It belonged to your mother," Remus said.

"Then I withdraw my concern."

Narcissa followed Draconia into the garden. Harry stayed near the hearth, and Lucius waited until the kitchen door had closed.

"Potter."

"I'm here."

"Did she sleep?"

Harry considered lying. Lucius would recognise it, and Draconia would resent it.

"Not much."

"Did she eat?"

"Hermione made her."

"Hermione has gone home."

"I noticed."

Lucius narrowed his eyes. Harry folded his arms.

"I can make toast."

"A formidable qualification."

"I'm also irritating enough to wait until she eats it."

"That I believe."

Lucius closed his eyes again.

"Do not allow her to mistake composure for obligation."

Harry stared at him. He sounded like a man who had spent years trying to explain something and had only now found someone he trusted to carry the meaning.

"Why don't you tell her?"

"I have."

"Did you use those words?"

Lucius remained silent.

"Right."

Harry went to make toast.

Draconia ate one piece beneath the pear tree and informed him that his butter distribution lacked discipline. He accepted the criticism as a success. Narcissa sat a few feet away on the stone wall, her shoes removed, a sight unusual enough that Harry made an effort not to stare. None of them discussed Lucius.

Winky entered the garden in the green dress. It hung loosely around her shoulders and reached her ankles. A corner of her tea towel protruded from a pocket she had sewn into the front, and she carried three cups on a tray.

"Winky is bringing tea."

Narcissa accepted hers.

"Thank you."

The elf stayed beside her.

"Is Mistress requiring anything else?"

"No."

Winky's ears lowered. Narcissa considered her for a moment.

"You might determine why the blue bedroom curtains insult anyone who attempts to open them."

The ears rose again.

"Curtains is insulting Mistress?"

"In French."

"Winky is investigating."

She disappeared with a crack. Harry looked at Narcissa.

"Do the curtains actually speak French?"

"Sirius's mother believed English lacked sufficient vocabulary for disappointment."

"Accurate," Sirius called from an upstairs window.

Narcissa raised her tea without looking back. Draconia's mouth curved, but the expression vanished when a barn owl passed over the orchard. It continued towards the village, and several seconds passed before her shoulders lowered.

Harry noticed. Narcissa saw him notice, and neither said anything.

Lucius slept through dinner. Once the Healer had arrived, Remus returned to his own house, promising to come back before they travelled to King's Cross. Narcissa stayed beside the sofa with a book open on her lap. In two hours, she turned three pages.

Winky cleaned the kitchen until Sirius persuaded her that the cupboards did not need alphabetising. She began polishing the door handles instead, and he surrendered. At the table, Draconia wrote letters while Harry sat opposite her. She addressed one to the Malfoy solicitor, another to Professor Snape, and a third to someone whose name she covered with her hand. Each page went into her green satchel.

"Drafts?" Harry asked.

"None of your concern."

"That means yes."

"It means none of your concern."

He returned to Cedric's letter. His reply described the attack but left out Nott's words about the diary; Cedric already knew enough to become a target. Harry thanked him for helping Eleanor, then crossed it out. Cedric would probably point out that Harry had done the same. He tried another opening.

Draconia folded a fourth sheet too firmly, tearing the parchment beneath her thumbnail. She stared at the small rip, then put the entire letter into the fire.

"Draco."

"Do not."

Harry leaned back.

"I haven't done anything."

"You were preparing to."

"Probably."

"Resist."

She gathered the remaining parchment and left. Harry listened to her cross the hall and climb the stairs. Sirius looked through the doorway.

"You possess a remarkable gift."

"She was already angry."

"And yet you nurtured it."

Harry threw his crumpled draft at him. Sirius caught it, read the first line and threw it back.

"Write what you mean."

"I did."

"You wrote, Thank you for your assistance during the disturbance."

"That is what I mean."

"You sound like a Ministry pamphlet."

Harry glared. Sirius sat opposite him.

"Try: Thank you for catching me every time Nott threw me into something."

"He only caught me twice."

"Accuracy improves gratitude."

Harry crossed out the stiff opening, turned the discarded draft over and began again.

Eleanor surviving matters more than Nott escaping. I know that, even when it does not feel true. Thank you for helping me choose correctly.

He paused with the quill above the page. Sirius waited without looking over his hand, and Harry added another line.

Also, you caught me twice. We will call it even if I catch you during the next disaster.

He signed it.

"Better," Sirius said.

"You weren't reading."

"Your face improved."

Harry folded the letter as the house settled around them. Lucius breathed unevenly in the next room. Narcissa turned another unread page, and somewhere upstairs Draconia crossed the same stretch of floor three times.

"Did you know?"

Sirius's chair creaked.

"About which disaster?"

"What the broken serpent meant."

"Roughly."

"Your family had things like that?"

"My family had a tapestry capable of burning people from itself. Subtle communication was not our strength."

Harry rolled the quill between his fingers.

"They called Lucius a traitor."

"Yes."

"Because he saved people."

"Because he chose which people mattered more than the rules he inherited."

Harry glanced towards the sitting room.

"Does choosing always cost something?"

Sirius took a while to answer.

"Usually."

"That seems unfair."

"It is." His voice softened. "The cost does not make the choice wrong."

Above them, Draconia's footsteps stopped.

Harry woke to breaking glass. Darkness filled his room, and he reached for his wand before opening his eyes. The wards still traced silver lines along the window frame. No alarm disturbed them, and the brass key lay warm against his chest.

Another sound came from below: the small, crystalline note of glass repairing itself. Harry left his bed. The clock read 2:18.

He crossed the passage barefoot. Sirius's door stood open, but his godfather was downstairs in the armchair beside Lucius, a wand loose across his lap. Narcissa slept at the other end of the sofa with one hand on her husband's blanket. Harry continued towards the kitchen, where moonlight divided the floor into pale rectangles and the last fire glowed in a red line beneath the grate. Every chair was empty.

The conservatory door stood ajar. Sirius insisted on calling it a conservatory, although it contained three dead plants, a cracked stone bench and windows too dirty to admit much sunlight. At night, the orchard pressed close against the glass, its branches shifting like hands along the walls.

Draconia stood by the potting table in a white nightdress beneath her open school robe. Her hair fell loose down her back, and one bare foot rested among the fragments of a drinking glass. Beneath her wand, the pieces dragged over the tiles and rose into the shape of a cup, meeting at the rim before sealing together.

She lifted it. Her hand shook, and the cup slipped, struck the table and broke again. For a moment, she only stared at the fragments.

"Reparo."

The pieces moved. One sliced across her finger on its way upwards, and Harry stepped through the door.

"You're bleeding."

Draconia spun, her wand pointing at his chest. They remained still for an instant before she lowered it.

"Leave."

Blood ran over her knuckle.

"I'm famously poor at following instructions."

"This is not amusing."

"I wasn't joking."

He stepped around the broken glass. Draconia moved between him and the table.

"I said leave."

"And I heard you."

"Then comprehension is the issue."

"Usually."

Her breathing changed. It remained quiet, but each breath stopped too high in her chest, and Harry recognised the effort required to make it appear ordinary. He set his wand on the stone bench.

"What are you doing?"

"Making it harder for you to accuse me of threatening you."

"I can accuse you of being intrusive without evidence."

"You often do."

He sat on the tiles beyond the glass, cold seeping through his pyjama trousers. Draconia stayed on her feet.

"You'll cut your feet," he said.

"The pieces are in front of me."

"There's one behind your heel."

She looked, and Harry used the moment to reach for a tea towel. Wrapping it around his hand, he gathered the larger fragments and placed them inside an empty plant pot. She watched without stopping him.

"You missed one."

He found it by the wall.

"Anything else?"

"Your technique was inefficient."

"I'll practise during the next emotional glass emergency."

She flinched, and he regretted the words at once.

"Sorry."

Draconia turned towards the window. Her reflection floated over the dark orchard: a pale face, loose hair, robes hanging from shoulders that had kept themselves straight since breakfast.

"I was thirsty."

"All right."

"The glass slipped."

"All right."

"There is nothing wrong."

Blood had reached her wrist.

"That one needs work."

She pressed her thumb against the cut.

"Go back to bed."

"I wasn't sleeping."

"That does not require my involvement."

"I heard you walking earlier."

"The floorboards are poorly fitted."

"They didn't make you cross the same bit three times."

She closed her eyes.

"Stop noticing."

The words were barely audible. Harry became still as her shoulders lifted with another unfinished breath.

"I can't."

"You manage with yourself."

He felt the truth in it more than the intended cruelty. Leaning against the stone bench, he looked up at her.

"Probably why I know what it looks like."

Her eyes opened. Moonlight caught the wetness she had kept from falling, and she turned away before he could acknowledge it.

"Father chose us."

Harry waited.

"I know."

"You keep saying that as though it makes everything better." Her voice sharpened. "It means they know what he chose."

"The people who sent the serpent."

"The people who stood in our drawing room when I was a child. The people whose children sleep in my dormitory. The people who know which wards protect the Manor and which Ministry officials Father paid and where Mother goes when she says she is visiting family."

Her words came faster, each following the last before she could stop it.

"Nott entered our home. He placed that book among my things while we were downstairs discussing how to find you. He used me to punish Father and nearly killed you instead. Now Father has declared himself in front of everyone Nott still knows, and the Ministry responds by locking him beneath a restraint ward until he bleeds."

"That wasn't your fault."

"Do not say that."

"It wasn't."

"You do not know what it was."

She turned towards him, anger breaking through the perfection of her expression.

"He changed because of me."

"Maybe."

The answer stopped her. Harry continued before he could think better of it.

"You changed because of him too. And Hermione changed because of both of us. Sirius changed because he got another chance. That's what people do."

"Not my family."

"Apparently your family does."

"You make it sound harmless."

"No." Lucius lay beyond the conservatory door with his wound newly sealed. "I think it hurts."

Draconia's expression twisted.

"He was bleeding."

"I know."

"They could have arrested him."

"I know."

"Mother walked into a fight without knowing how many people would recognise her."

"I know."

"Stop saying that!"

The shout struck the windows. Draconia covered her mouth and waited, but nothing moved in the passage. The cottage held the sound within the room. Harry stayed on the floor until she lowered her hand.

"When the Mark appeared," she whispered, "I thought it was him."

Harry knew whom she meant.

"Voldemort?"

She closed her eyes and nodded.

"For one second, I waited for my hands to stop belonging to me."

The confession brought the hospital wing back around Harry. He remembered Draconia beside his bed with the diary hidden beneath her robes, her face empty as Riddle took control. Her wand had flashed, followed by pain and darkness. Afterwards had come the writing on the wall.

Her body will lie in the Chamber forever.

He had gone alone because panic made every other option feel too slow, and he had found her beneath the stone face with Riddle standing over her.

Draconia opened her eyes.

"I could feel him looking through me again. I knew it was impossible. The diary is gone. You destroyed it. I watched it die." Her breath caught. "It did not matter."

Harry rose slowly, stopping beyond her reach.

"Draco."

"I remember hexing you."

"That was Riddle."

"I remember wanting you to stop asking questions. I remember being angry that you would not leave me alone. He used things which were already there."

"He made you hurt me."

"What if someone does it again?"

The first tear escaped. She wiped it away hard enough to redden her cheek.

"What if Nott takes me? What if Voldemort returns properly and discovers exactly how useful I am? What if I lead him to you or Hermione or my parents because I am still weak enough to become a door?"

"You aren't a door."

"You cannot promise that."

"No."

He moved a step closer.

"But I can promise that if someone tries to use you, we'll come."

"You came last time."

"Yes."

"You were twelve."

"So were you."

Her face collapsed. She tried to turn away, but the first sob bent her before she could finish the movement. Her hand caught the table, leaving blood on the wood. Harry reached her and took her wrist; she resisted at once.

"Don't."

"You're bleeding."

"I don't care."

"I do."

He took the tea towel from his own hand and wrapped it around hers. Draconia stared at his fingers, tears falling onto both their hands.

"I don't want to be used again."

Harry tied the cloth around her cut. Beneath the newspaper, the Malfoy name and the whispers waiting in Slytherin, he could see the girl who had woken in an underground chamber with part of her life missing. She had been twelve then. Nothing she had learned about appearing composed could undo what had happened to her.

"Then you don't have to be brave with me."

A broken sound escaped her, and her fingers closed on his sleeve. Harry understood the unspoken request and drew her against him.

For a moment, she remained rigid, her forehead pressed to his shoulder and one fist trapped between them. She seemed determined to endure even the embrace correctly, keeping her posture when everything else had failed. Then Harry placed a hand against the back of her head, and her knees gave.

He lowered them both to the tiles before she could pull him down. She gripped his shirt with her bandaged hand and cried against his chest, each breath torn from somewhere she had kept closed for too long. Harry held her without trying to quiet her. He could offer no promise large enough to make her safe, but he could stay.

At first, he worried about where to put his hands, whether he was holding too tightly or leaving her too much room to pull away. Then another sob shook her, and the awkward questions lost their importance. He steadied his back against the bench and let her weight rest against him. The tiles chilled his legs. Her fingers tightened whenever he shifted, so he stopped shifting.

Branches stirred against the windows. The kitchen fire settled into ash, and a pipe knocked twice overhead before falling silent.

Draconia tried to apologise. The word disappeared into his shirt.

"Don't."

"This is—"

"Allowed."

"I don't know how to do this."

"Neither do I." Harry rested his cheek against her hair. "Hermione would probably have a list."

Her broken laugh became another sob. Harry tightened his arms, and they stayed on the floor until her breathing began to slow.

Eventually, Draconia lifted her head. Her eyes were swollen, her face blotchy around the nose, and damp strands of hair clung to her cheeks. Without the composure she usually wore, she looked younger than Harry had ever seen her.

"If you tell anyone," she whispered, "I will deny everything."

"I'll say the curtains insulted you."

"In French."

"Obviously."

She wiped her face against his pyjama sleeve. Harry inspected the damp mark.

"That was deliberate."

"Yes."

"You're recovering quickly."

Her mouth trembled, this time almost forming a real smile.

Harry stood first and helped her from the floor, then cleaned the remaining blood from the table. Draconia repaired the glass once more. Her hand still shook, but the cup held, and he waited while she filled it with water and drank.

At the stairs, she stopped.

"Harry."

He looked back.

"Thank you."

The words seemed to cost her more effort than the tears. Harry gave her somewhere easier to take the conversation.

"Your butter criticism remains unforgiven."

She drew herself upright.

"Then tomorrow's lesson will begin at breakfast."

They climbed the stairs together.

Breakfast began with butter. Harry spread it over two pieces of toast while Draconia watched from the opposite chair. She wore a green dress with a high collar and enough composure to convince anyone who had not spent half the night holding her on the conservatory floor. Only a little swelling beneath her eyes remained.

Narcissa noticed at once. Her gaze moved to Harry, who concentrated on his toast.

"The corners," Draconia said.

"What about them?"

"You missed them."

"They're structurally unimportant."

"That is not how bread works."

He pushed a slice towards her.

"Demonstrate."

She took the knife. The lesson lasted several seconds and contained rather more criticism than the task required, but Harry listened seriously. When she finished, he inspected her work from both sides.

"Life-changing."

"Most education is wasted upon the unwilling."

Draconia ate the toast. Narcissa looked away, though Harry caught the relief in her face before she concealed it.

Lucius woke shortly afterwards. The potion had left him weak but clear-headed; he accepted tea, rejected porridge and lost both arguments to his wife. Draconia stood near the sofa. The room offered plenty of things to study—the mantel photograph, the Ministry letters, Winky trying to persuade a broom that it need not sweep while people were eating—but she looked at her father.

"I was frightened yesterday."

Lucius lowered his spoon. Harry became intensely interested in his breakfast, and Sirius attempted the same with less success.

"At the Ministry?" Lucius asked.

"Before. During. Afterwards."

He set the bowl aside, leaving the admission undisturbed between them.

"So was I."

Draconia's expression shifted. Harry had rarely heard either Malfoy parent acknowledge fear. They issued precautions, strengthened wards and moved their daughter behind them; the feeling itself seemed to belong in the architecture, safely concealed from conversation.

"You appeared very calm."

"I have had more practice appearing things than being them."

Sirius made a thoughtful sound. Lucius ignored it.

"I should not have dismissed you when you asked to attend."

"You said I was a child."

"You are."

Her face began to close.

"And," he continued, "you were a witness whose account deserved respect. Both things can be true."

Draconia looked towards Harry. He lifted one shoulder. Hagrid had told him before Hogwarts that people were usually more than one thing; the answer kept growing larger each time Harry thought he understood it.

She sat on the edge of the sofa. Lucius opened an arm, and she moved into it, resting her face against his shoulder with care for his wound. His hand closed over the back of her head. Near the window, Narcissa pretended to adjust the curtain, though her eyes shone.

Sirius carried his bowl into the kitchen, and Harry followed.

"Subtle," Sirius whispered.

"I learned from you."

"Tragic."

At the sink, he took the plate from Harry's hands.

"The cottage told me something broke last night."

Harry stiffened.

"It monitors the windows and glassware. Old security enchantment."

"Nothing got inside."

"I know."

"Then it's fine."

Sirius rinsed the plate.

"Is she?"

Beyond the doorway, Draconia remained beside Lucius, with Narcissa now seated next to them. All three occupied the sofa as though they had finally tired of inventing excuses for being close.

"Not yet."

Sirius nodded without asking what had happened.

"You can't make people safe. Took me an embarrassing number of years to learn that."

"That seems unhelpful."

"Most true things are." He set the plate aside. "You can give them somewhere they're allowed to be afraid."

Harry looked at him. Sirius touched the brass key beneath his shirt.

"You did that for me."

He left before Harry could answer.

The remaining days of summer passed beneath wards. Kingsley examined the Manor with three curse-breakers and found a narrow passage through the western boundary that had existed for at least twelve years. It responded to Nott's family signet and kept no record of use. Lucius ordered the entire network dismantled and rebuilt; Narcissa never asked what it would cost.

Kingsley's answer about Barty Crouch Junior arrived that evening. The Azkaban record contained a death certificate signed by the prison Healer and a burial entry, but no evidence that anyone outside the prison had viewed the body. Crouch claimed that Winky sometimes spoke of his dead son in the present tense when distressed. He refused further questions about a private bereavement.

Harry called Hermione through the mirror, and together they shared the report with Draconia before anyone else. She read it twice and asked precisely what Winky had said. She did not ask why they had allowed the elf to stop speaking. In the glass, Hermione bent over her notebook, copying every line.

"That proves nothing."

"It also disproves nothing," Harry replied.

Sirius placed both reports beneath an evidence charm and sent copies to Amelia Bones. The invisible person remained without a name.

"We return when it is secure," Narcissa said of the Manor.

The reconstruction outlasted the first promised days, and the Malfoys stayed at the cottage. Lucius objected each morning, usually falling asleep before he finished. Winky learned the house in less than a day. She negotiated with the French curtains, persuaded the pantry to reveal a hidden wine cellar, and discovered that the attic refused entry because it still considered Sirius disowned.

She and Sirius shouted at the ceiling for twenty minutes. The attic surrendered.

Harry wrote to Cedric, and Cedric replied. Eleanor woke on Wednesday and asked whether Ireland had won; seventeen scarves arrived before lunch. The Prophet printed Lucius's statement on page fourteen beneath an advertisement for wart removal. He cut it out and left it on the breakfast table without comment. Harry read it over toast whose butter reached every corner.

Fudge announced a World Cup Incident Review Panel. Sirius recognised four members as people who had attended his trial without objecting to the absence of a defence. Hermione sent six pages from home explaining why a panel selected by the office under review could not qualify as independent. Draconia read every page.

She began sleeping again, though badly. Twice, Harry heard her door open after midnight. Once, she found him on the stairs and sat beside him without a word. They stayed shoulder to shoulder until the dream loosened its hold enough for both of them to return to bed. Neither mentioned the conservatory, and neither seemed to need to.

On Thursday afternoon, Hermione returned through the gate with her parents and three trunks, although she required only one.

"Books," she explained.

Sirius stared at the smallest trunk.

"That one hissed."

"Defensive spell compendium."

"Naturally."

She hugged Harry, then Draconia, and drew back. Her gaze moved over Draconia's face, down to the healing cut on her finger, and finally towards Harry. He waited for the question.

"Your hair is dreadful," Hermione said.

Draconia touched it automatically.

"It is exactly as it always is."

"No. Sit down."

She guided her towards a chair and began repairing the loose braid. The questions went unasked, and Harry loved her a little for that. He barely recognised the thought before Hermione sent him to fetch a blue ribbon from her trunk. Finding the correct blue among eight apparently distinct possibilities kept him too busy to examine it.

Their Hogwarts letters arrived that evening. The book lists held nothing unexpected, although Sirius read Harry's three times in search of hidden Ministry requirements. Lucius examined Draconia's seal under magnification, while Hermione checked every title against the editions she owned.

"You purchased them last month," Harry said.

"I anticipated."

"What if they changed?"

"Then I would own additional books."

"A devastating outcome."

As departure approached, they packed in Harry's room because it had the most floor space. The cottage enlarged it twice before refusing further requests. Robes covered the bed, quills occupied the desk, and Hermione devised a system of three piles that Draconia immediately reorganised by weight.

Harry placed the bent shamrock clasp from the Cup inside the little wooden box that held his most important things. Draconia saw him do it and said nothing.

Near midnight, Hermione fell asleep against the foot of the bed, a Potions text open beneath one hand. Draconia labelled bottles beside Harry's trunk while he tried to pair his socks. Gradually, he discovered that none of them appeared to belong to the same family.

"You are doing that incorrectly," Draconia said.

"They're socks."

"They are different colours."

"Both are dark."

"One is black. One is blue."

"Feet cannot tell."

She took the socks from him. Harry leaned against the bed, shifting a pillow beneath Hermione's sleeping head without waking her.

"Draco."

"Mm?"

"When we get to school—"

She stopped labelling. Harry had yet to decide how to finish. Draconia would return to a dormitory filled with the children of people who sent broken serpents. Hermione would build a schedule so crowded that fear might struggle to reach her between lessons. He would sleep beneath the canopy where he had once listened to a basilisk moving through the walls.

Hogwarts had been an escape, a home and a battlefield, with the balance changing every year.

"You don't have to pretend it didn't happen."

Draconia looked down at the label between her fingers.

"Neither do you."

"That seems harder."

"Most true things are."

Harry stared at her.

"Sirius said that."

"Disturbing."

"You're becoming alike."

"Take it back."

Hermione stirred beside them.

"Why are you arguing?"

"Harry insulted me."

"Draconia compared herself to Sirius."

"I did no such thing."

Hermione closed her eyes again.

"Both of you go to sleep."

"Your authority is not recognised here," Draconia said.

"Then argue quietly."

She pulled the blanket over her head. Harry and Draconia looked at each other, keeping their laughter silent.

The girls went to their rooms shortly after one. Harry stayed on the floor beside his open trunk, trying to close the lid. Hermione's defensive spell compendium occupied the space usually reserved for shirts, and the trunk resisted every attempt to shut it. When he pressed down with both hands, the book hissed.

He stopped.

"I warned you," Hermione called from the passage.

"Go to sleep."

"Do not crush the spine."

"I'll crush something."

Her door closed. A few minutes later, Sirius appeared with a mug in each hand.

"Threatening books now?"

"It started it."

Sirius gave him a mug and examined the trunk. A corner of the compendium stuck out beneath Harry's winter cloak, its brass clasp fastened around the fabric.

"Compendium of Defensive Enchantments?"

"Hermione thinks school may be dangerous."

"Wild speculation."

He opened the clasp with his wand, removed three jumpers and laid the book flat beneath them. The trunk closed without resistance.

"You could have done that earlier," Harry said.

"Then you would learn nothing."

"I learned the book bites."

"A valuable education."

Sirius sat on the edge of the bed. With the packing finished, the room had returned to its ordinary size, the extra floor folding quietly into the walls. Above the desk, Harry's Quidditch poster hung beside the World Cup photograph Sirius had framed that afternoon. All three children wore green shamrock hats while Lucius disapproved in the background.

Harry drank from his mug. Hot chocolate, too much sugar, and no medicinal taste concealed beneath it. He took another mouthful simply because he could.

"Are you going to sleep tonight?" Sirius asked.

"Eventually."

"A time beloved by insomniacs."

"You're awake."

"I'm the responsible adult."

Harry looked at him.

"Temporary confusion," Sirius admitted.

He took a folded piece of parchment from his coat.

"McGonagall sent this with the school letters. It required a signature."

Harry accepted it. Most of the form covered Hogsmeade permissions, emergency medical treatment and acknowledgement that students must not enter the Forbidden Forest. The final section had been completed in McGonagall's narrow handwriting.

Student residence outside term: Hawthorn Cottage.

Legal guardian: Sirius Orion Black.

Relationship to student: Godfather.

Sirius's signature crossed the bottom. It resembled little of the careful script he used in letters; the first attempt had pressed hard enough to score the parchment. He had crossed it out and tried again. Harry ran a thumb over the words.

"You put the cottage."

Sirius's expression became cautious.

"That is where you live."

"I know."

"If you would rather use another name for it—"

"No."

Harry read the line again. Hawthorn Cottage. He had expected number four to remain on such forms forever, claiming him through nothing more than repetition, or perhaps some careful phrase about temporary accommodation pending Ministry review. Instead, McGonagall had written the name of this house.

It did not settle everything. Dumbledore still spoke in careful fragments about Lily's old protection, Privet Drive and the minimum number of nights it required. Sirius still argued over every one. Harry understood too little of the magic for a line on a form to resolve it, but the parchment answered a smaller question he had never known how to ask. When Hogwarts needed to know where he belonged outside term, it would be told Hawthorn Cottage.

He had chosen this house, even while older magic continued making demands of him.

"No," he repeated. "It's right."

Sirius's shoulders lowered.

"Good."

Harry offered the form back, but his godfather left it in his hand.

"Keep it."

"Hogwarts needs it."

"Copy."

Harry folded the parchment carefully and placed it in the wooden box beside the bent shamrock clasp. Sirius watched him.

"You know, most children object to paperwork as an expression of love."

"Most people aren't you."

"That sounded almost kind."

"Temporary confusion."

Sirius smiled. The quiet that followed felt different from the quiet before the Cup. There was no celebration waiting beyond it. Morning would come, the train would leave, and Sirius would return to rooms enlarged by the absence of the three children who had filled them over the past days. Harry's bedroom already seemed larger than he wanted.

"What will you do?"

"Develop interests."

"You don't have any?"

"I had several. Most were illegal."

"Remus will be disappointed."

"Remus was involved."

Harry smiled into his mug. Sirius turned his empty cup between his hands.

"I'll work with Amelia on the remaining Pettigrew evidence. Help Kingsley find whichever part of the World Cup report Fudge tries to bury. Finish the north fence."

"It has looked finished for weeks."

"Aesthetic deception."

"And?"

Sirius looked at him.

"Wait for you to come home."

Harry had no ready defence against that answer. He set his mug down.

"I will."

Sirius nodded. Neither pretended the promise could prevent another disaster. After three years at Hogwarts, Harry knew he might be hurt; Sirius might receive another owl before breakfast. Voldemort's shadow had already crossed a sky protected by half the Ministry. They could make no agreement that would control the future, but Harry could tell him where he intended to return.

He moved onto the bed beside his godfather. Sirius put an arm around his shoulders, and they sat beneath the moving photograph until the people inside began laughing again.

King's Cross smelled of hot metal, coffee and rain carried in on hundreds of coats. They arrived forty minutes early because Hermione had decided that the previous year's punctuality was no proof of a reliable pattern. With the Manor still being rebuilt, the Malfoys travelled with them, giving Lucius six opportunities to complain about using Sirius's Floo.

"We could have been late," Harry said after the fourth.

"I would prefer catastrophe."

"It's good to understand your priorities."

Winky had stayed at the cottage to supervise the last ward inspection. Before they left, she had pressed three parcels of sandwiches into Draconia's trunk and a fourth into Harry's.

"Winky is not knowing what Harry Potter eats."

"Anything," Narcissa had said.

"Often insufficiently," Lucius had added.

Harry had accepted the parcel without argument.

The station clocks showed ten twenty when they reached the barrier between platforms nine and ten. Commuters passed without noticing owls, trunks or families disappearing through brick. Sirius stood beside Harry with a hand on his trolley, and Remus joined them in a brown coat with repaired elbows. He carried no luggage of his own.

"You came," Harry said.

"Someone must prevent Sirius from boarding the train."

"I purchased a ticket," Sirius replied, producing a Muggle Underground pass.

Remus examined it.

"For Zone Two."

"They were unhelpfully specific."

Mr Granger laughed, and Lucius made an effort to look as though he had not.

Hermione crossed the barrier first with her parents. Draconia followed Narcissa, leaving Harry with Sirius and Remus until the crowd thinned.

"Ready?" Sirius asked.

Harry looked at the brickwork. Beyond it waited the scarlet train, the castle and another year of adults explaining the rules for managing danger only after he had broken them. Yet behind him was the first home he had ever expected to return to. Leaving it felt different from escaping number four, and he had still to discover how to do it.

"No."

Sirius smiled faintly.

"Good answer."

They crossed together.

Steam rolled across Platform Nine and Three-Quarters. Owls called over the noise, students leaned from carriage windows, and parents delivered advice that would mostly be forgotten by lunchtime. Beneath the iron arches, the Hogwarts Express waited, and Harry felt his chest begin to loosen.

Then he saw the newspapers. Lucius and Draconia's photograph appeared beneath several arms. Heads turned as the Malfoys passed, conversations lowering around them. Lucius noticed and kept walking, a hand on his daughter's shoulder. Narcissa took her other side.

Harry left his trolley with Sirius and moved beside Draconia before he had made a conscious decision to do it. Hermione joined them from the opposite direction. Together, the three reached the train.

"Subtle," Draconia said.

"I learned from Sirius," Harry replied.

"Tragic," Hermione added.

The platform watched them anyway.

Cedric found them near the luggage carriage, the cut above his eyebrow faded to a narrow pink line. Amos followed with a Hufflepuff scarf over one shoulder and stopped when he saw Lucius. An uncertain silence opened between them, and Cedric crossed it first, holding out his hand.

"Mr Malfoy."

Lucius looked at the offered hand, then took it.

"Mr Diggory."

"Eleanor's home. Her parents asked me to thank everyone." Cedric glanced towards Harry. "She remembers Ireland winning. Most of what happened afterwards is gone."

"Good."

"She also remembers you calling her an inconvenient time to stop breathing."

Harry considered this.

"That sounds possible."

Cedric smiled. Amos cleared his throat.

"Fine thing you did, Malfoy. The Roberts family."

Lucius looked deeply uncomfortable with the public gratitude.

"It required doing."

"Still." Amos offered his hand too. "Fine thing."

Lucius accepted it. Around them, several newspapers lowered. Nothing changed quickly enough for Harry to see, but he thought something might have begun.

The whistle sounded. Mrs Granger hugged Hermione, and Mr Granger reminded all three that emergency procedure nine did not justify inventing procedure ten without parental review. Narcissa adjusted Draconia's collar, then Harry's.

"There is food in your trunk."

"Winky gave me enough to survive until Christmas."

"Then do not return any of it."

Lucius approached after her. He no longer needed support to stand, though exhaustion left grey shadows beneath his eyes.

"Write if anyone troubles you regarding the article," he told Draconia.

"I can handle gossip."

"That was not the instruction."

She nodded. He turned towards Harry.

"Pay attention."

"I'm usually told I pay too much."

"Then continue irritating the correct people."

Harry smiled at what was almost affection.

Sirius waited until the others had finished, then offered him a small rectangular mirror. Harry recognised its twin inside his godfather's coat.

"The cottage wards will allow it through," Sirius said. "Hogwarts should too. If they do not, use the one Hermione charmed."

"That makes two emergency mirrors."

"Three," Hermione said. "I improved the first design."

"I feel extremely supervised."

"Excellent."

Sirius took his shoulder. Platform noise continued around them, but Harry's attention stayed on his face.

"You don't have to wait for an emergency."

Steam drifted across the mirror's glass.

"What else would I call about?"

"Quidditch. Homework. Malfoy committing murder over your socks."

"Reasonable murder," Draconia said.

Sirius's grip tightened once.

"Anything."

Harry met his eyes.

"All right."

"Convincing."

"I'll call."

Sirius pulled him into a hug, and Harry returned it without checking who watched.

For a moment, he could see nothing beyond his godfather's coat. Steam dampened the wool at Sirius's shoulder, and the familiar pressure of his hand settled between Harry's shoulder blades. Around them, trunks rattled over the platform and someone called for a child who had wandered ahead. Harry tried to remember the feel of being held here, plainly and without hurry, even while the train urged them apart.

"Next Cup," his godfather murmured.

"Next Cup."

The second whistle forced them apart. Harry boarded with Hermione and Draconia, looking back once at the people standing in the steam. He had once counted everyone who came to see him leave. Now the platform was crowded with reasons to return.

They found a compartment halfway along the train. Hermione claimed the seat facing forwards, Draconia set the food basket between them, and Harry stowed the trunks overhead before sitting by the window. London began to slide away, and for five minutes they behaved like ordinary students.

Hermione reviewed a timetable she had constructed without knowing the timetable. Draconia opened Winky's sandwiches and criticised the tea. Harry watched brick walls give way to gardens, then fields still wet from the rain.

The door opened. Pansy Parkinson stood in the corridor with Daphne Greengrass. Theodore Nott was absent, though Harry felt that absence almost as strongly as if he had been standing behind them. Pansy looked over Harry and Hermione before turning to Draconia.

"Is it true?"

Draconia lowered her cup.

"You will need greater precision."

Pansy's mouth tightened.

"Your father attacked them."

Nobody asked whom she meant.

"Yes."

Draconia offered no qualification. Pansy glanced along the corridor, where several students had slowed beside their door.

"My mother says he humiliated every old family in Britain."

"Your mother has always possessed an ambitious definition of herself."

Daphne looked down, hiding a reaction. Pansy flushed.

"People are saying your family has chosen Potter over its own kind."

Hermione became still beside Harry. Draconia looked at neither of them.

"People are correct."

The corridor fell silent. Harry watched her profile, remembering how she had broken in his arms only a few nights earlier because this same choice frightened her. Now she offered it to everyone as a fact, with all the fear still somewhere beneath it.

Pansy stared.

"You cannot mean that."

"I generally do."

"Draconia—"

"If your friendship depends upon my father permitting Muggles to be tortured, Parkinson, you have misunderstood its value."

Pansy stepped backwards as though the answer had pushed her. Daphne stayed at the door, her gaze meeting Draconia's.

"My father said Lucius did the right thing."

Pansy turned towards her.

"You didn't tell me that."

"You didn't ask."

Daphne entered just far enough to take a sandwich from the basket.

"Welcome back."

Then she left, with Pansy following without another word. Movement resumed in the corridor. Draconia picked up her tea, and her hand remained perfectly steady. Harry could see what that steadiness required.

He reached for a sandwich.

"Your kind?"

"Apparently."

"Do I have to learn the handshake?"

Hermione answered before Draconia could.

"There will not be a handshake."

"You haven't seen procedure ten."

"There is no procedure ten."

"That sounds like poor preparation."

Draconia laughed, and the tension broke.

By afternoon, rain covered the windows. Cedric visited with Cho, holding up Harry's letter before he sat down.

"Thank you for your assistance during the disturbance?"

Harry stared.

"I crossed that out."

"The impression remained."

"Sirius said I sounded like a Ministry pamphlet."

"Sirius was right."

"This friendship is becoming hostile."

Cedric's smile softened.

"The second attempt was better."

Harry remembered the line about saving Eleanor mattering more than pursuing Nott. It had been difficult to put on the page, and it felt more exposed with Cedric sitting opposite him, the folded letter still in his hand.

"You still don't owe me," Cedric said.

"You caught me twice."

"And you kept a first-year breathing."

"Then you owe me."

"That is not how gratitude works."

"It is how arithmetic works," Hermione said, without lowering her book. "Two catches against one airway spell leaves Cedric ahead by one intervention."

Cho considered them.

"Do all of your friendships require accounting?"

"Only the healthy ones," Harry replied.

Draconia poured Cedric tea.

"He is attempting to avoid saying he was frightened."

Harry turned towards her.

"Whose side are you on?"

"The side capable of recognising subtraction."

Cedric accepted the cup.

"For what it's worth, I was frightened too."

The admission settled between them so simply that Harry found he could leave it there. He nodded.

"We're still even."

"We can revisit it during the next disaster."

"That was my suggestion."

"I improved it."

Cho laughed. Hermione finally lowered her book, and within ten minutes she was challenging Cedric's explanation of Krum's final dive. Draconia defended him on principles that appeared to change whenever Bulgaria benefited.

Neville came by later with a cactus his uncle had sent from Tunisia. It tried to bite Draconia and immediately regretted doing so. Neville rescued the pot before she could discover whether the plant resisted fire.

"Gran read the article," he said, settling it on his knees. "She says the Ministry wouldn't recognise courage if it filed the proper paperwork."

Draconia blinked.

"Your grandmother said that?"

"Not exactly those words." He glanced towards the corridor. "But she said your father did the right thing. So did mine and Mum's old Healers."

Harry watched the unexpected support reach Draconia. She had answered Pansy's contempt without hesitation, but she needed a moment to find a reply for Neville.

"Thank you."

He nodded, relieved to have finished the difficult part.

"You still can't set the cactus on fire."

"Then remove it from biting distance."

The sweets trolley arrived soon afterwards. Harry bought enough for everyone and sent a packet of Drooble's Best Blowing Gum to Pansy without a note.

"Why?" Hermione asked.

"Confusion."

"That is not diplomacy."

"It prevented me sending Dungbombs."

"Growth," Draconia said solemnly.

They played Exploding Snap until Hermione acquired soot on her eyebrows and refused to acknowledge it. Draconia lost three games, accused the deck of ideological bias, and demanded another round. Harry laughed hard enough to hurt his healing ribs. For once, he found the pain worth it.

Later, Draconia fell asleep beside him. Her head lowered slowly onto his shoulder, and Harry became very still beneath the unfamiliar weight. Strands of her hair brushed his jaw whenever the train moved. She smelled faintly of the lemon soap Narcissa had packed and the smoke no washing charm had entirely removed from their clothes. One hand had curled around the fabric near his elbow before sleep overtook her. He could not tell whether she had meant to hold on, but he knew he would not be the first to move.

Hermione looked over her book. Harry waited for a comment, but she returned to the page. A minute later, her shoe pressed against his beneath the table, with enough care that he knew it was deliberate.

He glanced at her. She continued reading, although the page remained unturned. The touch might have been a question, an answer or an accusation; Harry could make no sense of it. He left his foot where it was.

Rain filled the window as the train carried them north. Evening gathered over the hills, turning the compartment glass into a dark reflection. Harry could see the three of them there: Draconia asleep against him, Hermione reading with her foot touching his, and his own face suspended between theirs.

His gaze returned to Draconia first. In sleep, she looked younger, free of the expression she wore for Slytherin, newspapers and families who sent broken serpents through the post. He remembered her crying against him in the conservatory and felt the quiet pressure of her hand on his sleeve. He looked away only when Hermione finally turned the page.

Beyond the reflection, the castle waited. Hogwarts had once meant escape because Harry believed home was somewhere danger could never reach. He knew better now. Home was the place where fear could show itself and someone would choose to stay.

The first distant lights appeared through the rain.
