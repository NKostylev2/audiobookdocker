The voice came from the bundle, high and cold, and Harry's scar blazed. He knew that voice. For a moment he could hear nothing else, not even Cedric calling his name. Pain drove him onto one hand, his fingers sinking into the wet grass, and the graveyard blurred around him. Two moons swam above the yew tree. A row of crooked headstones seemed to lean towards him before he blinked and forced them apart.

"Harry?"

Cedric caught his sleeve. Harry tried to answer, but his stomach was still turning from the journey. The Portkey's cold clung to him, deep in his bones. Behind them, the Triwizard Cup lay on its side between two graves, blue light spilling over names almost worn away by rain. A broken line of Flitwick's runes flickered along one handle. The bracelet had failed, but something of the charm had caught in the Cup's magic. Hogwarts. It might still take them back to Hogwarts. Harry needed to reach it before he found out whether might was good enough.

Cedric drew his wand and stepped in front of him. The rope joining their belts pulled tight; Harry could feel his friend's weight through it. Blood had already soaked through the bandage on Cedric's thigh, but his wand stayed level.

"Who's there?"

Three figures moved into the moonlight. Peter Pettigrew carried the bundle. He looked thinner than he had in the Shrieking Shack, his cheeks hollow beneath damp strands of hair. One hand held the bundle carefully against his chest. The other gripped a wand so hard that his knuckles showed white. Harry's stomach tightened. He had imagined seeing Pettigrew again, but never like this, with Cedric bleeding beside him and Hogwarts gone.

Barty Crouch Junior stood to Pettigrew's left. Without Moody's face, he looked narrow and restless, his mouth twitching as though he could barely keep himself from smiling. Black cloth covered one empty eye socket. His other eye never left Harry. The staff he had carried around Hogwarts rested against a nearby headstone, no longer needed now that he had two sound legs. Mr Nott waited beside a cauldron. His robes were clean except at the hems, and the black ring Harry remembered from the World Cup shone on his finger. Behind him, steam rose from a great iron pot balanced over a fire. The flames caught the silver in his hair whenever he moved.

"Potter," he said. "At last."

Cedric edged back until his shoulder touched Harry's. His breathing was quick, but he was trying to keep it quiet. Harry knew that effort. He was making it himself.

"The Cup," Harry whispered. "We go for it together."

"On three?"

Harry nodded. Cedric shifted his good foot, and the cord at their belts tugged. It was barely a movement, but Pettigrew saw it.

"Master—"

Something stirred inside the bundle. The cloth slipped, revealing a head scarcely larger than a baby's, though nothing about the face belonged to a child. The skin looked pale and wet. Red eyes opened above a flat nose and a thin mouth. Harry forgot the count.

"Kill the other one," the creature said.

Crouch raised his wand. Harry hit Cedric with his whole weight. They fell together as green light flashed over their heads and struck a marble angel. Its head burst apart. Fragments hammered the ground around them, and Cedric cried out as his wounded leg caught beneath him. Harry was already reaching for his wand. He cut the rope between their belts before Cedric could get up.

"What are you doing?"

"Sorry."

He pointed at the grass beneath Cedric.

"Vincula!"

The spell was Cedric's. They had practised it together, laughing when Harry's first attempt tied a chair to its own legs. Now the grass sprang up around Cedric's wrists and chest. He struggled, startled, and the cords tightened, dragging him behind the broad base of a gravestone.

"Harry!"

"Protego!"

Silver light ran down the cords. The grass thickened into roots, climbing over the monument and knitting together around Cedric. Harry felt the spell take hold of him, a sharp pull beneath his ribs. He could not tell how he had changed it. He only knew that the barrier stayed up when he held on, and began to sag when he let himself breathe too deeply. Cedric stared through the gaps.

"Let me out."

Harry shook his head.

"You can't fight them on your own!"

"Stay down. Please."

Crouch moved to one side, finding an angle past the stone.

"Avada Kedavra!"

The curse struck the roots. The shield charm did not stop it; the wood took the blow, blackening and splitting before the green light could reach Cedric. Harry felt the impact as though someone had driven a fist through his chest. He stumbled, and his magic seized the life behind the barrier. For one sickening instant, two heartbeats struck together beneath his ribs.

Then there was only one.

The roots closed. Silver faded to a dull, pale sheen, and Cedric disappeared behind a tangle of wood pressed against the gravestone. Harry stared at the place where his face had been.

No. No, he had not meant—

A faint beat answered beneath his own. Harry nearly buckled with relief. Cedric was alive. He could feel him, hidden so deeply that he had to stop searching before the others noticed what he was doing. Nott swept his wand over the barrier. Violet light passed through the roots, then sank into the ground. Harry held still. The second pulse pressed against his, trying to find its own rhythm, and he buried it beneath the frantic pounding of his heart. The violet light went out.

"Dead," Nott said.

Crouch lowered his wand. He looked at the sealed roots for another moment, then turned to Harry, satisfied.

"He's protecting the body," Nott added. "Leave it. He's only tiring himself out."

"Leave him. Take Potter."

"He changed the spell," Crouch said. There was wonder in his voice. "Did you see?"

"I saw."

"A great deal of effort for a corpse," Nott said.

"Careful, you fool!"

"Forgive me, Master!"

"He looks breakable," he said.

They collided hard. Harry could smell the man's breath. He drove his forehead into Crouch's nose and heard something break. "Accio Cup!"

The Cup lifted. Hope struck so hard that Harry reached for it before it had travelled a foot. Black fire wrapped around the handles and slammed it back into the grass. Nott stood between them.

"You're not leaving."

Harry's eyes flicked past him. The cauldron was leaning. Nott's fall had cracked one of the stones beneath it, and dark liquid was spilling over the rim. Pettigrew saw it too.

"The potion!"

Nott turned. Harry's Blasting Curse caught him between the shoulders and threw him against the pot. He grabbed the rim to stop himself falling in, but one sleeve plunged into the liquid. Cloth dissolved. Nott screamed and jerked his arm free, his skin blackened from wrist to elbow. Harry ran. The Cup was ten feet away. Then eight. Out of the corner of his eye, Harry saw Crouch snatch up his staff and twist it open. A wand slid from the hollow wood. Harry reached for the blue handle—

"Crucio!"

His legs vanished beneath him. The world became pain. Harry struck the ground and could not feel the impact through what followed. Every muscle seemed to pull in a different direction. The Cup lay beside his face, close enough to reflect him, but he could no longer make sense of the hand he needed to reach it. Behind him, Crouch held the spare wand. Harry saw it for a moment between two flashes of white, then lost it again. Cedric's heartbeat stumbled inside his chest. Harry clung to it. He had no spell left in his head, no words, only the need to keep the pain away from the boy behind the roots.

Not him.

When the curse stopped, Harry could not stop shaking. His fingers scraped the grass as he tried to move them. Crouch came closer, the lower half of his face covered in blood, his injured hand held against his chest.

"Still fighting?" he whispered.

Harry rolled onto his back and kicked his knee. Crouch fell. Harry reached for the Cup. A shoe came down on his hand. He gasped. Pettigrew leaned over him, pressing harder until Harry's fingers sank into the mud.

"You should have listened in the Shack."

Harry looked up into the face of the man who had handed his parents to Voldemort. For a moment, the pain in his hand mattered less than that.

"I did," he said. "That's why we knew you were lying."

Pettigrew hit him. Harry's cheek split against his teeth, and blood filled his mouth.

"Enough," Nott said.

Hands rose from the earth and caught Harry's arms and legs. He fought them, but they dragged him backwards, away from the Cup, his heels cutting useless lines through the grass. "Holly," he murmured. "James said—"

"Don't."

Pettigrew glanced up. Harry spat blood beside his shoes.

"You don't get to say his name."

"Wormtail. The cauldron."

"The dead leave inheritance. Let the father surrender what remains."

The bone dropped into the pot. White light spread through the liquid, and steam rolled over Harry's shoes. "Ask him for mercy."

Harry looked past him at the cauldron.

"Ask him yourself."

The hidden heartbeat lurched. Silver flared around Cedric's grave. Crouch glanced towards it.

"Still holding on?"

"Good," Crouch whispered. "He likes a fight."

He set the knife on the gravestone, took the bowl in his uninjured hand and carried it to Nott. Nott's fingers trembled as he accepted it. Harry noticed that through the pain. For the first time since they arrived, the man looked as though something mattered more to him than keeping his robes clean.

"Blood taken from the enemy," he said. "Let hatred return what death refused."

"You always are."

Harry backed away. The water beneath his feet went black and still, showing pictures where his reflection should have been. He recognised Hermione's street in Oxford. Cars stood along the pavement, shining with rain. In an upstairs room, she sat at her desk with her hair badly tied back, unaware of anything outside the window. Another picture showed Malfoy Manor. Peacocks crossed the gravel beyond the gates. Draconia passed through the wards, her bracelet catching the light. Harry almost reached for her.

The house numbers were blurred. The Manor grounds ended in a haze beyond the gate. He could feel Voldemort waiting, letting the familiar things draw him in. If Harry looked closely enough, if he thought of the right door or the turn in the road, the missing pieces would come with it.

"Where do they live?" Voldemort whispered.

Mr Granger appeared with car keys in his hand. The number on the front door began to sharpen. Harry looked away. The Manor gates opened. Coloured light passed through the wards, and he remembered Narcissa beside him, remembered watching them change to admit her. Voldemort pressed closer.

"Show me."

Harry could not forget the houses. Trying only made him see them more clearly. He thought of something else instead: Hermione passing him toast, Draconia pouring milk into his tea, the two of them arguing about rule nine on the train. The Grangers' front door opened into the Hogwarts library. Harry held on to the mistake and made another. The Knight Bus rattled through the Manor gates. Hedges from Privet Drive grew through the Slytherin common room. Sirius stood in the Chamber with a kettle, complaining that someone had hidden the tea. Voldemort tore the pictures apart.

Harry gave him more. Ink on Hermione's fingers, without the desk or the window. Draconia laughing, without the room around her. A chessboard, a warm green stone, three sets of footsteps in a school corridor. He kept the people and muddled everything that could lead to their doors. The house number blurred. The Manor wards flickered in an order he knew could never work.

"You cannot hide them all."

Harry drove his heel into the dark water. The pictures broke beneath it. He fell into Azkaban. Sirius sat behind bars, thin and hollow-eyed again. Grey ran through his hair. A Dementor passed beyond the cell, and the cold followed it into Harry's chest.

"You called me," Sirius said.

Harry went to the bars. "I'm here."

"You asked for help. I came."

"Sirius—"

"That's why I'm dead."

The bars opened. Sirius fell forwards, and Harry caught him. He was terribly heavy. Harry sank to his knees with the body in his arms, one hand trapped beneath a shoulder already cold through the coat. He knew it was false. He knew where Sirius was. None of that stopped him looking at the closed eyes, waiting for them to open.

"Is this what you wanted?" Voldemort asked.

Harry held Sirius more tightly. His hair brushed Harry's wrist. There was the scar on his knuckle from training, exactly where Harry remembered it. Beside it shone a silver ring. Harry stared. Sirius did not wear rings. He complained when a watch caught on his sleeve. He would have been unbearable about a ring getting in the way every time he worked on the motorbike. A laugh escaped Harry, cracked and frightened.

"You got it wrong."

The body opened red eyes. Harry pushed it away. Azkaban split around them, and Voldemort drove deeper. Pictures hit too quickly for Harry to turn from them. Vernon's fist hammered a locked door. Lily screamed. A dragon's claw closed around Draconia instead of him. Hermione fell from the Astronomy Tower, her books spinning into the air after her. Remus tore through Sirius's chest. Narcissa opened a cupboard and found Dobby hanging inside. Lucius knelt before Voldemort and lifted his wand towards his daughter. Cedric lay dead behind the roots. Harry tried to stop that picture. Voldemort held it there.

He could feel the other heartbeat, faint but real, and tried not to think about where it came from. He must not look at Cedric inside his own mind. Not while Voldemort was searching it.

"You know what happens to people who love you," the voice said.

Harry pulled away. It followed.

"Your family hid you. Your friends get hurt. Sirius lost twelve years because he trusted your parents."

Hawthorn Cottage appeared. Its door stood open, blood running across the threshold.

"No."

The key grew hot against Harry's real chest. The picture sharpened. Sirius lay beside the fireplace, Remus beneath the window. Hermione was on the stairs. Draconia stood at the end of the hall in black robes.

"You made him choose," she said. "My father died because of you."

Harry could not get air. Her voice was right, every pause, the way she said his name when she was trying not to show how badly he had frightened her. Behind it came another memory: her hand against his cheek, how close she had been, how her expression changed when he thought of Hermione. Voldemort found it. Harry felt the pleasure before he heard the words.

"Two of them."

He tried to shut the memory away.

"How kind of you, Harry."

Harry threw everything he could find between Voldemort and the girls. The corridor where they had fought the troll. Books piled on a library table. Hermione writing Love from beneath a letter; Draconia writing Potter as though she could hear him arguing with it. He buried the Grangers' street and the Manor gates beneath years in the cupboard, Quidditch scores and potion ingredients, facts he had forgotten he knew. Voldemort ripped through them.

Harry remembered the basilisk's fang. Not a bite—he had never been bitten—but the size of it in his hand, the point that had gone through the diary. He imagined it entering his own arm. He gave the pain everything he knew: bones growing back in the hospital wing, Crouch's knife, the crushed fingers beneath Pettigrew's shoe. He made it hurt. Voldemort recoiled. Harry came back to the graveyard screaming. The ropes were still around him. His throat felt torn, his shirt was soaked, and for a moment he could not tell whether his friends were alive. He looked wildly past Voldemort, searching for something that had not been inside the vision.

The cauldron. The yew tree. Lucius's pale hair behind a mask. Cedric's heartbeat. Voldemort had stepped back. The calm had gone from his face.

"You dare attack me?"

Harry swallowed blood.

"Had to."

The next curse cut from his temple to his jaw. His head jerked sideways, and one eye filled with blood.

"Where do they live?"

Harry tried to focus on the pale wand.

"Who?"

Pain slammed through him. The roots over Cedric flickered, and something moved through the spell, a small shift of weight that Harry could feel but could not see. He held on until the movement stopped. Voldemort lowered his wand.

"Sirius Black. Where is his house?"

Harry could not feel the ends of his fingers.

"Not sure."

"You carry a key."

"Might be a shed."

A cut opened his side. He screamed before he could stop himself. Voldemort pressed the wand to his chest, and the black lines beneath his skin joined, spreading over his ribs and up his throat.

"Where?"

Harry looked beyond him. Lucius was still beside the graves, still waiting. Harry could see only his eyes through the mask.

Wait.

"Belgium," Harry whispered.

Voldemort stared at him.

"What?"

Harry tried to smile.

"The flour went there."

The curse struck before he could manage it. After that, Harry lost pieces of the night. He remembered the cold stone against his back and the smell of his own burned shirt. He remembered trying to raise his head before Voldemort could force it up. Between curses, every small sound began to mean something: robes brushing the grass, a wand sliding over cloth, Crouch drawing breath as though he could hardly wait for the next spell.

There were questions about Hogwarts, about Dumbledore and Sirius, about the Malfoys. Voldemort asked how Harry had destroyed the diary. He asked what Harry saw when his scar hurt, and whether Harry could see into his mind as easily as he could see into Harry's. Harry lied when he could. When he could not find a lie, he said nothing. The silence always hurt more. Nott tested the roots whenever Harry refused to answer, his wand sending thin black light against the wood. Each blow pulled through the spell into Harry's chest. He learnt to brace for it, though he could never stop himself flinching.

The Death Eaters stood behind their masks. One shifted each time Harry screamed. Another leaned forward, watching. Most kept very still, frightened of attracting the wand that was turned on him. Harry knew that stillness from classrooms, though he had never thought he would see it here, among grown men who had chosen to come. Lucius stayed three graves from the Cup. Harry could not keep looking at him. Each glance had to be hidden inside something else: his head dropping after a curse, his eyes following Voldemort around the circle. Lucius never moved, but he never looked away for long either.

Harry counted the roots over Cedric when he could see them. Nine. Then seven. Then five. As each one faded, something in him grew harder to do. His hands went cold. Lifting his head took longer. Drawing breath began to feel like work he might not finish. For a while, dying seemed quieter than staying. The thought frightened him less than it should have. There would be no next curse, no waiting for a question, no need to keep his mind closed while someone searched for everyone he loved. He would not have to hurt any more. Then the other heartbeat pressed weakly beneath his own.

Cedric was still there.

Harry dragged air into his lungs. He could not stop. If he stopped, the roots would fall, and Cedric would be alone in the graveyard. Voldemort came close enough for Harry to see the thin veins beneath his skin.

"You can let go."

Harry's lips barely moved.

"You first."

The curse took him again. When it ended, Voldemort let him hang in silence for so long that Harry began to hope he had grown tired of it. Then the wand lifted beneath his chin.

"What happens if you get home?"

Harry tried to make sense of the question. Blood dripped from his face onto the grass.

"People shout."

"After that."

"Hospital wing."

"And then?"

Harry had no answer ready. Home was too far away. He could picture Sirius reaching for him, Pomfrey telling people to stand back, but the days beyond that belonged to someone who had survived this night. He did not yet know whether that person would be him. Voldemort touched the wand to his temple. The Great Hall rose around the graveyard. Stone walls enclosed the headstones. The yew tree reached into the enchanted ceiling, its branches black against the stars. The House tables stood where the Death Eaters had been, and everyone Harry knew sat watching him. Cedric lay at his feet. Dead. The roots were still around him, bright and useless.

"You came back alone," Voldemort whispered.

Amos Diggory stood at the Hufflepuff table. He looked from his son to Harry, and Harry wished he would shout. The quiet was worse.

"You left him."

"I didn't—"

"You saved yourself."

Mrs Granger was beside him now, her face white.

"We trusted you, Harry."

Narcissa held Draconia close against her side.

"We let you into our home. Look what happened."

Harry turned away. Hagrid would not meet his eyes. McGonagall took off her spectacles. Remus looked tired and old, his disappointment harder to bear than their anger.

"You should have known better," he said.

The doors opened. Sirius walked into the Hall with the brass key in his hand. He laid it on the staff table. Harry felt for the real one against his chest and could not find it inside the dream.

"I gave you somewhere safe," Sirius said. "You brought him there."

Voldemort entered behind him. Neville fell first. Luna was beside the Ravenclaw table. Fleur tried to shield Gabrielle; Viktor reached for a wand he never had time to raise. Green light flashed, and each body struck the floor with a sound Harry could not shut out. Hermione stood at one end of the Hall. Draconia stood at the other. Harry tried to run. Cedric's body was in the way, and when he stepped around it, the distance grew. He called the girls' names. Both turned towards him. Voldemort killed them together.

"Stop."

Harry heard the word leave his real mouth. The pleasure that answered it did not belong to anyone in the Hall.

"Ask properly."

He stood among the bodies with Cedric at his feet. Every face was turned towards him. Even the dead seemed to be waiting for an explanation, and he had none that could make it right.

You lost.

The thought sounded like his own.

You weren't strong enough.

Dumbledore rose behind the staff table.

"I was afraid of this, Harry."

Harry nearly believed him. Voldemort had found the fear Harry took everywhere, the one that made him apologise before asking for anything. Be useful. Get it right. Do not need too much. For years at Privet Drive, he had learnt that people could decide they had had enough of him, and there was nothing he could do except make himself smaller. The faces in the Hall looked as though they had reached that decision. Harry looked down at Hermione. Her eyes were empty. Draconia lay beside her now, one hand only inches from Hermione's. The gap between their fingers drew his gaze, and he could not work out why it bothered him more than the rest. Then he knew.

Hermione would have reached for her. So would Draconia. They could be furious with each other. They could argue until Harry wanted to hide under the table. But they would not have left that little space between them while the other was frightened and hurt. Voldemort had seen their faces. He had heard their voices through Harry's memories. He had not understood them. Harry lifted his head.

"You don't know them."

Voldemort stood behind Dumbledore's chair.

"I know what people do when hope fails."

"You know what you did."

The Hall trembled. Harry stepped over Cedric's false body. His legs worked here. He made himself remember that. This was inside his head, and the people on the floor were lies.

"Sirius didn't give me the key because he thought I'd win."

A crack ran down the nearest table.

"Hermione doesn't help me because I get everything right."

The stars above the yew tree split apart. Harry looked at Draconia.

"She chose me before either of us had anything to give."

The girls' fingers moved. The gap closed. The Hall broke around him. Harry was back against the gravestone. Voldemort's wand still touched his temple, and the night air was cold on the blood running down his neck.

"You had to invent them disappointed," he whispered, "because the real ones wouldn't cooperate."

Voldemort struck him with the back of his hand. Harry's head hit the stone. Light burst behind his eyes, and he sagged against the ropes. When he could see again, Voldemort's mouth had tightened. He no longer looked amused. He did not tell Harry to beg after that. He waited for it instead, leaving silence between the spells as though Harry might choose to fill it. Harry breathed when he could. He held the roots. He said nothing. Another strand of silver vanished. He felt Cedric move, a slight turn of a hand inside the loosening cords. A gleam ran through the wood. Harry coughed, hard enough to cover the small sound behind him, and the movement stopped.

Voldemort studied the remaining barrier.

"You cannot keep the dead safe for ever."

Harry looked at the Cup. It was three graves from Lucius, closer to him than it had been when he arrived.

"Didn't need for ever."

"What do you think you have gained?"

Harry could not let Cedric's face rise in his mind. He kept looking at the blue metal in the grass.

"Time."

Voldemort smiled.

"For what?"

Harry gave him no answer.

Cedric had never known how much effort it took to lie still. The Killing Curse had filled the gaps between the roots with green light. He had been certain it would reach him. Cold passed through his chest, his heart seemed to stop, and then Harry's magic closed around him so tightly that even his next breath had nowhere to go. He was breathing now. He could feel his heart. He simply could not find its beat where it ought to have been. Nott's violet spell had passed through him without a pause. When the man said dead, Cedric nearly answered. Then he saw Harry stand between the grave and the wands. He understood enough to stay silent.

The roots had closed until there were only thin seams of light. Cedric kept his head turned and his eyes half open, watching the graveyard through the nearest gap. His breaths were so small that the wet grass beneath his mouth hardly stirred. Mud pressed cold against his cheek; his wounded leg throbbed whenever his muscles tightened. He wanted to get up. The cords were loosening. He could turn one wrist, could almost bring a knee beneath him. He was older than Harry, bigger, and he had come through the maze on his own feet. He should not be lying here while Harry fought for both of them.

But each time he tried to move, silver ran through the roots and Harry flinched. Once would have been enough to make Cedric cautious. The second time made it clear. He stopped pulling. If the Death Eaters saw him moving before he had a wand in his hand, they would cast again, and Harry might not have enough strength to stop them. He lowered his forehead to the ground and listened.

He could not know what Voldemort was showing Harry. He heard the questions, the broken answers, the one terrible stop that came between them. He saw Harry shake after the curses ended and make himself lift his head when Voldemort returned. Whenever the man asked about a house or a name, Harry looked anywhere but towards the roots. Even being alive had become a secret Cedric needed him to keep.

The fire hissed beneath the cauldron. Scorched cloth hung from Nott's arm. Blood ran down the pale stone behind Harry, collecting at the bottom before spilling into the grass. Cedric found himself staring at Harry's left hand. The fingers no longer closed properly, but he kept trying, as though a fist might give him something to hold on to. For months, people had talked about Harry as if he were something to argue over. A cheat, a hero, Dumbledore's favourite. Someone who wanted attention. Someone who had more power than a fourteen-year-old ought to have.

There was nothing grand about the boy Cedric could see through the roots. He was frightened and bleeding, his voice rough after every scream, and still he would not let the shield go. Cedric wanted to tell him that he could stop. That he had done enough. He wanted, more than anything, to take a share of it. He could do none of those things without getting them both killed. So he began to remember.

Peter Pettigrew. Thinning hair, a short, hunched body, the hand he had cut off and the silver one Voldemort gave him. Crouch Junior, the black patch over his eye, blood drying around his mouth. He had tried to kill Cedric before either boy knew why they were there. Nott. Cedric did not know his first name, but he knew the crest on the ring. The man had been waiting beside the cauldron when they arrived. He had repaired its supports and spoken over the bone and blood.

Bone. Flesh. Blood.

Cedric repeated the order silently. He counted the masked figures, trying to hold each height and voice in his memory. Two favoured old injuries when they knelt. One turned quickly at Nott's name. Another had a Ministry clasp beneath his cloak. Family badges gleamed when robes shifted near the fire. He would remember all of it. If they got out, there would be someone who could say what had happened without asking Harry to relive every second. Lucius Malfoy stood three graves from the Cup. He did not stare at Harry, but Cedric had seen the brief glance between them. He could not know what they had agreed. He could only hope there was something to wait for.

The stone from the maze dug into his hip. Not long ago it had been a mouse, changed and changed back while they tried to find out whether the Cup was safe. Cedric worked two fingers towards his pocket, then stopped as the nearest root brightened. If he could get a wand, the stone might become a distraction. Until then, he would leave it where it was. He thought of Hermione bent over their plans in the library, refusing to let him mistake being frightened for giving up. Of Draconia outside the champions' tent, telling him not to let Harry sacrifice himself. Of Sirius waiting beside every dangerous doorway.

They had known how far Harry might go. Cedric had thought he understood. He had not imagined this. Anger steadied him. When the barrier fell, he would move. He would get to the Cup, get to Harry, do whatever he could to carry him back. And if they reached Hogwarts, he would tell the truth before anyone turned it into a story easier to hear. He would name Voldemort. He would name the men beside the fire. Harry would not have to stand alone while adults argued about whether his blood counted as proof.

One root dissolved against Cedric's ankle. Cold air slipped through the gap. He did not gulp it. He gathered his legs beneath him a little at a time, stopping whenever the silver stirred. Beyond the stone, Harry's head hung forward. His eyes stayed on the Cup. Three graves from Lucius. Four from Cedric. He made himself ready.

"Wormtail. His wand."

Harry raised his head. Pettigrew brought the holly wand forward in his silver hand. He held it too tightly, the metal fingers digging into the wood, and Harry watched with a fear that had nothing to do with being hit. Voldemort took it from him.

"Phoenix feather."

Harry stared at the familiar handle. It looked impossibly ordinary in that pale hand.

"Its brother failed to kill you thirteen years ago," Voldemort said.

The brother wand. Ollivander had told Harry about the two feathers. He had never wanted to think about it for long. Voldemort turned towards the circle.

"Some of you believed he was powerful. Tonight you will see how little there is behind the name."

He cut the ropes. Harry fell. His knees struck first, then his hand slipped, and his face hit the grass. Pain pulled through every wound. Three roots vanished over Cedric before Harry managed to brace himself on both arms. The wand landed beside his fingers.

"Get up."

Harry closed his hand around it. The wood was warm, still his, and that small familiarity almost undid him. He pushed himself upright. His left leg shook so badly that he had to lock the knee. Blood ran into one eye, and he turned his head to keep Voldemort in sight.

"Drop the shield."

"No."

"You will need everything you have."

Harry tightened his grip. Voldemort raised his wand in salute. Harry did not move.

"Bow."

"No."

Invisible force drove his head down. Harry's knee struck the mud. He planted his wand hand against the earth and resisted, his shoulder trembling as the pressure increased.

"I can make you."

"Then it isn't a bow."

Another root faded. Harry could not afford to spend strength on this. He let himself fall sideways instead, rolling out of the force and casting from the ground. His Severing Charm tore Voldemort's robes from shoulder to waist. Black cloth fell into the grass. Several Death Eaters stepped back. Voldemort looked down, and Harry used the moment to stand. The first curse split the grave behind him. Harry threw himself aside as marble burst across the circle. A fragment struck his temple. He fired a Blasting Curse at the ground in front of Voldemort, throwing dirt into the air, and ran while the man raised a shield.

The Cup was to his left. Nott and three masked figures moved to block it before he had taken more than a few steps. Harry turned between the graves instead, forcing the circle to shift if they wanted to keep him inside it.

"Running, Potter?"

Harry ducked behind an angel. He had no breath to answer. The stone moved around him. Wings lengthened into serpents, winding around his arms and throat. He blasted the nearest apart and scrambled over the base, sending a Disarming Charm through the dust. Voldemort knocked it aside. It caught Crouch. His spare wand flew into the darkness. Harry saw his face twist, then had to turn away. Black fire was coming towards him. His shield caught it, but flames crawled over the clear surface towards his hand. He dropped the charm and dived behind another grave before they reached the wand.

Names passed beside him. Mary Riddle. Thomas Riddle. Somewhere behind him, the cauldron still burned with bone taken from Voldemort's father's grave. Voldemort did not follow the path between the stones. He destroyed them. Granite shattered, carved wings broke, and each place Harry might have hidden disappeared beneath the next curse. Green light passed close enough to lift his hair. He threw himself between two masked figures, and both recoiled. Voldemort did not change his aim for them. They fled apart. The curse tore a black trench through the place where they had stood.

"Stay where you are."

The circle held, less tightly than before. Harry had no hope of matching him spell for spell. He had the graves, the fear of the people around them and whatever surprise he could make out of not yet being dead. He used all of it. A Stunner made Voldemort turn. Binding ropes caught his sleeve before he burned them away. A blue flame held him for two seconds. Harry summoned a spectator's mask and sent it spinning across Voldemort's face. A Trip Jinx dropped another man over a low grave; Harry planted a foot between his shoulders and scrambled over him as a curse struck the ground behind them. He landed badly.

His wounded leg folded, and the cut along his side pulled open. For a moment he could see nothing but white. He dragged himself behind a headstone and pressed his shoulder to it, trying to find his breath. None of his spells had hurt Voldemort. That was not what they were for. Every shield Voldemort cast gave Harry another second, and perhaps Lucius one too. The roots kept fading. Being farther away did not stop him feeling each one go. Harry turned past a low grave. Voldemort was waiting.

"Crucio."

Harry's shield came too late. Pain drove him against the stone, his wand slipping as his hand clenched. He caught it in both palms before it fell. The curse stopped. Voldemort aimed at his heart. Harry lifted his wand. He did not have time to think of anything except the spell he knew best.

"Expelliarmus!"

Green light left Voldemort's wand. Red light left Harry's. They met in a burst of gold. The wand pulled so hard that Harry nearly lost it. He gripped it with both hands and stared at the thread joining the two tips. Gold spread out from it, rising over the graves until a dome of light enclosed them. Sparks drifted slowly through the air. Beyond them, the masks became dark shapes. Voldemort's face changed.

"Break it!" Nott shouted.

Spells struck the dome and dissolved. Harry dug his heels into the ground as the thread dragged him forward. Voldemort pushed from the other side, both hands on his wand, his teeth bared with the effort. The light pulsed. An old man stepped out of Voldemort's wand. He was pale as smoke, but Harry could make out his coat, his lined face and the stubborn set of his mouth. Frank Bryce looked from Voldemort to the boy struggling at the other end of the light.

"Keep hold, lad."

A woman followed, one hand pressed to her temple. Bertha Jorkins blinked at the graveyard as though it were taking shape around her for the first time.

"He knew," she said, looking towards Crouch. "He knew what they did to me."

Voldemort tried to pull his wand away. The golden thread held. Two more shapes gathered inside the light. Harry knew them before he could see their faces. His mother's eyes found him. Then his father was there, looking first at Voldemort and then at the blood on Harry's shirt. Lily looked as young as she did in the photographs. Younger than Sirius, younger than the teachers Harry still wanted to call for. The sight of her face made his hands loosen before he could stop them.

"Harry," she said. "Hold on."

He tightened his grip. For the first time since they had arrived, someone was asking him to stay alive without asking for anything else.

"Echoes," Voldemort said. "Nothing more."

James did not look away from Harry.

"When the light goes, run. We'll give you what time we can."

Harry tried to answer. A sob came out instead, and the golden thread trembled. Beads of light moved towards Voldemort, forcing the green back towards his wand. Nott circled the dome. Black light shone from his ring.

"The ground," Bertha warned. "Watch the ground."

Dark roots broke through the soil beneath Harry. One caught his wounded leg. Another climbed his back, pressing into the cuts beneath his shirt. The black marks flared, and pain tore one hand from the wand. The gold faltered. Lily reached for him, though her fingers could not touch his shoulder.

"Behind you, Harry. There's a way through."

He looked. The Cup was beyond the dome. Lucius stood three graves from it. Cedric's shield had dwindled to a single arch of roots. James stepped towards Voldemort.

"Now."

Harry broke the connection. The dome burst apart. Gold swept over the graves. Death Eaters raised their arms against it, and Voldemort staggered back as the figures from his wand rushed towards him. Harry did not look to see how long they could hold him. He ran for the Cup. The last root over Cedric gave way. The other heartbeat tore free of Harry's chest. Cedric fell forward through the broken grass, drawing a full breath so loud that the nearest masked man turned.

"He's alive!"

Crouch stared. Nott's wand rose, but for one precious second nobody cast. They had been looking at a tomb. Now Cedric was moving inside it. Harry felt the shield's loss as an emptiness beneath his ribs. It had fallen because he could no longer hold it. He had nothing left to put between Cedric and the next curse. Cedric snatched up a piece of the broken monument and hit the nearest Death Eater across the mask. The man dropped with a cry. Cedric caught his wand before it struck the grass.

"The Cup!" Harry shouted.

Cedric turned towards him. Crouch hit him from the side. They fell among the graves. Cedric's wounded leg twisted beneath them, and he cried out, but kept hold of the wand. Crouch hooked his good arm around his throat. Cedric drove the wand handle into his face, forcing him back far enough to aim. Nott was aiming at Harry.

"Stupefy!"

Harry raised a shield. It bent the red bolt aside, but not far enough. The edge caught him in the chest and flung him against a grave. For a moment everything went dark. He came back to himself on the ground, still awake, his wand gone. Cedric Stunned Crouch. He pushed himself onto one knee. Nott struck him next. Cedric fell without a sound.

"No!"

Harry tried to get up. His legs would not take his weight. He clawed at the grass instead, searching for the holly wand, but it lay near Nott's feet. Voldemort emerged through the fading gold. One side of his robes smouldered. His wand was whole, and the red eyes went straight to Cedric.

"Lucius."

Lucius stepped out of the circle.

"My Lord."

Voldemort pointed towards the unconscious boy.

"Kill him."

Lucius lifted his wand. Harry watched him walk towards Cedric. The mask hid his face. Harry could not see the man who had stood beside him before the maze, could not know what he was thinking now. He saw only the wand and understood what Voldemort was asking him to prove. The Cup lay between them. Nott looked at it.

"Move that first. Potter is still trying to reach it."

Lucius stopped.

"Of course."

He aimed. The curse hit Nott in the chest. Nott flew backwards across the graveyard. Before his body struck the ground, Lucius turned his wand towards the Cup.

"Depulso!"

Blue metal shot through the grass, struck a gravestone and spun towards Harry. It landed beside his hand. Lucius tore off the mask.

"Go, Potter!"

The circle erupted. Spells came at Lucius from both sides, and he raised a shield wide enough to cover Harry and Cedric. Silver light bowed beneath the first blows. For an instant, Harry saw his face clearly: white with fear, his teeth clenched, no longer hiding what he had come to do. Voldemort screamed. Harry reached for the Cup. The handle was an inch below his fingers. He could close his hand and be gone. He could leave the graves, the red eyes, all of it. Cedric lay ten feet away. Harry looked at him and pulled his hand back. He tried to summon his wand without one. Nothing happened. He tried again, his arm shaking with the effort.

Lucius saw. He drove a line of silver light into the nearest headstone, fixing the shield to it. His left hand braced against the light while he cast with the wand in his right.

"Accio Potter's wand!"

Holly flew across the graves. Harry caught it against his chest. Green light passed where Lucius's head had been. He twisted aside, sent one curse towards Nott and another towards Pettigrew, then vanished with a crack. The shield stayed. Cracks ran through it as the next spells hit, but it stayed. Harry pointed at Cedric.

"Vincula!"

Grass wound around the boy's chest. Harry caught the other end, wrapped it around his bleeding wrist and pulled. Cedric moved a few inches. Harry pulled harder. Voldemort turned from the place Lucius had vanished. His eyes found Harry.

"You."

The rope cut into the wound on Harry's arm. His hand opened before he could stop it, and he caught the grass with the other, the wand trapped awkwardly against his palm. Cedric slid over the wet ground. Eight feet. Six. A masked witch stood beside the broken monument. She saw the body move and raised her wand. Harry planted both heels in the mud.

"Avada—"

He pulled with everything he had. Cedric jerked sideways. Green light crossed the place where his head had rested and hit the stone behind him. Fragments rained over both boys. The witch aimed again. Harry fired from the ground. His Stunner struck her mask, and she folded behind the grave. He did not wait to see whether she would get up. Crouch stirred. Pettigrew crawled towards the Cup, his silver hand digging into the grass. Four feet. Harry pulled once more. Cedric came within reach. Harry caught his limp wrist, dragging him the last few inches. The Cup lay beside his knee. He kept his own bare hand away from it while he brought Cedric's towards the second handle.

Voldemort raised his wand. Harry could hear his own breath coming in small, desperate gasps. He had to do this together. If the Cup took him before Cedric touched it, there would be no way back.

"Crucio!"

Pain struck as Cedric collided with him. Harry pressed Cedric's bare palm to one handle at the same instant he closed his own hand around the other. His wand was caught between his fingers and the metal. The graveyard stretched. Voldemort's face smeared into the darkness. Harry held the Cup and the rope while pain followed them into the journey, so fierce that he could no longer tell where his body ended. Black-violet light flashed across Cedric's closed eyes. Every dark mark beneath Harry's skin burned. Then the ground changed. Hogwarts rushed up beneath them.

The Cup struck the empty plinth and bounced into the grass. Cedric landed beside it, pulled sideways by the tether. Harry stumbled upright for one brief moment beneath the stadium lights. He still had the rope. The Hufflepuff stands began to cheer. The sound broke halfway across the pitch. People nearest the centre had seen the blood, the torn robes hanging from Harry's shoulder, the black lines moving beneath his throat. Applause stopped. Someone screamed. A brass instrument fell down the wooden steps, giving one crooked note as it struck each stair.

Amos Diggory hit the barrier hard enough to overturn the chair behind him. His wife stood beside him without making a sound. Fleur pulled Gabrielle into her arms and turned her face away. Krum gripped the rail, watching Harry as though he could not believe what he was seeing. The ruined anchoring bracelet hung open from Harry's wrist. Beside it, Draconia's finer chain flared white. At the edge of the field, its twin burned against her skin. She caught her wrist with one hand, the split alarm coin blazing in the other.

Dumbledore raised his wand. Red sparks shot above the pitch, and the waiting wards swept Harry with light. Warnings appeared over the grass, bright enough for those in the nearest stands to read.

Severe blood loss.

Dark curse.

Foreign magic detected.

Unstable magic.

Harry looked down at the stone on his wrist.

"Bit late."

His voice was too quiet to reach anyone else. Draconia vaulted the low rail around the officials' enclosure. Hermione followed so close that their shoulders struck. McGonagall called after them, but Sirius was already ahead, running across the ruined pitch. Harry saw them coming and flinched. For a moment they were the bodies Voldemort had shown him. Hermione's face was empty. Draconia's skin was bloodless. Sirius wore the grey of Azkaban beneath his coat. Harry blinked, trying to make the pictures stop. Hermione caught her foot in a torn root and fell onto both hands. Draconia reached back without looking, seized her arm and hauled her up. Sirius shouted Harry's name, his voice carrying across the field.

That was real.

Voldemort had not understood how they reached for each other. Harry watched the girls run together and tried to keep hold of that difference. He took one step. His knee folded. Sirius caught him before he hit the ground.

"Harry."

They went down together, Sirius's knee driving into the churned earth. Harry felt arms close around him, felt a hand slide over his back and stop at the torn cloth. Sirius was saying something, but the sound kept moving too far away.

"Look at me. Come on, Harry. Eyes open."

Harry had not realised they were closing. He opened them because he knew that voice. There was no joke left in it, no anger either, only fear. Grass brushed his cheek. Smoke drifted from the wrecked maze. His mouth was thick with blood, and Sirius's heart was beating fast beneath his ear. The Cup lay several yards away. Ministry officials backed from it while Madam Pomfrey knelt beside Cedric, two fingers beneath his jaw. Harry watched until Cedric's chest rose. Then it rose again. Pomfrey touched her wand to his shoulder and murmured a waking charm. His eyelids fluttered. Harry breathed.

Hermione and Draconia were almost there. Remus followed them, and Dumbledore was shouting orders near the plinth. Harry searched between them for pale hair, for the man who had taken off his mask. Narcissa stood with both hands over her mouth. Lucius was not beside her.

"Lucius," Harry whispered.

Draconia reached him as he said it. Her gaze went straight to his face.

"Where's my father?"

Harry tried to lift his head.

"Saved us."

He could not find enough breath for the rest. Cedric pushed against the grass beneath Pomfrey's hand. He looked towards Harry, then towards the people crowding the pitch.

"Lucius Malfoy saved us," he said. His voice shook. "Voldemort—"

The name carried beyond them. Fudge shouted, but Harry could not make out the words through the alarms and the footsteps. Hermione knelt beside Sirius. Her hands hovered over Harry, looking for somewhere she could touch without hurting him. Draconia dropped on his other side, the green coin still burning in her palm. Harry stared at them. They were alive. They were both alive, and they had come for him.

"He's back," he said.

Sirius held him closer.

"I know."

Harry tried to swallow.

"I lost."

"No."

Sirius bent towards him. Harry could barely see his face now, but he could feel him there.

"Couldn't stop him."

"You came home." Sirius's voice broke. "Harry, you came home."

The key pressed against Harry's chest. He tried to reach it, but his hand would not move. Hermione said his name. Draconia caught his wrist, gently enough that he felt her fingers before the pain. For a moment he knew exactly where they were, one on either side of him, and that Sirius was not going to let go.

Then he felt nothing.
