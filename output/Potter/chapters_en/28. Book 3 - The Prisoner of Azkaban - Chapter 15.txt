Harry did not turn.

"You implied it."

"I implied he was less stupid than James."

"Same thing."

Lupin's ears shifted towards Sirius's voice. Recognition flashed again. Harry clung to it.

"Moony," Sirius said.

The werewolf froze. Sirius dragged himself upright against the tree. His human face was grey, his injured arm held against his chest.

"Come on, Moony. You've already ruined my robes."

The werewolf's head tilted.

"You always hated these."

One step carried it nearer. Harry's stag lowered its antlers. Sirius did not reach for a wand. He had none.

"You know me," he said.

Lupin's lips pulled back from his teeth.

"Yes, I know. Terrible judgement. We've established that."

The chain around Lupin's body scraped over a root. He advanced. Sirius extended his uninjured hand. Harry's heart hammered.

"Padfoot," Sirius whispered.

The werewolf breathed against his fingers. A cloud slid from the moon, sharpening every shadow between them. Lupin's nostrils flared. Whether recognition broke or instinct merely overwhelmed it, Harry never knew. The jaws opened.

Harry's Patronus charged. The stag struck the werewolf and threw him away from Sirius. Lupin hit the ground, rolled and rose in a frenzy. Whatever fragile awareness had survived was gone.

Sirius seized Harry.

"Run."

They made it three steps. Lupin leapt. A silver phoenix descended through the trees. Its wings passed over the werewolf, leaving bands of light around his body. Lupin fell from the air. The bands wrapped his legs, chest and muzzle before lowering him gently into the snow. He fought them.

The phoenix brightened. The forest shone from root to canopy. Albus Dumbledore stepped into the clearing. His blue robes moved around him despite the stillness of the air. Snow rested upon his beard. He carried no visible anger, yet power travelled ahead of him strongly enough that Harry's skin prickled. The Dementors stopped at the edge of the trees.

Dumbledore looked at them.

"You will withdraw."

The creatures remained.

"The Ministry authorised us to capture Sirius Black," a voice whispered from beneath one hood.

Harry had not known Dementors could speak. The sound entered his bones.

"You were not authorised to approach students," Dumbledore replied.

"He is here."

The leading Dementor extended one decayed hand towards Sirius. Dumbledore lifted his wand. The movement was small. Every tree in the clearing bent away from him.

"You will withdraw."

The Dementors rose. They did not flee. Nothing so measured could be called fleeing. They drifted backwards until the forest concealed them, gathering beyond the grounds where darkness accepted their shapes. Dumbledore watched until the last disappeared. Then he turned towards Harry. Harry became aware that Sirius's hand remained locked around his shoulder, that his own knees were shaking and that blood had soaked through one sleeve.

"Professor," he said.

Dumbledore's gaze travelled over him.

"You were instructed to return to the castle."

"Yes."

"You instead entered the Shrieking Shack, confronted two fugitives, crossed an area occupied by Dementors and attempted to reason with a transformed werewolf."

"When you list it like that, it sounds bad."

"It is difficult to arrange favourably."

Sirius made a noise that might have been a laugh. His legs gave way. Harry caught him badly. Dumbledore's spell took Sirius's weight before they both fell. The headmaster lowered him onto the snow.

"Is he—"

"Alive."

Dumbledore knelt and placed two fingers against Sirius's throat.

"His injuries are serious."

Harry looked towards Lupin. The werewolf lay bound within phoenix light. His chest rose and fell rapidly.

"Professor Lupin?"

"The Wolfsbane Potion has preserved more of his judgement than appears evident. He has sustained injuries, but none should prove fatal."

"He missed tonight's dose."

"Yes."

Dumbledore looked towards Harry's stag. It remained at the edge of the clearing, keeping the Dementors beyond the trees.

"A corporeal Patronus," he said.

Harry nodded.

"Professor Lupin will be pleased."

"He saw it."

"In his present form?"

"I think part of him did."

Dumbledore's eyes softened. A silver cat entered the clearing. McGonagall's Patronus stopped before them and spoke in her voice.

Pettigrew is secured. Moody, Lucius Malfoy, Severus and the girls are returning from Hogsmeade. The Minister is approaching the gates.

The message dissolved. Harry felt Sirius react despite his unconsciousness. His body tightened at the word Minister.

"They'll take him," Harry said.

Dumbledore rose.

"No."

"They sent him to Azkaban without a trial before."

"They will not do so again."

"How do you know?"

"Because this time, Sirius will not stand alone."

Harry looked at him. The answer held more than reassurance. It held an admission. Dumbledore had sent Hagrid to Godric's Hollow. Dumbledore had placed Harry with the Dursleys. Dumbledore had accepted Sirius's guilt without demanding the hearing no one else offered. He, too, had a mistake to confess. Harry was tired of being surrounded by adults discovering regret thirteen years late.

He was also beginning to understand that what they did after discovering it mattered.

"You have to listen to him," Harry said.

"I shall."

"And Peter."

"Yes."

"And us."

Dumbledore's gaze rested upon him.

"Especially you."

The silver phoenix spread its wings over Sirius and Lupin. Their bodies rose gently from the snow. Harry followed Dumbledore towards the castle. For the first time since entering the tunnel, he allowed himself to believe Peter had nowhere left to run.

The hospital wing became a holding room, courtroom and battlefield before dawn.

Madam Pomfrey objected to all three uses. She placed Lupin behind a wall of reinforced screens at the far end of the ward. Dumbledore's restraints would remain until sunrise returned him to human form. Sirius occupied the bed beside Harry, unconscious beneath a series of healing charms. Hermione lay on Harry's other side while Madam Pomfrey sealed the curse wound across her ribs. Draconia sat upon a nearby stool as a cut above her temple knitted itself closed. Snape refused a bed until his shoulder was set. Madam Pomfrey set it without warning. The sound that followed silenced the room.

Lucius looked impressed.

"Not one word," Snape said.

"I said nothing."

"Your face did."

Harry turned towards Hermione.

"See? It isn't only me."

"Do not encourage either of them," Hermione murmured.

She looked painfully tired. Healing potion stained her lips purple, and her injured hand had been cleaned and wrapped. Despite Madam Pomfrey's threats, her notebook lay open upon the blanket. Peter waited inside a transparent enclosure conjured around the final bed. Moody stood guard beside him. The magical walls reached from floor to ceiling. Chains bound Peter's wrists and ankles to the bedframe, and six detection devices surrounded him. His recovered wand rested within a sealed evidence box on Dumbledore's desk at the centre of the ward. Peter had stopped crying.

His eyes followed every entrance. Professor McGonagall stood beside the hospital doors with her wand drawn. Her hair had escaped its tight arrangement and fell in grey strands around her face. Mud covered the hem of her dressing gown. Lucius occupied the space between the students and Peter. He had not left Draconia's side for more than a minute since returning. He did not touch her or ask repeatedly whether she was well. He merely placed himself where anyone approaching would have to pass him first.

Dumbledore left only long enough to collect the Minister. Fawkes returned before the first grey of dawn, opening a burst of flame above the nearest bed and depositing Dumbledore beside a furious Cornelius Fudge.

The Minister's lime-green bowler hat sat crooked upon his head. He gripped it with both hands when he saw Peter. For several seconds, the only sound came from Lupin's restraints shifting behind the screens. Fudge turned white.

"That," he said, "is Peter Pettigrew."

Moody leaned upon his staff. "Sharp eye."

Fudge approached the transparent enclosure. Peter looked at him. Recognition passed between them—not personal familiarity, but the strange intimacy between a government and the man whose death it had rewarded.

"This is impossible," Fudge whispered.

"It has been an unusually crowded night for impossible things," Dumbledore said.

Fudge removed his hat.

"But he died. We awarded his mother the Order of Merlin."

"Send her another," Moody suggested. "Apology this time."

"This is no occasion for jokes."

"Wasn't joking."

Fudge looked towards Sirius. Fear replaced disbelief.

"You have Black too."

"He requires medical treatment," Dumbledore said.

"He requires restraint!"

"He has not regained consciousness."

"He escaped Azkaban, broke into Hogwarts twice and assaulted children."

"He entered in pursuit of Pettigrew," Harry said.

Fudge appeared to notice the students.

"Why are they here?"

"Because we captured him," Draconia replied.

Lucius's mouth moved by the smallest amount. Fudge looked at her bloodstained robes.

"Miss Malfoy?"

"Minister."

"What are you doing in a hospital ward at this hour?"

"I was under the impression the blood explained that."

Lucius cleared his throat. Draconia did not apologise. Fudge's attention moved to Harry.

"Harry, thank goodness you are safe. We have been terribly worried. When I heard Black had reached the grounds—"

"Peter tried to kill Hermione."

Fudge's prepared concern faltered.

"He used the Killing Curse," Draconia added. "There were witnesses."

Peter began shaking his head. Moody struck the transparent wall with his staff.

"Still."

Fudge turned towards Dumbledore.

"This must be handled at the Ministry."

"No," Harry said.

"Harry, I understand you have endured a frightening—"

"You don't."

Fudge's face reddened. Dumbledore lifted one hand before the Minister could answer.

"Harry has a right to know what happened to his parents."

"Then that is all the more reason to keep him out of this."

Hermione pushed herself upright against the pillows.

"We are witnesses."

"You should give statements when your Healer considers you fit."

"I wrote mine already."

She held up the notebook. Fudge stared. Moody chuckled. Lucius stepped forward.

"Before anyone is moved, Pettigrew will be questioned in the presence of every relevant witness."

"This is not your decision, Malfoy."

"No. It will become the Wizengamot's decision after they receive copies of the evidence your office ignored."

Fudge tightened his hold upon the hat.

"What evidence?"

Lucius withdrew Moody's summary.

"No wand examination. No formal interview. Conflicting witness statements removed from the final report. A transfer order signed eleven hours after Black's arrest."

Fudge read the first page. His face changed.

"These were wartime emergency measures."

"So was my Mark," Lucius said. "I have discovered that explanations lose their charm when used by other people."

Snape looked towards him. Moody's magical eye spun. Fudge lowered the parchment.

"You cannot seriously expect me to entertain the claim that Sirius Black is innocent because Pettigrew has unexpectedly reappeared."

"No," Hermione said. "We expect you to entertain it because Pettigrew was the Potters' Secret Keeper."

Peter strained against his chains. Fudge looked at him.

"That has not been established."

"We have a map identifying him as an Animagus," Hermione continued. "His registered wand, which was used during his escape. Multiple witnesses heard him admit that he disclosed the Potters' location. His missing finger matches the only remains recovered after the explosion."

"And," Moody added, "I brought Veritaserum."

Fudge turned sharply.

"That cannot be administered without authorisation."

"I'm authorised."

"You are retired."

"Still hold emergency investigation status."

"That status was never meant—"

"Should've written it better."

Fudge appealed to Dumbledore. The headmaster stood beside Peter's enclosure.

"Veritaserum alone cannot establish guilt," he said. "Memories may be altered, and compelled answers depend upon the subject's understanding. Together with the physical and testimonial evidence, however, it will provide a useful beginning."

"Beginning?" Harry repeated.

Dumbledore met his eyes.

"A proper hearing must still follow."

Harry looked towards Sirius.

"How long?"

Fudge adjusted his collar. "There are procedures we must follow."

"You skipped procedure when you sent him to prison."

No one spoke. Harry's anger did not burn now. It had become cleaner during the night. He could hear each excuse before Fudge offered it and see the machinery closing around responsibility.

"We are doing this here," he said.

Fudge drew himself up. "You do not instruct the Minister for Magic."

"No," Harry replied. "Apparently no one does."

Lucius turned his face away. Narcissa would have laughed into her teacup. Fudge looked to McGonagall for discipline. She did not provide it. Dumbledore placed himself between the Minister and Peter's enclosure.

"Alastor?"

Moody placed three drops of clear liquid into a glass of water. Peter began struggling.

"No."

His voice broke.

"No, you cannot. I have rights."

Sirius stirred. Every head turned. His eyes opened slowly. Pain clouded them at first. Then he saw Peter inside the enclosure. He attempted to rise. Harry put one hand against his chest.

"Don't."

Sirius looked at him.

"Where—"

"Hospital wing."

"Peter?"

"Caught."

"The Dementors?"

"Gone."

Sirius's gaze found Fudge. His body became rigid. The Minister reached for his wand. Harry moved closer to Sirius. Dumbledore's voice remained calm.

"Cornelius, if you draw your wand in my hospital wing, Minerva will remove you."

McGonagall adjusted her grip upon hers. Fudge lowered his hand.

Sirius stared at him. "Come to finish the paperwork?"

Fudge swallowed.

"You escaped lawful custody."

"You forgot the lawful part."

"Sirius," Dumbledore said.

Sirius looked at him. The two men had not spoken in twelve years. Regret entered the distance between them, too large for the room. Dumbledore inclined his head.

"I failed to ask questions I should have asked."

Sirius laughed weakly.

"Get in line."

Harry felt the bitterness move through the bed beneath his hand. Dumbledore accepted it.

"We will ask them now."

Moody entered the enclosure. Peter tried to retreat, but the chains held him. Moody forced the glass between his lips and closed Peter's nose until he swallowed.

"Charming," Lucius observed.

"Effective."

They waited. Peter's breathing slowed. His face became slack. The fear did not disappear, but the effort required to shape it into performance fell away. Dumbledore stepped closer.

"State your full name."

"Peter Malcolm Pettigrew."

The sound of it filled the ward.

"Are you an Animagus?"

"Yes."

"What form do you take?"

"A rat."

"Did you register that ability with the Ministry?"

"No."

"Were you the Secret Keeper for James and Lily Potter in October 1981?"

Peter's lips parted. Sirius's hand closed around Harry's wrist.

"Yes."

The word was quiet. It destroyed twelve years. Fudge staggered backwards into a chair. Sirius made no sound at all. Dumbledore continued.

"Was Sirius Black ever the Potters' Secret Keeper?"

"He was supposed to be."

"Was the charm placed upon him?"

"No."

"Who suggested the change?"

"Sirius."

"Did he force you to accept?"

"No."

Peter's fingers moved against the chains.

"I wanted them to trust me."

"Did you disclose the Potters' location to Lord Voldemort?"

"Yes."

Harry stopped feeling his hand within Sirius's grip.

"Were you compelled by the Imperius Curse?"

"No."

"Was the secret extracted through torture, Legilimency or magic?"

"No."

"Why did you reveal it?"

Peter stared at the ceiling.

"He promised I would be protected."

Draconia's gaze lowered.

"Protected from whom?" Dumbledore asked.

"From him. From the war. From everyone."

"Did you know Voldemort intended to kill James Potter?"

"Yes."

"Lily Potter?"

"Yes."

"Harry Potter?"

Peter's eyes moved towards Harry.

"Yes."

Sirius tried to rise again. Harry held him down. The effort hurt them both.

"You knew," Sirius said.

Veritaserum removed Peter's ability to avoid him.

"Yes."

"You held him."

"Yes."

"You ate at their table."

"Yes."

"And you sent Voldemort to their house."

"Yes."

Sirius's face broke. The grief did not emerge dramatically. It loosened every part of him at once. His head dropped. The hand around Harry's wrist released and covered his mouth. Harry had spent years imagining justice as an answer powerful enough to alter what came before it. Peter's confession did not restore anyone. James remained dead in the hall. Lily still stood alone beside the cot. Sirius still carried twelve years of Azkaban within his body.

The truth arrived too late to save them. It was still the truth. Dumbledore waited before continuing.

"On the morning of the first of November 1981, did Sirius Black confront you in a Muggle street?"

"Yes."

"Who cast the curse that caused the explosion?"

"I did."

Fudge's hat fell from his hands.

"How many people did you kill?"

"Twelve."

"Did Sirius cast any offensive spell before the explosion?"

"No."

"What did you do immediately before casting?"

"I cut off my finger."

Peter's mother had buried that finger. Harry wondered whether she had wept over it. Whether Peter had read the announcement of his Order of Merlin while hiding beneath furniture in the Weasley home.

"Why?" Dumbledore asked.

"To make them think Sirius destroyed me."

"Did you transform into a rat and escape through the damaged street?"

"Yes."

"Where did you go?"

"First to an empty warehouse. Then to Ottery St Catchpole."

The hospital doors opened quietly. Professor Flitwick entered with Ron and Percy, whom McGonagall had sent for once Peter's identity was confirmed. Ron stopped just inside the ward. His face was pale; Percy's hand hovered behind his shoulder without quite touching it.

"The Weasleys," Hermione whispered.

"Why did you choose that family?"

"They were poor."

Ron flinched. Peter continued under the potion.

"They would be grateful for a pet. Arthur Weasley worked at the Ministry. His sons would attend Hogwarts. I could hear news. I could hide near people no one would suspect."

Ron's hands curled.

"Did you ever care about them?" he asked.

Fudge turned. "This is highly irregular—"

"Answer him," Moody said.

Peter looked at Ron.

"Sometimes."

The word hurt more than no would have. Ron swallowed.

"Did you ever protect me?"

Peter's eyes remained upon him.

"No."

Ron left. Percy followed immediately. Harry watched the doors close behind them. Another part of Peter's betrayal travelled beyond the Potters. It had entered ordinary bedrooms, family photographs and years of affection given to an animal that watched without returning it. Dumbledore's voice softened.

"Did Sirius Black betray James and Lily Potter in any way?"

"No."

Sirius looked up. Peter continued because the potion demanded it.

"He would have died first."

Sirius shut his eyes. Harry's grip moved from his chest to his hand. This time, Sirius held on.

"Did Sirius Black kill Peter Pettigrew or the twelve Muggles for whose deaths he was imprisoned?" Dumbledore asked.

"No."

The answer remained in the room. Fudge sank fully into the chair. No one rescued him from it. Moody removed a small crystal phial.

"We take the memory now."

Dumbledore touched his wand to Peter's temple. A silver thread emerged. Peter whimpered as it lengthened. Dumbledore guided the memory into the phial, where it twisted like smoke.

"Memories of the Fidelius Charm," he instructed.

Another thread followed.

"The disclosure to Voldemort."

A third.

"The confrontation with Sirius and the explosion."

The last emerged darkly, sparks moving within it. Moody sealed each phial.

"Independent Pensieve review," he said. "Three witnesses. No Ministry selection."

Fudge found his voice.

"The Department of Magical Law Enforcement must control—"

"No," Lucius said.

"You cannot dictate the handling of Ministry evidence."

"I can ensure that every member of the Wizengamot knows the Minister attempted to remove the evidence before breakfast."

Fudge rose.

"Are you threatening me?"

"Yes."

The simplicity silenced him. Lucius stepped closer.

"My daughter was nearly murdered tonight by a man your Ministry declared dead. Harry Potter was attacked by Dementors you stationed at his school. An innocent heir of an old family spent twelve years imprisoned without trial, which I assure you will concern people who have never previously objected to Sirius Black's suffering."

His gaze hardened.

"You may conduct this investigation transparently, Cornelius, or I will make the concealment more politically expensive than the mistake."

Draconia watched her father. He had not become good overnight. His instincts remained those of a man who understood pressure better than mercy. Yet for once, he aimed every unpleasant talent he possessed at the people who expected silence. Fudge knew it too.

"The Ministry requires temporary custody of both men," he said.

"No," Harry replied.

"Pettigrew cannot remain at a school."

"He won't," Moody said. "Shacklebolt has arranged a secure Auror site."

"And Black?"

"Stays here," Dumbledore said.

Fudge shook his head. "Absolutely not. He is an escaped prisoner."

"Whose original imprisonment has been discredited by a confession obtained in your presence."

"He still escaped."

Sirius opened one eye. "I promise to feel terrible about that."

"He entered Hogwarts illegally."

"Your Dementors entered it illegally," Harry said.

Fudge looked at him. Harry refused to look away.

"If Sirius goes back to Azkaban before the hearing, I go with him."

Hermione sat upright. "Harry."

"That is absurd," Fudge said.

"So is sending an innocent man back."

"You are a child."

"I'm the child whose parents you said he betrayed."

Sirius tightened his hand. Harry continued.

"You used my name when you wanted people frightened of him. You told everyone he escaped to kill me. If my opinion matters when it helps the Ministry, it matters now."

Fudge's face reddened further. Lucius looked almost proud.

"Mr Potter's statement will receive considerable public interest," he said.

"You will keep the boy away from the press."

Harry turned. "Will I?"

Lucius met his eyes.

"No," he conceded. "Probably not."

Dumbledore placed a parchment upon the desk.

"Cornelius, suspend the warrant authorising the Dementor's Kiss and transfer Sirius into Hogwarts custody pending an emergency hearing."

"I cannot suspend it without review."

"You are the Minister."

"That does not make me a dictator."

Moody made a choking sound. McGonagall removed her spectacles and cleaned them with movements sharp enough to damage the glass. Fudge looked around the ward. No ally appeared. Even Snape stood beside the window in silence. His face remained hostile whenever it turned towards Sirius, but he offered the Minister no support.

Fudge retrieved his hat from the floor.

"I can issue a seventy-two-hour suspension."

"Seven days," Lucius said.

"Three."

"The original investigation waited eleven hours before imposing a life sentence. Surely you can spare seven days to reconsider it."

Fudge glared.

"Five."

"Seven."

"Six."

"Seven," Lucius repeated. "And I will allow your office to announce the review before the Prophet learns why it became necessary."

Fudge stared at him. Then his shoulders lowered by half an inch.

"Seven."

Lucius smiled faintly. "Done."

Fudge summoned a quill. The warrant appeared upon the desk, a dense sheet of official parchment edged in red. He signed beneath a paragraph Dumbledore dictated, suspending active enforcement of Sirius's arrest warrant and every authority granted to the Dementors until a formal hearing concluded. The moment the quill lifted, the parchment flashed blue. Moody examined it with his magical eye.

"Binding."

Fudge looked towards Sirius.

"This does not exonerate you."

"No," Sirius said. "That would require admitting you were wrong."

"The Wizengamot will determine—"

"The evidence," Hermione said.

Everyone turned. She pointed towards the sealed box upon Dumbledore's desk.

"Pettigrew's wand has not been examined."

Fudge's mouth tightened. "The confession has only just ended."

"Which is why its condition and spell sequence should be recorded before it leaves this room."

Moody grinned. "Knew there was a reason I liked her."

He placed a strip of numbered parchment across the desk, broke the evidence seal in full view of the room and lifted the wand with the blue cloth beneath it. A brass hoop emerged from one of his coat pockets. Seven stones were set around its rim.

"Auror spell-sequence analyser," he said when Harry looked at it. "Won't recreate a twelve-year-old crime. Will preserve what the wand did tonight."

Moody passed the wand through the hoop. The stones ignited one after another, and lines of cramped script burned themselves across the parchment. The instrument recorded a violent containment-ward feedback, several offensive curses and the unmistakable magical signature of the Killing Curse. Beside each entry appeared an estimate of the interval between casts. Before tonight's sequence lay a silence measured in years.

Lucius read the line identifying the Unforgivable. His face emptied.

Hermione leaned forward despite Draconia's hand upon her shoulder. "The feedback signature matches the cage's containment enchantment. The curse traces can be compared with the damage to the road and my injury."

"And with witness statements," Moody said. He signed the bottom of the strip, passed it to Dumbledore and then to Fudge. "Chain witnessed. Seal unbroken until examination. No need for clever children to steal it."

"I could have opened it," Hermione muttered.

"Never doubted it."

Moody returned the wand to the box and sealed it with a fresh sequence. Fudge stared at the record in his hand. It did not show the street from twelve years ago; Peter's extracted memory and confession would have to bear that weight. It did prove that the supposedly dead man had used an Unforgivable Curse within the hour.

"That," Hermione said, settling back against her pillows, "should assist the hearing."

"Anything else, Minister?"

Cornelius Fudge put on his hat. It remained crooked.

"No Dementors," he said. "Black stays within the castle. He surrenders any wand and does not leave the grounds. The hearing will take place within seven days."

"And Pettigrew?" Harry asked.

"Transferred under Auror guard."

"No Azkaban," Dumbledore said.

Fudge frowned. "He murdered twelve people."

"He also possesses information concerning Voldemort's surviving followers," Moody replied. "Dead men don't answer follow-up questions."

Peter began struggling again. Fear had returned as the Veritaserum weakened.

"No," he whispered. "No, I told you everything."

"Not yet," Moody said.

Fudge left without saying goodbye. McGonagall followed to ensure he reached the gates without altering any instructions. The hospital doors closed. Quiet settled. Peter looked at Harry.

"You spared me."

Harry was too tired to answer.

"You stopped Sirius from killing me," Peter continued. "James would have done the same. He would have shown mercy."

Harry moved from Sirius's bed. He approached the enclosure. Peter searched his face for resemblance and found none Harry intended to offer.

"I didn't save you for you."

Peter's expression crumpled.

"I saved him."

Harry glanced towards Sirius.

"And Hermione. And Draco. And everyone else who needed the truth more than you deserved silence."

"Harry—"

"You don't get to call me that."

Peter's mouth closed. Moody struck his staff against the floor. The transparent walls contracted around the bed, forming a sealed transport box. Peter disappeared behind cloudy glass.

"I'll take him," Moody said.

Dumbledore nodded.

"Alastor."

Moody paused.

"Alive."

"Planning on it."

Lucius approached the enclosure. Peter saw him through the glass. He raised one bound hand. Lucius looked at the man who had offered him the comfort of old loyalties. Then he turned away.

Moody struck the base of the prison with his staff. An authorised Auror transport sigil flared beneath it, blue fire rising around them without heat. Moody and the enclosure vanished together.

Peter Pettigrew was gone. This time, witnesses remained.

The moon set shortly before six. Behind the reinforced screens, fur withdrew from Lupin's body. The sound of bones returning to human shape carried through the hospital wing until Madam Pomfrey cast a Silencing Charm.

She permitted no one near him.

"He will sleep," she announced. "All of you will do the same."

No one argued. Even Snape accepted a bed after two potions weakened his determination to remain upright. Lucius sent for Narcissa, then occupied the chair beside Draconia until his head lowered briefly against the wall. Hermione slept beneath three blankets. One hand remained outside them, stretched towards Harry's bed. Draconia had fallen asleep sitting up. Her shoulder rested against the side of Hermione's mattress. Lucius's cloak covered them both.

Harry lay awake. His body ached in too many places to separate one pain from another. Dawn entered through the high windows and moved gradually across the floor. The castle remained quiet, unaware that its most famous murderer had become an innocent man and one of its heroes had returned to life as a traitor. Sirius slept beside him. Without anger animating his face, he looked older and younger at once. Azkaban had carved years into him, yet sleep loosened his features until Harry could find the boy in Lupin's photograph. The man his parents had chosen.

Harry watched the sunlight reach his bed. Sirius opened his eyes. He stared at the ceiling first. Confusion crossed his face. Then he turned and found Harry.

"Did we win?"

His voice was barely present. Harry considered the night. Peter was alive and captured. Lupin had survived. Hermione and Draconia were safe. The Dementors had withdrawn. The Ministry had signed a piece of parchment admitting, temporarily and with great reluctance, that it might have destroyed the wrong life.

"We haven't lost yet," Harry said.

Sirius smiled.

"That sounds like James."

"It's mine."

"Right."

Sirius shifted, pain interrupting the movement.

"What happens now?"

"There's a hearing."

"Terrifying."

"You have to remain at Hogwarts."

"Cruel."

"No wand."

"I didn't have mine anyway."

"And you're not allowed to leave the grounds."

Sirius looked towards the windows. The sun had begun turning the mountains gold.

"I can live with that."

Harry thought of the note he had sent into the forest.

We know Peter is alive.

Do not go after him alone.

Please.

Sirius had gone anyway. Harry had followed. Neither of them had demonstrated remarkable judgement.

"We need rules," Harry said.

Sirius's brows rose.

"Rules?"

"Hermione has procedures."

"That sounds more serious."

"Draco added a process for adding them."

"Can I appeal?"

"No."

"Can I ignore them?"

"Probably once."

Sirius looked offended. "James usually let me get away with it three times."

"My rules."

A quiet laugh moved through him. It became a cough. Harry reached for the water before Sirius could pretend he did not need it. Sirius drank. When he returned the glass, his hand shook.

"You stayed," he said.

"You were unconscious."

"You could have left."

Harry looked towards Hermione and Draconia. They had left because he asked. They had returned because they promised.

"I said I'd be here."

Sirius's fingers closed around the glass. No one had trusted him with promises for twelve years. Harry could see the weight of this small one settling inside him, too fragile to accept carelessly.

"I'm innocent," Sirius whispered.

The words did not sound triumphant. Harry nodded.

"Yes."

Sirius covered his eyes with one hand. His shoulders began to shake. Harry looked away, giving him the privacy of not being watched without pretending not to hear. Outside, morning spread over Hogwarts. The light reached the foot of Lupin's hidden bed, Hermione's outstretched hand and the pale hair resting against her blankets. It caught upon the old photograph Dumbledore had placed on the table between Harry and Sirius: four boys waving from another year, before fear taught them to mistake secrecy for protection.

Three of those boys remained alive. For the first time, all three were known by their true names. Harry reached across the space between the beds. Sirius lowered his hand. Their fingers met above the floor.

Neither of them let go.
