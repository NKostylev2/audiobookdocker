Prologue IV

By the time Hermione reached the three-headed dog, Harry had stopped hoping this would be a short conversation.

Professor McGonagall listened without interrupting. She stood behind her desk with both hands resting upon its polished surface, her lips pressed into a line that became thinner as Hermione explained. Morning sunlight fell across stacks of marked parchment. Harry kept his eyes on the topmost essay, trying not to wonder how much of their account would become a detention.

Hermione had rehearsed it twice. She began with what the newspaper actually said about the Gringotts break-in, described Hagrid's parcel and admitted how they had found the forbidden room. Then she explained why Nicolas Flamel's name had led them to the Philosopher's Stone. She called that part a conclusion rather than a discovery. Harry noticed the distinction. He had argued with it often enough when they first read about the vault.

When she reached the match, her voice became less certain. Snape and Quirrell had both been watching Harry, both had appeared to be saying something, and his broom had steadied after Snape knocked into Quirrell. She did not say either man had cast the jinx. Professor McGonagall waited until Hermione had finished.

"And when," she asked, "did any of you intend to tell me that you had opened a door you were expressly forbidden to approach?"

Hermione hesitated.

"We didn't go looking for the dog," Harry said. "Filch was coming, and the staircase had moved."

"Which was the first of several mistakes," Draconia added. Her usual precision did very little to improve how it sounded.

McGonagall's gaze moved between them. "I see."

Harry suspected that she did.

"What you have discovered," McGonagall continued, "was not intended to become student knowledge. However, Professor Dumbledore is already aware of the danger surrounding the Stone."

"Then someone has tried to steal it?" Hermione asked.

"That is not what I said."

"But—"

"Miss Granger."

Hermione stopped. McGonagall's expression softened, although her voice remained firm. "The object is protected by enchantments devised by several members of staff. More importantly, Professor Dumbledore is here. I assure you that no thief could find the Stone in safer hands."

"What if the thief is a member of staff?" Harry asked.

The softness disappeared. "That is a very serious accusation."

"We aren't accusing anyone," he said. "Not exactly."

"You have described two professors speaking while watching a match," McGonagall said. "You have not described hearing either of them cast a jinx."

"No," Hermione admitted.

"Professor Flitwick has examined the broom. The staff are aware of what happened, and Professor Snape was understandably curious as to why his robes caught fire."

Hermione went pale. Harry looked at her, then at the desk.

"That was me," she said. "I thought he was making Harry fall. I wanted him to stop."

For a moment, McGonagall said nothing. Harry could hear the class in the corridor beyond the closed door, somebody laughing as though the morning were perfectly ordinary.

"I understand that you were frightened," she said at last. "Casting a fire spell in a crowded stand could have injured several people. You will tell a member of staff what you have seen, Miss Granger. You will not attempt to deal with it by setting another member of staff alight."

"Yes, Professor."

Draconia's gaze stayed on the desk. McGonagall straightened.

"Both professors are trusted members of staff. You have given me no grounds to accuse either of theft. I will speak to Professor Dumbledore about what you have told me. You will abandon this investigation, avoid the third-floor corridor and permit your teachers to concern themselves with the school's security."

"But Professor—" Harry began.

"No."

The word ended the discussion. McGonagall moved around the desk and opened the classroom door. "You have demonstrated considerable initiative. Now demonstrate judgement. Return to your lessons."

The door closed behind them with a soft, final click. Hermione released the breath she had been holding and stood for a moment with her books pressed against her chest.

"Well," she said, "she knows now."

Draconia adjusted the strap of her bag. "Of course she knows."

"Then we did the right thing."

"We told an adult," Harry said. "We didn't learn anything."

"We learned that Dumbledore is protecting it."

"Hagrid already told us he put it there."

Hermione frowned. "Professor McGonagall would not dismiss a credible threat."

"She dismissed three first-years," Draconia said. "That is not the same."

The corridor filled as a class ended nearby. Students poured around them in clusters, carrying books and half-finished conversations. Harry stepped closer to the wall so they would not be separated. Hermione held her ground. "We agreed to tell her."

"We did," Harry said.

"And we agreed not to go beneath the trapdoor unless somebody else went first."

"We did."

Hermione's expression sharpened. "You're both saying that as though the second promise is about to become relevant."

"It isn't," Harry said.

Draconia looked at him.

"Not yet," he amended.

Hermione shut her eyes. "Why do I bother?"

"Because without you, our plans are considerably worse," Draconia said.

Hermione opened one eye. It was not an apology, but coming from Draconia, it was close enough. They went to Charms.

For nearly three months, Harry saw nothing to explain the broom or the parcel. Lessons continued, McGonagall asked him twice whether the Nimbus had given him any more trouble, and winter slowly loosened its hold upon the grounds.

Snow retreated into grey piles beneath the trees. The lake darkened, reflecting longer afternoons, and the first determined shoots appeared in Professor Sprout's greenhouses. Hogwarts moved towards spring with the restlessness of a school that had begun counting the weeks until examinations. The Stone remained hidden. They heard nothing more about the forbidden corridor. Hagrid avoided saying Nicolas Flamel's name, and no fresh disaster interrupted the ordinary movement of the school. Harry could go several hours without thinking about the Stone, then find himself watching Quirrell across the Great Hall while his breakfast cooled. The lack of danger ought to have reassured them. Instead, it made the threat harder to measure.

The newspaper said the thief had entered a high-security vault and escaped. Harry knew nothing about how long that had taken, or whether the same person had followed Hagrid's parcel here. It was the lack of answers that troubled him. There might be somebody waiting for a chance to steal the Stone; equally, the danger might have passed while the three of them continued filling notebooks with theories. Hermione disliked speculation without evidence. Draconia disliked assuming that silence meant safety. Harry disliked waiting for something he could not see. They reached an uneasy compromise: no more visits to the third floor, no direct accusations and no investigation obvious enough to earn another meeting with Professor McGonagall. Beyond those limitations, all three continued watching.

Snape remained the easiest suspect. He continued teaching Potions with a particular interest in Harry's mistakes. A cauldron positioned half an inch incorrectly became evidence of carelessness; a potion one shade lighter than the example earned a remark about inadequate preparation. When Harry produced a passable Swelling Solution, Snape inspected it for so long that Harry began to wonder whether success itself had become suspicious. Draconia's work received a different sort of attention.

"Acceptable, Miss Malfoy," Snape said one afternoon, pausing beside her cauldron.

The potion inside was exactly the clear green shown in their textbooks. Daphne Greengrass's mixture, at the neighbouring table, had turned a cloudy brown.

Draconia inclined her head. "Thank you, Professor."

Snape moved on. Harry leaned across the aisle once the professor was out of earshot. "Acceptable?"

"Yes."

"It's perfect."

Draconia glanced into the cauldron. "It lacks subtlety."

"It's a potion for making things swell."

"Then perhaps subtlety would be counterproductive," Hermione murmured.

Draconia looked at her. Hermione continued crushing dried nettles with determined innocence.

Harry lowered his voice. "He always praises you."

"No, he praises my surname while attempting not to insult my work."

"You don't like it."

"I dislike being used as proof that his judgement is impartial."

Across the dungeon, Snape stopped beside Neville's table. Draconia returned to her potion before he could look back. Harry had mistaken Draconia's reluctance to accuse Snape for approval of him. Watching her turn back to the cauldron, he began to understand the difference. It did nothing to make Snape kinder to Harry, or to explain why Quirrell seemed so frightened of the Potions Master. Harry began noticing how often the two professors crossed paths.

Snape left the Great Hall moments after Quirrell on three separate evenings. Once, Harry saw him follow the Defence professor into an empty corridor, his black robes disappearing around the corner. Another time, Quirrell emerged from the staff room so quickly that he nearly collided with Hermione. His face was grey, and one trembling hand rose to protect his turban. Snape appeared in the doorway behind him. Quirrell fled without apologising.

"He's threatening him," Hermione whispered.

They stood near a window overlooking the courtyard. Draconia watched Quirrell vanish down the stairs. "Probably."

Hermione turned. "You agree?"

"That Snape is threatening him? Yes."

"Then—"

"That does not tell us why."

Hermione's expression tightened. "What innocent reason could he have?"

"Perhaps Quirrell knows about the Stone."

"Or Snape is forcing him to reveal one of the protections."

"Or Snape knows Quirrell tried to steal it."

Harry looked between them. "Or they're working together."

Both girls considered this.

Draconia frowned. "Possible."

Hermione drew a folded piece of parchment from her bag and added a line to the growing list of theories.

"You brought that to class?" Harry asked.

"I bring it everywhere."

"What if someone finds it?"

"The names are abbreviated."

Harry looked over her shoulder.

SS threatening QQ. Possible disagreement concerning PS.

"That's not difficult to understand."

"You only understand it because you know what it means."

"That's how words usually work."

Hermione folded the parchment with unnecessary sharpness. Draconia reached over and took it. "You need a cipher."

"I do not need a cipher."

"She'll make you one anyway," Harry said.

By dinner, Draconia had. Their investigation gradually became part of the term's routine. Hermione searched the library for magical protections and recorded anything that could conceivably be used to guard a small, powerful object. Draconia studied Snape and Quirrell with an attention that would have been unsettling had either man realised it. Harry watched for the details both girls missed while concentrating upon larger theories. They discovered little.

Hagrid became incapable of meeting Harry's eyes whenever the third floor was mentioned. Snape continued watching Quirrell whenever he thought nobody had noticed. Quirrell continued filling his classroom with garlic and claimed it protected him from a vampire he had encountered in Romania, although Draconia pointed out that his descriptions of the country changed every time he told the story.

By the time the Easter holidays approached, Hermione's list had grown considerably longer without becoming much more useful. Homework multiplied, rain softened the grounds, and Harry had to scrape mud from his shoes before Madam Pince would let him near their usual table. Nothing they saw provided an answer. Then, one Wednesday morning, Hagrid sent another note.

Come down after lessons. Got summat ter show yeh.

Don't tell anyone.

Draconia read the final instruction and looked at Harry. "That has never preceded a sensible decision."

"He might've found something about the Stone," Harry said.

Hermione folded the note. "Or Fluffy."

Hagrid had no news about either mystery. He had a dragon egg in his fireplace. It lay among the coals, black and immense, its rough shell reflecting the flames in patches of dull bronze. The hut was hotter than Harry had ever known it. The windows were shut, the curtains drawn, and Hagrid pushed the door closed behind them before Harry had finished taking off his scarf.

Harry stopped just inside the hut. "Is that what I think it is?"

Hagrid checked that the door was latched. "Depends what yeh think it is."

"A dragon egg," Hermione said.

Hagrid's expression brightened. "Knew you'd recognise it."

"That wasn't admiration."

Draconia moved closer to the hearth. Heat shimmered above the egg. Its shell was ridged like hardened scales, and every few seconds something shifted faintly beneath the surface. "Where did you get it?" she asked.

"Won it."

"From whom?"

"Bloke in the pub."

Draconia looked at Harry. "That answer has somehow become worse through repetition."

Hagrid lowered himself into the chair beside the fire. A thick book lay open across his knees. "I've always wanted a dragon."

"You cannot keep one," Hermione said.

"It hasn't hatched yet."

"That does not improve the situation."

"Hogwarts is in Britain," Draconia added. "Private dragon breeding has been illegal since the Warlocks' Convention of 1709."

Hagrid stared at her.

"Everyone knows that."

"I didn't," Harry said.

Hermione looked at him. "It was in Hogwarts: A History."

"Then everyone but Harry knows it," Draconia amended.

Hagrid turned protectively towards the fire. "No one's breedin' anythin'. I've only got one."

"One dragon can still burn your house down," Hermione said.

Harry approached the hearth. The egg looked too large to have come from any creature capable of fitting inside Hagrid's hut. He held his hand near it and felt the fierce heat rising from the shell. "What sort is it?"

Hagrid eagerly turned the book around. "Norwegian Ridgeback."

The illustration showed a dark-scaled dragon with wide wings and a line of sharp ridges along its spine. Beneath it, the text warned that Ridgebacks were unusually aggressive towards their own kind. Draconia read this aloud.

Hagrid waved one hand. "Books exaggerate."

Hermione scanned the next paragraph. "It says their venomous fangs develop within a month."

"Only little fangs."

"It breathes fire."

"Eventually."

"Hagrid," Harry said, trying not to smile, "where are you going to put it?"

The hut fell quiet. Hagrid looked towards his bed. Hermione followed his gaze. "No."

"Only while it's little."

"No."

"I've made room."

"You moved one chair."

"It were a large chair."

Despite their objections, all three returned to the hut two days later when the egg began to crack. Hagrid's note reached them during breakfast, written so hastily that the words sloped off the parchment.

It's hatching.

Hermione reminded them that their Transfiguration assignment was due tomorrow. Draconia pointed out that none of them would concentrate while imagining a dragon emerging beside Hagrid's wooden furniture. Harry was already standing. They left as soon as breakfast ended, their bags thumping against their sides as they ran across the wet grounds.

Inside the hut, the egg shuddered among the coals. Cracks travelled over its shell in branching lines. Hagrid knelt before the fireplace with a blanket held open in both hands, his beard tied back to prevent it catching fire. "Nearly here," he whispered.

The shell split. A pointed snout emerged first, followed by a small black head. The creature forced itself through the opening with furious jerks, scattering pieces of shell into the hearth. Wings unfolded against its damp body. They were too large for it, all thin membrane and delicate bones, while its narrow tail lashed behind it. The dragon stood unsteadily. Smoke drifted from its nostrils. Hagrid made a sound of pure delight. "Isn't he beautiful?"

The dragon sneezed. A jet of sparks caught the end of Hagrid's beard, and Hermione scrambled for her wand while Harry grabbed the blanket. Between them, they put it out before more than a few hairs had burned. Hagrid looked from the smoking blanket to the small black creature and announced that he would call him Norbert.

Harry crouched near the hearth. The dragon was not beautiful in any ordinary sense. Its head was too large, its wings were wet and wrinkled, and its orange eyes protruded slightly from its skull.

Even so, Harry could not stop looking. A few minutes ago, there had been nothing but an egg. Now the creature blinked at him, spread its astonishingly thin wings and tried to stand upon a piece of shell. Harry offered one finger before thinking about the teeth.

"Don't," Hermione warned.

The dragon sniffed him and turned away.

"He likes yeh," Hagrid said.

Draconia crouched on Harry's other side and reached cautiously towards a folded wing. Norbert snapped at her fingers. She pulled her hand back. "Nasty little—"

"He likes you too," Hagrid said.

"He has an imaginative way of showing it."

Within a week, Norbert had doubled in size. Within two, he occupied most of the space between Hagrid's bed and the table. His wings knocked cups from shelves. His tail left dents in the furniture. The first time he breathed a proper flame, he set the tablecloth alight. Hagrid called it an accident. The second time, Norbert watched the cloth burn with obvious satisfaction.

"He cannot stay here," Hermione said.

They had returned after dinner to find Hagrid attempting to fit a harness around the dragon's chest. Norbert resisted by biting the leather and beating his wings until ash filled the hut.

"Hagrid's house is stone," Harry pointed out, shielding his face.

"The roof is wooden."

"So's the door," Hagrid added, before realising this did not help.

Norbert sneezed. Smoke poured into Hermione's hair. She coughed and tightened the rope around the reinforced crate waiting beside the table. Hagrid stroked the dragon's ridged head. "He's only little."

"He has grown six inches since Sunday."

"Healthy appetite."

"He ate one of your boots," Draconia said.

"Only the left one."

Norbert lunged for the buckle on Hagrid's remaining boot. Hagrid moved it out of reach. Draconia crouched beside the empty crate, keeping it between herself and the dragon. Norbert leaned around the corner until one orange eye aligned with hers. She drew her sleeve out of reach of his teeth.

"Where did you obtain the egg?" she asked.

Hagrid's hands stopped upon the harness. "I told yeh. Won it."

"From whom?"

"Feller in the pub."

"What sort of person carries a dragon egg into a pub?"

"A traveller."

"You accepted an illegal dragon from an unidentified traveller?"

"When yeh say it like that, it sounds suspicious."

"There is no way to say it that doesn't."

Harry watched Hagrid. The egg had seemed like one more example of his fondness for dangerous creatures. Now, with the Stone hidden inside the castle, the circumstances felt different. "Did you see his face?"

Hagrid frowned, thinking back. "Not properly. He wore a hood. Lot of folk do in the Hog's Head."

"Did he know who you were?" Hermione asked.

"Don' think so."

"But he knew you liked dragons."

"I told him."

Draconia's attention sharpened. "Before or after he offered the egg?"

Hagrid opened his mouth. Closed it. "Before," he admitted.

Harry felt the old unease return. "What did you talk about?"

"Creatures, mostly. He said it must be difficult, lookin' after all the things I do. Asked what sort I'd handled."

"And you told him?" Hermione asked.

"Nothin' important."

"Hagrid," Harry said.

Hagrid looked offended. "I didn't tell him anythin' secret."

"You told us Nicolas Flamel's name while trying not to tell us anything."

"That was different."

"How?"

"There weren't a dragon involved."

Draconia rose. "What did the stranger ask about?"

Hagrid busied himself with the harness. Norbert scratched at the crate. "He might've asked what the largest creature I'd ever looked after was."

Nobody spoke. Hagrid looked towards the stove. "I mentioned Fluffy."

Hermione's hands stopped upon the rope. "What exactly did you tell him?"

"Only that he was a three-headed dog. Feller didn' believe me at first."

"So you convinced him," Draconia said.

Hagrid's ears turned red. "I didn' tell him where Fluffy was."

"That isn't what she asked," Harry said.

Hagrid looked deeply unhappy. "He asked how anyone could control a beast like that. I only said any animal can be managed if yeh know what calms it."

The silence inside the hut changed. Outside, wind moved through the trees at the edge of the forest. Norbert scraped one claw across the floor. Hermione's voice became very careful. "What calms Fluffy?"

Hagrid looked at her, then at Draconia and finally, at Harry.

"I shouldn' say."

Harry's stomach tightened. "What did you tell the stranger?"

Hagrid lowered himself slowly into his chair. "Music," he admitted. "Play a bit o' music, and Fluffy drops straight ter sleep."

Nobody spoke. Norbert gave a soft, rasping chirp. Hagrid seemed to hear his own words properly for the first time. "He didn' know Fluffy was at Hogwarts," he said quickly.

"He knew you worked here," Hermione replied.

"I might not've told him that."

"You are more than eight feet tall," Draconia said. "There cannot be many Hogwarts gamekeepers matching your description."

Hagrid's face fell. Harry looked at the dragon, then at the man who had been so delighted to receive him. The egg might have been exactly what Hagrid thought: a prize won from another enthusiast. But if the stranger had wanted to know about Fluffy, there could hardly have been a better way to make Hagrid talk. Harry looked towards the castle through the small window. Stone towers rose beyond the rain, familiar and enormous. Somewhere inside them, Fluffy guarded the entrance to the Stone.

"We have to tell Dumbledore," Hermione said.

Hagrid surged to his feet. "No."

All three turned towards him. "No one's tellin' anyone," he insisted. "Dumbledore trusted me with Fluffy. If he finds out I told some stranger—"

"He needs to know," Hermione said.

"I'll tell him."

"When?"

Hagrid looked away.

"Hagrid," Harry said.

"I'll handle it."

"You can't handle whoever broke into Gringotts."

"We don't know it were the same person."

Draconia folded her arms. "You do not actually believe that."

Hagrid's shoulders slumped. Before anyone could press further, Norbert climbed onto the table and bit through the handle of the teapot. Hermione shut her eyes. "There is still the matter of the dragon."

That problem, at least, had an answer. It took them longer to settle the other one. Hermione would not leave until Hagrid promised to tell Dumbledore what he had said in the pub. Harry stayed beside her, though watching Hagrid struggle with the promise felt much worse than asking questions had. The following day, Hagrid confirmed that he had spoken to the Headmaster. McGonagall also received Hermione's written account, which Draconia insisted should distinguish the stranger's questions from their suspicions about him.

Hagrid would tell them only that Dumbledore knew and the Stone was still protected. Harry wanted that to be enough. He tried to remember it whenever the thought of the hooded stranger returned.

Fred and George Weasley had an older brother who worked with dragons in Romania. Harry approached them after dinner. The twins listened without interruption while he explained that someone he knew had acquired a Norwegian Ridgeback. He did not mention Hagrid, but Fred guessed before Harry finished the first sentence.

"We always said it would be a dragon," George remarked.

"Lee thought manticore."

"Too temperamental."

"Not Hagrid. The manticore."

They agreed to contact Charlie. Four days later, a reply arrived. Charlie could arrange for several friends travelling through Scotland to collect Norbert from the highest tower at midnight. The dragon needed to be crated, secured and brought to them without attracting attention. Hermione spent the day identifying everything wrong with the plan. Draconia spent the same day correcting those flaws. Harry took the Invisibility Cloak from his trunk. A little before eleven, the three of them met Hagrid outside his hut. Norbert occupied a reinforced crate wrapped in chains. Smoke curled between the slats. Every few seconds, the box lurched sideways as the dragon threw himself against one wall. Hagrid stood beside it with red eyes.

"I packed his teddy bear," he said.

Hermione looked through the gap. A charred stuffed animal lay beneath one claw. "That was kind."

"And plenty o' rats for the journey."

"That was less kind," Draconia said.

Hagrid bent over the crate. "Now, yeh be good fer Charlie. Plenty of open space in Romania. Other dragons ter play with."

"He'll try to kill them," Hermione said.

Draconia nudged her. "What?" Hermione asked.

"Let him have the moment."

Hagrid pressed one enormous hand against the wood. Norbert bit at his fingers through the gap. Hagrid began crying. Harry looked towards the castle. The crate was heavier than the three of them could comfortably carry. Hermione and Draconia had practised levitating it while it was empty; now they would have to keep it steady with Norbert inside. Hagrid had offered to carry it, but the route would take him past the staff windows, and he could hardly disappear beneath Harry's cloak. He had already earned a very stern conversation about the egg.

Draconia approached Hagrid. Awkwardly, she put one arm around as much of him as she could reach. Hagrid froze. Then he gathered her into a hug large enough to lift her from the ground. Harry saw the exact instant Draconia regretted her decision.

"Hagrid," she gasped.

"Sorry."

He put her down. Her hair had become tangled in his coat, and her carefully fastened cloak now sat sideways across her shoulders. Hermione hugged him next. Harry did too.

"We'll make sure he gets there," Harry promised.

Hagrid helped them settle the cloak over their heads and the crate. It covered everything only while they remained close together. Hermione and Draconia cast the levitation charm, lifting the box just far enough to take most of its weight. Harry held the front rope and guided it towards the castle, stopping whenever a movement from Norbert pulled an edge of the cloak away from the wood.

The first flight of stairs taught them how little resemblance carrying the crate bore to practising with it. Harry had to pull at each turn while the girls corrected the spell, their shoulders pressed against his and their whispered instructions becoming increasingly sharp. By the fifth floor, his arms burned. By the sixth, Hermione was threatening to leave Norbert in the next broom cupboard.

A tongue of flame slipped through the slats as Draconia reached to steady a corner. It caught the exposed cuff of her sleeve. Hermione let the crate settle on the landing and beat the small flame out with the end of her scarf. For several seconds, they stood perfectly still, listening for anyone who might have heard them. Draconia stared at the blackened hole in her cloak. "I hope Romania is freezing."

They reached the highest tower minutes before midnight. The night beyond the battlements was cold and clear. Stars spread above the mountains, brighter than Harry had ever seen them from Surrey. Far below, Hogwarts rested in darkness. Four broomsticks appeared against the moon. Charlie's friends landed quietly. They fastened the crate beneath their brooms with a harness and checked every chain. Norbert fought the entire process, snarling whenever anyone came close. At last, the travellers mounted again. The crate rose and Hagrid's dragon ascended into the night, swinging beneath the broomsticks and releasing thin trails of smoke. His angry cries diminished as the group crossed above the grounds.

Harry watched until Norbert became a small black shape among the stars. Hermione stood beside him, exhausted but visibly relieved. "He'll be safer there."

"Everyone will be safer with him there," Draconia said.

She touched the burned sleeve of her cloak. Harry smiled. "He liked you."

"I hope he remembers me fondly from an enormous distance."

They began the long descent beneath the cloak, which felt enormous now that the crate was gone. None of them spoke about the stranger in the pub. Harry kept thinking about him anyway: a hood, an egg and a few friendly questions that had left Hagrid looking ashamed. Whoever he was, he knew how to put Fluffy to sleep.

Dumbledore had been told. Harry repeated that to himself as they made their way down. It did not tell him whether the stranger had tried to reach the Stone, or whether he was still waiting.

Their final weeks of lessons disappeared beneath revision. Professors assigned essays as though determined to prevent anyone enjoying the sunlight. Hermione's revision timetable took possession of their afternoons. Draconia complained that examinations were a crude measurement of intelligence, then prepared for them with ruthless precision.

Harry watched the third-floor turning whenever his route took him past it. He went no closer than they had promised. Snape and Quirrell continued teaching. The waiting became almost ordinary again, though never comfortable. It sat beneath every class and meal, a question none of them could answer: if the thief now knew how to pass Fluffy, what were they waiting for? Spring deepened around the castle. Examinations arrived before anything they could recognise as an attempt on the Stone.

Different colours on the timetable represented subjects, urgency and Harry's likelihood of becoming distracted. Draconia objected to being assigned scheduled breaks, then followed them precisely. Harry learned to keep his hand over the corner of the parchment; leaving it uncovered invited Hermione to add another useful half-hour. Harry discovered that revising with both girls was unpleasant but effective. Hermione insisted he understand every principle. Draconia identified which principles professors actually examined. Between them, there was no available route towards laziness.

"You have omitted the nettles," Hermione said.

"They're implied."

"Ingredients cannot be implied into a potion."

Draconia looked over Harry's shoulder. "The nettles are there."

Hermione pointed to his parchment. "That is a smudge."

"It says nettles."

"It looks like 'needles'."

Harry studied his own writing. "I know what I meant."

"The examiner won't."

"Then I'll explain."

"You cannot explain a written examination after submitting it."

Draconia tapped the page. "Rewrite the line."

Harry reached for a clean sheet. "Not the entire essay," Hermione said.

"I can't fit it between those two."

"You could if you wrote more neatly."

Draconia leaned back in her chair. "We have finally identified an impossible form of magic."

Harry threw the discarded parchment at her. She caught it without looking. Hermione tried to remain disapproving and failed. Those afternoons became Harry's favourite part of the term. Not because of the work. He would never have admitted this within Hermione's hearing, but revision remained a slow and unnecessarily ink-heavy form of suffering. It was the certainty of finding them there that mattered.

The same library table waited every day. Hermione arrived with books stacked against her chin. Draconia came from the dungeons with green parchment and sweets stolen from the Slytherin common room. Harry brought whatever food he had rescued from lunch and, occasionally, the correct textbook.

Around them, the year narrowed towards summer. They spoke about the holiday only when forced. Hermione would return to her parents. Draconia would go to Malfoy Manor. Harry would return to Privet Drive. He knew exactly how the kitchen would look and which expression Uncle Vernon would be wearing. Remembering it at their sunlit library table made the coming holiday feel much closer. Both girls disliked this.

Hermione expressed her dislike by developing plans. She wrote down the Dursleys' address and made Harry repeat the Grangers' telephone number, though he doubted his aunt would let him near the telephone. When he pointed that out, Hermione said it was still better to know it. She also reminded him three times that Hedwig could find her without needing a street address. Draconia expressed hers through increasingly hostile silence.

"They cannot prevent an owl from reaching you," she said one evening.

"They can stop me sending one back."

"Then use the school owls before the train leaves."

"For the whole summer?"

Draconia's quill pressed harder against her parchment. "There are other methods."

Hermione looked up. "Such as?"

"I'll determine one."

"You don't know how Muggle post works."

"It cannot be exceptionally complex."

"You need stamps."

"I can purchase stamps."

"With Muggle money."

"My family possesses Muggle money."

"Why?"

Draconia looked offended by the question. "Emergency bribery, presumably."

Harry laughed. Neither girl joined him. He set down his quill. "I'll write."

Hermione's gaze held his. "Even if they make it difficult?"

"Yes."

Draconia watched him for another second. "Good."

It sounded like a warning directed at the Dursleys. Harry returned to his essay, then stopped to write the Grangers' telephone number more carefully on a spare piece of parchment. Neither girl commented. He put it in the pocket of his bag where he kept their notes. The final examination ended on a hot June afternoon. Students burst from the classroom with the giddy relief of prisoners released early. Hermione immediately began reviewing her answers aloud. Draconia informed her that discussing completed examinations was a form of self-torture.

"You said that after Transfiguration," Hermione replied.

"It remains true."

"You discussed question six for twenty minutes."

"Because the wording was indefensible."

Harry walked between them, enjoying the sunlight and understanding very little about either complaint. They had reached the courtyard when an owl swooped over the wall. It carried a scarlet envelope. The letter descended towards Professor McGonagall, who was crossing the grounds below. She caught it with visible surprise. The seal broke the moment it touched her hand. A man's voice erupted across the courtyard.

"—MINISTRY REQUIRES THE HEADMASTER'S IMMEDIATE PRESENCE—"

Students stopped. McGonagall crushed the letter shut and hurried towards the castle. Harry felt every pleasant thought leave him.

"That could be genuine," Hermione said.

"Yes," Draconia replied.

"But we don't think it is."

"No."

Harry looked towards the castle doors. "They're getting Dumbledore away."

Hermione caught his arm. "We don't know that."

"Hagrid told someone how to get past Fluffy."

"Months ago."

"They've been waiting."

Draconia's eyes narrowed. "For examinations."

Harry turned to her. "The staff are occupied," she said. "Students are celebrating. Everyone expects the danger to have passed with the year."

Hermione released Harry's arm. "We tell McGonagall."

They found her office empty. At the foot of the stairs, a prefect told them McGonagall had gone to the gates with Dumbledore, who was leaving for the Ministry. Harry ran towards the great oak doors before Hermione could finish thanking him.

By the time they reached the gates, the Headmaster was gone. They did not see McGonagall on the path back to the castle. They hurried back to her office, but the classroom was empty and the adjoining door remained locked. Harry knocked until his knuckles hurt.

"Snape," Hermione said.

Draconia looked at her sharply.

"If he isn't the thief," Hermione continued, "he needs to know."

They ran to the dungeons. Snape's classroom was empty, and no voice answered when Harry hammered on the office door. Through its narrow window, he could see shelves of ingredients and a darkened lamp. He could not tell whether Snape had gone out or simply refused to answer.

"Quirrell," Harry said.

They searched the Defence Against the Dark Arts classroom. Empty. Harry stood in the corridor, breathless and sweating beneath his robes. "It's happening now."

Hermione looked towards the windows. Afternoon light still filled the castle. Students laughed somewhere below them, unaware. "We find another teacher," she said.

"And tell them what?" Draconia asked. "That Dumbledore received a message and two professors are absent from their rooms?"

"The truth."

"The truth has not changed since McGonagall dismissed us."

Hermione rounded on her. "Going beneath the trapdoor is not a better plan."

"We promised not to unless somebody else went first."

"We don't know that they have."

Harry removed Draconia's compass from his pocket. The brass was warm from his hand. He pictured the third-floor corridor, and the needle settled towards it.

Hermione looked from the compass to his face. "That tells us where the room is, Harry. It doesn't tell us whether anyone's gone in."

"I know."

"Then why are you holding it like an answer?"

Harry had no answer to that. Draconia touched the edge of the brass casing.

"It finds places," she said. "That's what it's meant to do."

Harry closed his hand around the compass. "I'm going."

Hermione's face changed. "Harry."

"I can't sit in the library while someone takes the Stone."

"You can if the alternative is being killed."

"What if they use it to bring Voldemort back?"

The name echoed through the corridor. Hermione flinched. Draconia did not, although some of the colour left her face.

"We don't know that," Hermione said.

"I know we don't. But Voldemort killed my parents. Hagrid said he's still out there somewhere. The Stone can keep someone alive, and someone tried to throw me off a broom. I can't stop thinking those things might be connected."

Harry looked from one girl to the other. "I'm going."

Draconia stepped to his side. "So am I."

Hermione stared at them. For a terrible second, Harry thought she might refuse. Instead, she shut her eyes and exhaled.

"Then you'll need somebody who can recognise what we're walking into."

Draconia's mouth twitched. "There she is."

Hermione opened her eyes. "If we do this, we do exactly what I say when we encounter unfamiliar magic."

"What if you don't understand it?" Harry asked.

"Then we listen to Draconia."

Draconia inclined her head.

"And if neither of us understands it," Hermione continued, "we leave."

Harry hesitated.

"Harry."

"We try to leave."

Hermione looked towards the ceiling as though requesting patience from it. "That is the best agreement we're going to get," Draconia said.

"Yes," Hermione replied. "I know."

They went first to Gryffindor Tower. Draconia waited on the stairs below the portrait while Harry fetched the cloak and Hagrid's flute. Hermione followed him only as far as the common room, where she wrote a hurried note with the facts they knew and the place they meant to check. Neville was coming down from the dormitory when they left. Hermione pressed the note into his hand.

"Find Professor McGonagall," she said. "Or Professor Flitwick. Please, Neville. Tell them we think someone is going after the Stone. We're going to the third floor."

Neville looked at Harry, then at the cloak bundled beneath his arm. "You shouldn't go up there."

"I know," Hermione said. "That's why we need you to find somebody."

He did not look reassured, but he took the note and hurried away. Harry watched him go. Help might come; for one moment, he nearly decided to wait for it. Then he thought of the empty classrooms and the Headmaster disappearing beyond the gates.

The third-floor door stood open. Harry stopped several paces from it. They had locked it behind them months ago, and since then every glimpse he had caught showed it shut. A growl sounded within. This time, he could not tell himself they had merely made the danger up. They entered beneath the Invisibility Cloak, pressed so closely together that Hermione's hair tickled Harry's face and Draconia's elbow dug into his ribs. Harry carried Hagrid's flute in one hand. A harp lay on its side near the wall, its strings still. Fluffy was awake. All three heads turned towards the moving disturbance in the doorway. Three noses lifted. Saliva hung in ropes from yellow teeth. "He can smell us," Draconia whispered.

Harry uncovered the flute. His first note came out thin and breathless. Fluffy growled.

"Keep playing," Hermione said.

Harry tried again. The tune was simple and uneven. He knew only the melody Hagrid had taught him at Christmas, and fear made his fingers clumsy. Yet the effect upon Fluffy was immediate.

One head sagged, then the second. The third fought longest, blinking heavily while Harry continued playing. At last, its jaw slackened against the floor. The great dog began to snore. They edged between its paws, keeping clear of the thick claws while Harry struggled to remember the next notes. Hermione slipped out from beneath the cloak and folded it into her bag. The trapdoor had already been opened. Draconia looked into the darkness below. "How far?"

Hermione pointed her wand into the opening. "Lumos."

The light caught the rough stone sides and faded before Harry could see the bottom. Hermione swallowed. "I can't see the floor."

Harry lowered the flute for a breath. The music stopped, and Fluffy's middle head stirred.

"Go," Draconia said.

Harry tucked the flute into his robe and jumped. Cold air rushed past him before he struck something soft and wet. Vines closed around his arms before he could understand what had caught him. Hermione landed nearby with a gasp. Draconia dropped beside them. The plant suddenly began tightening. Harry fought instinctively. Thick tendrils wrapped his chest and dragged him deeper into the mass.

"Stop moving," Hermione said.

"It's crushing me."

"It responds to movement."

Draconia had already gone still. Vines bound her shoulders and waist, but they had begun loosening as her body relaxed. Harry tried to imitate her. A tendril tightened around his throat. Panic made stillness impossible.

"Hermione."

"I know."

Hermione's wand arm remained free. She twisted against the plant, trying to remember. "It likes darkness and damp."

"Then stop describing it and tell us what hurts it," Draconia said through clenched teeth.

"No, this one—Devil's Snare. It hates sunlight."

Hermione raised her wand. "Lumos Solem!"

White-gold light flooded the chamber. The vines recoiled. Harry fell several feet to the stone floor and landed upon his shoulder. Draconia dropped beside him more gracefully; Hermione followed, tangled in her own robes. For several seconds, they lay beneath the shrinking vines. Harry looked at Hermione. "You knew what it was."

She was breathing too hard to appear pleased. "I read."

Draconia pushed herself upright. "Remind me never to criticise the greenhouse chapters again."

Hermione stared at her. "That may be the nicest thing you've ever said to me."

"I nearly suffocated. My judgement is impaired."

They continued. The next chamber glittered with wings. Hundreds of keys circled beneath the high ceiling, their silver and bronze bodies flashing through dusty light. A broom rested upon the floor. Beyond the swarm stood a locked door. Harry smiled despite everything. Hermione saw it. "No unnecessary dives."

"There's a door between us and the Stone."

"That makes the dive necessary, not sensible."

Draconia examined the keys. "One has been caught before."

Harry followed her gaze. Near the ceiling, a large silver key flew unevenly. One blue wing was bent. He mounted the broom. The moment his feet left the ground, fear became speed. Harry climbed into the swarm. Keys scattered around him, their wings beating against his face and hands. Several struck his shoulders with enough force to bruise, but the broom responded perfectly. The damaged key turned sharply. Harry followed, ignoring the smaller keys striking his knuckles. It dived towards the floor. He flattened himself against the handle, reached and closed his fingers around cold metal.

The key struggled viciously. Harry landed. Hermione caught the broom before he struck the wall. Draconia closed both hands over the key's wings. Together, they forced it into the lock. A giant chessboard waited beyond it. Draconia stopped at the threshold. Stone pieces stood upon squares large enough to hold a person, the black army facing white beneath a ceiling lost in darkness. Several had cracked faces or chipped weapons. Broken stone lay along the edge of the board, too pale and clean to have gathered much dust. Harry looked at the rubble, then at the open passage beyond the white king. "Someone came through," Harry said.

Draconia stepped onto a white square. The black pieces lifted their weapons. "We have to play."

Hermione examined the opposing ranks. "Can you?"

Draconia looked at her.

Hermione sighed. "Of course you can."

Draconia studied the black ranks, then directed Harry to a bishop's square and Hermione to a rook's. When they approached, the corresponding stone figures stepped backwards off the board. Draconia took the queen's place. Its carved face looked down at her for a moment before it, too, withdrew.

Harry stood where she had told him, trying to remember everything Neville had taught him before the Christmas holidays. He knew how his piece moved. He was much less certain what would happen if somebody captured it. The game began. Stone moved with the scrape of graves opening. Draconia directed them quietly. She did not hesitate. Her eyes moved across the board, calculating lines Harry could not see and consequences several turns away. Pieces obeyed. Knights charged. Pawns broke beneath stone swords. Each victory left rubble across the squares. Harry followed every instruction. Hermione questioned only one.

"If I move there, the bishop takes me."

"It won't."

"How do you know?"

"Because then Harry takes the bishop."

Hermione looked across the board at him.

Draconia's voice sharpened. "Move."

Hermione did. The bishop remained still. The white queen moved instead. Draconia had expected it.

"Harry, three squares forward and left. Diagonally."

He obeyed. The board tightened. More pieces fell. Their path towards the king narrowed until Harry began to understand the shape of Draconia's plan. It required the black queen to move into danger.

"No," he said.

Draconia did not look at him.

"Harry—"

"No."

Hermione followed his gaze. The white knight waited two squares from Draconia.

"If she moves there," Harry said, "it'll take her."

"Yes."

Draconia's voice remained calm.

Hermione searched the board. "There has to be another move."

"There are several. They all end with us losing."

"Then we find another."

"There isn't time."

The white queen raised its sword, waiting. Harry started to step beyond the edge of his square. Every piece on the board turned towards him.

"Get back," Draconia snapped.

"I'm not letting it hit you."

"If you leave your square, we forfeit."

"I don't care."

"I do."

Her composure cracked. For the first time, Harry heard her voice rise. She was looking at him rather than at the knight, and that frightened him almost as much as the weapon.

"Listen to me. This is what I'm good at."

Harry stopped. Draconia looked across the board at him. "You trusted Hermione with the vines. You trusted yourself with the keys. Trust me now."

The white pieces waited. Hermione's face had gone pale. "What happens after?" she asked.

"Harry moves. The king is exposed. You checkmate it."

"And you?"

Draconia glanced towards the knight. "I'll try to duck."

It was a terrible attempt at humour. Harry's throat tightened. "There has to be a way for all three of us."

"Not through this room."

Draconia stepped towards the required square. Harry moved again. Her eyes flashed. "Potter."

He froze. "Finish it."

Draconia entered the square. The white knight charged. Its stone arm swung. Draconia turned, but not quickly enough. The weapon struck her shoulder and threw her across the board. She hit the floor and did not move.

"Draco!"

"Harry!" Hermione's voice broke. "Your move!"

He could not look away. Draconia lay among fragments of a shattered pawn. One arm was folded beneath her. Her pale hair covered her face. Harry forced himself to look at the board. Draconia had told him where to go. He moved diagonally onto the square, and the white king turned towards him.

"Check," Hermione said, her voice shaking.

The king retreated to the only square left open. Harry looked back at her. She understood at the same moment he did and stepped down her file, bringing the king into her rook's line. Harry's bishop covered the last escape square.

"Checkmate."

The white king dropped its sword. Every piece became still. Harry ran. He crossed the board without waiting for permission and fell to his knees beside Draconia. Hermione reached them a heartbeat later.

"She's breathing," Hermione said.

Harry pushed the hair from Draconia's face. A bruise was already darkening near her temple. "Draconia?"

Her eyelids moved. "Did we win?" she murmured.

Harry laughed once, the sound unsteady. "Yeah."

"Obviously."

Her eyes closed again.

Hermione examined her shoulder. "We can't take her with us."

"I'm not leaving her."

"They've conceded. Harry, look at them. The king's put down its sword."

"What if they start again?"

"They won't."

"You don't know that."

"No," Hermione said. "But we can't take her through more rooms like this. We have to get help."

Harry looked down at Draconia.

Finish it.

He hated that she had understood what the board required. He hated more that she had been right. Hermione's hand closed around his. "We'll come back for her."

Harry nodded only after Hermione had checked Draconia's breathing again. They eased her clear of the broken stone, and Hermione took the Invisibility Cloak from her bag, and Harry folded it beneath Draconia's head. Her good hand closed weakly around his fingers, then loosened.

They left her beside the silent chessboard. Harry kept looking back until the doorway hid her from sight. The next chamber contained a long table, seven bottles and two walls of magical fire. Purple flame sealed the door behind them. Black flame blocked the path ahead. A roll of parchment lay beside the bottles. Hermione read it.

"What is it?" Harry asked.

"Logic."

"Can you solve it?"

Her eyes moved between the bottles. "Yes."

Harry waited. Hermione kept the bottles where they were and compared their sizes with the clues on the parchment. Twice she reached towards one, stopped and read the relevant line again. Her lips moved as she eliminated each possibility. Harry looked back at the purple fire. Draconia waited beyond it. "How long?"

"Stop talking."

He stopped. Hermione touched the smallest bottle. "This one carries a person through the black flame."

Harry examined it. There was scarcely enough liquid for a mouthful.

"And the way back?"

Hermione selected a round bottle at the opposite end. "This one."

Harry looked between them. "Only one of us can go forward."

"Yes."

Harry touched the table beside the smaller bottle. "I'll take this one."

"Harry, I know more magic."

"And you'll need it to get Draconia out."

"That doesn't mean you should be the one going on."

"Somebody has to go back," he said. "You'll get her out faster than I will."

Hermione's hand tightened around the smallest bottle. "You always do this."

"What?"

"Make yourself the last person."

Harry thought of the platform barrier, the troll and the broom throwing him above the pitch. This time, he had thought. Draconia lay unconscious because she had chosen to trust him with what came after. Hermione had solved the path and held the only way back.

"I need you to get Draconia and find a teacher. If Neville hasn't found one, you will."

"You need me here."

"I need you both safe."

"That isn't your decision."

"No," Harry said quietly. "It's yours."

Hermione looked towards the black fire. Her jaw tightened, and Harry waited for another reason he should turn back. She had plenty. He knew that. But then she looked towards the purple flames, and he remembered Draconia's fingers closing around his.

Harry held out his hand, unsure how else to say goodbye. Hermione did not take it. She stepped forward and wrapped her arms around him, holding so tightly that he had to put one hand against the table to keep his balance.

"You come back," she said into his shoulder.

"I'll try."

"No."

Hermione pulled away enough to look directly at him. Her eyes shone, but her voice remained fierce.

"You'd better come back."

Harry thought of his parents in the mirror. He thought of Draconia's compass, Hermione's letters and two girls appearing at breakfast after Christmas.

"All right," he said. "I'll come back, I promise."

Hermione released him. She pushed the forward bottle into his hand and took the other. Then Harry drank. Cold spread from his throat to his fingertips. He stepped into the black flame. The fire brushed his face without burning it. He stepped onto bare stone and waited for his eyes to adjust. At the far wall stood the mirror, its gold frame catching the dull light. A man stood before it.

Professor Quirrell.

Harry stopped with one hand still lifted, as though he could hold the black fire open behind him. For a confused moment, the room beneath Hogwarts became the unused classroom from Christmas. The same gold frame rose from the floor. The same clawed feet rested upon stone. Yet his parents did not appear in the glass. Only Harry stared back. Quirrell turned.

"You."

Harry's hand tightened around his wand. Quirrell smiled.

"An unexpected guest," he said. There was no stammer. "I was beginning to think Dumbledore would return before I had any company."

"You shouldn't be here."

Quirrell glanced towards the black fire. "Neither should you. Yet here we both are."

Harry took one step away from the flames. He could feel the wand in his hand. None of the spells he knew had ever been used against a teacher, and his fingers had begun to shake. Hermione's warning came back to him.

You assume you're the person who should take the risk.

"What did you do to Snape?"

Quirrell laughed softly.

"Nothing. Severus has spent the year making himself inconvenient."

"The broom."

"I wondered whether Miss Granger would realise."

Harry felt sick.

"She saw Snape."

"She saw him attempting to counter my jinx. She set his robes alight, Severus knocked into me, and I lost the concentration I needed. A remarkable accident."

Draconia had seen both men muttering. Harry had wanted the explanation to be simple: Snape disliked him, so Snape must have done it. He had told McGonagall the rest because it was true, then continued looking harder at the man he already distrusted.

"The dragon egg," he said. "Was that you?"

Quirrell's smile returned. "Hagrid is unusually easy to interest. He was very pleased to find somebody willing to listen."

"And the Ministry letter?"

"An urgent request can accomplish a great deal if it appears to come from the right place."

Harry thought of Hagrid's face when he realised what he had said, and McGonagall hurrying out of the courtyard. Both had believed they were doing what they should.

"Why the Stone?"

Quirrell's smile vanished. A voice answered from beneath his turban.

"Because I require it."

The sound was thin and cold. Harry's scar erupted. Pain split across his forehead with such force that his knees nearly gave way. He pressed one hand against it, though touch could do nothing to stop the burning beneath his skin.

Quirrell turned his back. His fingers rose to the folds of his turban. Harry understood that he should run. But he remained where he was. The last fold fell away. A face occupied the back of Quirrell's head. It was scarcely a face at all: pale skin drawn tightly over something unfinished, red eyes and narrow slits where a nose should have been. Its gaze settled upon Harry with a familiarity that made the pain in his scar deepen.

"Harry Potter."

The voice slid across the chamber. Harry could not breathe. He had heard it before. Not in the castle. Not in any memory he could properly reach. It lived beneath memory—in green light, a woman's scream and cold laughter severing the night.

"You killed them," Harry whispered.

The red eyes narrowed.

"Your mother need not have died. She chose weakness."

Anger cut through the pain.

"She chose me."

"Yes," Voldemort said. "And reduced me to this."

Quirrell shifted beneath him like a body carrying a parasite.

"The Stone," Voldemort continued. "The boy can retrieve it."

Quirrell faced the mirror.

"Tell me what you see."

Harry stepped before the glass. At first, his reflection stood alone. Then the reflected Harry smiled. He reached into his pocket and withdrew a blood-red stone. He placed it back and tapped the fabric. Something dropped into Harry's real pocket.

The Stone.

Harry kept his face still. "Well?" Quirrell demanded.

"I see myself winning the House Cup."

Voldemort made a sound of disgust. "He lies."

Quirrell seized Harry by the shoulder. "Give me the Stone."

Harry twisted away. Quirrell's hand closed upon his wrist. Smoke rose between their skin. The professor screamed. He released Harry and staggered backwards. Angry blisters spread across his palm.

"What is this?"

Harry did not know. Voldemort did.

"His mother."

The hatred in those words chilled Harry more than fear could. "Her protection remains upon him. Kill him without touching him."

Quirrell drew his wand. Harry moved first. He threw himself forward. His own wand clattered to the floor as he caught Quirrell's arm with both hands. The effect was immediate. Quirrell's skin cracked beneath Harry's fingers. Heat surged between them. Harry expected it to hurt, but the burning remained beneath Quirrell's skin, spreading wherever Harry's fingers pressed. Quirrell screamed and struck at him with his free hand.

Harry held on. He thought of his mother standing before the mirror. He thought of the choice she had made. The chamber blurred. Pain tore across Harry's scar. Voldemort's voice filled the room, ordering Quirrell to fight, to kill, to tear Harry apart. Quirrell's arm began crumbling beneath his hands. Harry wanted to release him but he couldn't. If he let go, Quirrell would reach the Stone. Voldemort would return. Everything beneath the trapdoor—Draconia's sacrifice, Hermione turning back—would become meaningless. Harry tightened his grip. A shadow ripped itself from Quirrell's body. It passed through Harry like winter given shape. Pain split across his scar. Then the floor rose towards him and everything disappeared.

Harry woke to the smell of medicinal potions and the sound of an argument being conducted in whispers.

"He has been unconscious for three days."

"I know how long three days is."

"You asked Madam Pomfrey this morning."

"I was confirming."

"You also asked yesterday."

"His condition may have changed."

"It did not."

"You don't know that."

"Neither do you."

Harry kept his eyes closed. The hospital bed was warm. Sunlight pressed red against his eyelids. His head ached, but the familiar voices beside him made the pain seem less urgent.

"If you keep arguing," he murmured, "I'm going back to sleep."

Silence fell. Harry opened his eyes. Hermione sat nearest the bed with a book open and unread upon her lap. Her hair looked as though it had not been brushed that morning. Draconia occupied the chair beside her, one arm held in a sling and a fading bruise visible at her temple. Both stared at him.

"You absolute idiot," Hermione said.

Harry smiled weakly. "Hello."

Hermione lifted the book as though she intended to hit him with it, then put it down so hard the bedside table rattled. Her hand closed around his sleeve instead. Beside her chair, Harry could see the recovered cloak folded over a stool, his wand resting on top. Draconia leaned forward. "Do you have any idea how irritating it is to wake upon a chessboard and discover you've continued without me?"

"You told me to."

"That is not relevant."

"It seems relevant."

"You promised to come back," Hermione said.

Harry turned towards her. She was angry. Beneath the anger sat three days of fear.

"I did."

"After nearly dying."

"That bit wasn't on purpose."

Hermione's eyes filled. Harry's smile disappeared.

"I'm sorry."

She looked down quickly, pretending to straighten the blanket. "You should be."

Draconia's good hand rested on the edge of the mattress. "What happened?"

Harry told them. He described Quirrell, the mirror and the face beneath the turban. He told them Snape had been countering the broom jinx and that Voldemort had used Quirrell to reach the Stone. Neither girl interrupted, although Hermione's fingers tightened around the book when Harry described Quirrell's skin burning beneath his hands.

"And the Stone?" Draconia asked.

"Gone."

Professor Dumbledore answered from the foot of the bed. Harry had not seen him enter. Hermione immediately stood. Draconia attempted to do the same, winced and remained seated with an expression suggesting this had been her choice. Dumbledore approached and drew a chair towards the bed.

"Mr Longbottom found Professor McGonagall," he said. "When I returned, she was already gathering help. Miss Granger met us after getting Miss Malfoy clear of the board."

Hermione looked at Harry. "Neville did exactly what I asked."

Harry looked down at the blanket. He had barely thought about the note once he reached the forbidden door. At least one part of their plan had worked as they intended. "The request from the Ministry was false," Dumbledore continued. "By the time I arrived there and discovered that, you had already gone beneath the trapdoor. I returned as quickly as I could."

"Did you get to the mirror?" Harry asked.

"Yes. In time to bring you out."

Harry tried to remember the end of the room: Quirrell's hand, the cold shadow, the floor. He had no memory of Dumbledore arriving.

"Nicolas and Perenelle Flamel have agreed that the Stone presents too great a danger," Dumbledore said. "It has been destroyed."

"But then they'll die," Harry said.

"In time. They have enough Elixir remaining to arrange their affairs."

Harry did not understand how anybody could speak so calmly about choosing death. Perhaps Dumbledore saw the question.

"To the well-organised mind, death is but the next great adventure," he said.

Draconia's expression suggested she found this answer deeply unsatisfactory. Hermione asked, "How did the Stone enter Harry's pocket?"

"The Mirror of Erised was the final protection. Only someone who wished to find the Stone but not use it could retrieve it."

Harry looked towards Dumbledore. "You put the mirror there."

"I did."

"You knew someone would try to get through."

"I believed it possible."

"You knew we knew about the Stone."

Dumbledore's blue eyes rested upon him. "I suspected."

Something in Harry hardened. "Then why didn't you stop us?"

Hermione and Draconia became very still. Dumbledore moved closer. The faint smile had left his face. "I had hoped," he said, "that the protections would prevent any student from reaching the final chamber."

"That isn't what I asked."

Draconia looked at Harry. He had heard her refuse a vague answer from their very first conversation at Madam Malkin's. He was beginning to understand why she did it. Dumbledore folded his hands. "No," he acknowledged. "It is not."

Harry waited. "I was not here when you went down," Dumbledore said. "I had believed the warnings and the protections would be enough to keep students away. They were not. I should have made that corridor impossible for you to enter. That was my mistake."

It was not a complete answer. Harry could feel other truths behind it, decisions made by adults who believed they saw farther than children and therefore possessed the right to choose what children faced. For now, the admission was enough.

"Why couldn't Quirrell touch me?"

Dumbledore's gaze softened. "Your mother died to save you. An act such as hers leaves a mark deeper than the scar upon your forehead. While he carried Voldemort, Quirrell could not bear to touch a person protected by that sacrifice."

Harry looked down at his hands. He had spent his life knowing almost nothing about Lily Potter. Still, she had been there. Not in the mirror.

In him.

"Professor," Hermione said, "was Voldemort actually here?"

The name no longer made her flinch as much. Dumbledore turned towards her.

"Yes. Though not as he once was. He survives by attaching himself to others, waiting until he can regain strength."

Draconia's face had gone pale. "Will he return?"

Dumbledore looked at each of them in turn. "I believe he will try."

The hospital wing seemed colder. This was no longer a mystery concluded beneath the school. It was a beginning. Harry looked at Hermione, then Draconia. Neither moved away. Madam Pomfrey swept from her office and discovered three students awake, Dumbledore discussing dark wizards and a box of sweets open upon the bedside table. She expelled everyone except Harry. Hermione hugged him before leaving. Draconia lingered. Her arm remained in its sling, and the bruise against her pale skin looked darker in the afternoon light.

"You let the knight hit you so I could go on," Harry said.

"Yes."

"You could've been killed."

"So could you."

"That's different."

"No," Draconia replied. "It isn't."

Harry remembered Hermione saying almost the same thing before the black fire. Draconia stepped closer. "I meant what I said on the board. I trusted you."

"I know."

"You are making it difficult to do so again."

"I came back, didn't I?"

"Barely."

Harry looked down. Draconia sighed. Then she reached into the pocket of her robe and placed the brass compass upon his blanket. The casing had been dented. "Professor Dumbledore found it beside the mirror. You must have dropped it."

Harry picked it up. "Does it still work?"

"Think of somewhere."

Harry pictured their table in the library. The needle turned towards the place where they had spent so many afternoons, and he let out a breath he had not meant to hold.

"Yeah."

Draconia's expression eased. Harry closed the compass in his palm. "How often did you come up here?"

She looked towards the door. "I didn't count."

Harry suspected she was lying. He did not ask again.

"Goodnight, Harry."

"Goodnight, Draconia."

Harry held the compass until sleep returned.

The end-of-year feast took place beneath green and silver banners. Slytherin stood fifty points ahead of Gryffindor, with Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw below them both. Their table was already celebrating the House Cup. Students wore silver ribbons around their wrists, and a serpent large enough to cross the ceiling had been conjured above them in green light. Draconia sat among them. Harry and Hermione took their places at the Gryffindor table. Ron leaned across the bench to ask whether Harry had really fought Professor Quirrell. Harry told him he was not going to talk about it. Ron appeared disappointed, then immediately began offering versions of the story that involved considerably more shouting than Harry remembered. The feast was about to begin when Dumbledore rose. Conversation quietened. "Another year has passed," he said. "Before we surrender ourselves to an excellent feast, I believe certain recent events require recognition."

Harry's stomach dropped. Hermione sat straighter. Across the Hall, Draconia looked suspicious. Dumbledore smiled. "It takes considerable courage to confront an enemy. It also takes courage to recognise when one cannot continue alone."

The Hall had become completely silent.

"To Miss Hermione Granger, for applying knowledge under extraordinary pressure and possessing the wisdom to seek help when her friends could not—fifty points to Gryffindor."

The Gryffindor table erupted. Hermione's face went scarlet. Harry cheered louder than anyone. Dumbledore raised a hand.

"To Miss Draconia Malfoy, for recognising the price of victory and offering herself in another's place—fifty points to Slytherin."

The response from the Slytherin table was uneven. Some cheered immediately. Others remained silent, uncertain whether the reason for Draconia's reward should be celebrated. Daphne Greengrass stood and applauded. After a moment, several others joined her. Draconia did not look at them. She looked at Hermione. Hermione raised both hands and clapped fiercely. A small, helpless smile appeared upon Draconia's face. "And finally," Dumbledore continued, "to Mr Harry Potter, for courage in the face of grave danger and the determination to protect others when he might have fled—sixty points to Gryffindor."

Noise shook the Hall. Harry barely heard it. Professor McGonagall adjusted the great hourglasses with a movement of her wand. Rubies poured into Gryffindor's measure. Emeralds filled Slytherin's. The final totals appeared. Gryffindor had won by ten points. For one awkward moment, the green serpent continued winding above the Slytherin table. Then the banners changed. Scarlet and gold swept across the ceiling. The serpent dissolved into a golden lion, which opened its mouth in a silent roar. Gryffindor exploded. Students struck the tables, shouted Harry's name and dragged Hermione to her feet. Ron nearly knocked a jug of pumpkin juice into Seamus's lap. Harry smiled because everyone expected him to. Then he looked across the Hall. The Slytherin table had gone quiet.

Several students stared at Draconia. She had earned fifty points and still lost the Cup to the two Gryffindors beside whom she had gone through the trapdoor. Flint leaned towards her and said something. Draconia's expression closed. Harry stood. Hermione caught his sleeve.

"What are you doing?"

"I don't know."

He climbed onto the bench. The Gryffindor table quietened around him. "Draconia!"

His voice carried badly across the Great Hall. Her head lifted. Harry raised his goblet. For several seconds, he felt foolish. Then Hermione climbed onto the bench beside him.

"To Draconia Malfoy!" she called.

Fred and George required no further encouragement. "To Malfoy!"

Gryffindors began raising their goblets. Some sounded confused. Others joined because celebration was celebration, and the twins had already begun pounding the table again. Draconia stared. Daphne nudged her. Slowly, Draconia stood. She lifted her own goblet towards Harry and Hermione. The first Slytherin to join her was Daphne. Then another first-year stood. A few older students followed with varying enthusiasm. The green-and-silver table did not cheer for Gryffindor's victory. It cheered for one of its own. That was enough. Harry sat down. Hermione remained beside him, smiling. Dumbledore watched from the staff table, his expression unreadable beneath the candlelight. For the rest of the evening, scarlet-and-gold banners hung above four House tables that looked slightly less separate than they had before.

The Hogwarts Express carried them south beneath a bright summer sky. This time, Harry did not search for an empty compartment. Hermione had already claimed one and arranged their luggage. Draconia arrived five minutes later with her inherited acquaintances in the corridor behind her. "You may go," she told them.

One of the boys sighed. "We know."

They left before she finished closing the door. Draconia sat opposite Hermione. Harry took the place between the window and both of them, his trunk above and Hedwig's cage secured beside the door. Fields slipped past outside. For several hours, they ate the remaining sweets from Harry's trunk and avoided discussing the approaching station. Hermione had created a summer correspondence schedule. Draconia criticised it until Hermione added her name.

"You'll write every week," Hermione said.

Harry looked at the chart. "Every Tuesday?"

"It prevents long gaps."

"What if nothing happens on Tuesday?"

"Then tell me what you had for breakfast."

Draconia examined the parchment. "You have assigned me Fridays."

"Yes."

"I may be occupied."

"With what?"

"I don't know yet."

"Then you are currently free."

Draconia looked at Harry. "How have you endured her all year?"

"Practice."

Hermione rolled the schedule and struck him with it. Harry laughed. The journey shortened. Buildings replaced fields. The sky above London turned hazy. Students changed from robes into Muggle clothes, and trunks began appearing in the corridor. Harry's good humour faded. Draconia noticed. "You have the compass?"

He touched his pocket. "Yeah."

"Hedwig can carry messages."

"I know."

"If they prevent you writing," Hermione added, "send Hedwig to me twice without a letter. I'll know you need help."

"Why twice?"

"Once might mean you forgot to attach it."

"That happened once."

"Twice."

"The second letter was in my pocket."

"Exactly. Twice in a row will mean something different."

Draconia leaned back. "I could ask my father to contact them."

Harry imagined Vernon Dursley opening the door to Lucius Malfoy. The thought was almost worth agreeing to. "No."

"Why?"

"He'd make it worse."

The girls fell silent. Harry looked through the window. King's Cross approached beyond rows of brick buildings. "I'll be all right. I've lasted ten years with them."

Hermione's mouth tightened. Draconia looked away. The train entered the station. Steam covered the platform. Families waited beyond the barrier, waving as students leaned from the doors. Harry saw Hermione's parents almost immediately. Mrs Granger stood on her toes, searching each carriage. Mr Granger had brought a camera. Farther back waited Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy. Narcissa saw Draconia and smiled. Lucius's gaze moved past his daughter and found Harry through the window. Harry could not read his expression. The train stopped. None of the three moved. The corridor filled around them. Doors opened. Voices called. Hermione stood first. "We have to go."

"I know," Harry said.

She hugged him. There was nothing awkward about it now. Harry held on, closing his eyes briefly against her hair.

"Tuesday," she said.

"Tuesday."

She released him and turned to Draconia. Their hug was shorter, but Draconia initiated it.

"Friday," Hermione reminded her.

"I heard you the first fourteen times."

Hermione left with her trunk. Draconia remained beside Harry. "My mother will want to meet you properly."

"Now?"

"If you prefer."

Harry glanced through the window at the families gathering in the steam. The Dursleys would be on the other side of the barrier. He could picture his uncle checking the time, Dudley complaining about the wait and Aunt Petunia pretending not to be interested in the wall.

"Maybe not today."

Draconia followed his gaze, then looked back at him.

"They're on the other side?"

Harry nodded.

"The incompetent ones," she said, as though there were several families to choose between and he had selected particularly badly.

Harry laughed despite the dread in his stomach. "Yeah."

She took hold of her case. "Tuesday," Harry said.

"I was assigned Friday."

"I know."

Draconia looked at him. Then she smiled. "Tuesday, then."

They stepped from the train together. Hermione met them upon the platform after leaving her trunk with her parents. She had evidently decided that one farewell was insufficient. Narcissa Malfoy approached from the other direction, elegant in pale travelling robes, while Lucius followed more slowly.

For a moment, their different worlds converged in the steam. Hermione introduced her parents. Draconia introduced hers. Mr Granger offered Lucius his hand. Lucius's gaze rested on it for a fraction too long before he accepted. Narcissa turned to Harry. "Mr Potter," she said.

She had Draconia's careful way of speaking, though her gaze was warmer than Harry had expected. "Draconia has told us a great deal about you."

"Mum."

"Only flattering things."

"That is not true."

Narcissa's eyes brightened. "Mostly flattering things."

Harry smiled. Lucius studied his scar, then his oversized clothes. "Your relatives are collecting you?"

"They're waiting on the other side."

"I see."

Harry could not tell whether that meant anything. Narcissa glanced towards the barrier, then back at her daughter. Behind Harry, another family was trying to manoeuvre three trunks around an owl cage, and somebody called for the passage to be kept clear.

"I should go," Harry said.

Hermione fetched a trolley while Harry lifted Hedwig's cage. Draconia took one handle of his trunk and Hermione the other. They settled it on the trolley before Harry could argue that he should do it himself. Harry pushed the trolley towards the barrier with both of them walking beside him. At the dividing wall, he stopped. Beyond it waited Privet Drive. Behind him stood the first year of his life he wanted to keep.

Hermione touched the sleeve of his oversized shirt. "Tuesday."

Draconia tapped the pocket containing the compass. "And if you get lost."

"I know."

Harry looked at both of them. A year ago, he would not have known how to name the feeling in his chest. "See you," he said.

Not goodbye.

Hermione understood. "See you, Harry."

Draconia lifted her chin. "Don't be late, Potter."

Harry pushed his trolley through the barrier. The Dursleys were waiting. Uncle Vernon began complaining before Harry had fully emerged. Dudley stared suspiciously at Hedwig. Aunt Petunia's eyes fixed upon the scar, then darted towards the solid wall behind him. Harry barely heard them. Something stirred inside his coat. He reached into his pocket and found a folded paper bird tucked beside the compass. One wing tapped against his fingers. Harry opened it. Two notes were written inside in different hands.

You forgot the sweets beneath the seat. I've put them in your trunk.—Hermione

Beneath that, Draconia had added:

We considered keeping them, but Granger said that would violate the friendship schedule.

Harry laughed. Uncle Vernon stopped. "What's funny?"

"Nothing."

It was not true. Harry folded the note and placed it safely inside his pocket. Then he took hold of his trolley and followed the Dursleys towards the station exit.

Privet Drive had not changed.

Harry had.

For the first time, he was not returning there alone.

There would be letters. There was a table in the library he could picture whenever the house grew too quiet, and two people who expected him to answer when they wrote. Harry closed his fingers around the note in his pocket and followed his uncle through the crowd.
