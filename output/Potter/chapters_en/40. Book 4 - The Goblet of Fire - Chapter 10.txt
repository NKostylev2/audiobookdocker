10

Harry woke before the alarm, with the uneasy certainty that something had been waiting for him to open his eyes.

For several seconds, the darkness above his bed was only darkness. The curtains enclosed his four-poster in familiar folds, shutting out the rest of the dormitory, while rain tapped against the tower windows and someone on the far side of the room breathed through a blocked nose. Harry lay still beneath the blankets, listening to the small, ordinary sounds of a morning that had not quite begun.

Then the blue line around his wrist pulsed, and the dragon returned to him all at once.

It was not a dream. Dreams usually possessed the decency to become unreal when he opened his eyes; this waited fully formed behind them, as clear as though he were still standing upon the hillside. Iron-black scales. Bronze horns. Fire sweeping over the handlers in a sheet wide enough to swallow everything beyond it. He could remember the heat against his face, although the air inside the curtains was cold.

Harry remained beneath the blankets and counted his breaths. Five things he could see would require light, and he was not ready to reach for his wand, so he settled for five things he knew were there: the trunk at the foot of the bed, his wand beneath the pillow, the Firebolt leaning against the inner curtain, Sirius's mirror upon the bedside table and the watch Remus had given him. He had turned the watch facedown during the night because its ticking became unbearable when he could not sleep, each small click reminding him that morning was getting closer.

Four things he could feel. Cotton beneath his fingers, the mattress against his back, cold air upon his face and the contract's warmth around his wrist.

The last did not belong in the exercise. He was meant to be finding things that reassured him.

Harry sat up and drew his wand from beneath the pillow. A low amber light spread through the curtains, revealing the dormitory in pieces when he pulled them aside: rumpled beds, discarded socks, a school tie dangling from the lid of somebody's trunk and the dark squares of windows holding back a wet November morning. The Firebolt stood exactly where he had left it, its polished handle catching the light.

The instinct to run arrived without anywhere useful to send him. It suggested the common room, the Owlery, the grounds and finally Hawthorn Cottage, as though enough distance might persuade the contract that Harry had ceased to exist. Sirius's mirror was within reach. Harry could wake him and say the one thing everyone had promised he was allowed to say: I cannot do this.

Sirius would come. That certainty helped more than pretending Harry did not want him to.

It would not stop the blue mark from tightening, remove his name from the Goblet or make the dragon less real. But Sirius would come, Hermione would arrive carrying too many contingencies, and Draconia would stand beside the door looking composed enough for all of them. Harry did not have to face the morning as though none of those people existed.

He reached for the Firebolt, closing his fingers around the smooth handle. Polished wood met his palm, solid and familiar; he knew every slight curve of it, every place his hands had learned to settle. It belonged to this room and this morning. Whatever waited beyond the castle, the broom was here.

Harry dressed without waking anyone. He checked his wand three times before noticing what he was doing and making himself stop. The Firebolt remained beside the bed, angled towards the window as they had agreed. Carrying it into breakfast would make the plan obvious and, more importantly, would violate the rule allowing champions to enter with only their wands.

At the dormitory door, he paused and looked back at it.

"Don't make me look stupid," he whispered.

The broom offered no commitment.

The common room was nearly empty, its usual clutter subdued by the grey light seeping through the windows. A fire smouldered beneath the mantelpiece, sending occasional sparks into the chimney. Neville had fallen asleep in an armchair with a book open across his chest, his head tilted at an angle that would hurt when he woke. One hand rested beside a plate containing several sandwiches, and a folded note had been propped against it.

Harry stopped beside the chair and bent to read.

If you wake before us, eat. Hermione said that last part must be underlined, but I thought the sandwiches made the point. — Neville

Beneath it, in a different hand:

She did insist. — Dean

And below that:

We considered poisoning them to improve your odds against the dragon. McGonagall objected. — F & G

Harry lifted a sandwich, disturbing the plate just enough to wake Neville. He blinked at Harry, rubbed one hand across his face and tried to sit up before remembering the book, which slid from his chest and nearly took him out of the chair with it.

"You're up."

"So are you."

Neville glanced towards the window. "Technically."

Harry sat opposite him. The sandwich was cheese and pickle, the bread a little dry at the edges. He ate because Neville watched with an expression too earnest to evade, and because arguing about breakfast required more energy than he possessed.

"Everyone wanted to come down," Neville said. "Hermione told them that surrounding you before dawn might not be calming."

"She was right."

"She usually is."

"Never tell her."

Neville smiled, although it faded when his gaze dropped to Harry's wrist. The blue line showed beneath his cuff, faintly luminous in the dim room.

"Does it hurt?"

"Not really."

"That means a little."

Harry considered lying, then gave up. Neville had become inconveniently familiar with the translation.

"A little."

Neville looked down at the book in his lap, running a thumb along its bent cover before speaking again.

"You don't have to win."

The words entered the quiet room without ceremony. Harry looked at him, the sandwich momentarily forgotten in his hand.

"Everyone keeps talking about points," Neville continued. "I only meant—you don't have to prove you belong in it. We know you didn't enter."

Harry swallowed his last mouthful. Beyond the windows, rain blurred the grounds into indistinct shades of grey, and somewhere upstairs a door opened as the castle began to wake. He had heard plenty of people tell him he could survive the task. Hearing that he did not need to justify being there loosened something different.

"Thanks."

"You should take another."

"That was less moving."

"Hermione counted them."

Harry took a second sandwich. By the time voices began drifting down from the dormitories, he had eaten most of it.

Hermione waited at the foot of the marble staircase, where the chill from the Entrance Hall crept beneath the doors. She wore her school robes under a heavy cloak and held a canvas bag so full that its contents had distorted the seams. Her hair had been tied back, though the damp air was already freeing strands around her face. She looked as though she had slept less than Harry and organised the lost hours alphabetically.

Her eyes moved over him, taking in his face, his hands and the sandwich he had brought downstairs.

"You ate."

"Good morning."

"Did you?"

"One and most of another."

"Sandwiches?"

"Neville was guarding them."

Some of the tension left her shoulders. She shifted the bag into her other hand, although the fingers of the first remained curled around the shape of its handle.

"I packed more."

"Naturally."

Draconia arrived from the dungeon corridor with her usual measured tread. At first glance, she did not appear tired, which Harry had learned generally meant she was exhausted. Every detail had been arranged with punishing precision: the silver clasp centred at her throat, pale hair secured at the nape and robes without a single crease. Only her hands betrayed her. One remained closed around the fine silver bracelet at her wrist, her thumb passing repeatedly over its small green stone.

Harry recognised the warded bracelet Narcissa had given her after the Chamber. He had seen Draconia touch it before difficult lessons and on mornings when she claimed to have slept perfectly well.

She stopped in front of him.

"Status."

"Alive."

"An ambitious starting point."

"I slept."

Hermione made a doubtful noise.

"Some," Harry amended. "I ate. My wrist is warm, not painful. My wand works. The Firebolt is beside my bed with a clear route to the window."

Draconia's gaze held his, searching past the report for the thing he had left out.

"And?"

Harry knew what she wanted.

"I remember the promise."

Her hand loosened around the bracelet, and the three of them turned towards the Great Hall as its doors opened.

Breakfast had been laid, but very few students seemed interested in eating it. The long tables hummed with anticipation; heads turned towards the doors, conversations dipped and began again, and more than one person pretended to be looking past Harry when he caught their eye. Rain clouded the enchanted ceiling and drummed against the real roof far above. At the staff table, Bagman spoke animatedly to Dumbledore while Professor McGonagall examined him with the expression of someone considering whether a body could be hidden beneath a Quidditch pitch.

Sirius and Remus sat beside Hagrid. Harry stopped inside the doorway when he saw them, and Sirius rose at once. He did not cross the Hall this time. Instead, he waited beside the Gryffindor table, leaving Harry to decide whether he wanted the distance closed.

Harry crossed it quickly.

"You came."

"There are dragons," Sirius said. "You couldn't have kept me away with another prison sentence."

Remus stood and squeezed Harry's shoulder, his hand warm through the cloth.

"How are you?"

"Everyone keeps asking that as though the answer might improve."

"Sometimes saying it aloud helps."

"Terrified."

The honesty surprised Harry. It had escaped before he could put something amusing in its place, leaving him uncomfortably exposed beneath the noise of the Hall. Sirius's face changed, though not with alarm. He looked as though Harry had finally answered the question he had actually been asking.

"Good," he said.

"Good?"

"Fear means you understand the dragon. Panic is what we avoid."

"And if fear becomes panic?" Hermione asked.

Sirius looked at Harry, waiting rather than answering for him. Harry repeated what they had practised, concentrating upon the order of the words.

"Find the present. Find the broom. Don't fight the dragon."

"And leave," Draconia said.

"And leave."

Hagrid leaned across the table. His beard appeared to have been combed and then reconsidered, and he lowered his voice with very little effect upon its volume.

"She's settled a bit."

Harry looked at him. "The Horntail?"

"Only scorched three handlers this mornin'."

"I'm delighted she's relaxed."

Hagrid attempted a reassuring smile. It made things worse.

Narcissa entered with Lucius shortly before eight, producing another wave of whispers along the tables. Lucius wore formal black robes and carried a silver-headed cane despite no longer requiring it for support. Narcissa's travelling cloak was the same blue she had worn outside Privet Drive, on the day Harry first saw an adult look at the bars on his window and understand what they meant. Seeing it here brought an unexpected ache to his throat.

Draconia went to them. Narcissa embraced her daughter, one hand resting briefly against the pale hair, while Lucius touched the back of Draconia's head with an unguarded tenderness he probably would have denied possessing. He noticed Harry watching, withdrew his hand and crossed the aisle.

"Mr Potter."

"Mr Malfoy."

Lucius's gaze descended to Harry's plate.

"That is not breakfast."

Harry looked at what remained of Neville's sandwich. "It was larger earlier."

"Eat more."

"You crossed the country to say that?"

"Among other things."

Lucius lowered his voice, and the shift in his manner drew Sirius's attention away from Harry's plate.

"Crouch will not attend."

Sirius straightened. "Confirmed?"

"The Ministry has appointed Weatherby to act in his place."

"Percy Weasley," Harry corrected.

"If you insist."

"Crouch still hasn't been seen?" Remus asked.

"Not since the night the Goblet selected its champions. His office receives written instructions each morning. The wards around his residence refuse Ministry entry on his authority."

Hermione sat beside Harry, easing her bag onto the floor without taking her eyes from Lucius.

"Could someone else be writing the instructions?"

"Three experts have authenticated the hand."

"That wasn't my question."

Lucius regarded her for a moment. The usual correction seemed ready upon his lips, then his gaze moved towards the staff table, where Percy's absence had left an empty chair beside Bagman.

"No," he said. "It was not."

Across the Hall, Bagman rose, and conversation dwindled as he lifted his wand to his throat.

"Champions will report to the enclosure at half past nine," he announced. "Spectators may begin taking their places from ten. The first task will commence at eleven precisely."

Applause broke out around the tables. Harry's stomach tightened, putting an abrupt end to any prospect of eating more, and Sirius placed one hand between his shoulders.

"Breathe."

"I am."

"Usefully."

Harry drew a slower breath. Bagman continued explaining the routes to the stands and the rules against interfering with a champion, sounding pleased with every arrangement. Around Harry, students leaned closer to hear, already discussing the best places to sit. He could only listen to the times, fitting each one into the narrowing space between breakfast and fire.

Nine thirty arrived whether he cooperated or not.

Professor McGonagall met him in the Entrance Hall, where the steady stream of students towards the grounds had become a press of cloaks, wet shoes and excited voices.

"The other champions have gone ahead." Her eyes moved towards the group around Harry. "You may say goodbye here."

"Goodbye seems dramatic," Harry said.

"Then do not make it accurate," she replied.

Hermione moved first. She wrapped both arms around him with enough force to press the air from his lungs, her canvas bag striking his hip as she pulled him close.

"You know the plan."

"I helped make it."

"If the Summoning Charm does not work—"

"I shield and move. I don't stand still trying it repeatedly."

"If the dragon leaves the nest—"

"I keep the eggs between us and force it to turn."

"If your broom is damaged—"

"Hermione."

She stopped. Harry returned the embrace, feeling the rigid line of her shoulders beneath the cloak.

"I know."

For a moment, her face pressed into his shoulder. When she spoke again, the words were quiet enough that he almost lost them beneath the footsteps passing nearby.

"Come back."

"I promised."

She released him reluctantly, smoothing the front of his robes where her grip had creased them, and Draconia took her place.

For a moment, she did nothing. Her parents stood several feet away, and Snape waited near the dungeon stairs with his arms folded within his sleeves. Students hurried past, glancing at them with open curiosity. Draconia seemed aware of every watching face, then made a small, deliberate movement towards Harry that dismissed them all.

She reached into the inner pocket of her robes and drew out a fine silver chain matching the bracelet she wore. A second green stone hung from its end, no larger than a fingernail.

"Mother renewed the wards this morning," she said.

"What does it do?"

"It warms if the wearer is injured and cools if they are affected by hostile magic. The stones are linked. Mine will carry the warning."

She fastened it around Harry's wrist beside the blue contract line, concentrating upon the clasp with rather more care than it required. Her fingertips lingered against his pulse after the chain was secure.

"I thought bracelets were meant to be gifts."

"This is a loan."

"In case the dragon steals it?"

"In case you fail to return it."

Harry looked at her. Beneath the careful wording, the reason for the loan was painfully easy to understand. He closed his hand around hers before she could withdraw it.

"I'll bring it back."

Draconia nodded, her fingers pressing once against his, then stepped away before her composure required further defence.

Sirius hugged Harry last. It was brief, fierce and entirely unconcerned with who watched, his hand settling at the back of Harry's head as though there were something he could keep safe by holding it there.

"Freedom," he said against Harry's hair.

Harry understood. The dragon waited beyond the castle, but so did the broom, and every open sky he had ever escaped into. He held that thought as Sirius let him go, then followed Professor McGonagall out into the rain.

The champions' tent had been erected behind a line of canvas screens at the edge of the enclosure. Rainwater gathered in the folds of its roof and spilled from the corners, wearing shallow channels into the grass. Even before Harry ducked through the entrance, he could smell smoke beneath the damp, mingled with the sharp tang of potion antiseptic.

Four cots stood against one wall, their blankets folded with brisk efficiency. A nearby table held water, bandages and jars of burn salve, each neatly labelled. Harry looked at the supplies for rather longer than he intended. Apparently, the organisers expected everyone to need the medical tent badly enough that they had put half of it here in advance.

Fleur paced near the entrance, silver hair swinging against the back of her blue robes with every turn. Krum sat upon a wooden stool, elbows on his knees and hands clasped so tightly that his knuckles showed white. Cedric stood beside the canvas wall, listening to the dragon beyond it. When another roar shook droplets from the roof, he lifted his head, and all four of them waited for the sound to finish.

Professor McGonagall gave Harry one last searching look before leaving. The canvas fell closed behind her, cutting off the grey grounds and the possibility of following her back to the castle.

Cedric looked at him first.

"You came."

"I considered sending a note."

"Didn't think the contract would accept it?"

"It seems particular."

Cedric smiled without much success. Fleur stopped pacing and turned towards Harry, her gaze moving briefly to the line showing beneath his cuff.

"You knew."

Harry met her eyes. "So did you."

Her mouth tightened, although she did not deny it. Krum looked between them, then towards Cedric.

"Ve all knew."

For several seconds, the four champions regarded one another across the tent. Whatever advantage the Tournament's first secret was supposed to provide had disappeared before any of them reached the arena. Harry found himself relieved by the admission; at least he would not have to pretend surprise while trying to remember how to remain alive.

"Well," Cedric said, "that's reassuring."

"Is it?" Harry asked.

"No."

Bagman entered carrying a purple silk bag. His cheerfulness had acquired a strained edge, and mud clung to the lower hem of his robes, but he greeted them with the determined enthusiasm of a man unwilling to allow weather, dragons or frightened children to spoil an excellent sporting occasion.

"Everyone ready?"

No one answered.

"Excellent. First order of business—selection."

He held out the bag, which twitched in his hand. Fleur reached first, her fingers emerging around a small green model dragon bearing the number two. It spread its wings and snapped at her thumb, and she shifted her grip with an expression of cold disapproval.

"Welsh Green," Bagman announced.

Krum drew a scarlet dragon with gold spikes and the number three; Cedric's was silver-blue and carried number one.

"Chinese Fireball. Swedish Short-Snout."

Only one remained. Harry put his hand into the bag, felt tiny claws seize his finger and withdrew the Hungarian Horntail. Number four hung around its neck. The model opened its mouth and produced a flame the size of a matchstick, then tried to climb towards his wrist.

Harry considered Hagrid's definition of settled.

"Of course," he said.

Bagman cleared his throat. "You will enter in numerical order. The objective is quite straightforward. Each nesting site contains one artificial golden egg. Retrieve it. How you do so will determine your marks."

"And the real eggs?" Cedric asked.

"Must remain unharmed. Injury to a nesting mother, destruction of an egg or assistance from outside the arena will result in penalties."

Harry looked towards the tent wall as the Horntail struck something beyond it. Chains answered with a metallic scream, and the canvas shivered in a gust of hot air.

What happens, Draconia had asked, if a chain breaks?

He had not liked the question during training. Hearing the restraints now did nothing to improve it.

Bagman handed each champion a red cloth bearing their number.

"When your number is shown at the entrance, you'll proceed. Healers will be standing by. Dragon handlers may intervene if the situation becomes unrecoverable."

"Define unrecoverable," Harry said.

Bagman's eyes flickered towards him. "You'll know."

"Before or after the healers?"

Cedric gave a short, startled laugh. Bagman pretended not to hear, straightened the mouth of the silk bag and turned as a horn sounded outside.

The first task had begun.

Bagman escorted Cedric from the tent. For a moment after the canvas fell shut, there was silence; then the crowd saw him, and the sound arrived like weather. Thousands of voices rose beyond the enclosure, joined by stamping feet and Lee Jordan's distant commentary. His words dissolved beneath the wind before they reached the tent, leaving only the excited rise and fall of his voice.

The Short-Snout roared. The cheering stopped abruptly, and Harry sat upon one of the cots before his knees could make the decision for him.

The model Horntail climbed his sleeve, dug tiny claws through the fabric and settled upon his shoulder. Its weight was hardly noticeable. Somewhere beyond the screens, its living counterpart struck the ground hard enough to make the water tremble in the glasses.

Hermione had written seventeen contingencies, and Draconia had added four more. Sirius had insisted upon three routes out of the arena, while Remus made Harry practise abandoning the egg when escape became impossible. Between them, they had considered broken charms, damaged brooms, changes in the wind and at least six ways the dragon might behave unexpectedly.

None of them had planned for waiting.

A crack sounded outside, followed by another. Krum lifted his head, and Fleur stopped moving. The Short-Snout shrieked; heat pushed through the canvas, carrying the smell of burned grass, and the crowd gasped as one.

Harry's fingers closed around the edge of the cot. He imagined Cedric on the ground, Cedric on fire, Cedric discovering that knowing about the dragon had not been the same as standing before it. He tried to dismiss the pictures, but the canvas gave him nothing else to look at, and every sound beyond it seemed to confirm the worst one.

The blue line tightened around his wrist. Beside it, the borrowed green stone remained unchanged, neither warm nor cold. No injury, then, and no hostile magic. Apparently, fear was too ordinary to deserve a warning.

A cheer began near one end of the enclosure and spread until the tent shook with it. Harry released the cot, discovering that the rough blanket had left marks across his fingers.

Cedric returned several minutes later. His right sleeve had burned away from shoulder to elbow, red salve covered a patch of skin along his jaw, and rain had plastered his hair against his forehead. Beneath his uninjured arm, he carried a golden egg almost the length of his forearm. It caught what little light entered the tent and reflected it onto the canvas as he set it carefully upon an empty cot.

"It moves faster than it looks," he said.

"You all right?" Harry asked.

"Ask me when my arm stops smoking."

The burned sleeve released a thin curl. Two healers entered behind Cedric and began cutting away the remaining cloth, working around him with practised speed.

"Mr Diggory," one said, "you are not supposed to advise another champion."

"I described its speed."

"That constitutes assistance."

Cedric glanced at Harry. "Then I definitely won't mention that they can change direction while charging."

The healer closed her eyes. Harry smiled for the first time since entering the tent.

Bagman returned for Fleur. She drew one slow breath, lifted her chin and followed him into the rain, the small Welsh Green held carefully away from her fingers until she handed it over.

Her task began more quietly. Music passed through the enclosure, a low melody winding beneath the tent walls. Harry could not distinguish the words, but the Welsh Green's first growls softened as the song continued. Chains scraped once, and the crowd's murmuring settled into an expectant hush.

Fleur was putting it to sleep. Harry pictured the plan: enchantment instead of force, the dragon's movements guided by her voice while she approached the nest. It sounded elegant, and entirely beyond anything he could have attempted. His singing had once prompted Dudley to throw a shoe through his bedroom doorway.

The melody faltered. An explosive snore followed, and flame washed across the far side of the canvas. The audience screamed. Fleur's singing stopped so completely that, for several seconds, Harry could hear only rain gathering on the roof.

Then came applause.

Fleur returned with her egg and the lower half of her robes blackened. A healer extinguished a line of sparks along the hem while Fleur stood rigidly still, colour high in her cheeks. Whatever fear she had taken into the arena had been overtaken by fury.

"It sneezed," she said to no one.

Harry looked at the model Horntail upon his shoulder.

"Don't," he told it.

The model snapped at his ear.

Krum was called next. He rose without speaking and went through the entrance, leaving the wooden stool rocking slightly behind him. The Chinese Fireball greeted him with a roar that drew an answering eruption from the crowd. Red spell-light flashed against the roof; the dragon bellowed, then released a thin, piercing cry unlike the aggressive noises Harry had heard before.

He recognised pain.

"Conjunctivitis Curse," Fleur said.

Another impact followed, heavy enough to shake the poles of the tent. Something cracked, and the handlers began shouting.

Krum returned carrying his golden egg, one side of his face streaked with mud. He appeared uninjured, but his expression had hardened, and beyond the canvas the handlers' voices remained urgent.

Cedric listened for a moment.

"It crushed two of its own eggs."

Krum's jaw tightened. The judges would penalise him, but Harry wondered how much the points mattered beside the sound the dragon had made. Krum set his egg down without looking at it.

Bagman re-entered.

"Harry."

The use of his name seemed to draw the tent walls closer. Harry stood, relieved to find that his knees held, and handed the miniature Horntail to Bagman. It hooked its claws into his sleeve and resisted until the official prised it loose.

"Best of luck."

"Is that official assistance?"

Bagman gave him a distracted smile. Harry looked at the other champions: Fleur watched him with a new gravity, Krum nodded once, and Cedric sat between the healers with his burned arm wrapped in white bandages.

"Remember," Cedric said, "I told you absolutely nothing."

"You were useless."

"Good."

The horn sounded for the fourth time. Bagman opened the canvas, admitting rain and noise together, and Harry stepped outside.

The enclosure had been built inside a hollow carved from the hillside. Temporary stands rose around it in steep banks, their wooden tiers crowded beneath rippling waterproof charms. House colours broke through the grey daylight: red and gold along the western side, green and silver in a narrower section near the judges, blue silk and dark crimson banners above the foreign students.

The hollow had once been grass. Now it resembled the centre of an old battlefield, with whole strips of turf burned down to glassy black soil. Boulders formed an uneven ring around the nesting ground, some natural and others dragged into place to provide cover that would matter only if Harry reached it before the fire. Beyond the painted boundary, dragon handlers stood at measured intervals, their wands already drawn. A translucent ward shimmered between the arena and the lowest benches wherever the rain touched it, outlining the frightened faces immediately behind the barrier.

The preparations should have reassured him. Instead, Harry found himself looking from the salve-stained sleeves of the nearest handler to the blackened ground, seeing all the things the organisers had expected to go wrong.

Most faces were indistinguishable at that distance, but he found the people he loved anyway. Sirius stood at the front rail beside Remus, with Hermione between them and Hagrid. Draconia occupied the first row of the Slytherin stand, Narcissa on one side and Lucius on the other. Her pale face held still against the dark robes surrounding her.

Harry touched the green stone at his wrist. Draconia saw, and her hand closed around the rail. Near the entrance, Professor McGonagall's mouth formed a single word.

Careful.

Then Harry moved far enough into the arena to see the dragon.

The Hungarian Horntail lay within a crater of blackened earth. Distance on the hillside had lied about its size: its head alone was longer than Harry's body. Iron scales overlapped along the neck like shields, rain gleaming along their edges, while bronze horns swept backwards from a skull ridged in bone. Its wings remained folded, yet even contained they rose above its back like collapsed sails. The tail circled the nest, every spike stained dark at the tip.

The real eggs filled the hollow beneath the dragon's chest, smooth and stone-coloured and nearly as large as cauldrons. Among them, gold caught the daylight. The artificial egg rested close to the Horntail's left foreclaw, tucked where a champion would have to come within reach of its head to touch it.

Of course it did.

Harry stopped behind the painted boundary. The crowd's noise diminished in his ears until he could hear rain hissing upon the scorched ground. The Horntail's nostrils opened, smoke curling through its teeth, and its yellow eyes fixed upon him.

The black earth vanished beneath a wash of remembered green.

Harry heard scales moving in the Chamber, the immense body sliding between pillars while Tom Riddle stood untouched beside Draconia's fallen form. The dragon's eye became the basilisk's blind, bleeding socket. Smoke caught in his throat, sharp as the remembered smell of venom burning through the diary, and his hand grew numb around his wand.

You came alone again.

Riddle's voice sounded so clearly inside his head that Harry almost turned to find him. The Horntail lifted its head, chains tightening with a heavy clatter, and Harry discovered that he could no longer remember the first movement of the Summoning Charm.

The stands waited. He knew they were waiting, knew he was standing still, but the knowledge seemed far away from the hand that refused to lift.

Rain struck his face. Mud pressed against the edge of his shoe. The green stone rested against his pulse, its small weight distinct beneath the contract's warmth.

Harry looked towards the Gryffindor stand. Hermione had both hands pressed against the rail. He could not hear her, but he knew what she would tell him.

Name what is real.

The rain was real. So was the wand in his hand, the boundary line beneath his feet and the Firebolt seven floors above him, half a castle away. There were no pillars here. Draconia was in the stands, watching him; when he looked towards her, he saw that her face had lost all expression. He knew that look too, and the fierce instruction she would have given him if she could.

Do not let it choose the next thing you do.

The Horntail inhaled. Harry raised his wand.

"Accio Firebolt!"

The spell tore from his wand just as the dragon breathed fire.

Harry moved before thought fully returned. He threw himself sideways, hit the ground and cast a Shield Charm as the first edge of flame reached him. For one impossible second, red-gold fire spread across an invisible surface above his head. Heat pressed through the spell, curling his eyebrows; the shield bowed towards him, thin as heated glass, and fractures raced across it.

He rolled behind a scorched boulder as the charm broke. Fire poured over the stone, and the crowd screamed. Harry flattened himself against mud turning instantly to steam, dragging at air that burned his throat. The sleeve covering Draconia's silver chain began to smoke.

"Still," he coughed, "preferable to singing."

The Horntail's head swung towards his voice, chains dragging across rock as it turned. Harry pressed himself closer to the boulder and listened for the broom, straining to hear the familiar rush of wood through air beneath the dragon's movements. Nothing came. No approach from the castle, no sound above the stands; only scales scraping stone and the terrible intake of another breath.

The Firebolt had failed. He pictured it caught against a closed window, stopped by an arena ward or lying uselessly beside his bed while the Horntail burned through his shelter.

Do not stand still trying it repeatedly.

Hermione's rule returned with enough force to move him. Harry ran, leaving the boulder as a second stream of fire struck it. Heat chased him across the crater, and the edge of his cloak ignited. He tore it free and flung it behind him without slowing, stumbling over a ridge of churned earth as the dragon followed the movement.

Its tail uncoiled. Harry saw the spikes sweeping towards him and threw up his wand.

"Protego!"

The tail hit the shield, and magic burst blue-white between them. Harry left the ground, travelled several feet and struck the earth upon one shoulder. Pain flashed down his arm. His fingers remained closed around his wand, clinging to it through the impact because he knew what losing it would mean.

The crowd had become a single distant roar. Harry pushed himself onto one knee, trying to draw breath past the pain in his side. Above him, the Horntail raised its tail again.

Something dark passed over the stands.

It flew through the rain with the speed of an arrow, dropped over the enclosure wall and curved towards him. Relief surged so abruptly that Harry almost stopped moving. He recognised the danger of that a moment later; Draconia had warned him that a plan beginning to work was no excuse to behave as though it had finished.

Harry forced himself upright. The Firebolt crossed the final distance, its handle sliding into his waiting hand, and he mounted as its momentum carried him beneath the descending tail. A spike passed close enough for the displaced air to sting his cheek.

Then the ground fell away.

Harry climbed, and the audience's roar changed behind him. Rain streamed across his glasses, wind caught his hair, and cool air moved over skin that still felt too hot. Beneath him, the Firebolt answered the pressure of his hands and knees with immediate, familiar precision.

Fear remained. It simply became small enough to fly around.

On the ground, the Horntail had occupied the whole world. From the air, Harry could measure its distance, angle and speed, and those were things he understood. The arena arranged itself below him: the dip in the earth, the height of the ward, the three chains dividing the dragon's reach into overlapping arcs. Rain became drag against his robes. The wind told him how sharply he could turn.

His back still remembered the cold stone beneath the school, and his scar prickled with the ghost of Riddle's voice. The memories followed him into the air, but they could not make the calculations for him. Harry adjusted his grip, keeping the wand secured against the handle, and began to circle.

The dragon's chains had been fixed to three iron posts around the crater, joined to a broad collar at the base of its neck and restraints behind its wings. The nest occupied the centre. The Horntail could reach the boundary on two sides, but not the third, where scorched stone rose beneath the judges' platform. Its tail moved as Harry moved, guarding the eggs even while its head tracked his flight.

The golden egg remained near its left foreclaw. Harry could reach it in less than five seconds; the dragon would need fewer to kill him.

He dived from the right. The Horntail tracked him at once, its neck turning with unnerving speed and its jaws parting as he approached. Harry waited until orange light appeared at the back of its throat, then rolled. Fire cut through the space he had occupied. He passed beneath the edge of the stream, close enough to feel heat through his shoes, and pulled up behind the dragon's shoulder.

Its head followed him, but its body could not turn fully without stepping across the nest. Harry saw the brief hesitation before it adjusted its feet, choosing where to place its weight rather than lunging after him.

The eggs mattered more to it than killing him did.

He climbed before the tail found him, hearing the spikes whistle beneath the Firebolt, and widened his circle.

"You're very protective," he called down. "I know someone like that."

The Horntail roared.

"She's shorter."

A ragged burst of laughter reached him through the rain. Harry could not tell whether Draconia joined it; looking for her now would mean taking his eyes off several tons of offended dragon, and she would never forgive him for dying to check whether a joke had worked.

He made a second pass from the left. This time, the dragon anticipated the turn. It did not breathe fire. Instead, its neck snapped upwards, bronze horns cutting across his path, and Harry hauled the broom vertical. The Firebolt climbed so sharply that his feet slipped from their holds. He locked both knees around the handle as a horn scraped the bristles, tearing several twigs free.

Harry reached clear air with his heart beating against his throat. Below him, the Horntail spread its wings, opening them wider than the nest. One downward beat flung ash, water and loose stones across the enclosure. The gust struck Harry broadside, forcing him to tilt the broom against it, while the dragon reared beneath him. Chains pulled tight behind its shoulders, dragging the wings back before it could lift completely.

The left post shifted in the earth.

Harry had hoped Draconia's question about a broken chain would remain theoretical. He flew higher and made himself think, resisting the urge to try another pass simply because he was already moving.

The first plan depended upon drawing the dragon's head away, crossing low over the nest and taking the egg before it turned. The Horntail had already learned his approach; a third attempt would meet teeth rather than fire. The second plan used a Conjunctivitis Curse, but Krum's dragon had crushed its own eggs while blinded, and Harry could not bring himself to repeat it. The third required the mother to leave the nest voluntarily. Watching the care with which it placed each claw, he abandoned that one too.

The fourth was Hermione's least favourite: improvise.

Harry wiped rain from his glasses. The stands blurred and cleared, and the golden egg caught the light beneath the dragon's chest.

"Accio egg!"

Nothing moved. An anti-summoning ward; they had expected one, although Harry felt entitled to resent having the suspicion confirmed.

The Horntail released a burst of fire too short to reach him. He watched its head, then its tail, trying to understand the rhythm of its movements. When he approached from the front, the tail covered the eggs. When he went behind, the head turned, carrying flame across the nest's edge. The chains held its body in the middle, allowing it to defend both directions without giving up its position.

Harry climbed until the damp cold found the sweat beneath his collar, then flattened the Firebolt into a wide circle. The Horntail pivoted below him, one molten eye never leaving the broom. Its tail dragged a furrow around the nest, each adjustment economical. It no longer wasted movement on feints that remained beyond its reach.

It was learning him.

Wood had once made Harry spend an entire practice flying the same approach at six different speeds. According to him, a predictable Seeker was merely an injured spectator who had not landed yet. Harry had considered this excessive at the time. He revised the opinion when a tongue of fire crossed the space ahead of him, placed precisely where the dragon expected him to turn.

Harry dropped instead. The Firebolt plunged towards the crater wall, drawing a startled sound from several hundred people. He counted the passing rocks, pulled level an arm's length above the ground and shot along the painted line, mud spraying in his wake. The Horntail's head followed, but its body remained over the eggs, held neatly in place by the restraints.

As its collar chain tightened, the left post rocked again.

Harry looked at it. Sirius's voice returned, patient through the memory of training: You are not fighting the dragon.

He did not need to fight it. He needed it to move.

Harry dived towards the left boundary, flying level with the Horntail's eyes and just beyond the longest reach of the neck chain. The dragon lunged; he retreated, felt the rush of its breath and kept himself barely out of reach. It lunged again, dragging the left post another inch through the softened earth.

Handlers shouted beyond the wall. One raised his wand, but Bagman called for him to wait. Harry barely heard either of them. He was watching the chain, the claw beside the egg and the place the dragon would have to put its weight if it lunged again.

He slowed. The Firebolt trembled beneath the demand, fighting the near-stillness with small, restless movements. Harry held it in front of the dragon's face, close enough to see rain steaming from the scales around its nostrils.

"Come on," he whispered.

The Horntail struck. Harry pulled backwards as its jaws snapped shut around rain. The collar chain rang, the post tore halfway from the earth, and the dragon's body shifted from the centre of the nest. Its left foreclaw lifted from the golden egg.

Harry saw the opening, but it was too narrow. The tail still lay across his route, and he could not reach the egg without passing beneath it. He climbed, turned and flew at the dragon again.

The crowd's noise changed as they began to understand what he was doing. A chant rose from the Gryffindor stand, then collapsed when the Horntail breathed fire. Harry rolled around the stream, but the end of the Firebolt clipped its edge. Twigs ignited. He slapped the small flames out with his palm and smelled burned skin before he felt the sting.

Another lunge, another retreat. The post bent towards him.

"Harry!"

Sirius's voice reached him from the stands, urgent enough to cut through everything else. Harry glanced down and saw the second neck chain wound around the Horntail's front leg. If the first post failed, the twisted restraint could pull the dragon sideways across its own nest.

He had wanted to give it room. Now he was close to giving it too much.

The Horntail gathered itself for another strike, and Harry accelerated towards it instead of retreating. Confusion checked the dragon's movement for half a heartbeat. He dropped flat against the broom and passed beneath its jaw, so close that scales filled the world above him. Heat radiated from the softer plates of its throat. A bronze horn caught the shoulder of his robes and tore the cloth without finding skin.

Then he was behind its head. Harry banked right across the dragon's back as it tried to turn. The tangled chain caught its foreleg, shifting its balance; one wing opened against the ground to stop it falling across the eggs, and the tail swept wide to steady it.

The centre of the nest stood unguarded.

Harry aimed for gold. The Firebolt dropped through thirty feet, then twenty, the nest rising towards him as the Horntail recovered its footing. Its head began to turn. At ten feet, he released his right hand from the broom and reached towards the artificial egg, shining between two stone-coloured shells.

The dragon inhaled. Harry's fingers touched metal, curled around it and pulled.

The egg did not move. It had wedged beneath the curve of a real shell, caught just tightly enough to resist his grip. Harry pulled harder, feeling the broom carry him past the best angle. Firelight brightened the ground around his shadow.

"You have got to be joking."

He kicked the broom sideways. The Firebolt turned almost upon itself, and Harry's shoulder struck the edge of the nest, sending pain down the arm already bruised by his fall. He leaned farther, took the egg with both hands and wrenched it loose.

The sudden release unbalanced him. Harry slid from the broom, one knee catching the handle while his left hand closed blindly around a foot grip, the wand still trapped against his palm. Beneath his other arm, the golden egg pressed hard against his chest.

He hung upside down three feet above the nest.

The Horntail opened its mouth, and Harry saw every tooth. There was no time to remount and no room to turn. He swung once beneath the Firebolt, released the foot grip and fell far enough for his stomach to rise into his throat. Then his fingers caught the handle farther forward. The broom dipped beneath his weight and shot across the nest as flame arrived.

Fire struck his back.

The charm upon his robes resisted for less than a second. Heat bit through the cloth, the green stone burned against his wrist, and Harry smelled wool, hair and something sickeningly like cooked meat. The recognition reached him with the pain. It was his own skin.

He screamed, the sound swallowed by the dragon's roar.

Harry dragged himself onto the Firebolt with one arm. The egg made balance impossible, so he shoved it between his chest and the handle, pressed his body over both and accelerated. Each movement pulled at the burn, but the boundary lay ahead, visible through a shifting veil of smoke.

So did the Horntail's tail.

It swept out of the smoke in a black arc. Harry turned, but not far enough. The flat between two spikes struck the Firebolt behind his legs. Wood cracked, and the broom spun beneath him, exchanging sky for ground and ground for fire. He clamped his arms around the egg as one foot tore loose. The wand slipped from his hand, falling towards the crater.

Harry reached after it. The movement almost threw him from the broom.

"No."

He pulled his arm back and chose the Firebolt. The wand disappeared among the black stones below.

The broom dropped. Harry fought the damaged handle, forcing his weight away from the twisted left foot grip. Its tail twigs had split, leaving it shuddering at every correction, but it levelled ten feet above the ground before dipping again.

Behind him, metal shrieked. The loosened post tore free, and the chain whipped across the enclosure, striking the wall beneath the stands as spectators ducked. The Horntail surged forward, one restraint gone and another sliding loose behind its wing. Handlers cast together. Red spells struck its scales, but the dragon remained upright and opened its wings.

One beat lifted its front claws from the earth. The crowd broke into screams, and Harry looked over his shoulder to see the Horntail coming after him. The remaining chains still held its hindquarters near the nest, but its body crossed half the enclosure in a leap, jaws open and fire gathering behind them.

The damaged Firebolt would not climb quickly enough. Harry had no wand, his back burned, and the golden egg trapped one arm against his chest. Twenty feet separated him from the boundary.

He thought of Draconia's fingers shaking at his collar, Hermione's burned thumb and Sirius telling him not to perform courage for an audience. Their promise returned without any need to search for the words.

Choose getting back over winning.

Harry threw the egg.

It crossed the boundary, struck wet grass and rolled beneath the barrier. The crowd saw him abandon the prize he had finally taken, but Harry felt only the sudden freedom of his empty hand. The Firebolt rose beneath the loss of weight. He seized the handle with both hands and dragged it upwards as the Horntail breathed.

Flame filled the space below him. The broom climbed through its edge, and fire caught his trouser leg. Harry kept one hand locked around the handle and beat at the cloth with the other, heat racing along his calf, then pulled his feet higher as he cleared the dragon's head.

The Horntail's jaws closed around the heel of his left shoe. Leather tore. The shoe vanished between its teeth, and Harry felt displaced air graze the sole of his sock. He shouted and hauled both legs upwards just as the dragon reached the limit of its final chain.

The restraint snapped tight. Its head stopped, and Harry kept going.

He crossed the boundary six feet above the ground. The Firebolt finally surrendered beneath him, dropping sideways without answering his grip. Harry hit the wet grass, rolled and lost the broom from his hands. His shoulder struck the barrier. White light filled his sight, and the next several seconds disappeared.

When sound returned, someone was shouting his name. Many people were.

Harry opened his eyes to rain falling upon his face. Beyond the barrier, the Horntail roared while handlers forced it back towards the nest. Flames climbed one section of the arena wall. The judges' platform remained standing, although Bagman had taken shelter beneath the table.

Professor McGonagall knelt beside him, her face colourless with anger or fear. He could not tell which, and suspected she could not either.

"Do not move."

"Wasn't planning to."

"Potter."

"Still here."

Her eyes closed for the length of one breath. Then the healers reached them, hands examining his neck and burned leg while a wand passed over his ribs. Someone cut away the remains of his robe. Harry turned his head far enough to see the golden egg resting in the grass beyond the boundary.

"Does that count?"

Bagman had emerged from beneath the table. He stared at the egg, then at the painted line, brushing rainwater from the front of his robes with a hand that was not entirely steady.

"You retrieved it from the nest and removed it from the arena."

"That wasn't yes."

Bagman looked towards the other judges. Dumbledore had risen, Madame Maxime spoke rapidly to Karkaroff, and Percy Weasley searched through a stack of regulations with both hands shaking.

Professor McGonagall stood.

"If anyone intends to dispute that Mr Potter completed the task," she said, "they may do so after I have finished escorting him to the hospital tent."

Nobody disputed it.

Harry allowed the healers to lift him. The movement sent pain through his shoulder and back, sharpening the edges of the world before they blurred again. The stands had not stopped cheering, but he scarcely heard them. He was searching the front rail of the Slytherin section.

He found Draconia once before the canvas screens hid the arena. Narcissa held one of her arms and Lucius the other. Draconia stared at him as though she could not yet decide whether what she was seeing meant he was alive.

Harry raised the wrist wearing her charm. The movement hurt, but he kept it lifted until she saw.

The hospital tent was warmer than the enclosure and smelled considerably worse. Burn salve carried the sharpness of mint over something bitter and mineral, strong enough to catch at the back of Harry's throat. Wet robes hung from pegs beside the entrance, dripping into the trampled grass, and used bandages filled a covered basin that rattled whenever the Horntail struck against its restraints outside.

Madam Pomfrey waited beside the nearest cot.

"Put him down."

The healers obeyed with the speed of people familiar with her authority. Harry lay upon his stomach while she removed the remains of his shirt, lifting the scorched fabric away with her wand rather than pulling it across the skin. Even so, the touch of air sharpened the pain until his hands closed around the blanket beneath him.

"Second-degree magical burn across the left shoulder and upper back," she said. "Lesser burns to the calf and palm. Severe bruising along the right shoulder. Two cracked ribs. Possible concussion."

"Possible?" Harry asked into the pillow.

"You are speaking, which makes assessment difficult."

"I can stop."

"A promise I do not believe."

Cold salve met the burn. Harry forgot every clever answer he had been considering. His whole body locked, and a sound escaped him before he could prevent it. Madam Pomfrey steadied him with one hand below the injured area, her voice losing its sharpness without becoming any less firm.

"Breathe."

"Everyone," Harry managed, "has become very interested in that."

"Because you remain very poor at it."

The salve drew heat from the damaged skin, slowly enough that Harry could feel each patch begin to cool. Relief followed, deep and strange, painful in a different way from the fire. He concentrated upon the weave of the blanket beneath his fingers and breathed until his hands opened.

Outside, Bagman called for the judges' scores. The crowd answered with uncertain applause, and Harry tried to turn his head towards the sound. Madam Pomfrey held him still.

"Your marks can wait."

"I'm not worried about the marks."

"Then there is no need to move."

"I want to know if it counted."

"You crossed the boundary with the egg."

"Technically, the egg crossed first."

"How fortunate the judges have you available to clarify."

The tent entrance opened, admitting a gust of damp air. Sirius entered without permission, followed by Remus with Harry's wand in one hand and the damaged Firebolt beneath his other arm. Professor McGonagall came behind them, apparently having failed to stop either man and decided to supervise the invasion instead.

Sirius saw Harry's back and stopped. The colour left his face so quickly that Harry lifted his head despite Madam Pomfrey's restraining hand.

"It looks worse than it is."

"It does not," Madam Pomfrey said.

"Helpful."

Sirius crossed the tent, his hands hovering above Harry without finding anywhere safe to settle. He looked at the salve, the cut clothing and the burn along Harry's calf, then returned to his face as though he needed to check it again.

"You were on fire."

"Briefly."

"Harry."

"It's out."

Something broke across Sirius's expression. Anger and terror seemed to reach him together, leaving him unable to decide which ought to speak first. Harry recognised both and wished, uselessly, that he could have made arriving back alive a little less frightening to watch.

Remus placed the Firebolt against the cot.

"He threw the egg," he said quietly.

Sirius looked at him.

"When the chain broke," Remus continued, "Harry released the object he was trying to protect. He used both hands to climb."

Sirius turned back to Harry, and some of the fear gave way to understanding.

"You chose to come back."

Harry rested his chin upon the pillow. "I said I would."

Sirius sat on the floor beside the cot. His fingers closed carefully around Harry's unburned hand, avoiding the scraped knuckles as though the smallest hurt deserved his attention.

"Yes," he said. "You did."

Remus set the wand where Harry could see it. The holly bore a new black mark near the tip but appeared whole, its familiar grain visible beneath a smear of mud.

"A handler found it near the nest."

"Does it work?"

"It appears intact. Ollivander will check it properly."

Harry looked at the Firebolt. Its tail twigs were blackened and broken, and a crack ran across the handle behind the foot grips. Seeing the damage properly made him remember the moment it had stopped answering him, the strange, helpless drop before the ground arrived.

"That?"

"Repairable," Remus said.

Sirius did not look away from Harry. "Replaceable."

Harry understood the correction. He had lost the wand, nearly broken the broom and thrown away the egg; all three could be found, repaired or replaced. Sirius was looking at him as though Harry had occasionally forgotten that the same rules did not apply to people.

"I like that broom."

"Then we'll repair it." Sirius's thumb moved across his knuckles. "The point remains."

Professor McGonagall stood at the foot of the cot. Her hat had been bent out of shape, rain darkened her shoulders, and a streak of mud marked the sleeve that had touched the grass beside him. She looked at Harry for a long moment.

"Thirty points from Gryffindor," she said.

Harry blinked. "For what?"

"Recklessness."

"During the task?"

"I have not decided whether I possess the authority."

"I don't think—"

"And thirty points to Gryffindor for an exceptional demonstration of Summoning magic under pressure."

Harry stared at her. "So nothing happened."

"Precisely."

Her mouth compressed, failing to conceal the faint tremor at its edge.

"Well done, Potter."

She left before the words required further explanation. Harry watched the canvas fall behind her, then lowered his head before Madam Pomfrey could object again.

The first score appeared outside. Bagman's amplified voice announced a nine from Madame Maxime, followed by Karkaroff's four. The audience booed loudly enough to disturb the loose canvas at the entrance.

Sirius looked towards the tent wall. "I could be back before they notice."

"You threatened one student this week," Remus said. "Maintain a sustainable pace."

Dumbledore awarded Harry nine. Bagman gave ten. Percy, after a delay long enough to suggest he had consulted every regulation available, raised an eight. Forty points placed Harry first by one, although Bagman's announcement was almost drowned by another roar from the Horntail.

Harry closed his eyes. He did not feel victorious. He felt burned, exhausted and profoundly grateful that the cot beneath him remained still when he loosened his grip upon it.

Madam Pomfrey covered the salve with silver gauze, its fine threads cool against his back.

"You will remain here until I am satisfied that your ribs are stable."

"How long?"

"At least an hour."

Voices rose beyond the entrance, Hermione's clearer and more determined than the rest.

"He asked for us."

"I haven't," Harry said.

Sirius smiled faintly. "You will."

The canvas opened, and Hermione entered first. She saw Harry on the cot and crossed towards him so quickly that Madam Pomfrey placed herself in the way.

"No pressure upon his back or ribs."

Hermione redirected at the last second, dropping to her knees beside Sirius and wrapping her arms carefully around Harry's head. Her hair fell across half his face. She smelled of rain and the ink she had used to write their plans, both so familiar that he closed his eyes again.

"You threw the egg," she said.

Harry's cheek pressed against her robes. "People keep mentioning that."

"You remembered."

"I usually do."

Hermione drew back far enough to look at him. Her eyes were wet, and the effort of holding the tears there showed in the tightness around her mouth.

"Your Firebolt took too long."

"I noticed."

"The windows in Gryffindor Tower had been sealed against the rain."

"That would do it."

"It broke through two."

Harry looked at the damaged broom. "Apparently, it learned from Sirius."

"I am an excellent influence," Sirius said.

Hermione laughed once, but the sound collapsed halfway through and became a breath against Harry's hair. She kept one hand beside his on the blanket, close enough that their fingers touched.

Draconia remained near the entrance. Narcissa and Lucius stood behind her, Narcissa's hand resting upon her daughter's back. Lucius held the golden egg beneath one arm with the offended dignity of a man required to carry an enormous ornament.

Harry looked at Draconia and raised his wrist.

"I brought it back."

She crossed the tent with measured steps, her expression carefully composed. Beside the cot, she inspected the silver gauze across his back, the salve upon his palm, his swollen shoulder and the trouser leg Madam Pomfrey had cut to the knee. Harry watched her looking, becoming acutely aware of each injury as her attention settled upon it.

"The bracelet indicated injury," she said.

"Accurately."

"It became hot enough to burn my wrist."

Harry looked at her left hand. A red mark circled the skin where the matching charm had carried the warning. Her sleeve had been pushed back to keep it from rubbing.

"Draco—"

"That was your plan?"

The calmness of the question was more alarming than shouting. Harry considered his answer, aware that there was very little he could say which would improve it.

"Some of it."

"Which part?"

"The broom."

"Not the broken chain."

"No."

"Not losing your wand."

"Also no."

"Not hanging upside down over a nest while a dragon attempted to burn you alive."

"That developed naturally."

Draconia's eyes flashed.

"Do not."

Harry's smile vanished. The tent quietened around them; even Madam Pomfrey stopped arranging bottles upon the table.

Draconia closed one hand around the bedframe.

"There is a voice you use when you are frightened. There is another for when you are hiding pain. And then there is that voice—the one you use after you nearly die, when you decide making everyone laugh means nobody is permitted to be angry with you."

Harry stared at her, the reply he had been preparing suddenly impossible to say.

"I'm not—"

"You were on fire."

Her control cracked on the final word. Her voice rose, thin and frightened, and she looked away at once, as though turning her face could undo what everyone had heard. Harry saw her swallow. Her fingers tightened around the bedframe until the knuckles turned colourless.

Narcissa took a step forward, but Draconia shook her head. Harry understood the refusal without needing words. She did not want this to happen here, in front of everyone, with nowhere to hide her face. He had spent years asking pain to wait until nobody could witness it.

"Could we have a minute?" he asked.

Madam Pomfrey began to object. Sirius rose before she finished.

"We'll be immediately outside."

He met Hermione's gaze but did not ask her to leave. Remus guided Lucius towards the entrance before paternal concern could become an argument, and Narcissa touched Draconia's shoulder as she passed. Snape had appeared soundlessly inside the doorway; he took one look at his goddaughter, then held the canvas aside for the others.

Madam Pomfrey remained long enough to help Harry turn partly onto his side, arranging a pillow to support him without pressing upon the burn. She gave the three of them a look that promised consequences for any disregard of her instructions, then followed the adults. Snape pulled the canvas closed behind her.

The tent became still. Harry, Hermione and Draconia remained between the curtained cots, with rain whispering above them and the sounds of the enclosure softened by the walls.

Draconia continued holding the bedframe. Without the adults behind her, she seemed uncertain what to do with the composure she had fought so hard to preserve.

"Come here," Harry said.

"You have cracked ribs."

"They're on the other side."

"That is not how ribs work."

"Draco."

She looked at him. Harry opened his arm, offering what space he could without shifting against the dressing.

Draconia lasted one second. Then her knees struck the floor, and her arms went around him with enough force to pull him halfway from the pillow. Pain crossed his ribs, but he held her as she buried her face against the unburned part of his chest. He felt the first shudder move through her before he heard it. The first sob barely made a sound; the next caught in her throat and escaped despite her attempt to swallow it.

Harry had never heard her cry like this. In the Chamber, she had woken confused, emptied by possession and surrounded by adults. Afterwards, he had seen the aftermath in smaller, carefully hidden pieces: nightmares denied at breakfast, doors checked twice, blank pages avoided and anger whenever anyone asked whether she was afraid. She had always found a way to put herself back together before anyone could see too much.

Now she could not seem to stop shaking. Harry tightened his arm around her shoulders, uncertain whether he could hold her firmly enough to help.

"I'm here."

Draconia struck him once with the flat of her hand, finding the bruised shoulder. Harry hissed, and she pulled back just far enough to look horrified.

"Sorry," she gasped.

"You could choose somewhere else."

She hit the mattress instead, then pressed her face against him again.

"I thought it had you."

Harry held her tighter. "I know."

"When the broom turned—when you fell—I could not move." Her words broke against his chest. "I was back beneath the school. I could see you fighting it, and I was lying on the floor again, and there was nothing I could do because I had already let something use me to hurt you."

"You didn't let it."

"My hand held the wand."

"Riddle made it move."

"It was mine."

"The hand I used to write in the diary was mine too. That doesn't make what he did your choice."

Draconia's breathing caught. Harry had never said it quite so plainly. He had wanted to believe she understood, because she spoke about the Chamber so rarely, and because asking her to remember it felt cruel. But whenever it slipped into conversation, she described the possession as though it had happened through her rather than to her. Riddle had taken her secrets, her loneliness and her love for the people around her, and she still seemed to believe that surviving made her responsible for what he had done with them.

Harry rested his cheek against her hair.

"You did not hurt me."

"I hexed you."

"He did."

"I led you into the Chamber."

"He took you. I followed."

"You nearly died."

"I came back."

"You always say that as though coming back makes leaving harmless."

Harry closed his eyes. Beyond the tent, the crowd was still celebrating. Inside it, he could feel Draconia's fingers gripping the fabric at his side, and there was no answer that would make what she had watched disappear.

"No," he said. "It doesn't."

She went still against him, listening.

"I was scared too."

The admission came more easily than it had that morning. He was tired of trying to make fear sound smaller so everyone else could stop worrying about it.

"When I saw it, I heard him. Riddle. I couldn't remember the spell." Hermione's hand settled against his side, clear of the dressing. "I looked at both of you. I knew what you would say. That brought me back."

Draconia lifted her face. Tears had undone the careful work of the morning: her eyes were red, her lashes clumped together, and strands of hair had escaped where Harry's hand moved through them. She looked furious about being seen and too exhausted to hide anything else.

"Then why did you go near its head?"

"It seemed better before I did it."

"That is not an answer."

"It's the only one I have."

Hermione made a sound between a laugh and a sob. Draconia looked towards her as two tears finally escaped the restraint she had been maintaining. Hermione wiped them away impatiently, leaving a damp streak across her cheek.

"He threw the egg," she said.

Draconia's gaze returned to Harry. "I saw."

"He chose to leave."

"I saw that too."

Harry touched the bracelet between them. "I promised."

Draconia took his wrist in both hands, pressing her thumbs around the green stone as though she needed to verify that it remained solid. The chain had left a faint impression against his skin.

"You are never borrowing this again."

"I thought it worked."

"That is why. Feeling you burn was not a useful experience."

"Fair."

She unfastened the chain, then stopped before removing it. Harry waited while she looked down at the two matching stones, hers against the reddened skin of her own wrist. After a moment, she closed the clasp again.

"Keep it until tonight."

"Why?"

"Because I asked."

It was not the reason, but Harry did not make her find another one.

Hermione had gone very quiet. Looking past Draconia, Harry found her sitting with both hands clasped between her knees. Tears remained on her face, yet something tighter than crying held her still. One thumb moved across the side of the other in short, repetitive strokes. Blue ink stained the nail, the same ink she had used to write their contingencies.

"Hermione."

"I am fine."

Draconia lifted her head from Harry's chest. Even with her own face wet, she managed a look of profound disapproval.

Hermione closed her eyes. "I know. That phrase is banned."

Harry held out his hand. She stared at it, then at the pillows supporting him.

"You also have cracked ribs," she said.

"Everyone has become very informed about my ribs."

Hermione moved closer, though she did not fold into him as Draconia had. She took his hand in both of hers, holding it hard enough that the pressure almost hurt. Harry let her, watching the effort she made to keep her voice level.

"The broom took nine seconds longer than the fastest trial," she said. "I counted. Then your shield broke after less than two. You were out of sight behind the boulder for four, and when the tail hit you, you did not move for three." She drew a careful breath. "When you fell from the broom, I stopped counting."

Harry's fingers tightened around hers.

"I knew the plan," Hermione continued. "I knew every spell you had practised. I knew the ward specifications and the handlers' positions and how long Madam Pomfrey said dragon fire takes to cross enchanted cloth. None of it meant I could do anything."

Harry looked at the ink on her hand. He remembered her bent over their notes long after everyone else had given up for the evening, refusing to leave a question unanswered if there might be a book that could settle it. She had worked as though enough preparation would eventually force the task to become safe. Now she sat beside him, still trying to find the missing piece that might have stopped it hurting him.

"You did do something," he said.

Hermione shook her head.

"I looked at you when I froze. I knew what you would tell me."

"I couldn't even reach you."

"I still knew."

Her grip shifted. She traced the uninjured side of his palm, her fingers moving with the same care she usually gave to something delicate and irreplaceable.

"I hate watching you be brave," she whispered.

"I wasn't brave for most of it."

"That isn't what bravery means, and you know it."

"I was mostly sarcastic."

"That part was obvious."

Draconia gave a damp, unwilling laugh against him. Hermione's mouth trembled, then she leaned forward until her forehead rested beside Draconia's against the unburned part of his chest. Harry could not embrace them properly with one arm, so he settled for drawing them as close as he could.

When he shifted against the pillow, pain travelled along his ribs and both girls began to withdraw. He tightened his hold before they could get very far. They remained there while rain moved across the canvas roof, Hermione's fingers still wrapped around his and Draconia's breathing slowly becoming steadier. Harry stopped searching for something useful to say. For a little while, being close enough to feel that all three of them were breathing seemed sufficient.

When the adults returned, Narcissa looked once at her daughter's face and asked no questions. She knelt behind Draconia, restoring the clasp at the back of her hair with quiet fingers. Draconia lowered her head to let her, using the small task to recover while everyone else's attention moved towards Harry.

Lucius placed the golden egg upon the cot. Mud streaked one side, and a shallow dent marked where it had struck the grass.

"Your property," he said.

"I threw it."

"So I observed."

Harry waited for criticism. Lucius glanced briefly towards Draconia, then rested his hand upon the egg to stop it rolling against Harry's leg.

"An object may be recovered," he said. "People are less cooperative."

Harry heard Sirius's lesson in a different voice. He set his hand upon the cool metal shell, feeling the dent beneath his thumb.

"It still counted."

"Then your decision was both sensible and irritatingly successful."

"High praise."

"Do not become accustomed to it."

Remus had taken the wand to Ollivander while they were outside, and the wandmaker arrived to return it shortly afterwards. The black mark near the tip had been polished away, although a faint discolouration remained within the grain. Harry accepted it carefully, its familiar warmth settling into his palm.

"No fracture," Ollivander said. "The core remains responsive. I advise against dropping it near another nesting dragon."

"I'll add that to the plan."

Madam Pomfrey permitted Harry to sit after sealing his cracked ribs. She replaced the silver dressing, applied salve to his palm and performed three separate tests for concussion, watching his eyes more closely than he found comfortable.

"What day is it?"

"The twenty-fourth of November."

"Where are you?"

"A tent beside the first task."

"Who is Minister for Magic?"

"Unfortunately, Cornelius Fudge."

Sirius laughed. Madam Pomfrey did not.

"His judgement appears unaffected," Remus said.

"That remains debatable."

Harry was allowed to stand fifteen minutes later. His back protested, his ribs objected to every breath beyond a shallow one, and the burned leg held his weight only after he remembered not to straighten it completely. Someone had found him a replacement shoe, although it rubbed against the heel the dragon had so nearly taken instead.

Hermione took his left arm. Draconia reached towards his right, noticed the injured shoulder and took his hand instead.

"I can walk."

"We know," Hermione said.

Neither released him.

Outside, the handlers had restored the enclosure. The Horntail lay over its nest again, restrained by six new chains and a semicircle of exhausted wizards. Smoke drifted from the ground, and the broken post had carved a trench through the mud beneath the stands. One handler was examining the collar with a wand held very carefully away from the dragon's teeth.

Harry stopped at the canvas opening. The Horntail turned its head, and its eyes found him. His body remembered the fire before his mind could object; every muscle along his back tightened beneath the dressing.

Draconia's fingers closed around his hand. Hermione touched the inside of his wrist, giving him another small, familiar thing to feel. Harry looked at the golden egg beneath Lucius's arm, then back at the dragon.

"We're finished," he told it.

The Horntail released smoke through its nostrils. Harry turned away before it did and walked towards the judges' platform.

The champions gathered there for the final standings. Cedric wore fresh robes over his bandaged arm. Fleur's skirt had been shortened unevenly above the burned hem, and Krum had cleaned the mud from his face but not his boots. All four looked older than they had inside the tent, their earlier silences replaced by the weary understanding of people who no longer needed to imagine what waited beyond the canvas.

Bagman raised his wand to his throat.

"After one task, the standings are as follows! In fourth place, with thirty-six points—Fleur Delacour!"

Beauxbatons applauded. Fleur acknowledged the sound without smiling.

"In third, with thirty-seven points after a deduction for damage to protected eggs—Viktor Krum!"

Karkaroff's expression suggested the deduction would become an international incident.

"In second, with thirty-nine points—Cedric Diggory!"

The Hufflepuff section shook beneath stamping feet. Cedric glanced at Harry and mouthed, One point.

"And currently leading with forty points—Harry Potter!"

The sound struck Harry harder than the tail. Gryffindor erupted, red-and-gold banners dropping from the upper rail while Lee Jordan's amplified voice abandoned all pretence of neutrality. McGonagall removed the spell from him, but he continued shouting. Hagrid lifted both arms and accidentally knocked the hats from three people behind him.

The applause spread. Ravenclaw stood, and several Hufflepuffs followed Cedric's example. The Slytherin section resisted until Narcissa rose beside the front rail and began to clap. Lucius joined her with measured dignity. Snape's hands met precisely twice.

Draconia remained beside Harry. She could not clap without releasing his hand, and apparently had no intention of doing that.

Harry looked across the stands. For weeks, the school had watched him as a liar, a cheat or an inconvenience shaped like a famous name. Now admiration had replaced suspicion upon many of the same faces. Students who had avoided his eye at breakfast shouted for him from the benches, looking as though they had supported him all along.

He did not entirely trust the change. Nothing about him had become more honest while flying, and the dragon had not proved that he entered unwillingly. It had simply given them a better story to tell.

But Dean was shouting himself hoarse. Neville held the repaired banner above his head, Cedric was applauding, and Sirius's face contained so much pride that looking at him hurt worse than the burn. Harry let himself hear those voices, allowing the others to fall away for a moment. He had wanted to come back to them. Here they were.

Krum offered his hand once Bagman stopped speaking.

"You fly vell."

"You curse dragons well."

Krum glanced towards the Chinese Fireball's enclosure, regret passing through his expression.

"Not vell enough."

Harry followed his gaze. Beyond the screens, the handlers were still working around the nest. He understood that the damaged eggs mattered to Krum more than the deduction did.

"You got the one you were meant to."

Krum looked at him, then nodded, his grip firm and brief.

Fleur approached next, carrying her egg against one hip.

"You are reckless," she said.

"I'm told there's a voice for that."

Her gaze moved to Draconia, whose red-rimmed eyes had been softened by cosmetic magic but not entirely concealed. Something knowing entered Fleur's expression.

"I expect there is."

She kissed Harry once upon each cheek before he understood what she intended. Draconia's grip changed, and Hermione developed a sudden interest in the ground. Fleur walked away with the smallest suggestion of a smile, leaving Harry to consider whether international sporting events came with rules he had not been given.

"International diplomacy," he said.

"Naturally," Draconia replied.

Cedric reached them last.

"One point," he repeated.

"I'll lose it later."

"Please don't." Cedric lowered his voice. "The badger badges have already been changed."

"Into what?"

Cedric pointed towards a group leaving the stands. Ernie Macmillan's badge now read:

SUPPORT HOGWARTS'S CHAMPIONS

When pressed, it changed to:

BOTH OF THEM.

Harry looked at Cedric. "Your idea?"

"Mine said 'Stop Being Idiots.' Professor Sprout suggested revisions."

Harry laughed. The movement hurt his ribs, but he could not quite make himself regret it.

The feast began at four in the afternoon. It had not appeared on any schedule; Hogwarts created it through a collective refusal to return to lessons, filling the Great Hall long before the usual evening meal.

Food occupied every inch of the tables. Students who had changed out of their wet clothes retold four performances they had all watched from the same stands, improving each account as it travelled along the benches. Someone claimed Harry had flown directly through the Horntail's mouth. Another insisted he had broken the chain intentionally with a curse cast from his feet.

"That one is almost impressive," Harry said.

"I corrected him," Hermione replied.

"Of course."

"Your wand was on the ground. Accuracy matters."

Draconia sat opposite them at the end of the Gryffindor table. Her presence received fewer objections than it once had, and the visible line of Snape's gaze from the staff table discouraged the rest. She had rearranged her hair and restored the clasp at her throat, but whenever Harry shifted upon the bench, her eyes moved immediately to him.

Harry ate under supervision. Hermione filled his plate, while Draconia removed anything that required cutting with his injured hand. Sirius occupied the bench beside him and stole potatoes whenever Narcissa looked away. Remus spoke with Professor McGonagall about the broken restraint, and Lucius was engaged in a discussion with Mr Granger, who had arrived with his wife after the task, about the structural wisdom of placing spectators near airborne fire.

"There were wards," Lucius said.

"I saw where that chain landed. There were seats directly above it."

"The wards were inadequate."

"Then we agree."

Lucius appeared unsettled by this.

Mrs Granger hugged Harry once Madam Pomfrey confirmed she would not worsen the burns, then held him at arm's length to look at him properly. Narcissa adjusted the collar of his replacement robe. Hagrid cried into a tablecloth and insisted that the Horntail had always possessed a difficult temperament, as though this explained matters sufficiently.

For one strange hour, Harry sat among too many people who cared whether he had eaten, breathed or returned. He kept expecting the attention to become unbearable, to feel the familiar need to find a quiet corner where nobody could ask him anything. Instead, the warmth of the Hall and the ordinary fussing around his plate reached something exhaustion had left undefended.

At one point, Sirius reached across him to confiscate a carving knife before Harry could attempt the roast himself. Two minutes later, Narcissa removed the gravy boat from Sirius after he poured it over the tablecloth while arguing with Remus. Mrs Granger reached for a napkin just as Hermione cast a cleaning charm. It took Hermione two attempts, and her mother looked so delighted by the successful magic that even Lucius remembered not to be condescending.

Harry watched the adults irritate, correct and make room for one another. Nothing about the gathering was tidy. Sirius and Lucius could not agree upon where Harry ought to recover over Christmas. Hagrid continued defending a dragon that had tried to eat Harry's foot. Mr Granger asked questions about enchanted burns which made Madam Pomfrey regret permitting him near the medical notes. Narcissa monitored Draconia without admitting she was doing so, and Remus quietly moved the bread within Harry's reach whenever conversation distracted everyone else.

It did not resemble any family Harry had known how to imagine. Perhaps that was why he could sit among them without feeling that he had taken somebody else's place.

Sirius leaned close while Draconia and Hermione argued over whether Harry required another helping of vegetables.

"Too much?" he asked.

Harry looked around the table. "A little."

"We can leave."

The offer made it easier to stay. Harry shook his head, settling more comfortably against the cushion Madam Pomfrey had insisted upon putting behind him.

"Not yet."

Sirius smiled. "All right."

He remained beside him as the dishes changed and the candles burned lower, letting Harry listen without requiring him to join every conversation.

They opened the golden egg after sunset. Professor McGonagall lent them the same Charms classroom they had used for training, possibly because every other student who asked to witness the opening had been refused. Cushions covered the floor, and the damaged Firebolt rested upon a desk awaiting collection by a broom specialist. Rain continued beyond the high windows, softer now, silvering the glass.

Sirius and Remus had left for Hawthorn Cottage, and the Grangers had returned to Oxford. Narcissa and Lucius waited in a private room near the dungeons, having accepted that Draconia would not leave until Harry returned the matching charm. For the first time since he woke, nobody was watching him except the two people who already knew most of what he was trying to hide.

Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia upon the cushions. The golden egg occupied the floor before them, its dented side turned towards the light. Without the mud, it looked almost decorative. Harry found that rather offensive, considering what it had taken to get it.

"There may be a defensive enchantment," Hermione said.

"Bagman told us to open it."

"Bagman also described nesting dragons as a manageable risk."

Draconia drew her wand. "Open it from a distance."

Harry examined the seam around the egg. Two small handles met at the top, fitted so neatly that he had to run a finger between them before finding a grip.

"I fought a dragon for this. I'm opening it."

"That logic does not improve the safety."

"It improves my mood."

He took a handle in each hand and pulled. The egg unfolded, and sound struck the room.

It was not music, speech or anything produced by a creature Harry wanted to meet. A chorus of shrieks filled the classroom, high and wailing, as though dozens of ghosts were being murdered inside a metal bucket. The noise struck the windows and came back at them, setting Harry's teeth on edge.

He slammed the egg shut. Silence returned so abruptly that his ears continued ringing, and all three of them stared at the innocuous golden shell.

"A clue," Hermione said eventually.

"Perhaps the task is identifying who approved it," Draconia replied.

Harry opened the egg again. The screaming resumed. Hermione covered her ears, Draconia shouted something he could not hear, and he shut it after two seconds.

"Still horrible," he said.

"What did you expect to change?" Hermione demanded.

"I wanted to confirm consistency."

Draconia looked at him. It was the phrase she had used in the courtyard the previous night, and Harry watched her recognise it. For a moment, her expression resisted. Then a laugh escaped her, startled and unguarded, quite unlike the controlled breath she used when politeness required amusement.

Hermione joined her. Harry tried not to because of his ribs, then failed, pressing a hand against his side while the sound filled the classroom. Draconia's shoulder rested against his, and Hermione wiped beneath her eyes again, although this time she was smiling.

The golden egg sat closed between them, containing another danger, another deadline and a mystery none of them yet understood. Tomorrow, they would begin taking it apart. Tonight, Harry listened to the girls laugh and felt the warmth of the green stone slowly easing against his pulse.

The dragon had taken blood, skin and, judging by the faces waiting in the hospital tent, several years from everyone who loved him. It had not taken him. Harry had promised to come back, and for once the promise ended with all three of them alive enough to laugh.
