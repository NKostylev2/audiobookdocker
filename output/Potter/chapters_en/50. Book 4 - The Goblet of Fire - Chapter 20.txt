Harry stopped moving.

Hermione saw his fingers open. A moment earlier they had still been curled around the grass rope, holding on even after his head had fallen against Sirius's shoulder. Now his hand slipped down the front of Sirius's coat. The rope stayed wound around his bleeding wrist, a thin silver light running through its crushed green strands, but Harry no longer seemed to know it was there.

"Harry?" Draconia said.

He did not answer. Around them, the pitch had gone terribly quiet. Thousands of people sat beneath the stadium lights, looking down at the place where the champions had returned. Smoke rose from the ruined maze. The Cup lay on its side in the grass, and somewhere above them a child began to cry. Hermione could hear it, but the sound seemed very far away. She was watching Harry's chest, waiting for it to rise again. Draconia reached for his wrist. Her fingers shook as she searched, then moved to his throat.

"I can't find it. Hermione, I can't—"

"Here."

Hermione took her hand and guided two fingers beneath Harry's jaw. For a dreadful second she felt nothing either. Then the pulse beat against their fingertips, quick and weak and uneven. Draconia made a sound that was almost a sob. Hermione kept their hands in place, afraid either of them would lose it if they moved.

"There," she said. "He's still here."

Madam Pomfrey reached them with a stretcher floating at her side. She had already checked Cedric and woken him from the Stunning Spell; now she knelt beside Harry, her skirts brushing the wet grass. One of her grey plaits had come loose. She glanced at the blood on his clothes, the dark lines beneath his skin and Sirius's arms locked around him, then brought her wand close to his face.

"Put him on the stretcher, Mr Black."

Sirius did not seem to hear. He was bent over Harry, saying his name under his breath.

"Sirius," Remus said, kneeling beside him. "We need to let her help."

"He's breathing."

"Yes," Madam Pomfrey said. "And I intend to keep him breathing. Support his head while we move him."

That gave Sirius something to do. He slid one hand beneath Harry's neck and helped Remus lower him onto the canvas. Hermione moved with them, unwilling to let go until Madam Pomfrey needed the space. The brass key had twisted on its cord, caught against Harry's throat; Remus eased it free and laid it on his chest. The rope was worse up close. Grass had cut into the torn skin of Harry's arm, and some of the strands had disappeared beneath the swelling. Beside it hung the broken remains of Flitwick's anchoring bracelet. Draconia's smaller ward charm still glowed farther up his forearm, though its green light flickered whenever Harry's breathing faltered.

Hermione touched the knot. It tightened at once, silver flashing through the grass. Cedric cried out from the other end.

"Don't cut it," he said.

Madam Pomfrey turned. "Mr Diggory, this is constricting an open wound."

Cedric had propped himself on one elbow. His face was pale, and he still looked only partly awake, but he was staring at the rope with sudden alarm.

"I know. But it's still working. It tightened when you tried to move us apart."

"Then I shall undo it."

"Please don't. Not yet." He caught a handful of the grass where it crossed his chest. "It's Harry's magic. Take us together until you know what it's doing."

Amos Diggory dropped to his knees beside him. He seized Cedric's shoulders, looked at his face as though he needed to check every part of it, then pulled him into his arms. Cedric winced but did not pull away. His mother knelt on his other side, one hand against his hair.

"Dad," Cedric said, his voice changing. "I'm all right."

He was not, Hermione thought. None of them were. But Amos held him tighter, and for a moment Cedric let him. Then he looked towards Harry again.

"Together," he said. "Please."

Madam Pomfrey's mouth thinned. She summoned a second stretcher, brought it level with the first and joined the frames with a sharp metallic click. Only when Cedric had been lifted beside Harry did the rope loosen. Harry's arm lay between the two stretchers, supported by a folded cloth so its weight would not pull on the knot.

Hermione stared at it. This was nothing like the neat bindings Harry had practised. The grass had been twisted around his wrist and pulled hard enough to cut him. Blood had soaked into the knot. She did not yet know how they had escaped, or what Lucius had done, or why Harry had arrived holding a rope instead of Cedric's hand. She knew only that Cedric was alive beside him, and that Harry's magic would still not let them be separated.

"We can ask him later," Draconia whispered.

Hermione realised she had been looking for too long. She nodded, although later seemed like something they would have to earn. The stretchers rose. Sirius stayed beside Harry, one hand resting lightly over his chest. Hermione and Draconia followed at the rail, while the Diggorys gathered around Cedric. Remus went ahead to clear the way. People stepped back when they saw his face, even before he asked them to move.

Behind them, the stadium began to stir. A few voices became dozens, then hundreds. Students called questions down from the stands. Parents shouted for their children. Ministry officials crowded around the Cup, raising a cage of white light over it until Hermione could no longer see the metal beneath. Bagman stood at the champions' plinth with his hands hanging at his sides. Fleur was arguing with a steward at the barrier; Krum had already climbed over it and was making his way towards them.

"No one leaves the grounds!" Fudge shouted.

The response was immediate. People who had been sitting still got to their feet. Benches scraped, smaller children were pulled close, and the stairways filled with bodies trying to leave before the order could be enforced.

"He'll frighten them," Hermione said.

Draconia did not answer. She was watching the black line at Harry's jaw. It disappeared beneath his collar, and Hermione was frightened of how much more there might be where they could not see. Fudge came around the front of the stretchers and stopped in their path. His bowler hat was gone. Without it, with his hair damp and his face flushed, he looked less like a Minister than a man who had lost control of something and wanted someone to blame.

"Miss Malfoy. Where is your father?"

Draconia looked up slowly.

"I don't know."

"He left the grounds in response to a summons. Where did he go afterwards?"

"If I knew, I would be with him."

"I require an account of—"

"Move," Sirius said.

Fudge looked at him. "Mr Black, this is Ministry business."

"So was that Cup. Now Harry's bleeding on a stretcher, and you're standing in front of it. Move."

Sirius spoke quietly. Hermione found that more frightening than shouting would have been. Blood had dried over his shirt and coat, and his hand had not left Harry's chest.

"Are you threatening me?"

"Cornelius."

Dumbledore stepped between them. He had lost his usual unhurried manner; his robes brushed the grass as he came, and he looked directly at Fudge rather than around him.

"The gates are sealed. Amelia is securing the pitch. Your questions can wait until these boys have been treated."

"And Malfoy?"

"We shall speak to him when he returns."

Draconia looked at Dumbledore then, searching his face. He met her eyes, but could offer nothing more. Fudge finally stepped aside, and the stretchers moved on. Hermione kept one hand on the rail. It was easier than looking at all the blood, and it let her feel each small movement of the stretcher beneath her palm. She had read about blood loss and curse damage, had made lists of the signs and practised what to do. Now every fact seemed to tell her another way Harry might die. His skin was cold. His breathing was wrong. Something dark was moving beneath it. She could not remember the page that told her how to bear knowing.

His fingers twitched when someone shouted behind them. Hermione glanced back, angry with the voice before she even knew whose it was. Then she looked at Harry again. She did not know whether he could hear them, or what the noise might mean to him if he could. The castle doors stood open at the top of the steps. Every window was lit. A few hours earlier those lights had seemed warm and welcoming; now Hermione wanted to reach them before anything else could happen. Teachers stood aside as the stretchers climbed. Hagrid held one of the great doors open, its upper hinge hanging crookedly where he had pulled too hard. His face changed when he saw Harry.

"Is he—?"

"Alive," Hermione said quickly. "He's alive."

Hagrid nodded, though tears were already caught in his beard. Hermione said it once more as they passed him. Draconia's hand found hers beside the stretcher, and neither let go as they entered the castle.

The first healing charm made Harry scream.

He did not wake. His back arched, the rope between the stretchers blazed silver, and the black lines beneath his skin gathered around the blue light of Madam Pomfrey's wand. She stopped at once. Harry fell back, gasping, and Sirius reached towards him. Snape caught his wrist.

"Wait. The curse reacted to her spell."

"Let go of me."

"Then listen to Poppy before you touch him."

Madam Pomfrey laid her wand aside. "Sirius, stay at his head. Talk to him. Leave his chest clear."

It was the second time she had given him an instruction he could bear to follow. Sirius pulled free of Snape and moved to Harry's shoulder. The hospital wing had changed while they were crossing the grounds. The other patients had been moved behind a screen at the far end, and the curtains around the nearest beds were gone. Cabinets stood open. Lamps hung low over the linked stretchers, lighting every wound with a brightness Hermione wished she could turn down.

Snape's robes had been fastened in haste. He carried a fitted case full of potions, silver instruments and folded dragon hide, with three small vials tucked into a separate compartment. He opened it on the nearest table and began setting things out. Bill Weasley was already examining the broken anchoring bracelet. Dumbledore stood at the foot of Harry's bed, his face grave beneath the lamplight.

Hermione and Draconia had not been told to leave. She suspected no one wanted to spend the time arguing with them, but she stayed very still in case they changed their minds. Remus stood behind Sirius. On the adjoining bed, Cedric's mother held his hand while Amos rested a palm on his ankle, as though he too needed to feel that his child was really there. Madam Pomfrey cut away Harry's shirt. Hermione's breath caught. There were more wounds beneath it: thin cuts across his ribs and shoulder, a deeper one at his side, dried blood over places that had started bleeding again. The cuts were too carefully spaced. Someone had chosen where to put them.

Draconia gripped her hand so tightly it hurt. Hermione was grateful for the pain. It gave her somewhere to put her attention while Madam Pomfrey checked each wound. Snape moved his wand above the dark lines. Violet shapes appeared in the air, turned and faded. He repeated the examination over the long knife wound in Harry's arm, then paused over his scar. His expression grew harder with every pass.

"A blood curse," he said. "Introduced through that wound. The Cruciatus Curse helped it spread."

"Can you get it out?" Sirius asked.

"Not by forcing it. You saw what happened when Poppy tried to heal him. It's caught in his own magic."

Bill held up a piece of the bracelet. Its tiny rune had split down the middle.

"The anchor broke when the Cup took them," he said. "The return address held, though. That's how it found Hogwarts again."

"And this came back with him," Dumbledore said.

Bill nodded. "Attached to his blood and his magical signature. The castle knows Harry. It may not be able to tell that something arriving with him doesn't belong here. We need to keep this room separate from the boundary wards."

Hermione looked towards the windows. Beyond them were the grounds, the stadium and all the people who still did not know what had happened. She remembered the basilisk travelling through the pipes, finding a road through places no one thought to watch. Beside her, Draconia had gone very still. Hermione knew that expression. She remembered a diary and Draconia's hands stained with ink. Without thinking, both girls reached for Harry.

"Not yet," Snape said.

They stopped. His gaze moved from their hands to their faces.

"Let us make it safe first."

Hermione drew her hand back. Draconia did too, more slowly, and they stood shoulder to shoulder while Snape opened one of the old vials. A wisp of smoke curled from it. He mixed three drops with a thread from Harry's ruined shirt, touched the bowl with his wand and watched the liquid turn black.

"Poppy, no more healing charms until we have this contained. We can treat the blood loss with potions."

"He isn't awake enough to swallow."

Snape glanced at the silver tubes in his case. "The vapour method. Slowly."

Madam Pomfrey nodded and took one of the tubes. For a few minutes there was no more talk except what the two of them needed to work. Snape thinned the blood-replenishing potion into a mist, and Pomfrey enclosed Harry's bed beneath a clear canopy. The medicine gathered there a little at a time, moving with his breath. Bill laid a second ward around it, drawing each rune towards the bed rather than the castle. Dumbledore brought Fawkes to the headboard. The phoenix settled, looked down at Harry and began to sing.

Hermione had never heard him sing like that. It was only a few low notes, repeated softly, but the sound eased something in her chest. The dark lines under Harry's skin slowed. Madam Pomfrey counted his breaths, checked his pulse and went back to the potions. No one told them it was over. Sirius kept talking. At first Hermione thought he was speaking to Remus, until she realised he was describing Hawthorn Cottage. A tile above the kitchen had come loose. Remus wanted to fix it without magic, which Sirius thought was making unnecessary work for everyone. The motorbike needed a new fuel line. Dobby had taken offence at the pantry shelves and was rearranging them. Harry lay still beneath the canopy. Sirius leaned closer.

"Your blue mug's still by the sink," he told him. "I put it away once, but it looked wrong without it there. You can laugh at me for that when you wake up."

Hermione watched Harry's chest rise. She did not know whether he could hear Sirius, but she hoped he could. She hoped that somewhere beneath the pain he was thinking of the kitchen, the mug and the house waiting for him, rather than whatever place had sent him back like this. Time passed in small tasks. A bottle emptied. Bill redrew a rune. Madam Pomfrey replaced a bloodstained cloth. The light in Draconia's ward charm faded from green to cloudy white, and its chain remained too hot to remove until Snape cooled it. Hermione lost track of the clock. She only looked up when it began to strike midnight. Cedric had dozed while they worked. Now he stirred and tried to sit, but Madam Pomfrey put a hand on his shoulder.

"Lie down. Your knee hasn't finished setting."

"The shroud," he said, still looking half asleep. "Did it break?"

"You're in Hogwarts, Cedric," his mother told him. "You're both here."

He looked at the rope stretched between the beds. Most of its silver light had faded, though a few strands still brightened when Harry moved.

"I remember it breaking," he said quietly. "Before Nott Stunned me. Harry couldn't hold it any longer."

He lay back, his mother's hand resting against his cheek. "He held it as long as he could."

Draconia turned away. Hermione saw her press her fingers to her mouth, trying to make herself breathe quietly. She went as far as the privacy ward before her shoulders began to shake. Hermione followed, opening the door for her because Draconia seemed unable to find the handle.

Narcissa was waiting beneath one of the high corridor windows. Lucius's cane rested across her knees, the silver serpent held between her fingers. Draconia remembered him leaving it with her before he went, but seeing it there now made his absence worse. He never went anywhere without it. The chair beside her mother was empty. The Grangers sat opposite. Remus had come out of the ward a few minutes before and was standing near the window, looking down at the grounds. Hagrid occupied the floor beside the doors; a broken chair had been pushed discreetly beneath a bench. Farther along the passage, McGonagall was speaking to Amelia Bones. They stopped when the girls appeared. Draconia looked at the empty chair.

"He should be back."

Narcissa stood, leaving the cane on her seat. "I know."

"Harry's been here for hours. He said Father helped them. So did Cedric. If Father got away, why isn't he here?"

"I don't know, darling."

It was worse than a promise would have been, and somehow easier to bear. Her mother was frightened too. Draconia could see it now, in the crease beside her mouth and the way she held her hands out without quite knowing what to do with them.

"I asked him to go," Draconia said.

"He would have gone anyway."

"I told him to bring Harry back."

Her voice broke on Harry's name. Narcissa crossed the space between them and put her arms around her. Draconia had meant to remain standing, to ask sensible questions and work out where her father might have gone. Instead she buried her face against her mother's shoulder. The first sob hurt her throat. After that she could not stop them.

"I sent him," she said into the fabric.

"No. You asked him to help someone you love. He chose to go."

"Because of me."

Narcissa held the back of her head. "Because of you, and Harry, and because he could. He knew what he was doing. You didn't make that choice for him."

Draconia clung to her. They were not a family who cried in corridors. Comfort at home usually came as a hand on her shoulder, a favourite meal appearing without comment, her mother straightening a collar that did not need straightening. Now Narcissa held her hard enough to make her ribs ache, and Draconia was glad of it. Near the hospital doors, Mrs Granger reached for Hermione. Hermione hesitated, then went into her arms, and Draconia heard the small, broken sound she made. On any other day they would both have been embarrassed. Tonight it helped to know she was not the only one who could no longer hold herself together. The doors opened. Draconia lifted her head so quickly her neck hurt.

Snape came out carrying a basin of dark water. There was blood on his cuff and a tiredness about his face that she had never seen in a classroom.

"He's stable," he said, before anyone could ask. "Still unconscious. The containment is holding. Removing what's left of the curse will take longer."

Sirius appeared behind him. He had one hand on the doorframe, and for the first time since the pitch Draconia saw his shoulders drop. Hermione shut her eyes against her mother's shoulder. Draconia tried to take a full breath. It caught halfway down, but Narcissa rubbed her back until she managed another. Then footsteps sounded at the end of the corridor. Lucius came around the corner between two Aurors. His robes were torn open along his side. One sleeve had been cut away, exposing his bandaged forearm and the blackened skin around the Dark Mark. Blood had dried at his temple and behind his ear. He had no wand; Tonks carried it, while Kingsley kept a hand near his elbow, ready to steady him.

Lucius looked at Narcissa, then Draconia, then the hospital doors.

"Harry?"

"Alive," Sirius said. "Stable."

Draconia ran. Her father braced himself just in time to catch her, and she felt him wince as her arms went around him. She loosened her grip at once, then struck his chest with one weak, furious fist.

"You took too long."

"I know."

"We didn't know where you were."

"I couldn't send word. I'm sorry."

That nearly undid her again. He held her with his good arm, his cheek pressed against her hair. Beneath her hand his heart was beating. She kept her fist there, feeling it, while Narcissa came to them.

Her mother touched the blood at his temple. "How much of this is yours?"

"Enough to make that a poor question."

"Lucius."

He looked at her properly then. She caught his face between her hands and kissed him, carefully at first, then with a suddenness that made Tonks turn away. Kingsley found something to examine in the nearest window. Draconia stayed between her parents and did not move when her mother's arm closed around her too. Sirius crossed the corridor. Lucius watched him come, his familiar reserve returning as though he had reached for a coat.

"You got the Cup to Harry," Sirius said.

"Yes."

"And his wand."

"Yes."

Sirius glanced at the torn robes. "Then you left."

Narcissa stiffened. Lucius met Sirius's eyes.

"A Killing Curse missed me by inches. I had anchored the shield, but I couldn't hold them off much longer. If I stayed, I would have died in front of it. Leaving gave Harry a few seconds while they turned on me."

Sirius looked towards the ward. Draconia could see how much he hated having to imagine those seconds.

"He nearly didn't make it."

"I know."

Lucius offered no further defence. After a long moment Sirius held out his hand. Her father looked down at it, surprised enough to forget to conceal it.

"Thank you," Sirius said roughly. "Don't make me say it twice."

Lucius took his hand. "I heard you."

They shook once, awkwardly, and let go. Draconia watched them through the tears she still could not quite clear from her eyes. A few hours earlier she would have laughed if someone had told her she would see that. Now she wanted to remember it exactly. Fudge arrived before either man could say anything else.

"Lucius Malfoy, you are required to submit to questioning about your participation in an illegal gathering—"

"He has already surrendered his wand," Amelia said, coming down the corridor. "He returned under Auror escort."

"He answered the Dark Mark."

"With our knowledge, to locate the missing champions."

"I did not authorise—"

"Then include that in your statement, Cornelius. Let the healer see him."

Fudge's eyes moved to the exposed Mark. Draconia wished her father would cover it, though she knew he could not bear a sleeve against the burned skin.

"You admit that there was a gathering?"

Lucius eased Draconia's hands from his robes. "The Dark Lord summoned us. I went to find Harry and Cedric. I attacked Nott, returned Harry's wand and sent him the Cup. I will give you the names of the men I recognised when I have been treated."

"And You-Know-Who permitted you to leave?"

"Look at my arm, Minister. Does this resemble permission?"

Fudge did look. The Mark stood dark and swollen amid the blistered skin, with thin cracks spreading away from it.

"He tried to pull me back through it," Lucius said. "I made five jumps before I broke his hold. The last took me to a cliff above the North Sea. I couldn't immediately risk another. I had to wait until I could trust myself to reach Hogsmeade without leaving half of me behind. Then I walked to the school."

Tonks lifted his wand slightly. "His Patronus reached the north gate before he did. Once he arrived, he surrendered his wand and waited while we verified his identity. We escorted him from there."

"Malfoy can cast a Patronus?"

"A peacock," Lucius said. "You may ask the gate guards."

For an instant Draconia remembered the first time she had seen it spreading its silver tail across the drawing room. She had been much younger, certain her father could do anything. She pressed closer to his uninjured side.

Amelia pointed towards the classroom beside the wing. "Healer first. We can take the account in there afterwards."

"Take mine now," Cedric said.

He stood in the hospital doorway with his arm over his mother's shoulders. His knee was held straight in a yellow brace; Amos supported him on the other side. Madam Pomfrey followed, plainly unhappy with all three of them.

"You should be in bed," Fudge told him.

"I know. But I heard you asking questions on the pitch. I want what happened written down before anyone decides what we meant."

Amelia studied him. "We can record your account beside your bed."

Cedric glanced back into the ward. "Harry needs quiet. Could we use the classroom? Just long enough to get it down."

"Ten minutes," Madam Pomfrey said. "Then I examine you again. And if your pulse changes, we stop."

Cedric nodded. "I want my parents there. Professor Dumbledore too. Could you make a Pensieve copy as well as the written record? And have witnesses seal it?"

"Yes," Amelia said. "I'll explain how before we begin."

Hermione was looking at him. He caught her eye and managed a small smile.

"I remembered your lists."

"That's a good one," she said, her voice still thick.

"Harry said you'd tell me if I missed anything."

Draconia looked towards the doors. Even inside the maze, he had apparently been talking about Hermione as though they would all be together afterwards. It was a small thought, but she held on to it.

Cedric turned to both girls. "Will you come?"

Fudge objected. "They are minors, and personally involved."

"So is Harry," Cedric said. "That didn't stop what happened."

He paused, shifting his weight against his father's arm. When he spoke again, his voice was quieter.

"They should hear it from me. I don't want them finding out from a newspaper."

Draconia looked at Hermione. There was fear in her friend's face now, a different kind from the one they had carried out of the ward. Draconia felt it too. Cedric had been there. In a few minutes they would know what they had spent hours trying not to imagine.

"All right," Hermione said.

Draconia nodded, still holding her father's sleeve.

McGonagall moved the desks aside while Amelia prepared the record. Cedric sat in a padded chair near the fire, his injured leg resting on a cushion. His mother pulled a blanket over his knees. Amos remained behind him, close enough to help without being asked. Hermione took a seat beside her parents. She watched Amelia place a silver recording quill on the desk and lay a clean memory vial beside it. The small, ordinary preparations helped. A chair, a bottle of ink, the date spoken clearly for the record: things people did when they intended to listen.

Sirius stayed by the door, from which he could see the entrance to the hospital wing. Remus stood near him. Lucius had accepted a bandage across his side and sat against the wall with Narcissa and Draconia. His cane was back in his hand. Bill came in last, wiping rune ink from his fingers with a cloth that had already become more ink than fabric. Fleur and Krum arrived before Amelia began. Fudge turned towards them at once.

"This is a private inquiry."

"Two of our fellow champions disappeared," Fleur said. "I would like to know how. Madame Maxime would also like an account which has not passed through your office first."

Krum nodded. "We stay."

Dumbledore indicated the spare chairs. Fudge looked at him, then at Amelia, but neither moved to support him. Fleur sat down beside Krum. The classroom door closed, and the sounds of the corridor grew faint. Amelia explained the quill and the memory vial. Cedric would give his spoken account first. If he still wished to provide a memory afterwards, Dumbledore would help him extract it, and copies would be sealed before witnesses. He could stop at any point. If he did not remember something, he should say so.

"Don't try to fill a gap for us," she told him. "What you remember matters more than a tidy account."

Cedric looked relieved by that. He nodded and placed both hands on the arms of his chair.

"Your full name, please."

"Cedric Amos Diggory."

The quill began to write.

"Start when you reached the centre of the maze."

Cedric looked at the desk. For a few seconds he said nothing. Then he described the Cup standing on its plinth, the tests he and Harry had made and the magic that had only appeared when they tried to finish the task. They had not rushed at it. Hermione listened to that twice, because Fudge asked whether they had touched an untested object. Cedric explained the mouse, the stone and the spells that had found no active Portkey. They had tried waiting. They had tried the emergency exit and the agreed pause. The contract had tightened instead, until breathing became difficult and there was no safe way to remain in the clearing.

Hermione lowered her eyes to her hands. They had made plans for this. Adults had approved them, spells had been tested, and Harry had carried a way out. Somehow the task had taken each promise apart. Bill asked one question about the anchoring runes. Cedric remembered a brief flash from Harry's wrist as they were pulled away. Bill wrote that down separately, beside his notes on the broken bracelet, and Amelia asked Cedric to continue. He described the graveyard, the cauldron and the three men waiting there. Peter Pettigrew carried a small, wrapped creature. Barty Crouch Junior stood near a headstone with a patch over his empty eye. The third man was Theodore Nott's father.

"You are certain of Pettigrew?" Fudge asked.

"Yes."

"You knew him before this?"

"I knew what he looked like. Harry recognised him too. Crouch spoke to him by name."

Sirius's jaw tightened by the door. Hermione remembered the years Pettigrew had let everyone believe he was dead. Now he had stood in front of Harry again, alive and helping the man who had murdered his parents.

"Continue," Amelia said.

Cedric drew a breath.

"The creature told them to kill me. Crouch raised his wand. I didn't understand what was happening quickly enough. Harry did."

His hands tightened on the chair.

"He knocked me down. The curse passed over us and hit a stone angel. I remember pieces of it falling. Harry was already moving when I looked up."

Mrs Diggory pressed a hand to her mouth. Amos looked at the window, then back at his son.

"Harry bound me behind a grave," Cedric said. "I tried to get out. I thought he was leaving himself alone, and I could help."

"So he restrained you against your will," Fudge said.

"Yes. He also put a shield in front of me."

"Preventing you from assisting him."

Cedric looked up. "A few seconds later Crouch cast the Killing Curse again. It hit the roots where my chest had been. If Harry hadn't put them there, you wouldn't be taking my statement."

The quill scratched across the parchment. Hermione heard the fire settle, and rain began to tap against the window. She had not noticed the weather changing.

"The wood broke," Cedric continued. "Everything went green. Then I felt something pull inside my chest. I couldn't find my own heartbeat. I thought the curse had reached me."

He laid a hand over his heart, keeping it there as he spoke.

"Then I could feel Harry's. Mine was still there, but underneath it. I don't know how else to explain it. I could breathe. I could think. But the magic around me was hiding all of that."

Bill stepped away from the wall. "The physical barrier caught the curse. That part is important. A living person didn't simply block it with a Shield Charm. The spell changed after the wood took the impact."

"Can you account for the change?" Amelia asked.

"Not yet. I can examine the residue. I can't give you a name for something I haven't seen before."

Cedric nodded. "The roots closed over me. I could see out through little gaps, but from outside it must have looked like a grave. Nott checked it with his wand."

"What did he say?"

Cedric swallowed. "'Dead.' That was all, at first. Crouch lowered his wand. Then Nott said Harry was protecting the body and they should let him tire himself out. Voldemort told them to leave me."

Fudge flinched at the name. Cedric noticed, but did not correct himself.

"They thought they'd killed me," he said. "They stopped looking for a way through."

Mrs Diggory caught Amos's sleeve. Hermione saw his hand cover hers. Neither had made a sound since the account began.

"You were injured and exposed to unknown magic," Fudge said. "Your impression of what happened to your pulse—"

"May be imperfect," Amelia interrupted. "He has said that. Their words are a separate matter. Cedric, did you hear them clearly?"

"Yes. I was only a few feet away."

Amelia nodded, and the quill moved on.

"Did Harry know you were alive?"

"He must have. If I moved, the spell brightened. He kept his face turned away from me so they wouldn't notice. I realised what he was doing, and I stopped trying to get out."

Hermione pictured Cedric lying beneath those roots, listening to people discuss him as a body. She tried not to picture Harry on the other side. The effort failed. She gripped the edge of her chair and looked towards the closed door, beyond which he lay asleep under clean sheets.

"You remained conscious?" Dumbledore asked.

"Until Nott Stunned me near the end."

"And the connection allowed you to see what Harry saw?" Fudge asked.

"No." Cedric looked directly at him. "I saw through gaps in the roots. The spell let me feel when it was failing. Sometimes Harry's pain came through it. It didn't tell me what he was thinking."

Cedric glanced at Amelia. "Is he going to interrupt every time I say something he doesn't like?"

Amelia set her monocle on the desk. "Cornelius, let him finish. You will have an opportunity to ask questions afterwards."

Fudge folded his arms. Cedric looked down again, and his mother placed a hand on his shoulder. Madam Pomfrey came forward to check his pulse. No one spoke while she counted. Then she adjusted the cushion beneath his leg and returned to her place beside the door.

"Harry had to keep the shroud working," Cedric said. "Each time they cursed him, it thinned. I could feel my pulse trying to come free. Then he pulled it back beneath his. Over and over."

"Could he have ended the spell?" Amelia asked.

"I think so. It was his binding. He was holding it."

"Did he try?"

"No."

The answer was very quiet. Hermione had expected it, but hearing it was worse. The quill wrote the single word as neatly as all the others. She wanted it to hesitate, to leave a stain or tear the parchment. Something to show that it had understood. Cedric described the ritual. Bone taken from the grave. Pettigrew cutting off his own hand. Crouch drawing blood from Harry's arm while Nott held a bowl beneath it. The wrapped creature went into the cauldron, and Voldemort came out.

He gave the details he remembered: the face, the voice, the long white fingers. The way Harry's scar hurt when Voldemort touched it. The summons through the Dark Mark and the masked figures who arrived afterwards. He had not seen Karkaroff or Snape. He had seen Lucius kneel. Draconia looked at her father. Lucius was staring at the floor between his shoes.

Hermione felt her mother's hand close around hers. Lucius looked up at once.

"I needed him to believe I still thought as he did," he said. "I am sorry you had to hear it."

Hermione nodded. She understood. It still hurt to hear her friendship described that way, and she was grateful he had not asked her to pretend otherwise.

"Did he believe you?" Draconia asked.

"Enough to let me remain near the Cup."

Lucius's hand closed around his cane. Draconia moved her chair closer to his. The account became harder after that. Cedric spoke about the cuts, each made while the others watched. Nott examined the shroud whenever Harry refused to answer a question. Voldemort used the Cruciatus Curse, stopped long enough to speak, then used it again. Cedric's voice stayed steady, but he had stopped looking at anyone. He spoke to the desk, pausing when he needed to swallow. Hermione wanted to tell him they had heard enough. She knew he would not stop while there was still something Harry had endured that Fudge might call an accident.

Then Cedric described the moments when Harry went still beneath Voldemort's wand. His eyes followed things that were not in the graveyard. Sometimes he answered words no one else had heard. Afterwards Voldemort demanded Sirius's address, or asked about the Malfoys, or returned to questions about the people Harry knew.

"He was in Harry's mind," Cedric said. "I couldn't see what he found."

Hermione looked at her parents. Her father sat with his hands clasped between his knees. Her mother's grip had tightened.

"Did Harry tell him anything?" Hermione asked.

"Nothing useful that I heard."

"You can't know what happened inside his head."

"No. I can't." Cedric met her eyes. "I'm telling you what he said aloud. Voldemort asked where Sirius lived. Harry said Belgium, because the flour had gone there."

For a moment no one reacted. Then Mr Granger lowered his head, and a small, helpless laugh escaped Hermione. Draconia made the same sound beside her father. Hermione remembered Harry making an equally hopeless excuse across a library table, insisting he was serious while Draconia tried not to laugh. Hearing that familiar voice in the middle of this account hurt more than she expected.

"Did Voldemort believe him?" Amelia asked.

"No. He cursed him again."

The laughter stopped. Cedric rubbed one thumb over the arm of his chair.

"Harry still tried to make jokes," he said. "Even when he could barely talk. Some of them were awful."

"They usually are when he's scared," Hermione whispered.

Cedric nodded. "I know."

He took a little water from the glass his mother offered, then set it down with both hands.

"He didn't ask for mercy. Voldemort kept waiting for it. I could tell when he'd left a pause for Harry to beg, because everyone else went quiet too. Harry wouldn't say it."

Hermione looked down at her lap. Draconia had turned her face towards Narcissa, and her mother put an arm around her.

"I'm not saying he wasn't frightened," Cedric added. "He was. I could hear it. But he kept the shroud. Whenever Nott looked at me, Harry found something to say that made them look back at him."

Sirius turned away from the door. He had been so still that Hermione had almost forgotten he was there.

"There was one point when he asked Voldemort to stop," Cedric said. "Something was happening in his head. I couldn't see it, but it was different from the questions before."

He paused. Hermione waited, scarcely breathing.

"When he came out of it, he said, 'You had to invent them disappointed, because the real ones wouldn't cooperate.'"

Sirius closed his eyes.

"I think he was afraid you'd be disappointed," Cedric said. "I don't know what he was shown. I only know what he said afterwards. Then we came back, and I heard him tell Sirius he'd lost."

Draconia wiped her cheeks with the heel of her hand. "Oh, Harry."

Hermione reached across the space between their chairs. Draconia caught her hand, and they held on while Cedric took another drink of water. The room seemed smaller now. Her parents, Narcissa, Sirius and Remus were all close enough to touch, and Harry had apparently needed to argue for their kindness in a place where none of them could reach him. Cedric continued with the duel. He described the wands joining, the golden light rising around Harry and Voldemort, and the figures coming out of Voldemort's wand. He could not name all of them. An old man, a woman, then two younger people who spoke to Harry differently. Sirius made a sound and put one hand against the wall. Remus moved beside him.

"He knew them," Cedric said softly. "I could see that much."

He told them how the roots had begun to disappear. When the last one failed, his own pulse returned so strongly he could not hide the breath he took. The Death Eaters saw him move. He threw a piece of stone at one of them, took a wand and tried to reach Harry. Crouch tackled him. Cedric managed to Stun him, then Nott raised his wand.

"That's where my memory stops," he said. "Until Madam Pomfrey woke me on the pitch."

Amelia turned towards Lucius. "Then we'll take your account of the next part."

Lucius shifted carefully in his chair. Narcissa watched the movement but did not reach to help him.

"The Dark Lord ordered me to kill Cedric," he said. "Nott told me to move the Cup first. He had seen Potter trying to reach it."

"And what did you do?" Amelia asked.

"I attacked Nott. Sent the Cup towards Harry. Raised a shield and returned his wand."

Bill glanced up from his notes. "Where did the wand land?"

"In his hand. I saw him catch it."

Lucius described anchoring the shield to a headstone, buying himself a moment to use his wand. He had meant to put both boys within reach of the Cup. Cedric was too far away, and Harry's first attempt to summon him had failed.

"Harry had a handle directly in front of him," Lucius said. "He put out his hand. Then he looked at Cedric and drew it back."

Hermione felt Draconia's fingers tighten around hers. She remembered the bloody knot, the grass buried in Harry's skin.

"I had to leave when the next Killing Curse came," Lucius continued. "The shield remained for a few seconds. I hoped those would be enough. I did not see their final departure."

"Neither did I," Cedric said. "When I woke, the rope was around my chest and Harry's wrist. He was beside me. He told us Mr Malfoy had saved us. That's how I knew to say it."

Bill came to the desk and laid down his notes.

"We have physical evidence for the rest. The grass carries the same binding spell Cedric taught Harry. It's been stretched by a heavy pull. The residue on the Cup shows both boys travelled in contact with it. That belongs in the record as an examination, separate from what either witness remembers."

Amelia nodded. The quill turned to a fresh page.

Cedric looked at Fudge. "Harry brought me back. I woke beside him. I won't pretend I remember how he did every part of it, but that doesn't change where I was when I opened my eyes."

Hermione understood the rope now. She did not know each spell, each movement or how many attempts it had taken. She did not need to invent them. Harry had seen a way home and refused to take it until Cedric could come too. The marks on his wrist told her what that refusal had cost. Fudge stood beside the coldest window. His face had gone pale and damp. For a few seconds Hermione thought he might finally listen. There was too much here to dismiss: Cedric's account, Lucius's, the Cup, the Mark, the blood curse still being treated thirty feet away. Then Fudge straightened his cuffs.

"Two fugitives carried out an elaborate ritual," he said. "One of them spent months at Hogwarts with access to Moody's collection of Dark objects. The other is an experienced practitioner. They could have created any number of convincing illusions."

"I saw him," Cedric said.

"You were subjected to unknown magic and a Stunning Spell."

"After I saw him."

"And Mr Malfoy has every reason to seek protection by supplying a useful account."

Narcissa stood. Lucius touched her wrist, and she remained beside him, her face white with anger.

"I gave you the names of men who will kill me for speaking them," Lucius said. "Do you think that is convenient?"

"I think your position is complicated."

Hermione watched Fudge look around the room. He did not seem to be searching for a missing fact. He was looking at the people who would expect him to act if he accepted the ones he had been given.

"The Ministry will acknowledge an attack by Crouch Junior and Nott," he said. "We will investigate the ritual. Until our examiners establish the nature of it, there will be no public announcement of You-Know-Who's return."

"Your examiners will have access to the sealed record," Amelia said. "They will not replace it."

"You will receive instructions in the morning."

"Then you will receive my objections in writing."

Cedric gripped the arms of his chair. Amos steadied him as he moved, and Madam Pomfrey took a step forward.

"Don't stand," she warned.

Cedric stayed seated. He did not take his eyes off Fudge.

"If you're going to print that, print what I said beside it. Put my name on it."

"Mr Diggory—"

"People can read both. Let them hear from someone who was there."

Amos laid a hand on his shoulder. "You may put my name beside his."

"And mine," Mrs Diggory said.

Fleur stood, her hands resting on the back of her chair. "I will give Madame Maxime my account of what I saw tonight. Harry and Cedric disappeared through your Cup. They returned injured. I heard what they said. I won't tell Beauxbatons they were confused because it would make things easier for you."

"I will speak too," Krum said.

Lucius remained seated, but his voice was clear. "You have my testimony about the summons and the men who answered it. Use my name."

Fudge looked from one face to another. At last he turned towards the door. Sirius moved aside just enough to let him pass. The door shut hard behind him, making Cedric flinch before he could hide it. For a moment no one spoke. Then Madam Pomfrey came to Cedric, checked his pulse and told him the classroom had heard quite enough for one night. Hermione watched Amelia lay a hand over the recording quill to still it. The pages were already thick with writing.

Dumbledore came to Cedric's chair and asked whether he still wished to provide the memory. Cedric nodded. His mother held his hand while a fine silver thread passed from his temple into the waiting vial. Amelia sealed the copies in front of Dumbledore and Fleur, recording all three of their names before the Diggorys helped Cedric back to bed. Whatever the Minister chose to say in the morning, there was a record now. He had not been able to make them leave without one.

Harry woke in the graveyard. Stone pressed against his back. Something held his wrists, and his mouth was full of the taste of earth. He could smell the open grave beside him. Beyond it, out of sight, Voldemort was waiting. Harry reached for the shroud. Nothing answered. Cedric was exposed. They would see him, they would finish what they had started, and Harry could not find enough magic to hide him again. He pulled at his hands. Cloth caught beneath his fingers where his wand should have been. Above him a red light glowed, round and dim. He tried to turn away from it.

"No."

Someone was coming closer. Harry pulled harder. His wrist came free of the fold that held it, and a hand reached for him. He struck before he could see the face. The movement barely carried his fist beyond the edge of the pillow. Glass broke nearby, followed by the sharp, familiar smell of Dreamless Sleep.

A pillow.

Harry stared at the white cloth beneath his hand. Then at the curtains, the cabinet, the rain-dark window beyond them. Pieces of a room began to fit together, though none of them felt trustworthy yet. A clear ward curved over the bed. Two silver tubes ran from potion bottles to his arm. Fawkes perched on a cabinet, looking at him with bright gold eyes. Sirius was beside him. He had caught Harry's wrist, but his fingers were loose around it.

"Harry. It's me."

The face changed. For a moment Sirius wore prison grey, and a silver ring gleamed on his hand. Harry saw bars where the curtains had been. His breath stopped. One part of him knew he was in the hospital wing; the rest was already waiting for Voldemort to explain why none of it was real. Sirius let go of his wrist. He took the brass key from the blanket and put it in Harry's palm.

"Hawthorn," he said. "The front door sticks when it rains. There's a loose tile over the kitchen. You left your blue mug by the sink."

Harry closed his fingers. The key was solid, its worn teeth pressing into his skin. He rubbed his thumb over the little mark beside the handle and tried to remember turning it in the lock.

"Remus snores," Sirius added. "He denies it, but I have witnesses."

The curtains came back. Harry looked at Sirius's bare hand.

"You don't wear rings."

Sirius glanced down. "No."

"There was one. Before."

"There isn't now. Just me."

Harry breathed. The first breath hurt so badly he nearly lost it. The next was a little easier. Rain ran down the window, and the bed stayed a bed when he blinked. Sirius remained beside him, waiting while Harry worked out what he could believe. Then the pain came properly. His arm burned beneath the bandages. Each breath pulled at his ribs, and his muscles ached in places he had not known muscles could ache. His jaw hurt. He was thirsty, but the thought of swallowing made him feel sick. Beneath everything else, something cold seemed to tighten each time his heart beat. He looked down. Clean linen covered him to the shoulders.

"Cedric?"

"Alive."

Harry searched past Sirius. The next bed was empty. A coil of grass lay on the bedside table, one end still looped around his wrist. The far end had been cut. No silver light moved through it.

"Where is he?"

"Back in bed at the other end of the ward. He gave his statement first. Pomfrey's trying to get him to sleep."

Harry tried to push himself up. Pain caught beneath his ribs, and the ward above him flashed. He fell back before Sirius needed to stop him.

"Easy. Let me get Poppy."

"Fudge heard him?"

"Yes. So did Amelia, Dumbledore and a roomful of people who won't let the Minister forget it."

"Lucius?"

Sirius's face softened. "Back. Hurt, but back. Narcissa and Draco are with him."

Harry shut his eyes. He had not realised how much of him was still waiting for that answer.

"Hermione?"

"Outside with her parents. She and Draco sat here until Pomfrey sent them out. She threatened to sedate me as well."

"Probably should have."

Sirius laughed, but the sound caught. Harry opened his eyes again. His godfather had changed his shirt. There was still blood beneath two of his fingernails, and a red trace in the crease of his palm. He had washed without getting all of it off. Harry remembered the pitch. Grass against his face, voices above him, Sirius catching him before he fell.

I lost.

You came home.

He looked towards the window.

"How long have I been asleep?"

"About seven hours. It's nearly four."

Seven hours did not fit anywhere in his head. The cauldron was still close enough to touch. So were the green light, his mother's voice and the weight of Cedric at the other end of the rope. Yet someone had cleaned him, bandaged him and put him in a bed while he knew nothing about it. Madam Pomfrey came through the curtain. She must have heard the broken vial. She checked the ward before coming closer, then held a cup where Harry could reach it.

"A little water first. Small sips."

Sirius helped him lift his head. Harry managed two mouthfuls before the effort exhausted him. Pomfrey did not fuss. She checked his pupils, touched two fingers to his wrist and asked whether the pain was worse anywhere than it had been when he woke.

"I don't know," he said. "Everywhere."

"All right. We can start with that."

She adjusted one of the potion lines. Harry watched her hand until she had finished. When she stepped back, she told Sirius to call her at once if the marks moved or Harry's breathing changed. The broken glass vanished with a sweep of her wand, and she left them a fresh dose of Dreamless Sleep. Harry waited until she had gone.

"What did he do to me?"

Sirius looked at his bandaged arm. "A blood curse. Snape says the torture helped it spread. He and Poppy have it contained. The marks are fading."

"Contained isn't gone."

"No. It isn't."

Harry could see the end of a dark line above the bandage. It moved so slightly he might have imagined it, but he could feel the cold ache that went with it.

"Can Voldemort find me through it?"

"Bill says the instructions that tied this curse to him broke during the return journey. What remains is still hurting you, but it isn't carrying messages back to him. And these wards keep it from spreading."

Harry looked up. "What about my scar?"

Sirius hesitated. "That's older magic. They aren't treating it as the same thing."

"So he could still—"

"We don't know enough yet. I won't tell you we do. Bill and Snape are working on it."

Harry hated the answer and was grateful for it. After the graveyard, he did not think he could bear another promise made because someone hoped it would be true.

"What happens when I leave Hogwarts?"

"We work that out before you go."

"That isn't much of a plan."

"It's four in the morning, and you only just woke up. Let us manage the next hour first."

Harry moved his hand towards his scar. The silver tube tugged at his arm, and panic rose so quickly it left no room for thought. He looked down, saw the potion bottle and forced himself to loosen his fingers. Sirius had reached to help him, then stopped with his hand halfway across the blanket.

"You can touch me," Harry said.

"I didn't want to frighten you again."

"I thought I was still there."

"I know."

Sirius sat on the edge of the bed. He put his hand palm-up beside Harry's, close enough to reach without pulling on the tube. Harry looked at it. No one in the graveyard had waited for him to decide anything. Even the movement of his own body had stopped belonging to him. He put his fingers into Sirius's hand. Sirius closed his carefully. There were calluses near his thumb from working on the motorbike. His skin was warm. Harry held on.

"He got inside my head."

"Cedric told us what he could see."

"He couldn't see most of it."

"No."

Harry watched their hands rather than Sirius's face. "He made everyone dead. Then he made them talk to me."

Sirius's thumb stopped moving.

"You said I'd brought him home. To the cottage."

"Harry—"

"I know it wasn't you. It had your face. You gave the key back and said you'd given me somewhere safe, and I'd brought Voldemort there."

He wished he had not said it. The words made the false Sirius seem closer, as though he had found a way to follow Harry into the ward. Harry tightened his grip on the real hand beside him.

Sirius drew a breath. "I gave you that key because it's your home. A bad night doesn't change that. Neither does him."

"It wasn't just you."

"You don't have to tell me all of it now."

Harry nodded, but his mouth had begun to shake. He pressed his lips together. For a moment he almost managed to make it stop.

"He came back because of me."

"He came back because three murderers trapped you and stole your blood."

"They used it. It worked."

"Yes. And you tried to stop them."

"Not hard enough."

Sirius leaned closer. "You were tied to a grave. You were fourteen, and they had planned this for months. What were you supposed to do that you didn't try?"

Harry had no answer. He hated that too.

"I couldn't beat him."

"You weren't meant to be there fighting him."

"But I was."

Sirius looked down at their joined hands. When he spoke again, his voice was rougher.

"Yes. You were. I can't change that, however much I want to. But you kept Cedric alive, and you got both of you home. I'm not going to let him take that from you as well."

"Lucius helped."

"I know. I thanked him. You can imagine how much he enjoyed it."

Harry almost smiled. It did not last. The question had been there since he woke. It had been there on the pitch too, beneath every word he had managed to say. Harry tried to swallow it, but Sirius was still holding his hand, and somehow that made it harder to leave unasked.

"Are you disappointed?"

Sirius went completely still. Harry saw the hurt in his face and immediately wished he could take the question back.

"I didn't mean—"

"You can ask me." Sirius took Harry's hand between both of his. "I'd rather you asked than lay here thinking you already knew."

Harry waited. His eyes were burning now.

"No," Sirius said. "I'm not disappointed in you. I'm angry that they took you, and I'm frightened by how close we came to losing you. But I don't wish you'd fought better, Harry. I wish you hadn't had to fight at all."

Something wet reached Harry's temple. He turned his head towards the window, but Sirius did not let go.

"Your mum and dad wouldn't ask that of you either. Nor Remus. Nor the girls. We wanted you back. Frightened, hurt, whatever happened—we wanted you back."

Harry could not speak. He watched a thin grey edge appear in the sky beyond the glass. Dawn was still a long way off, but the darkness had begun to change. Sirius stayed beside him while he cried, quietly enough that Harry could pretend he was only having trouble breathing. After a while Sirius brushed the hair away from the bandage at his temple.

"He knew I was scared," Harry whispered.

"Of course you were."

"I didn't want him to."

"I know."

Sirius did not turn it into a lesson. Harry was grateful for that. He was too tired to find courage admirable, or to believe that being afraid could mean anything except what it had felt like among the graves.

"Try to sleep," Sirius said. "Poppy left another dose."

Harry looked at the vial. "Sorry about the first one."

"She has more."

Sirius helped him drink it. The taste clung bitterly to his tongue, and he washed it down with another mouthful of water. His grip loosened as the potion began to work. He fought it for a moment, uneasy at the thought of losing the room again.

"You'll stay?"

"Yes."

"What if they need you?"

"They can come and find me here."

Harry looked at him until the lines of his face began to blur. Sirius settled back into the chair, keeping their hands together where the potion tube would not pull.

"I'm here," he said once more.

This time Harry let himself believe it. He closed his eyes, and the last thing he felt was Sirius's thumb moving gently over his knuckles.

Harry woke to an argument.

"I said two visitors at a time," Madam Pomfrey was saying.

"There are two of us," Hermione said.

"And Mr Black."

"He's asleep."

"That does not make him furniture."

Harry opened one eye. Hermione stood at the foot of his bed, clutching three books. Draconia was beside her with a basket covered by a blue cloth. Madam Pomfrey blocked the gap in the curtain, her arms folded. Beyond her, Sirius slept in his chair with his head tilted at an angle that would certainly hurt when he woke.

Neither girl looked rested. Hermione's hair had escaped its tie, and ink marked the fingers wrapped around her books. Draconia still wore yesterday's robes. One cuff was stained with blood, and she had pinned her hair up crookedly. Harry looked at them with a sudden, aching affection. They belonged here, arguing their way towards his bed as they had after every other disaster. For a moment he almost believed the graveyard had been a nightmare. Then he shifted his arm, and the pain reminded him. Madam Pomfrey noticed his eyes were open.

"Ten quiet minutes," she told the girls. "And no upsetting him."

"Morning," Harry managed.

Hermione's books slipped from her arms. Draconia reached the bed first, then stopped so suddenly Hermione nearly ran into her. Both of them were looking at him, their hands hovering above the blanket. Harry saw the Great Hall. Their bodies lay beside one another on the floor, and their fingers were inches apart. He knew the room was false. He had broken it himself. Still, the image came so clearly that for a second he could not see the girls in front of him.

Hermione bent closer. "Harry? What's wrong?"

He swallowed. "Take her hand. Please."

Draconia looked at Hermione. Neither asked him to explain. Their hands met over the blanket, and Hermione laced her fingers through Draconia's. Harry watched them hold on. The floor of the false Hall faded, leaving the hospital bed, the blue basket cloth and Hermione's books scattered beneath it. He took a breath.

"Thank you."

Hermione sat on his left. Draconia took the chair on his right, moving it carefully around the potion stand. They kept holding hands until Harry could stop looking at them. Madam Pomfrey checked the ward above his head, then picked up Hermione's books and set them on the table with a pointed glance at the oldest one.

"Conversation," she said. "No research."

Hermione nodded. Pomfrey withdrew, leaving the curtain partly open so she could hear them.

Harry glanced at the basket. "Breakfast?"

Draconia looked down as though she had forgotten it. "Possibly."

"Possibly?"

"I packed it before anyone told me what you could eat."

Hermione lifted a corner of the cloth. "Six pears. Bread. And mustard."

"He likes mustard."

"Not usually with pears."

Harry managed a small smile. "Thank you for not putting it on them first."

Draconia laughed. It was too loud in the quiet ward, and broke before it had finished. She looked away, blinking hard. Harry lifted his hand towards her but could not reach without pulling on his arm. Hermione noticed and helped him the last few inches. His fingertips brushed Draconia's cheek. She turned back. Her eyes were red.

"Your father came home," Harry said.

"Yes."

"Sirius told me."

"He took so long. I thought—"

She stopped. Harry could imagine what she had thought. He remembered Lucius vanishing beyond the shield and Voldemort turning towards him. He had not known whether anyone would get out after that.

"He helped us," Harry said. "I wouldn't have got the Cup without him."

Draconia nodded and caught his hand before it could fall back to the bed. Hermione took the other, lifting the potion line clear so it would not tug. They sat like that for a while. Rain silvered the windows. Fawkes tucked his head beneath a wing, and Sirius slept on, one hand resting against the edge of the mattress. Harry had expected questions. He had answers for some of them and excuses ready for the others. He did not know what to do with this quiet, in which neither girl seemed to need anything from him. He let his eyes close for a moment. Their hands stayed where they were.

"Cedric told us what he saw," Hermione said at last.

Harry's fingers tensed.

"All of it?"

"What he remembers. He was careful about the parts he couldn't know."

"He shouldn't have made you listen."

Draconia looked at him. "We asked to stay."

"You didn't need to hear that."

"I'd rather hear it from him than read a lie about it tomorrow."

Harry looked up at the clear ward. He understood, but understanding did not make it easier. He had wanted the girls to see him home, perhaps a little hurt, perhaps tired enough to escape their questions until he could bear them. Now they knew about the grave and the knife and all the things he had done badly while they waited for him.

"We aren't angry with you," Hermione said.

"You should be."

"Why?"

He watched a drop move through the silver tube. "He knows."

Both girls grew still.

"What does he know?" Hermione asked.

Harry wished he had not started. Before the maze there had been moments he still did not know how to describe: Draconia's hand against his face, Hermione standing so close he had forgotten what he meant to say, both of them looking at him as though something important was about to happen. Voldemort had found it before Harry had managed to name it.

"About you," he said. "How much you matter."

Draconia's grip softened. "He already knew we were your friends."

"Not just that."

Hermione looked down at their hands. Colour had begun to rise in her face. Draconia kept looking at him, but her usual composure had become something uncertain and hopeful, and Harry nearly lost the words altogether.

"He saw how I feel about both of you," he said finally. "He said that gave him two ways to hurt me."

Neither girl spoke. The silence frightened Harry more than an answer would have.

"He can use you now," he added. "Because of me."

Draconia drew her chair closer. "He could try to hurt us because we're your friends. That was already true."

"It's worse if he knows—"

"We know," Hermione said softly.

Harry looked at her. Her face was pink, but she did not look away.

"We know it frightens you. It frightens us too. But you don't have to decide what happens to our friendship while you're lying here hurt."

"Maybe if I kept away—"

Both hands tightened around his.

"No," Draconia said.

Harry blinked at her. She took a breath, trying to steady her voice.

"You don't get to leave us because he's frightened you into thinking we would be better off. Ask us first."

"I wasn't going to—"

Hermione raised her eyebrows.

Harry looked down. "I hadn't finished thinking it."

"Then don't finish alone," she said.

He sat with that for a moment. Most of the people who had left him had done it without asking. Sometimes there had been a reason: death, prison, an elf hiding letters. The reasons had come later, after he had already learned what the empty places felt like. He had nearly offered the girls another one.

"He showed me you dead," Harry said.

Draconia's face lost its colour. Hermione's hand grew still around his.

"Both of you. Then you spoke. You said it was my fault."

Hermione moved closer. "That wasn't us."

"I know."

"Harry—"

"I worked it out." He looked at their joined hands. "You were beside each other. But you didn't reach. Neither of you."

Draconia followed his gaze. Her fingers closed more firmly around Hermione's.

"He got that wrong," she said.

Harry nodded. "I told him."

"What did he do?"

"Hit me."

The answer came out before he could soften it. Hermione looked at the bandage along his cheek. Draconia's eyes filled again, and he wished he had found some other way to say it.

"I hate him," she whispered.

Harry had heard Draconia angry before. This was different. There was no sharpness to entertain an audience, only a quiet certainty that made him squeeze her fingers as well as he could.

"I'm not very fond of him either."

She tried to smile and failed. Hermione bowed her head over Harry's wrist, careful of the bandage.

"You don't have to make us laugh," she said.

"Sometimes it helps."

"All right. Just don't do it because you think we'll go away if you're sad."

Harry closed his eyes. The words landed somewhere tender enough that he could not answer them. Draconia lifted his hand and kissed his uninjured knuckles. A moment later Hermione turned her face and did the same. He looked at them. Hermione went pink from her neck to her hairline. Draconia suddenly appeared deeply interested in the basket on her lap. Neither let go of his hand. Harry's heart was beating too quickly, but for once it had nothing to do with the graveyard.

"Before the maze," he said slowly, "you were going to tell me something."

The girls exchanged a glance. It was quick, but he caught it.

"You planned it?"

"Harry," Hermione said, sounding both fond and alarmed, "you've had three potions and barely any sleep."

"I can still listen."

"For another minute, perhaps," Draconia said.

He looked from one to the other. "Was it important?"

Hermione brushed his hair away from his forehead. "Yes."

"Then I want to hear it."

Draconia met his eyes again. "You will. When you can stay awake for the answer."

He wanted to argue that he could, but sleep was already drawing the room out of focus. Hermione adjusted the potion tube. Draconia set the basket on the floor, abandoning the pears and mustard until he could appreciate them properly.

"You're coming back later?" he murmured.

"We're here now," Hermione said.

That was enough for the moment. He could feel them on either side of him as his eyes closed. The graveyard waited somewhere beyond sleep, but this time it had to contend with the warmth of two hands and the memory of their fingers reaching for each other.

Cedric came that afternoon on crutches. He wore hospital pyjamas beneath a Hufflepuff dressing gown, and his braced leg floated stiffly a few inches ahead of him. Amos followed with two chairs, watching every step as though the floor might try to take his son away. Madam Pomfrey let them through the curtain, then checked Harry's ward before deciding they could stay.

"Ten minutes," she said. "And neither of you is to get up."

"I wasn't planning to," Harry told her.

"You weren't planning to last night either."

Sirius opened one eye from the next bed, where he had finally been persuaded to lie down. "I'll make sure he behaves."

Madam Pomfrey looked at him for a moment. "I shall check for myself."

She left the curtain open. Cedric lowered himself into a chair, his mouth tightening as Amos helped settle his leg. There was a bruise beneath his jaw and a shallow cut near his hairline. Harry remembered Crouch tackling him and a broken mask spinning across the grass. He had been too far away to help. Cedric looked at Harry's bandages. Harry watched him try to keep looking at his face instead.

"You don't have to pretend they aren't there," he said.

"Sorry."

"That's worse."

Cedric gave a small, reluctant smile. He took something from his pocket and laid it on the blanket: a loop of grass, mostly dried brown, with a faint silver strand caught inside the knot.

"Pomfrey cut my end once it stopped answering you," he said. "She told me leaving it on would do more harm than good. I thought you might want it."

Harry looked at the knot. At once he could feel Cedric's weight dragging against the other end. The knife wound opening beneath the grass. The Cup waiting beside his hand. Voldemort turning towards them. The ward above his bed brightened. Cedric noticed and put his hand over the rope.

"Or I can keep it."

Harry swallowed. "Would you?"

"Of course."

He put it away. The patch of blanket it had occupied was empty again, and Harry concentrated on the small folds in the linen until the graveyard loosened its hold. They sat quietly for a moment. Before this, Harry would have found something to say about the Tournament or the weather or Cedric's team. He had known him for years without quite knowing how to be alone with him. The World Cup had begun to change that: Eleanor beneath the trees, the letter Harry sent afterwards, Cedric finding him beside the pitch. They had trained together before the Goblet chose them. They had shared clues and waited for each other at the lake. Reaching the Cup together had made sense because of all those earlier choices.

Now Harry knew the feel of Cedric's heartbeat better than the sound of his laugh. Cedric had watched him scream and bleed and try to hide both. Neither of them had asked for that closeness, and Harry did not know what to do with it.

Cedric looked at the window. "I remember everything before the Stunner."

Harry's mouth went dry. "You were behind the grave."

"I could see through the roots."

"Not all of it."

"No. Enough."

Beyond the glass, students walked beside the lake in small groups with teachers at their backs. Ministry tents stood near the pitch. Harry could see the ruined edges of the maze between them. It looked smaller from here, and that seemed wrong.

"You told everyone," he said.

"I told the people taking the statement. And your friends."

"Hermione said."

Cedric waited, then asked, "Are you angry?"

Harry thought about saying no. "A bit."

"Because I told them what happened?"

He nodded.

"I didn't know how else to make them listen," Cedric said. "Fudge was already talking as though we'd got confused. Harry, I couldn't let him do that while you were asleep."

Harry looked at his bandaged wrist. He knew Cedric was right. It did not stop the account from feeling like something private had been carried out of the graveyard and laid on a desk for strangers to examine.

"There were things I didn't want them to hear."

"I know." Cedric's voice gentled. "I didn't tell them what I couldn't see. I didn't guess at what was in your head."

Harry glanced up. Cedric was watching him carefully.

"Thank you," Harry said at last.

Cedric nodded. For a moment they both seemed to find the conversation easier. Then Cedric drew a breath and spoiled it.

"You could have let the shroud go."

"They'd have killed you."

"You nearly died keeping it."

Harry started to say that nearly was not the same as actually. Cedric saw the answer forming and shook his head.

"Don't. I've heard you do that all afternoon. It was close enough that my mother won't leave the room unless Dad stays with me. Yours mattered too."

Harry glanced at Amos, who had taken a seat near the foot of the bed. His face was tired, but he met Harry's eyes.

"It did," Amos said.

Harry looked away first.

"He came back anyway," he said. "After all that. Voldemort has a body."

"And I'm here."

"That doesn't cancel it out."

"I'm not asking you to cancel it out. I'm asking you not to talk as though keeping me alive was nothing."

Cedric's fingers tightened around the handle of a crutch. Harry had been speaking as though Amos was not there, as though Cedric's life could be weighed without the man who had spent the night afraid of losing him.

"It wasn't nothing," Harry said quietly.

"Good."

Cedric sat back. The effort had tired him; Harry could see it in the way he held his shoulders. He waited until Cedric had taken a breath before speaking again.

"I don't want you to think you owe me."

"I know. Your godfather checked that with me already."

Sirius opened his eye again. "Politely."

"Very firmly," Cedric said.

Harry managed a smile. Cedric did too, but his faded as he looked towards the curtain.

"I'm not going to follow you around looking grateful for the rest of my life," he said. "That's not why you brought me back. I can help. Starting with telling people what happened, whether Fudge likes it or not."

"He won't."

"I know."

Cedric touched the pocket holding the rope.

"I heard them call me dead. Then I watched you let them believe it. I understood why, but it was horrible. I kept wondering whether the next curse would make it true."

Harry's smile vanished. "I'm sorry."

"You saved me. I'm not asking you to apologise." Cedric rubbed a hand over his face. "I just don't want to act as though I came home and it all stopped. If you wake up thinking you're there, I'll understand. And if I do, perhaps you will."

Harry looked at him for a long moment. "I will."

"Then we can start there."

It was easier than a vow. Harry had heard enough grand promises before the task, and Cedric did not seem to want another one either. They could write. They could talk when they were able. Neither needed to explain why a patch of grass or a sudden flash of green might make the room disappear for a moment.

"What did Fudge decide?" Harry asked.

Cedric's jaw tightened. "An attack by Crouch and Nott. He thinks—or wants everyone else to think—they made a false resurrection to frighten people and weaken the Ministry."

"Does he believe that?"

"I don't know. I think he's more frightened of what he'd have to do if he believed us."

Harry looked at Amos. "Will the papers print it?"

"They'll print his statement," Amos said. "I've sent Cedric's account as well. We will see what the editor is willing to put beside it."

"And if they leave it out?"

Cedric sat a little straighter. Harry remembered him on a sodden Quidditch pitch, convincing his team they could play through weather that made everyone else want to go inside. The same stubbornness was there now, quieter and harder to miss.

"Then I'll tell someone else," he said. "He doesn't get to be the last person who speaks."

Madam Pomfrey appeared at the curtain before Harry could answer. She checked Cedric's leg, glanced at the clock and announced that both champions had used up their strength for the afternoon. Amos rose to help his son.

Cedric paused with one crutch beneath his arm. "Tomorrow?"

Harry nodded. "Tomorrow."

It was a small promise. For the first time since the graveyard, it felt like one they might actually be able to keep.

Lucius came after sunset. He had accepted clean robes, though apparently the hospital gown had been beyond negotiation. His left sleeve was pinned above the bandage, and he used his cane for every other step. Narcissa walked beside him. Draconia followed, watching him with the same expression Amos had worn earlier. Harry had been looking out at the grounds, where lamps burned around the Ministry tents. He turned as they reached his bed. Lucius paused at the foot of it, and for a moment the familiar scrutiny made Harry want to straighten his collar, though he was wearing hospital pyjamas and had no collar worth straightening.

"You look better," Lucius said.

"Sirius said I looked awful this morning."

"Both can be true."

Harry glanced at the cane. "You're limping."

"Poppy has been kind enough to point that out."

Draconia pulled a chair closer before her father could turn standing into a point of pride. He sat with a small, careful breath. His hand went towards his left cuff, found the pinned sleeve and fell back to the cane.

Harry watched the movement. "Thank you."

Lucius looked up.

"For the Cup," Harry said. "And my wand. For coming."

"You made good use of them."

"I wouldn't have had them without you."

Lucius glanced towards Narcissa. She did not rescue him from the conversation.

"No," he said at last. "You would not."

Harry nodded. He had expected his thanks to become an argument, and was relieved when Lucius let them remain what they were.

"You went because Draco asked?"

"The Mark gave me a route to you. I was the only person there who could answer it without being recognised as an enemy on arrival."

"That wasn't what I asked."

Draconia looked at her father. He met her eyes, then Harry's.

"My daughter asked me to bring home someone she loves," he said. "Of course I went."

Harry forgot what he had meant to say. Draconia looked down at the blanket, her cheeks colouring. Narcissa's hand settled briefly on her shoulder. Lucius let the silence pass without remark.

"You could have died," Harry said eventually.

"Yes."

"Voldemort knows now. About you."

"There was little room for ambiguity after I attacked Nott."

The answer was dry, but Harry could see his hand tighten on the cane.

"What happens next?"

Narcissa moved closer to the bed. "We won't be returning to the Manor."

Harry looked at Draconia. Her face had become carefully still. He thought of the rooms she had grown up in, the portraits and the long corridors, the way Narcissa seemed to know which window would catch the light at any hour.

"Can he get through the wards?"

"Not easily," Lucius said. "But the estate has been altered by generations of my family. Some admired the sort of magic he uses. I won't assume that every door is one I know how to close."

"Where will you go?"

"Dumbledore has offered us a protected house," Narcissa said.

Sirius sat up in the neighbouring bed. "And we've offered Hawthorn."

Lucius looked towards him. "Temporarily."

"Yes, Lucius. You can keep telling yourself that."

Narcissa gave Sirius a warning glance, but there was a trace of amusement in it. Draconia looked between them, working something out.

"There are four bedrooms, aren't there? What about Professor Lupin?"

"Remus offered you his room," Sirius said. "We're clearing the study for him. Your parents can take the guest room. Harry keeps his."

"When I come?" Harry asked.

Sirius's expression changed. He had heard what mattered beneath the question.

"When you come," he said. "We'll talk about the rest of summer once Pomfrey lets you leave this bed."

Harry nodded, trying not to show how much he wanted that to be soon.

"The elves are moving what we need," Lucius said. "Most of it has gone into Gringotts storage. Dobby insisted on checking the rooms again before he left."

Harry turned back at once. "He's safe?"

"Quite. He is at Hawthorn, correcting Black's kitchen."

"Apparently every cupboard was wrong," Sirius said.

Harry smiled. "Then he's all right."

For a moment it was easy to picture them there: Dobby reorganising the pantry, Remus rescuing books from the study, Draconia discovering which stairs creaked. Then Harry looked at Lucius's bandaged arm, and remembered why they had to go.

"I'm sorry you can't go home."

Lucius studied him. "Harry, I chose to help you. I am not asking you to pay for that choice."

"But the Manor—"

"Is a house my family kept filling with things it should have left behind. I knew that long before tonight. If anyone is responsible for how difficult it has become to leave, it is me."

Draconia moved beside his chair. Narcissa's fingers rested on its back. Harry looked at the three of them and tried to let the answer stay with the person who had given it. Lucius's gaze moved to the dark line visible above Harry's pyjamas.

"He marked you."

"Not like yours."

"No." Lucius looked down at his bandaged arm. "I agreed to mine. You did not."

He seemed to need Harry to understand that. Harry nodded. When Lucius stood, Narcissa took his uninjured elbow without making a fuss about it. He paused beside Harry's pillow and laid a hand briefly against the back of his neck, the same touch he had given him before the maze. Harry remembered what he had said then.

Come back.

He looked up, and Lucius met his eyes. This time they had both managed it.

Snape came at midnight dressed for travel. His robes were fastened to the throat, his hands covered by dragon-hide gloves, and the plain cloak over his shoulders bore no school crest. Harry had been drifting in and out of sleep. He woke properly when Snape placed two potions on the bedside table. One went into the reservoir above the bed. As it mixed with the medicine already there, the dark lines beneath Harry's skin drew back. Snape watched them before adjusting the flow.

"Where are you going?" Harry asked.

"Out."

Harry looked at the long cuff covering his left arm. Voldemort had noticed the empty places in the circle. Karkaroff had not come. Snape had not come. Lucius could never go back.

"You're going to him."

Snape did not answer. That was answer enough.

"He knows you didn't come when he called," Harry said.

"Yes."

"He'll kill you."

"He may try."

Harry pushed against the pillow. His arm shook with the effort, and Snape put a hand on the bedrail rather than on him.

"Lie down, Potter. You will not improve the situation by reopening your wounds."

"Dumbledore asked you?"

"We discussed it. I agreed."

Harry sank back. He remembered Snape standing between them and a werewolf, remembered him coming into the Chamber after the basilisk was dead. He had spent so long finding the professor difficult that moments like those still surprised him, even when he knew better.

"Why now?"

Snape checked the reservoir again. "Lucius is exposed. The Dark Lord has servants in places the Ministry still refuses to examine. Someone has to find out what he intends to do next."

His eyes moved briefly to Harry's arm. "Before he does it."

Harry gripped the blanket. "He'll use Legilimency."

"I expect so."

"I can tell you what it felt like."

Snape looked at him then. Harry had expected impatience. Instead the professor pulled the chair a little closer and waited.

"The feeling came first," Harry said. "Before the picture. He made me frightened, then showed me something that felt like it belonged to the fear. Sometimes I knew it couldn't be true, but it didn't seem to matter."

His voice had begun to shake. He looked at the potion bottle until it steadied.

"If I fought the picture, the feeling was still there. And when I made something false, he looked for the part that didn't fit."

"You made a false memory?"

"A pain. I knew what a few things felt like, so I put them together and made them worse."

Snape studied him. "Did he accept it?"

"For a moment. Then he knew I'd made it. But it hurt him. He pulled away."

The professor was silent long enough that Harry began to wonder whether he had said something foolish.

"It was a dangerous thing to attempt," Snape said finally. "But I understand why you attempted it."

Harry nodded. He had not been expecting that either. Snape removed a glove and drew back his cuff. The Mark burned black on his arm. Harry looked at it, thinking of Lucius's blistered skin and the pull he had felt from Voldemort's mind.

"He understands fear," Snape said. "Ambition. The wish to survive. Those are things I can give him without giving him everything."

"He didn't understand the girls," Harry said quietly.

Snape looked at him.

"He got them wrong. That's how I knew."

The professor covered the Mark and replaced his glove. "Then hold on to that. It may be more useful than any picture he showed you."

He indicated the second bottle on the table.

"The silver potion is for a sudden change in the marks. If they reach your throat, take it and call Poppy at once. Do not wait to see whether they recede."

"I won't."

Snape held his gaze until Harry stopped thinking of the occasions on which he had made precisely that promise and done something else.

"I've explained it to Black as well," Snape said. "One of you may remember."

He checked the ward a final time and stood. Harry watched him fasten his cloak. There were too many things he wanted to say: thanks for the medicine, a warning about the graveyard, something about how much he did not want another person to go there because of him. None of them came out.

"Professor."

Snape stopped at the curtain.

"Come back."

For a moment the professor's shoulders were perfectly still beneath the cloak. Then he inclined his head, just enough for Harry to see.

"Rest, Potter. Let someone else do the walking tonight."

He left. Harry listened until the footsteps faded beyond the hospital doors. After a while he loosened his grip on the blanket, but he did not fall asleep again for a long time.

The Ministry's written statement reached the ward before breakfast, while the sky beyond the windows was still dark. Percy Weasley brought it in a sealed packet, looking as though he had been awake for most of the night. His collar was buttoned unevenly, and ink marked the side of his hand. He gave the packet to Dumbledore without looking towards Harry's bed. Sirius read over Dumbledore's shoulder. Remus stood by the window. Hermione and Draconia had returned with breakfast trays, though neither had eaten much. Harry watched the adults' faces and knew he would dislike the page before Sirius handed it to him.

It was less than a sheet long. Crouch Junior and Mr Nott were named as the men responsible for an attack on the Tournament. Harry and Cedric had been taken outside Hogwarts and injured by ritual Dark magic. The ritual, according to the Ministry, had been designed to imitate the methods of He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named. There was no Pettigrew. No summons. No circle of masked men who had stood around Harry and watched him bleed. Lucius appeared once, as a former associate whose account remained under investigation. At the bottom, after a promise of a thorough inquiry, came the sentence Harry could not stop reading.

There was no credible evidence of Voldemort's return.

He read it again. The page was clean, the words carefully chosen. It used his name as though that made it an account he had given. Harry could remember the knife entering his arm, could still feel the cold movement beneath his skin, and someone had decided those things were less credible than a sentence that cost nothing to write.

"They can't just say that."

No one answered at first.

"Everyone saw us come back."

Hermione moved closer. "They admit you were attacked. They're disputing who did it."

"The part that matters."

The ward above his head brightened. A dark line stirred at the base of his throat. Draconia reached for the silver potion, but Harry took it before she could lift it from the table. The medicine tasted of iron and mint. He swallowed, then sat still while its cold spread through his chest. Hermione turned towards Madam Pomfrey's office and called for her. Pomfrey came at once, checked his breathing and watched until the marks began to fade. She left the curtain open when she went back, keeping him in view.

Harry looked at Dumbledore. "Cedric saw him."

"Cedric has sent out his own account," Dumbledore said. "With his parents' support."

"Will the Prophet print it?"

"I cannot promise that it will print it fairly."

Harry set the Ministry page down. He had thought coming home would mean he no longer had to convince anyone the graveyard was real. Now it seemed the argument had only changed rooms. An owl struck the closed window. It flapped backwards, outraged, then tried again. Remus reached the latch before a third collision. A breath of wet morning air entered with the bird, which carried five papers tied together with red cord. It dropped the bundle across Harry's blanket, took a piece of Hermione's toast and settled on the bedpost to eat it.

"You could have asked," Hermione muttered.

The owl ignored her. The first paper was French. Fleur stood in the photograph outside the hospital wing, Gabrielle close beside her and Madame Maxime behind them. Harry could not read all the headline, but Voldemort's name was unmistakable. Hermione leaned over his shoulder and translated the opening lines: an attack on the champions, disputed testimony, questions for the British Ministry. The second paper was Bulgarian. Krum's photograph appeared beside one of the altered Cup. Harry recognised the same name there too. They had not claimed to see what happened in the graveyard. They had said what they had witnessed at Hogwarts, and what they had heard afterwards, and they had allowed the witnesses to speak.

The Daily Prophet came third. The Ministry statement filled the front page. Cedric's response began on page six beneath a headline calling him a confused champion. Sirius took a sharp breath through his nose. Remus touched his shoulder, and Hermione gently removed the paper from his hand before he could crumple it. The fourth was the Quibbler.

TWO CHAMPIONS RETURN. TWO CHAMPIONS NAME VOLDEMORT. HOW MANY WITNESSES DOES THE MINISTRY REQUIRE?

Harry opened it. Cedric's statement ran in full, carrying his name and those of his parents. Lucius's account appeared alongside it. Amelia was named as the official who had recorded the testimony; Fleur and Krum had supplied accounts of events at Hogwarts. A note from Xenophilius Lovegood offered permission to reprint the record, provided no one altered it. Harry turned a page, then another. The account kept going where the Prophet had cut it short. No paragraph interrupted Cedric to explain that he might have been mistaken. Harry set the paper beside the Ministry statement and looked at the space between them. The fifth did not belong to any publisher he knew.

Hogwarts students had made it. The ink changed colour halfway down a column, the photographs lurched rather than moved smoothly, and one edge had been trimmed with scissors. Across the front, above three columns of names, stood a simple sentence.

WE HEARD THEM.

Harry read the lines beneath it. These students had seen the champions return. Some had heard Harry or Cedric name Voldemort. Others had seen Lucius leave when the Mark burned, or watched Harry collapse on the pitch. They wrote down what they had actually witnessed and signed beneath it. Hufflepuff names came first, followed by Gryffindor, Ravenclaw and Slytherin. Neville's signature was near the top, Luna's curling underneath. Pansy Parkinson had signed in sharp green ink. Susan Bones, Daphne Greengrass and both Creevey brothers were there too, along with three Durmstrang students whose names had been written out carefully by someone else. There were more on the last page. The columns had run out of room, so people had signed in the margins.

"Who made this?" Harry asked.

Hermione found the little line of print at the bottom. "Ginny, Luna and Colin."

Draconia studied one blurred photograph. "Colin usually does better than this."

"I don't think they had much time," Hermione said.

"No." Draconia smoothed the page where the owl had creased it. "I suppose they didn't."

Harry looked at the headline again.

We heard them.

Not just him. Cedric had come back too. Lucius had returned. People had been there when they spoke, before the Ministry had time to decide which words it could afford to acknowledge. Harry had expected the truth to become another thing he carried alone. Yet here were names filling the edges of a badly printed page, ordinary students insisting that what they had heard mattered. Fudge could make their words hard to find. He could call the witnesses confused and bury their accounts beneath his own. He could not undo the fact that they had spoken. Hermione reached across the papers and took Harry's hand. Draconia took the other. He left the student paper open on the blanket, reading the names until his breathing eased.

He had brought Cedric home. Now Cedric was speaking, and other people were listening. For the first time that morning, Harry did not feel alone with what had happened.
