6

Hermione's absence revealed itself through ordinary things.

Her place at the Gryffindor table remained empty the following morning, though a plate had appeared with all the others and a clean goblet waited beside it. Nobody removed them. Students merely arranged themselves around the vacant space, leaving enough room for a girl who would not be coming to breakfast.

Harry sat beside it.

The enchanted ceiling showed a colourless winter sky. Weak daylight entered through the high windows, gleaming upon the silver dishes and turning the floating candles pale. Snow remained along the outer ledges, but rain had begun falling during the night, dissolving it into grey slush that ran down the glass in uneven trails.

Conversation filled the Great Hall. It no longer possessed the careless volume of an ordinary morning. Voices remained low, chairs were drawn closer together and heads turned whenever the doors opened. Students glanced towards reflective surfaces before passing them, though Professor McGonagall had ordered most mirrors removed from the corridors.

Harry noticed none of them watching him until the bench opposite emptied.

Three Gryffindors gathered their breakfasts and moved farther down the table without offering an explanation. One left his goblet behind and returned for it only after Harry looked away.

A week earlier, Harry might have made a joke.

That morning, he could not think of anyone he wanted to reassure.

Draconia arrived several minutes later. Rather than order someone from their seat, she stopped beside Harry and remained standing.

Her appearance had deteriorated overnight. Shadows bruised the skin beneath her eyes, and her pale hair had been tied back without its usual precision. One sleeve concealed most of her left hand. The green satchel crossed her body, held close beneath her folded arm.

"You haven't eaten," she said.

Harry looked at the untouched food surrounding Hermione's empty plate. "Neither has she."

Draconia's face tightened.

He regretted the answer but lacked the energy to retrieve it.

She sat opposite him.

The Slytherin table watched. Harry's housemates watched too. Draconia ignored both and placed a piece of toast upon his plate.

"You should eat before we visit her."

"I'm not hungry."

"Neither am I."

"That weakens your argument."

"It makes me a hypocrite, not incorrect."

Harry pushed the toast towards her. Draconia divided it, returned one half and bit into the other without enthusiasm.

For several minutes, they ate in silence.

Hermione would have objected to the arrangement. She would have insisted toast did not constitute breakfast and begun transferring eggs onto both their plates while explaining the effect of malnutrition upon concentration. Harry could hear the lecture clearly enough to know when she would pause for breath.

The voice in his memory did not fill the place beside him.

Professor McGonagall rose from the staff table.

Conversation diminished without her asking for silence. The professors behind her appeared no better rested than their students. Snape sat rigidly beside an untouched cup of tea. Professor Flitwick's usual stack of cushions had been reduced to one, while Professor Sprout still had dirt beneath her fingernails, suggesting she had come directly from the greenhouses.

Professor Dumbledore occupied the central chair.

His face remained calm, but the brightness which ordinarily softened his eyes had disappeared.

"Following yesterday's attack," McGonagall said, "students will continue travelling between lessons under staff supervision. No student is to leave a classroom, common room or the Great Hall without an escort. Anyone discovered walking alone will be returned to their House and placed in detention."

A nervous murmur passed through the tables.

"The faculty has identified the creature responsible for the attacks," she continued.

Draconia's hand stopped around her toast.

Harry watched her.

"To prevent panic and discourage profoundly ill-advised attempts to locate it, that information will not be discussed beyond the staff. You are required only to understand that the danger is real and that these rules exist to protect you."

A Ravenclaw near the front of his table raised his hand.

"What about the students who were attacked?"

Professor Sprout answered.

"They remain stable. Mandrake Restorative Draught will revive them once the plants have matured."

"When will that be?"

"As soon as nature permits," Sprout said, in a tone suggesting she had already attempted to negotiate.

Dumbledore rose.

"My colleagues are searching every part of the castle," he said. "Until the danger has passed, I ask you to remember that fear is most useful to those who wish to control you. Be cautious, but do not surrender your judgement to rumour."

His gaze travelled over the four tables.

It paused upon Harry and Draconia.

"Help one another," Dumbledore said. "No House stands apart from what threatens this school."

The bell rang shortly afterwards.

Students rose in organised groups, each collected by a professor or prefect. Harry remained seated until the Gryffindor table had nearly emptied.

Draconia stood beside him. "Hospital wing."

"We have Transfiguration."

"McGonagall will survive ten minutes without us."

"Will we survive her?"

"Uncertain."

Harry collected his bag.

They left together.

Madam Pomfrey permitted them seven minutes.

She announced the limit while opening the hospital-wing doors and repeated it when Draconia attempted to negotiate. The ward behind her smelt of medicinal herbs, clean linen and the sharp solution used to wash potion equipment. Morning light lay across the polished floor, but screens had been drawn around the occupied beds, dividing the brightness into narrow white corridors.

"You will not touch the patients," Madam Pomfrey said. "You will not move anything, attempt experimental counter-curses or ask whether sufficiently determined shouting might wake them."

Draconia glanced at Harry.

"I wasn't going to shout."

Madam Pomfrey swept away, muttering about students and brass instruments.

Harry approached Hermione's bed.

Her spectacles had been removed and folded upon the cabinet. Without them, her face looked younger. Her eyes remained open, fixed upon the canopy, while her hands rested neatly over the blankets. A small ink stain marked one finger, missed by whatever cleaning charms Madam Pomfrey had used.

Draconia stopped on the opposite side.

Neither spoke at first.

Harry had planned what he might say during breakfast. He had considered telling Hermione that the professors accepted her basilisk theory, complaining that McGonagall had kept the page she found or informing her that she had caused Madam Pince to discover three missing books.

All of it sounded ridiculous now.

Draconia reached towards Hermione's hand before remembering Madam Pomfrey's rule. Her fingers stopped above the blanket and withdrew.

"Professor Sprout says she'll wake up," Harry said.

"I heard."

"She said the Mandrakes are nearly mature."

"I heard that too."

Harry pulled the chair closer. "You don't sound convinced."

"I believe Professor Sprout."

"That wasn't what I meant."

Draconia's gaze remained on Hermione.

"She was frightened," she said. "She went anyway."

"She took Penelope and a mirror."

"She believed that was enough."

"It should have been."

"No." Draconia's voice lowered. "It shouldn't."

Harry studied her.

The green satchel rested against her hip. Its silver clasp caught the morning light, while the leather appeared darker near one lower corner, almost damp.

"What should Hermione have done?"

"Stayed away."

"From the library?"

"From all of it."

"She was trying to stop people being attacked."

"And now she is one of them."

Harry rose.

The chair legs scraped against the floor.

"You warned us this would happen."

Draconia looked at him. "There had already been attacks. It was not difficult to predict another."

"You said one of us would be next."

"I was afraid one of you would be."

"Why?"

Her fingers closed around the satchel strap.

Harry had watched that movement too many times to dismiss it.

"Because of the notebook?"

Draconia's face became unreadable.

Harry waited.

Behind the screens, Madam Pomfrey poured a potion. Glass touched glass, followed by the quiet movement of her shoes across the ward.

Draconia turned away from Hermione. "We should go."

"We have four minutes."

"They will not improve this conversation."

"You won't even show it to me."

Hermione lay silent between them where she ordinarily would have forced the question forwards.

The fact seemed to reach Draconia too. Her composure slipped enough for Harry to glimpse the fear beneath it.

"I made a mistake," she said.

His anger vanished.

"What mistake?"

Draconia's lips parted.

Nothing followed.

She pressed one hand against her temple. A tremor moved through her shoulders, and her eyes briefly lost focus.

Harry went around the bed. "Draco?"

She recoiled when he touched her arm.

The reaction was immediate enough to hurt them both.

Draconia stared at his hand. "Don't."

Harry released her.

"I'm trying to help."

"I know."

"Then let me."

"I can't."

"Because you don't trust me?"

Her gaze snapped to his. "That has never been the problem."

Madam Pomfrey appeared around the screen.

"Time."

Draconia stepped away.

Harry looked down at Hermione once more.

"We'll come back," he promised.

Her open eyes did not move.

In the days that followed, Hogwarts attempted to defend itself.

Mirrors were positioned at difficult corners so professors could inspect corridors before leading students through them. Pipes were mapped, sealed and monitored with charms which caused entire sections of wall to glow whenever movement passed inside. Nearly Headless Nick and the other ghosts patrolled places living teachers could not reach, though Sir Patrick Delaney-Podmore objected to the suggestion that ghosts should place their heads through plumbing.

Hagrid's surviving roosters were brought into the castle.

Their cages appeared outside bathrooms, upon staircases and beside the entrances to the dungeons. At irregular intervals, one would crow loudly enough to interrupt lessons and startle every student nearby.

Harry learnt from Hagrid that most of the flock had already been killed.

"Found 'em strangled over the last few months," Hagrid said as he carried another cage towards the entrance hall. The rooster inside clucked nervously, feathers ruffled by the rain. "Thought it was foxes at first, but there weren't any tracks. Not even when the snow came."

Draconia stared at the bird.

Harry asked, "Did anyone know roosters could kill a basilisk?"

"Anyone who'd read the right book, I s'pose."

"That includes half of Hogwarts," Draconia said.

"Not everyone had a reason to kill them."

Her eyes moved towards Harry. "Do not begin."

"I asked a question."

"You implied an answer."

Hagrid glanced between them. "Maybe yeh two ought ter—"

"We're fine," Harry and Draconia said together.

Neither sounded convincing.

Lessons continued beneath the new restrictions. Professors taught with classroom doors locked and curtains pulled across windows. Students practised spells in uneasy silence, constantly aware of the mirrors positioned near every entrance.

Harry stopped hearing most of what was taught.

Without Hermione, each room felt incorrectly arranged. He caught himself turning whenever parchment unrolled beside him, expecting to find her already three lines ahead. He left gaps in his notes for explanations she would ordinarily insist upon adding later. Once, during Charms, he whispered a complaint about the length of Professor Flitwick's essay and waited for Hermione to remind him that twelve inches was not excessive.

Nobody answered.

Harry did not repeat the joke.

His appetite diminished gradually enough for him to pretend not to notice. He ate when Draconia sat opposite him and forgot when she did not. Sleep became less reliable. Some nights he lay awake listening to water move through the pipes; others he fell asleep fully dressed and woke without feeling rested.

The familiar dullness from Privet Drive entered through those small failures.

It did not resemble sadness. Sadness required enough energy to want things to be different. This was quieter. It encouraged him to expect less, speak less and cross each day without asking whether any part of it mattered.

Draconia fought it whenever she was near.

She appeared outside Gryffindor lessons carrying food she claimed Narcissa would force her to inventory. She dragged Harry to the hospital wing each afternoon and insulted him until he ate supper. When students moved away from him in corridors, she walked closer.

Her presence kept him tethered.

It also became increasingly unreliable.

Draconia missed two breakfasts and arrived halfway through a third without remembering that she had promised to meet him. She appeared in the hospital wing one afternoon carrying Hermione's Arithmancy book, though neither she nor Harry studied the subject. Ink marked the side of her hand. When Harry pointed it out, she wiped it against her robes and claimed she had been writing an essay.

"What essay?" he asked.

"Potions."

"We handed that in yesterday."

Draconia looked at him.

The confusion lasted only an instant. "Then Transfiguration."

"We don't have one."

"I am capable of completing work before it is assigned."

"You're also capable of lying better than this."

She placed the book beside Hermione's spectacles. "Perhaps I'm tired."

"You are tired."

"Then we have solved it."

The next morning, Draconia did not remember the conversation.

Harry began recording every time she vanished.

He kept the list folded behind Hermione's basilisk notes and hated himself whenever he added another line.

Professor McGonagall asked Harry to retrace the night of the first attack.

She met him after Charms with Snape and Draconia waiting beside the classroom door. Snape carried a rolled map of the castle's plumbing, while Draconia wore the same exhausted composure she now carried everywhere.

"Show us where you first heard it," McGonagall said.

Harry glanced towards the students being escorted downstairs by Professor Flitwick. "You believe me now?"

"I believed that you heard something when you first reported it."

"That isn't the same."

"No," McGonagall said. "It is not."

They began at the side door of the Great Hall.

Harry led them along the route he and Hermione had followed on Halloween, pausing whenever memory required him to choose between corridors. The castle looked different in daylight. Places which had seemed threatening beneath guttering torches now appeared merely old, filled with winter sun and the fading traces of hundreds of students.

The second-floor passage remained closed.

A rope barred its entrance, and a wooden sign warned students away. The writing had been removed from the wall, though a faint discolouration remained above the torch bracket where Mrs Norris had hung.

Harry stopped.

"The water came from that direction."

Snape unrolled the map. "There is a bathroom beyond the adjoining passage."

"A disused one," Draconia said. "The Slytherin girls avoid it."

McGonagall looked at her. "Why?"

"It floods. It smells unpleasant, and the ghost cries whenever anyone attempts to use a sink."

"A compelling collection of reasons," Snape said.

They followed the damp trail through the adjoining corridor.

The bathroom door hung crookedly upon one hinge. A tarnished plaque identified it as the girls' lavatory, though someone had scratched OUT OF ORDER into the wood beneath.

McGonagall pushed it open.

Cold, damp air met them.

The bathroom contained a long row of cracked sinks, several chipped mirrors and cubicles whose doors had warped with age. Water covered part of the floor, reflecting the greenish light from the windows. One tap dripped steadily despite being twisted shut.

Snape inspected the room with his wand raised.

Harry stepped around a puddle.

A sob echoed from the final cubicle.

Draconia stopped. "That would be the other reason."

A translucent girl rose through the wooden door.

She wore thick spectacles and robes belonging to an older generation of Hogwarts students. Her dark hair had been divided into two lank plaits. Tears streamed down her pale cheeks, though they vanished before reaching the floor.

"Come to laugh?" she demanded. "Everyone always does. Even when nobody visits, they eventually remember I'm here and decide to be cruel."

McGonagall lowered her wand. "Nobody has come to laugh."

The ghost's eyes narrowed. "You say that now."

"Who are you?" Harry asked.

She turned towards him with affronted dignity.

"Moaning Myrtle."

"That can't be your real name."

"It is what everyone calls me."

"I'm Harry."

"I know who you are." Myrtle drifted closer. "Everyone knows who you are. They talk about you outside my bathroom because they think dead people cannot hear through doors."

Harry glanced at Draconia.

She looked away.

McGonagall stepped forwards. "Myrtle, did the water found in the corridor on Halloween come from this bathroom?"

"Probably. Peeves threw a wastepaper basket through me."

Myrtle's face crumpled at the memory.

"He said I couldn't haunt anything properly. So I flooded the room."

Harry looked at the shallow water around their shoes.

"How long have you been here?"

"Nearly fifty years."

Snape's head lifted.

McGonagall's attention sharpened. "You died at Hogwarts?"

"Right here."

The answer altered the air inside the bathroom.

Fifty years.

The Chamber had last been opened fifty years ago. One student had died.

Harry stepped closer. "How?"

Myrtle's expression became suddenly suspicious.

"Why?"

"Because—"

"Everyone always wants the horrible parts. Nobody asks what Olive Hornby said about my glasses or why I was crying or whether I had anyone to talk to before I died."

"I'm asking now."

"You weren't there."

"No."

"Then you can't possibly care."

Snape's patience broke. "How did you die?"

Myrtle released a wounded shriek and plunged headfirst through the toilet in the final cubicle.

Water exploded from every bowl.

McGonagall raised a shield before the wave reached them. Draconia stepped behind Harry, escaping most of it. Snape was not as fortunate.

Water dripped from the end of his nose.

Nobody spoke.

Harry made the mistake of meeting Draconia's eyes.

Her mouth trembled.

Snape turned towards them.

"Not one word."

"We didn't say anything," Harry replied.

"You considered it."

Snape left the bathroom with his robes trailing water.

McGonagall followed, but Harry looked back at the final cubicle.

Somewhere inside the pipes, Myrtle continued sobbing.

Dumbledore left the castle the following afternoon.

The Board of Governors had summoned him to London to answer questions concerning the attacks, the basilisk and his decision to keep Hogwarts open while a creature capable of killing with its gaze remained somewhere inside the walls. Professor McGonagall announced his departure at lunch with such cold disapproval that nobody mistook the summons for his choice.

"He will return as soon as possible," she said. "In his absence, every existing restriction remains."

At the staff table, Dumbledore rose.

Fawkes rested upon his shoulder, scarlet plumage burning against the grey afternoon. The phoenix's dark eyes passed across the Hall before settling briefly upon Harry.

Dumbledore followed the bird's gaze.

"Help will always be given at Hogwarts," he said, "to those who remain loyal to one another."

Then he left.

Fawkes remained behind.

Harry watched the phoenix disappear through a door beside the staff table.

Across the Hall, Draconia held the green satchel against her body.

"What's wrong?" Harry asked later.

"Nothing."

The word had begun to frighten him.

Rain swept across the hospital-wing windows that evening.

The storm had gathered above Hogwarts shortly before sunset, swallowing the mountains behind low cloud and turning the grounds into shifting shades of black and silver. Wind pressed against the high panes in long, uneven gusts. Sometimes the rain softened into a whisper; moments later it returned hard enough to make the glass tremble inside its lead frame.

Lamps had been lit along the walls. Their warm light reflected in rows of potion bottles, catching upon liquids of purple, blue and amber until the locked cabinets appeared filled with fragments of stained glass. At the far end of the ward, the iron stove ticked softly as it cooled. Curtains surrounded the occupied beds, their white fabric shifting whenever a draught found its way beneath the doors.

The room should have felt peaceful.

Harry had begun to distrust quiet places.

Madam Pomfrey admitted him and Draconia after dinner because, as she explained, both had completed an entire day of lessons without falling, fighting, being cursed or requiring replacement bones.

"You may remain for fifteen minutes," she said. "I have work in the dispensary, and Professor Sprout has sent three different samples from the Mandrakes which she claims are meaningfully different."

"Are they?" Harry asked.

"They are all screaming vegetation."

"That sounds like a no."

"It means I need some quiet to find out." Madam Pomfrey pointed towards Hermione's bed. "If I return to find either of you experimenting upon my patients, I will ask Professor Snape to supervise your punishment."

"That is excessive," Harry said.

"It is preventative."

Draconia glanced towards the shelves of potions. "What if Potter injures himself without my involvement?"

"Leave him where he falls."

"That seems practical."

"I'm still in the room," Harry reminded them.

Madam Pomfrey disappeared through the dispensary door and closed it firmly behind her.

The humour faded with her footsteps.

Harry sat beside Hermione.

Her bed had been placed nearest the high windows, where the last of the storm-darkened evening showed through the glass. Her spectacles rested folded upon the cabinet, and the pages of a book had been arranged beneath them to prevent the lenses from scratching against the wood. Madam Pomfrey had tamed Hermione's hair as much as anyone could, though several curls had already escaped and spread across the white pillow.

Draconia remained near the end of the bed.

She had not removed the green satchel. The strap crossed her chest and disappeared beneath one arm, held tightly enough that the leather had creased against her robes. Since Dumbledore's departure, she had spoken less and watched the doors more often.

Harry began telling Hermione about their investigation of the bathroom.

"You would have liked Myrtle," he said. "She flooded Snape."

Draconia came around to the other side of the bed. "Granger would not approve of assaulting a professor."

"She might make an exception."

"She would insist that personal satisfaction does not alter school rules."

"She would wait until Snape left before laughing."

"That is possible."

Harry looked at Hermione.

He could hear the answer she might have given, delivered with great offence at the suggestion she found anything funny about humiliating a teacher. She would have denied it too quickly. Draconia would have noticed. An argument would have begun and continued until Madam Pomfrey returned to expel them.

Harry waited for the imagined scene to become real.

Hermione remained motionless.

The rain filled the silence she left behind.

Draconia lowered her gaze. "She will wake up."

"I know."

"You don't sound convinced."

"I believe Professor Sprout."

Draconia looked at him.

Harry's mouth nearly formed a smile. "You said that to me."

"I remember."

"It was irritating."

"I was attempting reassurance."

"You sounded like Hermione."

Draconia turned towards the petrified girl. The lamplight softened the exhaustion around her eyes.

"I'll accept that as a compliment until she can object."

Harry lowered his gaze to Hermione's hands. They rested upon the blanket with unnatural neatness. The ink stain remained on one finger, small and ordinary enough to hurt.

"I keep thinking she'll start talking if we wait long enough."

"She never used to wait for us to listen."

"That was almost affectionate."

"Do not tell her."

"I'll write it down."

"You value your life too little."

The answer came automatically, sharpened into Draconia's usual dryness. It reassured Harry for less than a second.

He reached inside his robes and withdrew a folded piece of parchment.

The paper had softened along its creases from being opened too many times. Harry placed it on the blanket between them and smoothed it flat with one hand.

Draconia looked down.

"What is that?"

"Every time you vanished."

Her gaze moved across the writing.

Harry had recorded each date, the length of the absence and what Draconia remembered afterwards. Four incidents filled the first week. Six occupied the second. The most recent entries had grown more detailed as his concern sharpened.

Tuesday—missed breakfast. Claimed she overslept. Pansy said bed was empty.

Wednesday—arrived at Charms with Astronomy book. Did not remember leaving common room.

Friday—found near greenhouse three. No cloak. Could not remember leaving History of Magic.

Today—missed twenty-three minutes between Potions and lunch.

Draconia read the final line twice.

"You timed me?"

"There are clocks everywhere."

"You've been watching."

"I notice things too you know."

"I preferred it when you didn't."

"So did I."

Harry heard the bitterness in his answer and regretted it. Draconia's attention remained on the parchment.

"This just proves I'm tired."

"It proves you're losing it."

"You cannot prove the loss of something which cannot be measured."

"I measured it."

"That is exactly the sort of sentence Granger would admire."

"She'd have a better chart."

"Yes."

"She'd also ask what's inside the satchel."

Draconia's head lifted.

The colour left her face so gradually that Harry might not have noticed if he had not spent weeks studying every change she attempted to conceal.

"I have already told you."

"You said the notebook was irrelevant."

"It is."

"You said it came from Narcissa."

"I thought it did."

"You don't anymore."

Her hand closed over the leather. "Leave it."

"No."

"Harry."

"Mrs Norris was attacked on a day you couldn't remember. Hermione was attacked after you warned us to stop searching. Dobby tried to injure me rather than let me return to Hogwarts, and he couldn't answer when I asked whether the danger came from your house."

Each connection tightened Draconia's face.

The lamps flickered.

Only once, and faintly enough to be blamed upon the storm. Harry saw the green satchel shift beneath her arm.

"You think I attacked them," she said.

"No."

The answer came before she finished.

Draconia blinked.

Harry stood. Hermione's bed still separated them.

"I think something is happening to you."

"I'm fine."

"Stop saying that."

"You say it constantly."

"And you tell me when it isn't true."

"That doesn't mean you get to do it to me."

"Why not? You're my friend too."

The word struck harder than Harry intended.

Draconia looked towards Hermione.

"You flew into a Bludger for me," Harry continued. "You stood beside me while the school decided I was the Heir. You don't get to do all of that and then tell me your problems are private."

"This is not Quidditch."

"No. It's worse."

"You don't understand what you are asking."

"That's because you won't tell me."

"Because telling you may place you in danger."

"I'm already in danger."

"You always are. It's exhausting."

"That sounds like your problem."

"It is!"

Draconia's voice travelled across the ward.

The potion bottles inside the nearest cabinet rattled. One of the lamps dimmed, its flame contracting until the glass around it became green.

Both of them looked towards the dispensary.

Madam Pomfrey did not emerge.

Harry stepped around Hermione's bed.

Draconia retreated.

Her back met the locked cabinet, making the coloured bottles tremble once more. She looked trapped, not by Harry but by the satchel pressed between her arm and her body.

"I'm not trying to accuse you," he said.

"You brought evidence."

"I brought a list because Hermione isn't here to make one."

"That does not improve it."

"I'm frightened."

The admission stopped her.

Harry had not planned to say it. Fear was easier to disguise when described as irritation, suspicion or curiosity. Speaking it plainly made him feel younger than he wanted to feel.

"I'm not frightened of you," he continued. "I'm frightened that whatever is happening will take you before you decide we're allowed to help."

The anger disappeared from Draconia's face.

Rainwater streamed down the windows behind Harry. The storm had hidden the grounds completely, leaving the hospital wing suspended within darkness.

Draconia's fingers loosened around the satchel.

"I made a mistake," she whispered.

Harry remained where he was. "What mistake?"

"I thought I understood it."

"The notebook?"

Her gaze dropped.

A small nod followed.

Harry's pulse quickened. "What does it do?"

"It writes back."

The answer barely rose above the rain.

Harry glanced towards the satchel. "What does it write?"

"Whatever I need."

"That isn't an answer."

"It was kind."

Shame entered Draconia's voice, making the simple word difficult to hear.

"At first," she continued, "it asked questions. It remembered things I wrote days earlier. It never interrupted or decided what I meant before I had finished explaining."

"You had us."

"I know."

"Then why didn't you speak to us?"

Draconia's attention moved towards Hermione.

"Because you were happy."

Harry stared. "What?"

"The first year had ended. The Stone was safe. Nobody was trying to kill us for once, and both of you were happy."

"You weren't."

"That did not seem important."

"It was important to us."

"It should not have had to be."

"That wasn't your choice."

Draconia looked at him.

"I know that now."

The surrender in her voice frightened him more than another argument would have.

Harry took one step closer. "Show me the notebook."

She shook her head.

"If it's hurting you, give it to me."

"You should not touch it."

"Then we'll give it to Snape."

"No."

"He already knows something is wrong."

"He suspects."

"That is usually enough for him."

Draconia's hand rose towards the silver clasp.

The movement stopped inches away.

Her fingers trembled. The tendons stood out beneath her wrist, but the hand refused to move closer. Draconia tried with the other. It halted at the same distance, suspended in the air while strain spread across her face.

"I can't."

Harry watched both hands fighting against an invisible restraint.

"Because you don't want to?"

Her eyes flashed. "Because it won't let me."

The satchel moved.

Not much. Only a faint contraction beneath the leather, subtle and deliberate, like the pulse of an animal sleeping beneath cloth.

Harry's skin prickled.

A dark stain appeared near the clasp.

At first, he mistook it for water. Then it spread across the green leather in branching black lines. Ink gathered around the silver fastening before sinking back into the material.

Harry reached towards it.

Draconia jerked away. "No."

The word emerged twice.

One voice belonged to her.

The other came from somewhere deeper.

Harry froze.

The second voice had been lower and smoother, almost lost beneath Draconia's own. It left an echo inside the hospital wing after both mouths should have fallen silent.

Draconia heard it too.

Her face filled with horror.

"Get Madam Pomfrey," she whispered.

Harry turned.

The dispensary door slammed.

The lock twisted by itself, metal grinding hard enough to send vibrations through the surrounding wall. Madam Pomfrey tried the handle from the other side.

"What in heaven's name—"

The lamps flickered again.

Green light replaced gold across the ward. Shadows lengthened beneath the beds, and the rain-darkened windows reflected the rows of white curtains back into the room.

Harry drew his wand.

Draconia stumbled away from the cabinet. One hand clawed at the satchel strap, trying to drag it over her head. The leather tightened against her shoulder.

"Help me."

Harry reached her.

He caught the strap and pulled.

The satchel felt hot enough to burn through his fingers. It clung to Draconia's robes, the leather resisting every attempt to lift it. Harry placed one foot against the base of the cabinet and pulled harder.

The silver clasp opened.

A page turned inside.

Neither of them had touched the diary.

Draconia convulsed.

Her head snapped backwards. A shudder travelled through her body with enough force to strike her against the cabinet. Bottles collided behind the glass, filling the ward with the fragile music of crystal.

"Draco!"

Harry caught her before she fell.

Her fingers closed painfully around his shoulders. For an instant she clung to him, face pressed against his robes, every muscle shaking.

"I can hear him," she whispered.

Harry tightened his arms around her. "Who?"

"He knows you're here."

"Who knows?"

Draconia went rigid.

Her grip loosened.

Harry drew back far enough to see her face.

Her eyes opened.

The grey had darkened until they appeared almost black.

The terror vanished. Her breathing became even, and every sign of strain left her features with such completeness that the calm itself appeared unnatural.

Draconia straightened.

Harry released her and stepped back.

"Draco?"

She looked at him.

The face belonged to his friend.

The patience inside it did not.

"She did warn you," Draconia said.

Her voice carried a cadence Harry had never heard from her, smooth and faintly amused. It reminded him of a teacher waiting for a slow student to discover an obvious answer.

Harry kept his wand raised. "Let her go."

A smile touched her mouth.

"She has been trying to say that for months."

"Who are you?"

"You'll know soon enough."

"I'd prefer now."

"I know. She wrote that patience is not among your virtues."

Harry's stomach turned.

"You read what she wrote."

"I read everything."

Draconia's hand lowered towards her wand.

Harry aimed at her chest. "Don't."

Her fingers closed around the handle.

The movement was not easy. Her wrist trembled so violently that the wand struck twice against the inside of her sleeve before coming free. A tear escaped one darkened eye and travelled down her cheek, but the calm expression did not change.

Draconia was still inside.

She was fighting.

"Draco, listen to me."

The wand rose towards him.

Harry did not cast.

He knew half a dozen spells which might disarm her. Any one of them could miss and strike Hermione, Colin or Penelope. More importantly, casting at Draconia felt impossible while tears moved down her face.

Her wrist jerked sharply to one side.

A red spell struck the cabinet.

Glass exploded.

Harry ducked as broken bottles and coloured potion rained across the floor. Blue liquid began smoking where it touched the stone. An orange potion formed small, furious bubbles that pursued one another beneath the nearest bed.

Madam Pomfrey pounded upon the dispensary door.

"What is happening out there?"

Harry moved sideways, placing himself between Draconia and Hermione.

"You missed."

The other presence smiled through her.

"She did."

"Good."

Draconia's wand moved again.

"Incarcerous."

Ropes shot from its tip.

Harry slashed his own wand through the air. "Diffindo!"

The first rope split. A second caught his wrist and dragged his arm sideways, but Harry twisted free before the remaining coils could bind him.

He aimed at Draconia.

"Expelliarmus!"

She blocked the spell.

Red light struck her Shield Charm and ricocheted into the ceiling, showering them with plaster. One of the curtains tore from its rail.

Harry moved closer.

He needed to reach her before the thing inside her decided to stop playing.

"Draco, you fought it before."

Her wand tracked his movement.

"You warned me."

The hand holding it shook.

"You made the first spell miss."

Another tear crossed her face.

Harry took one more step.

"That means you can hear me."

The dark eyes flickered.

Grey surfaced for an instant.

"Harry."

Her voice was her own.

He lowered his wand slightly.

"Come here."

Draconia's breathing broke.

The wand dipped.

Harry reached for her.

Then the other presence looked towards Hermione.

Draconia's arm turned, directing the wand at the petrified girl.

Harry stopped.

"No."

"Put yours down," the voice instructed.

"Don't touch her."

"The wand."

Harry's fingers tightened around it.

Draconia's wand remained aimed at Hermione's chest. The girl beneath the blankets could not move, defend herself or even see what threatened her.

Harry lowered his wand.

"That's better."

"You need her alive," Harry said. "Whatever you are, you used Draco to keep the others alive."

The calm smile sharpened. "How observant."

"So hurting Hermione achieves nothing."

"It would hurt you."

Harry's throat tightened.

The thing inside Draconia understood him because she had written about him. It knew precisely where to aim.

"You said I'd know who you are," Harry replied. "You can't explain if you kill me now."

"I have been patient for months."

"That makes one of us."

The smile widened.

Draconia's wand swung back towards him.

Her arm fought the movement.

The tip trembled between Harry and the floor, forced upwards by one will and down by another. Her face twisted. Grey returned around the edges of her pupils.

"Run," she whispered.

Harry raised his wand.

The darkness swallowed her eyes again.

"Stupefy."

Red light crossed the ward.

Harry attempted to turn.

The hex struck him squarely in the chest and lifted him from his feet. The ceiling swept across his vision. He hit the floor beside Hermione's bed hard enough to drive air from his lungs.

Her spectacles cracked beneath his shoulder.

Pain flashed along the back of his head.

His wand rolled beneath the bed.

Harry tried to rise.

Nothing moved.

Draconia stood above him, blurred by the darkness gathering around his vision. The wand remained aimed at his chest, though her hand shook violently.

The spell could have struck his face.

It could have struck his heart.

She had aimed where Madam Pomfrey could repair the damage.

"You missed again," Harry managed.

A tear fell from Draconia's jaw.

Her body turned away from him.

One hand opened the satchel and withdrew the slim diary. Its false green covering appeared nearly black beneath the hospital lamps. The silver initials glimmered once before vanishing from the leather.

"Draco."

Her face twisted.

The person controlling her smiled.

The girl beneath him did not.

Harry saw the struggle in the rigid line of her jaw, the tears moving silently across her cheeks and the desperate flex of the hand not holding the diary.

Then her wand turned towards the dispensary.

"Colloportus."

The sealed door glowed red around its frame.

Madam Pomfrey struck it from inside with enough force to shake the wall.

Draconia ran.

She crossed the ward with the diary held against her chest. Her shoes splashed through the spilled potions, leaving dark footprints across the polished floor.

At the doors, she stopped.

Her head turned.

For one final instant, grey returned to her eyes.

Harry thought she might come back.

Then the doors opened and she disappeared into the corridor.

Harry tried to follow.

His fingers twitched against the floor. No other part of him obeyed. Darkness spread from the edges of the ward, swallowing the overturned chair, the broken cabinet and the rain-streaked windows.

Hermione lay above him, her petrified hand hanging over the edge of the mattress.

Harry dragged his fingers towards it.

He could not reach.

The last thing he heard was Madam Pomfrey breaking through the dispensary door and shouting his name.

Consciousness returned in fragments.

The scent of burnt potion came first. Beneath it lay clean linen and the sharper odour of whatever Madam Pomfrey had used to repair the bruising across Harry's chest.

Voices moved somewhere nearby.

They were low, hurried and incomplete. Harry caught individual words without understanding the sentences surrounding them.

Missing.

Staircases.

No breach in the wards.

Find Snape.

Harry opened his eyes.

White curtains surrounded him.

For several seconds, he could not remember why he was in a bed. Then he saw the rain-dark window beyond the fabric, and the hospital wing returned in one violent rush.

Draconia's face.

The second voice beneath hers.

Red light.

Harry sat upright.

Pain spread across his chest and shoulder. His head throbbed where it had struck the floor, and the sudden movement turned the curtained space sideways.

He reached for the screen.

Several metal rings tore from their rail as he dragged it open.

The hospital wing had changed.

Rain no longer struck the windows. Night pressed against the glass instead, turning each pane into a black mirror. Lamps burned throughout the ward, brighter than before, and the remains of the shattered potion cabinet had been cleared from the floor.

Hermione lay in the bed beside him. New spectacles rested upon her cabinet. Her petrified face remained unchanged.

Draconia's satchel lay open near her hand.

The diary was gone.

Madam Pomfrey turned from the far end of the ward. Professor Snape stood beside her, wet robes clinging to his shoulders and his face white with controlled fury. McGonagall spoke urgently with two prefects near the doors while Professor Flitwick examined a map floating in the air.

"Where is she?" Harry asked.

Every face turned.

Madam Pomfrey reached him first. "Lie down."

"Draco took the book. Where did she go?"

"You were Stunned less than an hour ago."

"She was possessed."

Snape crossed the ward in three strides. "What did you see?"

Harry pushed the blankets aside.

The floor tilted when his feet touched it, but he caught the bedframe and remained standing.

"She spoke differently. It wasn't her voice—not entirely. She tried to fight it, and then she hexed me."

"What book did she take?"

"The notebook she's been carrying."

Snape's gaze moved towards the open satchel.

"Describe it."

"Dark green leather. Silver initials. Except the colour changed when she took it out, and the initials disappeared."

"What initials?"

"D.M."

Snape looked at McGonagall.

Something silent passed between them.

"Did the object provide a name?" he asked.

"Draco said it never did."

"What did the voice say?"

"That I would learn who it was soon. It knew things she had written about me."

Harry's stomach twisted at the memory.

"It used Hermione to make me lower my wand."

Madam Pomfrey looked towards the broken spectacles beside Hermione's bed.

"Miss Malfoy threatened her?"

"It wasn't Draco."

Harry heard the desperation in his own voice.

"She fought it. Her spells missed. The Stunner could have hit my head, but she moved her wand."

Snape's expression did not soften, yet something inside it changed.

"I know," he said.

The certainty steadied Harry more than reassurance would have.

McGonagall dismissed the prefects and approached. "Miss Malfoy has not been seen since she left this ward."

"She ran into the corridor."

"There is no record of her passing the western staircase," Flitwick said. "The portraits saw nobody."

"She knows hidden routes," Snape replied.

"She might not be the one choosing them," Harry said.

Madam Pomfrey put one hand against his shoulder. "You are in no condition to go anywhere."

"I'm fine."

"You were unconscious."

"I'm awake now."

"That is not the medical triumph you imagine."

Harry looked towards the open satchel.

Its contents had been placed upon Hermione's blanket: spare parchment, three quills, a sealed letter from Narcissa and the silver hand mirror Draconia had taken from Pansy. The mirror's glass reflected only the ceiling.

A bell began ringing somewhere below.

It was not the bell used for lessons or curfew. The sound came from the entrance hall, deep and relentless, each toll vibrating through the floor and the frames of the hospital beds.

McGonagall turned towards the doors.

"What is that?" Harry asked.

Nobody answered.

A silver hare burst through the wall, landing before McGonagall, scattering light across the ward. It spoke in Flitwick's own high, strained voice even though the professor stood only several feet away.

"Message on the second floor. The girl has been taken."

The hare then vanished, erupting into silver mist. The words struck Harry harder than the Stunner had.

McGonagall's face became still. "Severus—"

Snape was already moving.

Madam Pomfrey's grip tightened around Harry's shoulder. "You will remain here."

Harry looked at Hermione. Then at Draconia's open satchel. He could not leave either of them behind.

Without consciously deciding to, he seized the satchel and pulled its strap across his chest. The silver mirror struck softly against the quills inside.

Madam Pomfrey caught his sleeve. Harry tore free.

The fabric ripped beneath her fingers.

"Mr Potter!"

He reached the doors before Snape could turn.

"Potter!"

Harry ran.

The corridor outside the hospital wing was empty.

Professors had ordered every student into their common rooms, leaving the castle stripped of its ordinary movement. Torchlight stretched across vacant floors. Classroom doors stood closed, and abandoned suits of armour watched from alcoves with their weapons raised.

Portraits woke as Harry passed.

"Potter is awake!"

"Stop him!"

"He's going towards the message!"

One wizard hurried through the edge of his frame and vanished into the next portrait, carrying the warning ahead.

Harry ignored them.

His chest burnt where the hex had struck. Each breath scraped his throat, while the floor shifted whenever he turned too sharply. The strap of Draconia's satchel cut across his shoulder, striking his side with every step.

He ran faster.

Staircases began moving as he reached them.

The first swung away from the second floor. Harry jumped the narrowing gap, caught the opposite railing and dragged himself across before the steps separated completely.

The second staircase attempted to carry him upwards.

"Not now."

Its stone banister offered no apology.

Harry climbed over the side, dropped onto the landing below and nearly collapsed when pain shot through his ankle. He recovered against the wall and kept running.

Voices gathered ahead.

The corridor near the second-floor landing had been lit by more than twenty floating lamps. Their white light left nowhere for shadows to gather.

Professor Flitwick stood beside the wall with two prefects, both pale and silent. A tapestry had been torn down and lay crumpled upon the floor.

Dark letters glistened upon the exposed stone.

They had been written in blood.

HER BODY WILL LIE IN THE CHAMBER FOREVER.

The message occupied almost the entire wall. Each letter had been painted with slow, deliberate strokes. Narrow red trails descended from them, following cracks in the stone before gathering in small pools along the floor.

The smell of iron reached Harry. One drop formed beneath the final letter. It swelled, trembled and fell.

Harry heard it strike the stone above everything else. Her body.

Draconia in the hospital wing, crying while another voice used her mouth.

Draconia telling him to run. Draconia stopping in the doorway long enough to look back. Harry's thoughts narrowed until the corridor contained only the message.

Professor Flitwick saw him.

"Harry, you must return to the hospital wing."

"Where did you find this?"

"It appeared minutes ago."

"Did anyone see Draco?"

"No. Professor Snape and Professor McGonagall are organising a complete search."

Harry stared at the blood.

A search meant classrooms, towers, dungeons, cupboards, grounds and corridors.

There was no time to search the entire castle.

Nearly fifty years, Myrtle had said.

I died right here.

The Chamber had been opened fifty years earlier.

One student had died.

Harry's gaze moved towards the passage leading to the abandoned bathroom.

The answer arrived with terrible simplicity.

Myrtle had not chosen that bathroom as the place she haunted.

It was where she died.

Harry turned.

Professor Flitwick caught the edge of his satchel. "Where are you going?"

"To ask her how."

"Ask whom?"

"The girl who died."

Harry pulled free.

"Harry!"

Flitwick's voice followed him towards the abandoned bathroom.

He did not slow.

Moaning Myrtle emerged from the final cubicle when Harry slammed the door.

"You again," she said. "Have you come to shout at me like that horrible professor?"

"How did you die?"

Myrtle blinked.

Harry crossed the flooded tiles. "You said you died here fifty years ago. How?"

Her surprise became pleased importance.

"I told you nobody ever asks properly."

"I'm asking."

She drifted down until her shoes disappeared through the floor.

"I was crying in that cubicle because Olive Hornby teased me about my glasses. Then someone came in."

"The boy speaking strangely."

"Yes. Hissing and spitting near the sinks. I opened the door to tell him to use his own bathroom."

"What did you see?"

Myrtle's voice softened.

"Eyes."

Harry's heart struck painfully against his ribs.

"Where?"

"Beside that sink." She pointed towards the cracked row of basins. "Great yellow eyes. I died immediately. Then I went to find Olive Hornby."

Harry approached the sink she indicated.

It appeared identical to the others except that its tap had never worked. Green corrosion covered the copper. Harry wiped it away with his sleeve.

A tiny snake had been engraved along one side.

He looked towards Myrtle. "Did the boy touch this?"

"I don't know. I was dead."

Harry gripped the basin.

"Open."

The word emerged in English.

Nothing happened.

He pictured the snake from the Duelling Club, its body coiled across the floor. He remembered the voice moving through the walls and allowed the unfamiliar language to rise from a part of him that understood without learning.

"Open."

The engraved snake lifted its head.

Every tap in the row began to move.

Porcelain ground against stone. The sinks rotated backwards while pipes separated beneath them, sliding into the walls. The floor shuddered, and the central basin descended until a circular opening appeared beneath it.

A vast pipe dropped into darkness.

Cold air breathed from below, carrying the smell of wet stone, stagnant water and earth buried for centuries.

Myrtle peered over Harry's shoulder.

"I've never seen that before."

Harry removed his glasses.

Draconia's silver mirror lay inside the open satchel he had taken from Hermione's bed without remembering doing so. He left it face down beside the sink, then folded his glasses into the safest pocket of his robes.

If the basilisk still had its eyes, sight would kill him. Behind the bathroom door, footsteps pounded through the corridor.

"Potter!"

Snape.

Harry swung one leg over the edge.

Myrtle stared. "You're going down there?"

"My friend is inside."

"That doesn't seem very sensible."

"She'd say the same."

The bathroom door flew open. Harry let go.

The pipe swallowed him.

Its inner surface was slick with centuries of slime. Harry plunged through absolute darkness, gathering speed as the tunnel curved beneath the school. His shoulders struck metal at every turn. Cold moisture soaked through his robes, and rust scraped the palms he used in a useless attempt to slow himself.

Snape's shout followed him for several seconds.

Then even that disappeared.

The chute dropped more steeply.

Harry's stomach rose into his throat. The pipe twisted left, then right, and ended without warning.

He shot from its mouth and struck wet stone.

The impact drove the air from his lungs. Harry slid through shallow water until his shoulder collided with a wall. Pain flashed down his arm, and he lay still until the darkness stopped moving around him.

Water dripped nearby.

Each drop fell at slow, enormous intervals, its echo travelling into spaces Harry could not see.

He drew his wand.

"Lumos."

A pale beam illuminated a low stone tunnel.

Animal bones littered the edges of the passage. Rat skeletons, bird skulls and the remains of creatures Harry could not identify lay half-submerged in black water. Thick pipes ran overhead, disappearing through the rock towards the castle.

A trail crossed the filth.

Narrow footprints disturbed the slime ahead of him.

Draconia's.

Harry forced himself upright and followed.

The tunnel descended gradually. Its ceiling rose until he could no longer touch it, and the walls changed from rough earth to fitted stone carved with worn serpents. Damp air pressed against his skin. Far ahead, metal scraped across stone.

The basilisk was moving.

Harry extinguished his wand.

Darkness returned so completely that he could not see his hand.

He continued by touch, one palm against the wall. The carved scales led him forward. Twice he slipped in the water. Once his fingers touched a coil wider than his arm, cold and papery against the stone.

Harry relit his wand.

A shed snakeskin stretched across the tunnel.

It was translucent and enormous, wide enough that the serpent which left it could have swallowed a person without difficulty.

Harry stepped around the empty head.

His fear had become physical: a hard pressure beneath his ribs, tightening with every breath. It told him to wait for Snape, McGonagall or anyone older than twelve.

Then he imagined the message upon the wall.

Her body will lie in the Chamber forever.

Harry kept walking.

The tunnel ended at a circular door carved with seven intertwined serpents.

Their stone bodies gleamed with moisture. Each head faced the centre, mouths open around narrow fangs.

Harry approached.

"Open," he hissed.

The snakes moved.

They uncoiled from one another, retreating along invisible seams. The stone door divided and slid into the surrounding walls.

The Chamber did not resemble a room built beneath a school.

Beyond the doorway, the floor descended along a broad flight of black stone steps worn smooth at their centres. Green flames burnt inside iron baskets positioned along both walls, but their light reached only a fraction of the enormous space below. The ceiling arched somewhere beyond sight, supported by ribs of stone that curved together like the bones of a buried creature.

Harry descended slowly.

Each footstep returned to him from several directions. The Chamber seemed built to confuse sound, stretching small noises until the movement of his shoes became the approach of an unseen crowd. Water ran through channels carved along both sides of the staircase before spilling into a shallow pool which covered most of the floor.

Two rows of pillars extended towards the far end.

They had been carved into vast serpents. Their stone bodies coiled from floor to ceiling, scales overlapping with such care that the green light appeared to move across them. Some held enormous braziers inside open mouths. Others bent their heads towards the central path, watching anyone who entered with polished emerald eyes.

Between the pillars stood statues of hooded witches and wizards. Their stone faces had been chiselled away, leaving smooth ovals beneath the hoods. Harry could not tell whether age had erased them or whether Salazar Slytherin had never considered his followers important enough to possess faces.

The water around the statues was not empty.

Bones lay beneath its surface.

Most belonged to animals: rats, birds and creatures small enough for age to have scattered their skeletons. Farther into the Chamber, the remains became larger. A rib cage curved from the water beside one pillar. Harry could not tell what animal it had once belonged to and decided not to investigate.

The air carried the smell of mineral water, rust and reptile musk. It pressed hot and damp against Harry's face despite the winter cold above. Every breath tasted ancient, as if the Chamber had sealed away the same air for a thousand years and resented being forced to share it.

At the far end stood a colossal statue of Salazar Slytherin.

The founder sat upon a throne cut directly into the cavern wall. Stone robes flowed around his feet in folds taller than Harry, while one hand rested upon the head of a serpent whose body formed the arm of the throne. Slytherin's face was long, severe and deeply lined. A beard descended nearly to his chest, and his enormous stone eyes gazed across the Chamber with the cold patience of someone who expected history to justify him eventually.

Harry reached the bottom step.

Water closed around his shoes.

"Bit much," he muttered.

His voice raced up the pillars and returned in fragments.

Much... much... much...

Even Draconia would have considered the statue excessive. She would have examined its colossal face, tilted her head and announced that anyone requiring a likeness forty feet tall had been compensating for a deep personal inadequacy.

The thought hurt.

"Draconia?"

Her name crossed the water and vanished beneath the ceiling. No answer followed. Harry lifted his wand higher.

A pale figure lay at the foot of the statue.

Harry ran, splashing water around his legs. The movement disturbed years of accumulated dust, sending black clouds through the shallow pool. Harry almost fell upon the final step but caught himself against the carved base of Slytherin's throne.

Draconia had collapsed onto one side.

Her silver hair spread across the wet stone, darkened where it touched the water. One arm rested above her head. Her wand lay several feet away, while the other hand remained curled around the slim diary.

Its cover no longer looked entirely green.

The colour moved when Harry's wandlight crossed it, peeling backwards in thin layers until black leather showed beneath. The silver initials had vanished. No title or name remained upon the surface.

Harry dropped to his knees.

"Draco."

He touched her cheek.

Her skin was cold.

"No."

Harry slid one arm beneath her shoulders and lifted her carefully. Her head fell against his chest without resistance. She felt frighteningly light, each breath so shallow that he found it only after pressing two trembling fingers beneath her jaw.

A pulse answered, weak and slowing.

"Wake up."

Draconia's eyelids did not move.

"You don't get to hex me and then die before I can say something about it."

His voice shook badly enough to ruin the attempt at humour.

Harry pushed damp strands of hair from her face. The girl who met him in Madam Malkin's would have objected to the condition of her robes. She would have insulted the Chamber, corrected his pronunciation and informed Salazar Slytherin that enormous statues were evidence of insecurity.

She would have done anything except lie silent in his arms.

"You said you were still here."

A laugh sounded between the pillars. It was soft, young and pleasantly amused. Harry lowered Draconia to the floor and turned.

A boy stood in the central aisle.

He appeared sixteen or seventeen, dressed in an old-fashioned Hogwarts uniform. His robes remained immaculate despite the filthy water surrounding his shoes. Dark hair fell neatly across his forehead, and his features possessed the polished handsomeness of someone accustomed to being trusted on sight.

Green light passed through the edges of his body. The centre looked almost solid.

He smiled at Harry.

"Not for much longer."

Harry positioned himself between the boy and Draconia.

"Who are you?"

"Tom Riddle."

The name disturbed a memory: a polished trophy inside a glass cabinet, its brass plate reflecting torchlight.

"Special Services to the School," Harry said.

Tom's smile widened. "You have been researching."

"I saw a trophy."

"How disappointing."

"It didn't mention possessing students."

"No. Hogwarts has always lacked imagination in its awards."

Harry raised his wand. "What did you do to her?"

"I listened."

"That wasn't the question."

"It is the answer."

Tom began walking towards them. His shoes made no sound upon the flooded floor, and the water did not move around his ankles.

"She needed someone who would listen," he said. "Draconia Malfoy spent her life surrounded by people who heard only what they expected. A surname before a sentence. A duty before a desire. An heir before a child."

"She had us."

"Yes. That complicated matters."

Tom glanced towards Draconia with faint irritation.

"At first, she gave me very little. A complaint about Pansy Parkinson. An observation concerning Professor Lockhart's incompetence. Questions intended to determine whether I was clever enough to interest her."

"She was testing you."

"She tests everyone."

"And you still fell for it."

Tom's smile tightened.

Harry felt a flicker of vicious satisfaction. Draconia would have appreciated that one.

"She was lonely," Tom continued. "Although she would consider the description insulting."

"She wasn't alone."

"Those are not the same thing."

Harry's wand remained trained upon his chest. "You're very talkative for a book."

"I am not a book."

"You live in one."

"I am a preserved consciousness."

"A diary."

Tom's eyes darkened. "I am a memory of the greatest wizard Hogwarts has ever produced."

"You're stationery with opinions."

The amusement vanished from Tom's face.

Harry was frightened enough that the sight improved his mood considerably.

Tom's attention shifted towards Draconia again. "She wrote about Granger with irritation, admiration and an exhausting refusal to decide which she felt more strongly. She wrote about her parents, her House and the burden of a family name everyone believed explained her before she spoke."

Then he looked at Harry.

"Mostly, she wrote about you."

"Shut up."

"Harry said this. Harry did that. Harry forgot to eat. Harry looked tired at breakfast. Harry was being avoided. Harry kept smiling today. Harry had once again interpreted mortal danger as a personal invitation."

"You've made your point."

"Have I?"

"Yes. You read a twelve-year-old girl's diary and became unbearable about it."

Tom laughed.

"She would tell me anything if she thought it might help you. It was almost too easy."

Harry's anger became cold.

"What did you make her do?"

"I showed her the Chamber. I allowed her to continue Salazar Slytherin's work."

"She wouldn't."

"She did."

Tom lifted one translucent hand. The water between them darkened. Images formed above its surface, distorted and colourless, each unfolding in silence. Draconia walking alone through Myrtle's bathroom.

Draconia standing before the engraved sink with empty eyes. Draconia kneeling inside Hagrid's chicken enclosure while white feathers drifted around her robes. Draconia painting dark letters across the corridor wall while Mrs Norris hung motionless above her.

Harry looked away.

"She didn't know."

"She suspected," Tom replied. "There is a difference."

"You controlled her."

"At first, she would wake in corridors with mud upon her shoes or blood beneath her fingernails. She accepted whatever explanation I offered because the truth was less comfortable."

Tom closed his hand. The images vanished.

"As I became stronger, the missing hours grew longer. She began finding evidence I could not conceal. By Halloween, even Draconia's talent for self-deception had limits."

"You possessed her."

"I improved her."

"You hollowed her out and called it an achievement."

Tom's face sharpened. "She resisted once she understood. Irritatingly."

Harry looked down at Draconia.

"Mrs Norris should have died," Tom continued. "Draconia delayed the command long enough for the animal to see the basilisk reflected in the water. Creevey's camera was not chance either. She heard him approaching and forced his hands upwards before he turned the corner."

"Hermione?"

"A more determined interference. Your brilliant little friend brought a mirror. Draconia warned you both before I silenced her, but she could not prevent the attack."

"You didn't beat her."

"I am standing," Tom said. "She is dying."

Harry's wand trembled.

Tom noticed.

"When she dies, I will no longer require the diary. Everything she poured into its pages will belong to me."

Harry understood.

"You're stealing her life."

"Giving it purpose."

"You sound like Lockhart."

The insult struck harder than Harry expected.

Tom's features briefly lost their polish.

"I am Lord—"

"You're Tom."

Tom moved with sudden speed. His hand closed around Harry's wrist.

The fingers were not entirely solid, but the shock loosened Harry's grip. His wand dropped towards the water.

Tom caught it. He examined the holly as if Harry had handed him an interesting object.

"You asked who I am."

"I'm beginning to regret it."

Tom used Harry's wand to trace letters through the air. Red light formed above the water.

TOM MARVOLO RIDDLE.

The letters separated. They moved through one another, turning in silence until a different name burnt beneath the stone face of Salazar Slytherin.

I AM LORD VOLDEMORT.

The Chamber disappeared around Harry.

For one terrible instant, he saw green light inside a small upstairs room. He heard a woman scream and felt the edge of a memory too old to belong to him. His scar split with pain, hot enough to send him staggering.

Tom watched with fascination.

"Do you understand now?"

Harry pressed one hand against his forehead. "You killed my parents."

"Not yet."

The answer was worse than denial.

"This version of me was preserved before I became the wizard who visited Godric's Hollow," Tom said. "I was curious when she wrote about you—the infant who defeated my future self."
