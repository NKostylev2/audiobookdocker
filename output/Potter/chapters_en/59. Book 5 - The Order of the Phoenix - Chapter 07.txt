7

"Will we still get dinner?"

The boy across the awning had asked three times. He sat with a borrowed blanket around his shoulders and one shoe in his lap, looking from the train to whichever adult passed closest. Harry wanted somebody to answer him. A promise about dinner would have been easier to believe than another announcement that everything was under control.

Sirius's watch lay open beside the healer's chart. Its second hand moved past a streak of dried blood on the glass, passed twelve and started again. Harry watched it complete six circuits before he realised he was counting. There was nothing left for him to lift, hold or fight. Waiting ought to have been easier than it was.

Even after the Ministry clerks arrived, students cleared for transport were kept inside the perimeter. The red-ribbon patients went first, under healer escort; the rest waited while officials argued about release points. Aurors established a perimeter around the stopped train. Hit Wizards walked the tracks in pairs, searching for any ward spike which had not discharged. Healers continued sorting injuries beneath the white awnings while Ministry clerks attempted to sort witnesses into categories which bore no resemblance to where anyone had actually been.

Students who had fought were placed beside students who had hidden. Students from the rear carriages were separated from those who had seen the bridge. When a fourth-year mentioned the Dark Mark, a clerk sent her to a table marked POTENTIALLY INFLUENCED TESTIMONY. Harry saw two other students sent there after pointing at the same sky. Soon that queue stretched beyond the awning. A younger boy kept asking whether he had done something wrong. The prefect beside him said no, more loudly each time.

Harry was watching a healer check the boy's bare foot when Umbridge made her first attempt to confiscate Hermione's notebook. She did not attempt it personally. A thin wizard from the Department of Magical Transportation approached the stretcher carrying a green evidence pouch and the expression of someone who had been promised that children were easier than adults.

"Miss Granger," he said, "I will require all informal accounts gathered at the scene."

Hermione looked up from the fourth-year Hufflepuff whose statement she was recording. The bandage around her head had slipped over one eyebrow. Madam Pomfrey was examining the younger children at the next awning; the attending healer had already told Hermione to lie back and stop writing. Hermione had obeyed for nearly a minute. Now she was recording a fourth-year's account while insisting that taking dictation did not count as reading.

Harry could see how hard she was concentrating. Twice she lost her place and had to ask the boy to repeat himself. On the second occasion she pressed the heel of her hand against her forehead and kept it there.

"Why?" she asked.

The wizard held open the pouch. "To prevent witness contamination."

"These accounts were taken before the Ministry interviewed anyone."

"Precisely."

"Then you can see what they remembered before anyone asked leading questions," Hermione said. "That's why we kept the accounts separate."

The pouch remained open.

"They are not official statements."

"I didn't say they were official. I said they're what people remember."

Draconia opened her eyes upon the neighbouring stretcher. The Blood-Binding Curse had left her pale enough for the blue veins at her temples to show. One hand rested over the dressing beneath her ribs. The other remained linked with Hermione's between the two stretchers, their fingers loosely hooked.

"Are you allowed to take her book?" she asked. "Where does it say that?"

The wizard looked at her. Then at the Malfoy name written upon the healer's chart. His certainty altered.

"Commissioner authority permits the collection of material relevant to student welfare."

"Does that mean you're allowed to ask for a copy, or allowed to take the only one?" Draconia asked.

"No one said seizure."

"Then why are you asking her to put her book in an evidence pouch?"

Harry sat on the grass between them. The wizard glanced at Draconia's dressing, then back at the open pouch. She looked too tired to keep her eyes open for long, but her gaze did not leave him while she waited for an answer.

"The material will be returned after review," the wizard said.

"Then give her a receipt. Your name, how many pages you're taking and when she gets them back."

Hermione lifted her quill. "And don't change their words," Hermione said. "Some of them couldn't write much. That doesn't mean you can finish it for them."

The wizard lowered the pouch.

"I will consult my supervisor."

"Do that," Hermione said.

He left. Harry waited until the man was beyond hearing.

"You don't know which statute applies."

Draconia closed her eyes again. "Neither does he."

Hermione added a note at the bottom of the statement. "I just wanted him to put his name on it," Hermione said.

"You made it sound more complicated."

"I wanted him to think before he took it."

Harry looked between them. "You two are frightening when you agree."

"He should have asked nicely," Draconia said, and closed her eyes.

The healer appeared behind Hermione and removed the notebook from her hands.

"You were instructed not to read or write."

"I was listening."

"Your quill was moving."

"It is self-inking."

"It is not self-thinking."

Hermione considered arguing. The healer pointed at the pillow beneath her head. Hermione lay back. Harry accepted the notebook before it could be confiscated for medical reasons. The most recent pages contained five separate statements, two rough carriage diagrams and a list entitled MINISTRY REQUESTS WHICH REQUIRE FORMAL OBJECTION.

The first item had been underlined twice. Below the diagrams, Hermione had written a girl's account of hiding beneath a seat while her older brother held his cloak across the broken window. The last sentence ended halfway through. Beside it, in Hermione's smaller writing, was a reminder to ask whether the girl wanted to finish later. Harry closed the book. It was easy to admire Hermione when she argued. He sometimes forgot how much attention she paid to people who could not. Around them, the field changed by degrees.

Parents began arriving at the perimeter only to be held outside it. Reporters gathered along the road. Every flash of a camera made Harry's hand move towards his wand. Sirius noticed the first three times and shifted until his body blocked the nearest lenses. On the fourth, he conjured a black canvas screen between the medical awnings and the road. Umbridge ordered a clerk to remove it. The clerk took one look at Sirius and decided the screen protected patient privacy.

Remus sat upon an overturned crate nearby. His cane rested between his knees. He appeared calm until anyone in Ministry robes approached Harry, at which point his hand tightened around the carved handle.

Ron moved from group to group with the Gryffindor witness list tucked beneath his torn prefect robes. He counted students, checked names and sent corrections back to Hermione through Ginny. Cedric remained with the emergency-broom teams until every rescued child had been examined. Cho had acquired a burn across her palm. Anthony's ankle was swollen. Ginny's personal broom consisted of a handle, fourteen bristles and determination.

Those waiting for transport kept passing lists and corrections between the awnings. A clerk told Ron twice that his count had already been taken; Ron showed him the missing name and waited until he added it. At the thirty-seventh minute on Sirius's watch, Dumbledore returned from the damaged tracks. Ash covered the hem of his robes. The usual gentleness in his face had hardened into something Harry had only seen in the Chamber and after the graveyard: not anger displayed, but anger brought under such complete control that everyone nearby became aware of its size. Professor McGonagall walked beside him carrying a list of students in one hand and a brass shield boss in the other.

"The train cannot continue," Dumbledore announced.

His voice reached the entire field without seeming loud.

"The carriages will remain here until every hostile enchantment has been removed. Students cleared by the healers will travel to Hogwarts in supervised groups. Those requiring immediate care will be taken directly to the hospital wing. No Ministry interview will delay treatment."

Umbridge approached from the evidence tables.

"Headmaster, the Department has not authorised mass transport from an unsecured site."

Dumbledore turned towards her.

"Then it is fortunate that Hogwarts has."

"The field may still contain interference capable of redirecting a Portkey."

"Professor Flitwick and I have established an independent route anchored to the castle's heartstone."

"The Ministry cannot accept liability—"

"I have not offered it."

The field became very still. McGonagall looked down at her list. The corner of her mouth did not move, but Harry had known her long enough to recognise satisfaction in the angle of her spectacles.

Dumbledore continued. "Ambulatory students will depart by House in groups of twenty. Prefects will accompany first- and second-years. Each medical Portkey will carry four stretchers, a healer and up to two accompanying passengers. Every object has been examined by Professor Flitwick, myself and Auror Shacklebolt."

Harry heard only one word. Portkeys. The field changed beneath him. Grass became stone. Afternoon became night. The brass shield boss in McGonagall's hand became a Triwizard Cup, its handle slippery beneath Harry's bloodied fingers.

Harry stopped breathing. No one near him had moved. His body moved anyway. One foot slid backwards. His hand closed around the mirror beneath his jacket. The scar across his forearm burned where rope had held it to the gravestone. He could smell yew smoke through the field's clean wind.

Cedric's hand on the Cup. The first journey and the desperate return blurred together: blood on the blue handle, the world turning inside out, stone appearing beneath his feet. Harry knew the cauldron had been there before the blood on his hands. Knowing the order did not make the pictures come back in it.

"Harry."

Remus did not touch him. He remained several feet away, leaving the distance visible.

"Tell me where the Cup is."

Harry stared at the shield boss.

"There isn't one."

"Good. What is there?"

"A Portkey."

"Describe it."

Harry swallowed. "Brass. Round. Scratched near the centre. McGonagall's holding it."

"Destination?"

Dumbledore answered before Harry could.

"The hospital wing. West aisle, beside the fourth window. The connection is direct and will activate only upon my count. There is no secondary destination and no keyed individual."

He had approached quietly. He stood far enough away not to crowd Harry, hands visible, the explanation complete. Harry dragged air into his lungs.

"Could someone change where it takes us after we've touched it? Voldemort, or one of them?"

Nobody flinched at the name.

"Not without breaking three anchors inside Hogwarts and the ward Professor Flitwick is currently holding," Dumbledore said. "If any anchor changes, the object will fail rather than redirect."

"Mine didn't take me anywhere on the train. They broke."

"They were damaged by a hostile curse," Dumbledore said. "This safeguard is different: the connection closes if the destination changes. Professor Flitwick has tested that response."

"Can I check?"

Dumbledore showed him the checking spell slowly, keeping his wand clear of the shield boss. Harry tried it with his own. The first attempt produced only a faint glow; his fingers were stiff and he had missed the last movement.

"Once more," Dumbledore said. "There is time."

Harry traced the three-point ward again. Golden lines appeared, each one travelling away from the Portkey and ending in a small image of Hogwarts. He waited for Flitwick, standing beside the departure point, to check the result as well. The professor nodded only after following each line with his wand. He looked at them until he could follow all three without losing track. Three anchors. One destination. No graveyard. Harry lowered his wand.

"Thank you," he said.

Dumbledore inclined his head as though Harry's request had been ordinary. Perhaps it should have been.

"You do not have to take it," Sirius said.

He had come to stand at Harry's left.

"We can wait for the field to clear and fly."

Harry looked towards Hermione and Draconia. Their stretchers belonged to the next medical group. Hermione was awake but too unsteady to walk. The healer had confirmed that Snape was preparing the counter-potion for Draconia's wound. The kit at Hogwarts was ready; she no longer needed to be diverted to St Mungo's. Waiting meant allowing them to disappear without him while he remained beside a broken train beneath Voldemort's Mark. His fear wanted to make the choice sound sensible.

"I'm going with them," he said.

Sirius nodded once. "Then so am I."

"Two accompanying passengers," the healer reminded them. "Mr Potter should be on that trip as a patient, in any case."

Remus lifted his cane. "I'll take the next group. Meet me beside that fourth window and you can tell me whether Dumbledore has counted it properly."

Harry looked at the white knuckles above the cane handle. Remus was trying to make waiting sound ordinary. Harry nodded, grateful for the attempt, and managed a small smile. The healers moved the four stretchers into a square. Draconia and Hermione lay side by side. A burned Hufflepuff girl occupied the third. The fourth held a Ravenclaw boy whose leg had been broken beneath a luggage rack. The brass shield boss was placed upon a low stand between them. Harry knelt at Draconia's side. Her eyes opened.

"You look ill," she said.

"You're not looking especially well yourself."

"I'm allowed. I've been lying down for hours."

Harry tried to smile. "I was hoping nobody would notice."

She followed his gaze to the Portkey. Understanding sharpened her face. Her hand emerged from beneath the blanket. Harry took it. Hermione reached across the gap between their stretchers and caught the cuff of Harry's other sleeve.

"We know where this one goes," she said.

"Apparently the fourth window."

"West aisle," Draconia added. "I was listening too." She shifted her fingers against his. "You won't have to check it alone."

The healer fastened the Portkey's transport ribbons to the four stretchers and checked each link. Harry touched the brass with his free hand. Sirius placed one hand upon his shoulder and the other upon the rim, leaving the healer room to take the final point. Dumbledore raised his wand.

"On three. One."

Harry looked at Draconia. Grey eyes. Pale hair. Blood beneath the bandage, not upon stone.

"Two."

Hermione's fingers tightened around his sleeve. Sirius stood behind him.

"Three."

The hook caught Harry behind the navel. The world vanished. Wind seized him. His feet left the grass. For a fraction of a second there was no field, no hand in his and no proof that the destination remained the one promised. The graveyard opened beneath him.

Then stone struck his knees. Harry released the Portkey and kept Draconia's hand. White curtains moved in warm air. Beyond the fourth window, afternoon light lay across the Scottish mountains. The lake shone between their lower slopes, so still that Harry stared at it, expecting the surface to tilt with the carriage floor. Somebody's stretcher wheel squeaked against the stone. He flinched. Sirius's hand stayed on his shoulder without tightening.

Hogwarts. No cauldron. No Voldemort. Hermione still held his sleeve, and Sirius's hand remained upon his shoulder. Harry bent forward and breathed against the side of Draconia's stretcher until the room stopped turning. Madam Pomfrey arrived like a weather system.

"Mr Potter, sit down before you fall down. Miss Granger's writing materials go on the cabinet. Keep the field dressing on Miss Malfoy until the neutralising wash is ready—I don't want that curse disturbed."

The attending healer opened her mouth. Madam Pomfrey did not permit the answer.

"Curtains four through seven. Clear the tables. Black-curse kit, concussion draught and three measures of Murtlap. Mr Black, if you obstruct the aisle, I will treat you as furniture."

Sirius moved. Harry did not. Pomfrey looked down at him. Her expression changed by less than an inch.

"Did the Portkey cause pain?"

"No."

"Nausea?"

"Not exactly."

"Graveyard?"

Harry looked at her. Madam Pomfrey had treated him after the Third Task. She had seen him arrive covered in blood and dark magic, heard him wake shouting beneath curtains he could not remember. Of course she knew.

"A bit," he said.

"Sit upon the nearest bed before your legs make the decision for you."

Harry sat. The hospital wing filled around them. More Portkeys arrived in bursts of displaced air. Stretchers appeared between beds. Healers crossed the polished floor. Curtains opened and closed. The familiar scent of dittany, clean linen and potion steam thickened until Harry could no longer smell smoke upon his clothes. The castle received its students through the hospital wing.

Not through the oak doors beneath floating candles. Not across the black lake in small boats while Hogwarts rose from the mountain like a promise. First-years arrived with blood on their robes and treatment tags tied around their wrists. Harry watched one small boy stare at the ceiling as if waiting for it to break.

Hogwarts had been Harry's escape at eleven. For these children, it began as somewhere the wounded were counted. He hated the attackers for that almost as much as he hated them for the train.

Severus Snape entered the hospital wing carrying a case of black glass bottles. His robes were fastened incorrectly. Harry noticed because Snape never did anything incorrectly in public. One hook near his throat had missed its loop, leaving the collar lower on one side. His hair was damp around the temples. Whether he had run from the dungeons or merely walked with greater urgency than dignity allowed, Harry could not tell. Snape saw Draconia. He stopped. The pause lasted no longer than a breath. Then his face became unreadable again.

"Blood-Binding Curse?"

Madam Pomfrey cut away the field bandage. Black light traced the wound beneath Draconia's ribs, pulsing with each beat of her heart.

"Modified," she said. "The curse renews the opening whenever clotting begins."

Snape placed the case beside her bed.

"When was she struck?"

Harry answered. "Before carriage nine came off. We waited thirty-seven minutes after the Ministry arrived, and she was hit well before that. I don't know exactly."

Snape looked at the field healer's notes, then at him. "Was she bleeding at the same rate throughout?"

"The field healer slowed it down. Before that, the pads kept soaking through."

"Then tell me what happened between the injury and the first dressing. That will be more useful than a guess."

Harry heard accusation. Perhaps none had been intended.

"She was hit after we restored carriage nine. Before it derailed. Hermione's Patronus had just reached us. We fought two boarders. I put gauze against it before that. We had to fly from nine when the coupling broke, and Hermione changed the dressing after we got her out of six."

Snape's hand had gone still upon the bottles.

"Carriage nine derailed."

"After the students left."

"You were upon it?"

"Not when it hit the ground."

Sirius made a strangled sound from the next bed. Harry ignored him. Snape looked at Draconia.

"Did she lose consciousness before the Stunning Spell?"

"Nearly. Once, while the last children were leaving nine. Her knees went and I had to hold her up."

Draconia opened one eye. "I remember that. I didn't quite pass out."

The sound Snape released might have been relief, though he disguised it as irritation immediately.

"And you continued casting," Snape said, glancing at the notes.

"There were still children in the carriage."

His hand paused over the case. "You have finished that task, Miss Malfoy. Let us do ours."

Snape selected three bottles. Clear. Silver. A red so dark it appeared black until held to the light.

"This curse was used during the first war to exhaust targets whom the caster wished to keep conscious," he said. "Untreated, this modification can renew blood loss faster than a standard replenishing dose can replace it. The field dressing suppressed it, but could not remove it. We must neutralise the curse before closing the wound."

Harry looked at the black pulse.

"Can you do it?"

Snape's gaze sharpened.

"Yes."

One word. No comfort added. It was enough. Madam Pomfrey mixed the silver potion with two drops from the red bottle. Smoke rose from the basin. Snape added the clear liquid one measure at a time while the steam changed from grey to white. Draconia watched his hands.

"Did you know the caster?" she asked.

Snape did not look up. "No."

"He knew my name."

The glass stopper clicked against the bottle.

"Many do."

"He said Father chose badly."

Snape's face remained lowered above the potion.

"Then he considered loyalty to a murderer a more intelligent choice. His judgement may be disregarded."

Draconia was silent. Harry watched Snape's hand pause over the stopper. He had recognised the curse immediately. That did not mean he knew the man, but Harry could not forget how familiar its name had sounded in his mouth. Then Snape asked Draconia whether the pressure against her side was worse than before, and Harry let the question go. There would be time to wonder about him when she was no longer bleeding. Snape dipped a strip of gauze into the finished potion.

"This will hurt."

"How much?" Draconia asked.

"I'm afraid it will be sharp. It should ease when the black residue lifts." He looked at Pomfrey. "If it does not, tell us. Don't try to endure something we need to know about."

Harry moved closer.

Draconia glanced at him. "You don't need to watch them do it."

"I'm watching you," Harry said. "Unless you'd rather I moved."

He sat beside her and offered his hand. She took it. Snape laid the potion-soaked gauze over the wound. Draconia's entire body arched. Her fingers crushed Harry's, and he nearly pulled back before he understood what he was doing.

He held still. The smell of the wash was sharp enough to sting his nose; a bead of it ran down the side of the basin and smoked on the cloth beneath. Madam Pomfrey asked Draconia to breathe out slowly. She tried, lost the breath halfway through and tried again. Harry wanted to tell them to stop. He could see Snape counting drops with absolute care. Stopping now would leave the curse in her. Knowing that did not make it easier to watch.

Black light rose through the gauze in twisting threads. Madam Pomfrey held the dressing in place while Snape spoke the counter-curse. His voice remained level. Draconia did not scream. The sound caught behind her teeth and became a broken breath instead. Harry looked only at her face.

"You can insult me," he said.

Her eyes watered. "I require...concentration."

"You've never needed it before."

Her nails cut his palm.

"Your hair," she managed, "is appalling."

"There you are."

Her grip tightened as Snape laid another drop along the dressing. Harry kept his hand still and listened when Pomfrey told her to breathe out.

"The jacket," Draconia whispered, when she could speak again. "Don't let Winky throw it away."

"Wasn't going to."

She nodded, and he knew the small ordinary promise had helped her hold on. The black threads snapped. Draconia cried out once. Then the light vanished. The wound beneath the gauze became only a wound.

Madam Pomfrey immediately poured a blue potion along its edge. Skin began drawing together in a thin pink line. Harry realised he had stopped breathing again. Snape sat back. Sweat shone at his temple.

"The curse is neutralised," he said.

Draconia's hand loosened around Harry's.

"Thank you, Professor."

Snape closed the potion case.

"Do not thank me until you have obeyed Madam Pomfrey for at least twelve consecutive hours."

"An unreasonable condition."

"Then consider us even."

His gaze moved to Harry's hand. Four crescent cuts marked the skin where Draconia's nails had broken it.

"Potter."

"They're small."

"Let Madam Pomfrey clean those before you put your hand near another dressing."

Harry looked at Draconia. Colour had begun returning to her face.

"Worth it," he said.

Snape followed his gaze. Whatever reply he had prepared did not arrive. He fastened the missed hook at his collar and left the hospital wing without deducting a single point. Harry found that more alarming than the curse.

By late afternoon, the arrivals had slowed. The hospital doors opened to admit a house-elf carrying clean uniforms instead of another stretcher. Harry watched him unload the basket, carefully laying each pile of clothes beside the correct bed, and had to stop himself asking whether the little Ravenclaw shirt meant its owner had got back too.

Remus arrived with Harry's trunk and the two owl cages. Hedwig refused to settle until he opened her cage; she flew to Harry's bedpost and stayed there, puffed up and indignant. Hermione's books had been recovered with her trunk, although one had lost its cover. The luggage van had been searched separately. Harry heard a teacher reassure a child that losing their school things would not cost their family another set.

The Sorting had been delayed until half past nine, after the healers finished examining the first-years. Those already cleared were waiting in an empty classroom with the Heads of House. Harry imagined the little boy he had seen beneath the Portkey sitting there, staring at another unfamiliar ceiling, and wished somebody would let him call home before asking him to put on the Hat.

Hermione sat propped against three pillows with a monitoring charm glowing faintly at her wrist. Whenever she reached for her notebook, the charm flashed blue and sounded a small warning chime. Madam Pomfrey had explained that dizziness might return before Hermione noticed it herself. Hermione had agreed to leave the book alone; she had not agreed to like the reminder. She had tested it four times.

"You are strengthening it," Draconia said.

"I'm finding out what sets it off."

"Reaching for that notebook. You've tried four times. I think we've established that."

"You tried to stand twenty minutes ago."

"I required water."

"There was a glass beside you."

"It was empty."

Hermione glanced at the half-full glass beside her. Draconia looked away. "It was nearly empty."

Harry lay in the bed between them. Madam Pomfrey had assigned it after a more thorough examination found two hairline rib fractures beneath the bruising. The field healer had checked him twice without finding a break; Pomfrey explained that the fine cracks were easier to see now that the swelling had been treated. Harry wished knowing exactly what was wrong made it hurt less. His shoulder had been strapped. The cut across his cheek was closed, his scar had stopped bleeding and the potion beside his hand promised six hours of dreamless sleep in letters large enough to constitute a threat. He had no intention of drinking it. Sirius occupied the chair at the foot of his bed.

He had been cleaned, examined and bandaged after considerable argument. Someone had found him another coat. It was brown, too short in the sleeves and belonged to a Ministry clerk who had not been consulted. Remus sat beside the fourth window with his cane against the wall and a cup of tea growing cold between his hands. None of them had eaten since reaching the castle. Madam Pomfrey noticed.

A tray appeared upon Harry's blankets. Soup, bread, sliced apple and a cup of water arranged themselves beneath his nose. Identical trays landed before Hermione and Draconia. A fourth struck Sirius lightly in the knees. Remus accepted his before the magic could become forceful.

"I am not hungry," Harry said.

Four people looked at him.

"I was informing the soup."

"It remains unconvinced," Remus said.

Harry tore a piece of bread. Beside the tray lay his mirror, its frame scorched and its lower corner cracked. Professor Flitwick had repaired the glass while Harry's shoulder was being examined. The linking charm had survived, he said, but the grounding field had smothered the signal inside the train.

"You got the first message?" Harry asked Sirius.

"Every word. Remus contacted Kingsley while I got the bike out."

"I didn't know if—"

"I know." Sirius leaned forward. "I tried to answer. Couldn't get through."

Harry traced the unbroken corner with one finger. Sirius had heard him. Somebody had been coming even when neither exit worked and the only voice left inside his head was the wrong one. His stomach had closed sometime between the first whistle and the bridge. Food possessed texture without taste. He chewed because everyone was watching and swallowed because Draconia had already lifted her spoon despite the pain caused by sitting upright. Hermione ate three mouthfuls before attempting to reach her notebook with the other hand. The warning chime sounded. Harry smiled into the soup.

"Cruel," she said.

"I didn't invent it."

"You are enjoying it."

"A little. You look so offended by it."

Draconia lifted her glass. "Give her a day. She'll find a way round it."

The doors opened. Narcissa entered first. She crossed the ward quickly, her posture as straight as ever, but Harry could see the strain in the hands she kept rigid at her sides. She had to pass three curtained beds before reaching Draconia, and her eyes checked every gap between them.

Lucius followed half a step behind. His travelling cloak had been fastened over evening robes. The silver clasp sat crooked beneath his throat. Harry had never seen Lucius Malfoy wear anything crooked. Draconia put down her glass.

"Mother. Where were you?"

"At the perimeter first. Then waiting downstairs while they treated you." Narcissa's voice stayed level with effort. "They told us you were stable. Professor McGonagall kept sending updates. I wanted to hear it from you."

Narcissa reached the bed. Her gaze moved from Draconia's face to the potion line at her wrist, the dressing beneath her ribs and the black residue collected inside a sealed vial upon Madam Pomfrey's table. She touched nothing until she had counted every sign of injury. Then she placed one hand against her daughter's cheek. Draconia leaned into it.

The movement was small. It broke something in Narcissa. Not visibly. Her expression remained composed. Her shoulders stayed straight. Only her thumb changed, passing once beneath Draconia's eye as if removing something which was not there.

"You were meant to call when you arrived," Narcissa said.

"The train was delayed."

Narcissa laughed once. The sound trembled. She bent and kissed Draconia's forehead. Lucius stood on the other side of the bed. He looked at his daughter for a long time before speaking.

"Who?"

The question contained no uncertainty about what he intended after receiving an answer.

"I did not recognise him," Draconia said.

"Describe him."

"The man who spoke to me had brown hair. A narrow face. Younger than Professor Snape, I think. He knew my surname. The rider who cut me was still masked."

Lucius's eyes moved to the black residue.

"What did he say?"

Draconia glanced towards Harry. He understood that she was deciding whether to soften it. She did not.

"That you chose badly."

Lucius became still. A trolley wheel squeaked outside the curtains. Lucius watched a healer carry a stained sheet towards the linen room, then looked back at his daughter. Harry could not read his expression, but he saw how carefully Lucius kept his hands still.

"I see," he said.

Draconia watched his face.

"Do not."

His gaze returned to her.

"Do not what?"

"Decide this happened because you left them."

Lucius's jaw tightened. "They know you because I kept those people in our lives throughout your childhood."

"No."

"Draconia—"

"You did not cast the curse. You did not board the train. You did not make him choose a child because her father embarrassed his master."

Her voice shook on the final word. Harry had heard the same argument in a different shape throughout his life. If he had not survived, Voldemort would not have come. If he had refused the Tournament more effectively, Cedric would not have reached the graveyard. If he had not been upon the train, nobody would have attacked it. Guilt rearranged violence until the victim appeared at its centre. Lucius knew that when speaking to Harry. He did not know it for himself.

"She is right," Harry said.

Lucius looked at him.

"They demanded I surrender. I didn't. That doesn't make everyone they hurt my fault."

The sentence felt false inside Harry's mouth. He said it anyway. Lucius seemed to hear the effort.

"No," he said. "It does not."

"Same rule."

Silence held between them. Then Lucius nodded. Not agreement completed. Agreement attempted. Draconia reached for his hand. Lucius took it.

"I don't want you to go back," she said. "Even if they say it would keep me safe."

Pain crossed his face. Pride followed it.

"I am not going back," Lucius said. Then, after a moment, "Though you have acquired some exceptionally Gryffindor habits."

"I have been exposed to poor influences."

Harry raised his spoon. "You're welcome."

Lucius looked towards him properly for the first time. He saw the bandaged shoulder, the cut upon Harry's face, the sling and the blood still embedded in the seams of his jacket upon the chair.

"You stayed with her."

"Mostly she stayed with me."

"Potter caught me when I fell from the train," Draconia said.

Harry stared at her.

"You could have omitted that."

Narcissa's hand stopped upon her daughter's hair.

"Fell from the train."

"Only partly," Harry said.

"There are degrees?" Sirius asked.

"She was holding the roof."

Narcissa turned her face towards Harry. He had seen that expression directed at Ministry officials, reporters and one shopkeeper who implied Draconia's school robes required less expensive fabric. Harry tried to retreat into his pillows. His ribs objected.

"I pulled her back," he said.

Narcissa crossed the space between the beds. Harry expected an interrogation. Instead, Narcissa leaned down and embraced him, taking care of the strapped shoulder. For a moment he kept his good hand on the blanket, uncertain whether she meant him to hug her back. Then her palm settled behind his head, and he let himself lean into her.

Her arms closed around him and one cool hand rested against the back of his head. She smelled of the floral perfume which lingered in Hawthorn's hallway and smoke from the emergency Floo. Harry kept looking over her shoulder at Draconia until Narcissa eased back far enough to let him see her properly.

"She's here," she said quietly.

Harry nodded. He could still feel Draconia's wrist slipping against his palm on the roof. Narcissa did not ask him to explain why he was shaking.

"Thank you," she whispered.

Harry did not know where to place his hands. He settled one against her back.

"She saved me too."

"I know. She told me."

"Several times. Hermione too—"

"We'll thank her as well." Narcissa eased back enough to look at him. "You don't have to make your part smaller, Harry."

His name in her voice ended the list. Narcissa drew away. Her eyes were bright, but nothing fell from them. Her fingers paused at his throat, where she would usually straighten a collar. Finding only the loose neck of the hospital shirt, she adjusted his blanket instead.

"You have not finished your soup."

Harry looked at Sirius. "Does everyone receive the same training?"

"It's a conspiracy."

Lucius picked up the bread from Harry's tray and placed it in his hand.

"Eat more."

Harry laughed. The sound startled all of them. For a moment, he did not have to force it.

The Grangers appeared inside a standing mirror shortly before five. Professor McGonagall carried it into the hospital wing herself. The tall oval frame came from the visitors' room, where Professor Flitwick had linked it to a second mirror in London. The school had used the Grangers' emergency contact instructions as soon as the attack was reported. An escort had found them at King's Cross and taken them somewhere private to wait.

McGonagall explained this while setting the mirror down. Harry watched Hermione's shoulders loosen before her parents even appeared. They had been contacted. They had not spent the whole afternoon wondering why no one would tell them where she was. Mrs Granger stood on the other side with one hand over her mouth. Mr Granger was still wearing the coat he had worn to King's Cross. They had not gone home. Hermione sat straighter. The monitoring charm chimed as she sat up too quickly. Hermione waited for the dizziness to pass, then tried again more slowly.

"Mum. Dad."

Mrs Granger touched the glass. Hermione raised her hand to meet it. Their palms aligned upon opposite sides of a surface neither could cross.

"Are you hurt?" Mr Granger asked.

"A concussion. The healer says it is mild."

"You were unconscious."

Hermione glanced towards Harry.

"Briefly."

"How briefly?"

"I'm not sure of the exact time—"

"She woke soon after we stopped," Harry said. "The healers checked her straight away."

Hermione looked at him.

"They can give you the times," he added. "I can't."

"Thank you, Harry," Mr Granger said.

Hermione looked away, embarrassed at how readily Harry had supplied the part she wanted to get past. He understood wanting her parents to worry less. He also knew they would worry more if they thought she was hiding things. Mrs Granger examined every inch of her daughter's face visible through the mirror.

"Can we come there?"

McGonagall stepped beside the frame. "The castle wards do not ordinarily admit Muggle visitors by emergency Floo. Professor Flitwick is establishing a route to Hogsmeade, after which I will escort you personally. It may take an hour."

"We'll wait," Mrs Granger said.

The answer came before the explanation finished. Mr Granger had not taken his eyes off his daughter's bandage. He kept rubbing one hand over the other, the same movement he made while drying them before dinner at home, except there was no towel and no reason to stop. Hermione swallowed.

"It's a long way," Hermione said, then heard herself and shook her head. "I do want you here. I just didn't know if they'd let you come."

Mr Granger stared at her.

"You were attacked by masked terrorists on a school train."

The word changed the room. Terrorists. No euphemism. No faction, incident, disruption or disputed return. A plain Muggle word for people who used fear and violence against civilians to achieve power. McGonagall's hand tightened upon the frame.

"Yes," she said. "She was."

Hermione's eyes filled.

"I'm sorry."

"For what?" her mother asked.

"We knew there might be danger. We didn't know this, but we knew enough that you might have—"

"Kept you home?"

Hermione nodded. Mrs Granger lowered her hand from the glass.

"We may still ask you to come home."

Hermione's face tightened.

"Mum—"

"Ask," Mrs Granger repeated. "Not order. Not tonight. Tonight we want to see you. Tomorrow, when you have slept and we can sit in the same room, we will talk about what staying means."

Mr Granger leaned closer to the mirror.

"We'll listen to you," her father said. "But don't decide you have to stay just because your friends are staying. We need to talk about what will actually keep you safe."

Hermione looked down. Harry recognised the conflict in her. Harry thought she would want to remain. He wanted her to remain too, and felt ashamed of how quickly that wish came before anything else. Her parents were asking her to think beyond tonight, when being together still felt like the only answer that mattered.

"All right," she whispered.

Mrs Granger placed her palm against the glass again.

"We love you."

Hermione's hand rose.

"Love you too."

The mirror remained open while Professor Flitwick worked on the route. Mr and Mrs Granger sat within its reflected room. They asked Draconia about her wound, thanked Harry for staying with Hermione and listened while Sirius described the attack without softening it into something safe enough for parents. Nobody told them not to worry.

After a while Mrs Granger asked whether Hermione's slippers had reached the castle. Hermione said they were in her trunk. It was such a small, familiar question that her face crumpled before she could stop it. She pressed her free hand over her mouth and nodded again. Her mother stayed by the glass. Harry looked down at his blanket to give them what little privacy the mirror allowed. He understood wanting to ask about slippers when the real question was whether your child would still be there when you arrived.

Ron arrived while the first-years were still waiting to be Sorted. Downstairs, Harry could hear faint voices from the entrance hall as students went into the Great Hall for the sandwiches and soup the kitchens had sent up. Ron carried three boxes. The uppermost was labelled GRYFFINDOR—CARRIAGES 1–4. The second bore Ravenclaw blue. The third had no label and leaked parchment from one corner. He set all three beside Hermione's bed. The monitoring charm flashed when she reached for the uppermost box.

"How many?" she asked.

"Statements? One hundred and six. Complaints about the statement process? Twenty-eight. Drawings from first-years who couldn't describe the masks? Eleven. One recipe for treacle tart which Dennis Creevey swears was inside his pocket already."

Hermione stared at the boxes.

"One hundred and six? Already?"

Ron stared back.

"We started three hours ago."

"There were eight hundred and twelve students."

"Some of them are eating."

"I know. I just don't want them to forget—"

"They can write tomorrow," Ron said. "Nobody's refusing them a turn."

Draconia's eyes opened. "Weasley is correct."

Ron looked around as though expecting another person.

"I'll try to remember you said that."

"Only that one thing. Don't become ambitious."

He pulled a folded packet from beneath his robes and handed it to Harry.

"Those are from the corridor where we fought. I marked the ones who saw you after the girls went down."

Harry accepted it. The paper felt heavier than it should.

"How many?"

"Seven."

"Only seven?"

"Most people had the good sense to hide."

Harry opened the packet. The first statement belonged to a third-year Gryffindor. The handwriting shook across the page.

Potter put a shield in front of us. Something hit it and the windows broke. Later I saw a green curse go through the next carriage after he ducked. He told us to stay down. When Malfoy and Granger were hurt, the glass started floating. I thought he was going to kill the man. He didn't.

Harry closed it. Ron watched him.

"They aren't accusing you."

"It sounds like they should."

"Why?"

"I forgot they were behind me."

"For a few seconds."

"That is enough time."

"Yeah," Ron said.

Harry had expected disagreement. Ron sat upon the foot of the empty bed opposite.

"It is," he continued. "That's why I wrote it down."

Harry looked at him.

"You're not meant to agree when I say something awful about myself."

"Hermione can tell you when you're wrong. I'm talking about what I saw."

"I can hear you," Hermione said.

"I know," Ron said, turning red. "That wasn't an insult."

The warning chime sounded again when Hermione reached for the packet. Ron returned his attention to Harry.

"You scared me," he said.

No accusation. No admiration either. Harry looked at the witness packet in his hands.

"I scared me too."

"Good."

"That seems rude."

"If you'd enjoyed it, we'd have a bigger problem."

Harry remembered the attacker raising his hands. The glass suspended in the air. The thought which had entered his mind in a voice too close to Voldemort's.

Show them what happens.

"Part of me did," he said.

The confession left quietly. Sirius's chair stood empty. He and Remus had gone with McGonagall to meet the Grangers at Hogsmeade. The Malfoys were speaking to Dumbledore outside the ward. Hermione and Draconia remained close enough to hear, but neither interrupted. Ron rubbed a thumb against a burn in the borrowed wand he had captured.

"I know it's nothing like this," Ron said, looking down at the borrowed wand. "But Fred and George locked Percy in a wardrobe once, and I left him there. Two hours. I knew where he was."

Harry blinked. "What?"

"I could've opened it. Didn't. He'd spent all morning telling me I was going to fail Potions. Hearing him shout through the door was brilliant."

"This comparison has become insulting."

"Wait. Mum came home. Fred and George ran. I opened the wardrobe and Percy thanked me."

Ron looked down at the wand.

"I liked having him need me to open it." Ron rubbed a thumb over the wand's burned handle. "I'm not proud of that. I just mean wanting to hurt someone isn't the same as wanting it all the time, or having to do it."

"I was going to do worse than lock someone in a wardrobe."

"Probably."

"Again, you're bad at this."

"I stopped you."

Harry had no answer.

Ron shrugged one shoulder. "I don't know what you need to do about it. Remus might." Ron shifted on the bed. "But if it happens while I'm there, I'll say something. You'll have to listen."

"That isn't all."

Ron looked uncomfortable. "Fine. I'm saying I'll be there. If I can."

Harry looked up. Ron picked a flake of soot from the captured wand, then seemed to remember it was somebody else's and put it on the bedside table for Sirius to hand over. His ears had gone pink. The promise was awkward and unfinished. Harry believed it more easily for that, and turned the packet over.

"Thank you."

Ron became deeply interested in the cardboard boxes.

"For the statements?"

"For my jacket."

"Right."

"And the people trying to kill me."

"Bit harder."

"I noticed."

"Your aim's decent."

"Generous."

"Don't tell anyone."

Harry held out his hand. Ron looked at it. Then shook. His grip was warm, firm and slightly awkward. The gesture did not rewrite four years. It did not place Ron inside every memory the trio had made without him, or turn all their awkward beginnings into something easy. Harry would still need time to learn when Ron was joking and when he meant a thing. For once, he wanted that time. It marked one carriage. One fight. One person Harry would trust behind him when the next curse came. That was enough. For now.

"Potter," Draconia said.

Harry released Ron's hand.

"What?"

"Your soup."

"It's cold."

"A tragedy."

Ron stood. "I'll ask the elves to send toast."

"With butter," Hermione said.

"And tea," Draconia added.

Harry looked between them. "Was friendship always dietary supervision?"

Ron reached the door.

"Around you? Apparently."

He left before Harry could throw the bread. Umbridge entered before the toast arrived. She brought four Ministry clerks, two Hit Wizards and a parchment bearing enough seals to require its own table. Madam Pomfrey met them at the doors.

"No."

Umbridge smiled. "I have not yet stated my purpose."

"You have brought six people into a ward containing concussion patients. Your purpose is already too loud."

"This is a matter of student safety."

"So is sleep. Return tomorrow."

Umbridge unfolded the sealed parchment.

"Under the authority granted to the Special Commissioner for Educational Standards, and in cooperation with the Department of Magical Law Enforcement, I am required to secure unauthorised witness material which may obstruct the investigation into today's transport incident."

"Attack," Harry said.

Umbridge's gaze found him between the beds.

"You should be resting, Mr Potter."

"I was hoping to rest after it."

Madam Pomfrey's mouth tightened. Harry thought it might have been approval. Umbridge crossed the ward. The clerks followed. The Hit Wizards remained near the doors, hands visible but close to their wands. Hermione looked at the boxes beside her bed.

The blue monitoring charm flashed before she could sit forward. Draconia's fingers shifted beneath her blanket. Harry knew the gesture. Her wand had been placed inside the bedside cabinet after treatment. She was calculating the distance. He reached for the bread on his tray instead.

Not because he felt calm. Because six Ministry employees had entered a hospital to take pieces of paper from injured children, and Harry suspected that hexing them would be described as proof the children required supervision. Umbridge stopped beside Hermione's bed.

"I understand you have encouraged students to produce accounts outside the formal process."

Hermione settled more upright, keeping her hands on the blanket until the brief dizziness passed. The blue charm stayed quiet.

"I asked students to record what they remembered before discussing it with other witnesses."

"Without Ministry guidance."

"Before Ministry guidance."

"The distinction is immaterial."

"It matters which came first," Hermione said. "You can see what they wrote before anyone told them what to call it."

The nearest clerk examined the ceiling. Umbridge touched the uppermost box. Harry's wand entered his hand. He did not remember drawing it. The Hit Wizards moved. Sirius's voice came from the doors.

"Harry. Put it down."

Everyone turned. He stood beside Remus with both hands empty and his borrowed coat still too short. Mr and Mrs Granger were between them, windblown from the walk through Hogsmeade. Professor McGonagall stood behind the group. Lucius and Narcissa had returned with Dumbledore. For once, the adults had arrived before Harry cast. Sirius walked into the ward.

"Harry," he said.

Harry looked at the wand in his hand. He lowered it. Not because Umbridge deserved trust. Because Sirius had asked with only his name. Umbridge watched the exchange. Harry knew she would remember it.

"Mr Black," she said, "your guardianship does not extend to Ministry evidence."

"It extends to Harry. You do not interview him, examine him or use his hospital bed as a convenient place to provoke him without my consent and his."

"Mr Potter is a material witness to an ongoing investigation."

"Then send an appointment."

"Delay may compromise recollection."

"Your people spent an hour labelling everyone who mentioned the Dark Mark as influenced testimony. Their recollection appears safest away from you."

The Hit Wizard on the left looked at the floor. Umbridge turned towards Dumbledore.

"Headmaster, this interference is precisely why the Minister authorised Commissioner oversight."

"Sirius is exercising a legal right confirmed by the Wizengamot," Dumbledore replied. "Mr Potter has received medical treatment, endured a traumatic transport and has not slept. An interview tonight would be both clinically unsound and evidentially poor."

"And the boxes?"

Hermione's mother crossed the room. She stopped beside her daughter's bed and looked at Umbridge. Her voice was quieter than Harry expected, but Hermione's hand tightened on the blanket when she heard it.

"Those statements belong to the children who wrote them."

"Madam, magical evidence procedures are necessarily complex."

"Then explain them."

Umbridge blinked.

Mrs Granger continued. "Start with the safeguarding basis for removing a child's written account without their parent or guardian present. Then explain who will retain it, who may copy it, whether it can be altered by magic and how the child obtains an independent record of the original."

Mr Granger stood beside her.

"After that, perhaps explain why your investigators did not provide the same protections when interviewing injured children in a field."

The hospital wing had become very quiet. Hermione stared at her parents. Harry recognised the expression. Pride so large it became embarrassing. Umbridge recovered.

"Muggle procedure does not determine Ministry jurisdiction."

Lucius moved from beside Draconia's bed.

"No," he said. "The Magical Evidence Preservation Act does. Section fourteen requires an unaltered duplicate to remain with any witness under seventeen when a written account is removed before formal deposition. Section twenty-two prohibits the seizure of privileged correspondence between a student and their recognised educational adviser."

Umbridge looked at the boxes.

"Miss Granger is not an educational adviser."

"We gave copies to the Heads of House," Draconia said. "They're the educational advisers. The pupils kept their own accounts."

"You were not authorised to conduct an investigation."

"We counted people and asked whether they wanted to write down what they'd seen. We didn't question prisoners or decide who was guilty."

"Every page concerns the attack."

"They'd just escaped from it. What else did you expect them to write about?"

Harry bit the inside of his cheek. Sirius did not bother. He smiled openly. Umbridge's gaze passed over all of them. Hermione in bed. Draconia beside her. Harry with his wand lowered but not returned to its holster. Two sets of parents. A lawful guardian. Lucius Malfoy quoting legislation. Dumbledore refusing to rescue her from the conversation. Her smile returned. That frightened Harry more than anger would have.

"Very well," she said. "The Ministry will issue individual preservation notices tomorrow. Until then, the material will remain sealed in the Headmaster's office."

"No," Hermione said.

Umbridge turned. Hermione's face had gone pale beneath the bandage, but her voice remained clear.

"The students who finished an account kept the original. These are copies. We gave another set to the Heads of House."

One of the clerks stared at her.

"You produced three copies in the field?"

"Copying Quills," Hermione replied. "There were six among the Ravenclaws."

Draconia looked towards the boxes. "I instructed Pansy to distribute the originals after they were numbered."

"And I told the Gryffindors not to sign anything they didn't read," Ron said from the doorway.

He had returned with toast and stopped behind the Hit Wizards. Umbridge faced him. Ron lifted the tray slightly.

"Dietary supervision."

Harry looked down before his expression betrayed him. Umbridge's fingers tightened upon the sealed parchment.

"You have distributed unverified accusations throughout the school."

"We distributed descriptions," Hermione said. "Verification comes next."

"Through whom?"

"Their teachers can compare them with the escort's report. The students can explain what they meant." Hermione took a breath. "Taking the pages away won't make them more accurate."

Her voice wavered on the last word. She looked towards her mother, who put a hand over hers without taking the answer away from her. Umbridge stepped closer.

"Truth is not determined by the number of frightened children repeating the same claim."

Harry remembered the field. The table marked POTENTIALLY INFLUENCED. The Ministry officials deciding fear invalidated the people who had been given reason to feel it.

"No," he said. "It is determined by what happened."

Umbridge looked at him.

"And what happened, Mr Potter?"

The question was a trap offered as an opportunity. Everyone in the room knew it. Harry considered the complete answer. Somebody had prepared an attack along the train's route and demanded him by name. Masked attackers had used the Dark Mark. They had used curses Snape recognised from the first war and called Lucius's defection a bad choice. They had demanded Harry by name. The Ministry had arrived after students saved one another and immediately begun controlling words. Too much truth gave Umbridge places to cut. Harry chose the part she could not rename.

"We came back," he said.

Her eyes narrowed.

"That is not what I asked."

"It's what happened."

Behind her, Ron set the toast upon a cabinet. Hermione let her hand rest on the blanket. Draconia watched Harry with a softness which made his face warm despite the room. Umbridge refolded the parchment.

"Beginning tomorrow, all student material concerning the attack will be subject to Commissioner review. No organised collection, comparison or distribution of accounts may occur without written approval. Owl post related to the incident may be delayed for safeguarding inspection. Any gathering of more than three students for the purpose of discussing disputed events must be registered with my office."

The rules arrived too late. Harry saw the realisation reach her one line at a time. The first accounts had already been collected and copied. There were still hundreds to take, and more work than Hermione could do from a hospital bed. But the pages Umbridge had come to seize were no longer the only record. Eight hundred children already knew which words the Ministry did not want spoken. Hermione smiled. It was not a kind smile.

"Of course, Commissioner."

Umbridge looked at her for several seconds. Then at Dumbledore.

"I will require an office with direct access to the Floo network, the student post registry and all staff timetables before breakfast."

"Your office has been prepared," Dumbledore said.

"Has it?"

"Professor Binns has graciously surrendered the room beside his."

McGonagall coughed. Umbridge's smile strained.

"How thoughtful."

"He did not appear to notice."

For the first time that evening, Dumbledore's eyes twinkled. The expression vanished when Umbridge handed him the sealed parchment.

"Commissioner Directive One," she said. "Effective immediately."

Dumbledore read it. His face revealed nothing. He passed it to McGonagall. Harry watched her eyes move down the page.

"Inspection of student correspondence," she said. "Registration of discussion groups. Prior approval for any publication distributed upon school grounds. Commissioner attendance at lessons involving defensive magic."

"Temporary measures," Umbridge replied, "until the present fear has been responsibly managed."

"Whose fear?" Harry asked.

Umbridge looked at him.

"All of ours, Mr Potter."

"Are you frightened of the people who attacked us, or of what we'll say about them?"

Her smile became almost gentle.

"Adults learn not to display every feeling which passes through them."

Harry thought of Narcissa crossing the room without running. Lucius accepting Draconia's hand. Sirius laughing because he had nearly cried. Remus leaving space around a frightened boy instead of pretending the fear did not exist.

"Then you should understand why frightened children might still know what they saw," he said.

Umbridge's expression hardened. She turned towards the doors. The clerks followed. The Hit Wizards went last. One glanced back at the boxes before leaving, not with suspicion but calculation. Madam Pomfrey shut the doors behind them.

"If anyone else enters my ward carrying legislation," she announced, "I will prescribe it a suppository."

Mr Granger blinked. Sirius sat down abruptly and covered his face with both hands. Laughter moved through the room. It hurt Harry's ribs. He laughed anyway.

Dumbledore remained after everyone else settled. The parents arranged themselves beside beds. Mrs Granger occupied Hermione's chair with one hand around her daughter's wrist. Mr Granger began reading the witness-copying charm as if it were a dental journal he intended to challenge. Narcissa sat beside Draconia and combed the tangles from her hair with her fingers. Lucius stood near the window speaking quietly to Remus. Ron distributed toast, accepted no credit and left for Gryffindor Tower with the prefect escort McGonagall had arranged for the late visitors. Sirius did not move from Harry's bedside. Dumbledore placed Commissioner Directive One upon the blanket. The Ministry seal glowed pink at the bottom.

"Can you stop it?" Harry asked.

Dumbledore did not answer immediately. He drew the chair from the next bed and sat so that they faced one another at the same height.

"Parts," he said. "Professor McGonagall and I can prevent direct interference with medical correspondence and private communications protected by guardian privilege. We may challenge the breadth of inspection. The Ministry possesses authority to review educational activity and school safety, particularly after an attack upon school transport."

"An attack they are pretending wasn't Voldemort."

"Yes."

"That seems convenient."

"It is."

Harry looked at the seal.

"You knew she could do this."

"I knew the office had been granted powers broad enough to attempt it. I did not know the train would provide a pretext on the first day."

"Did the attack help her?"

Sirius swore softly. Dumbledore did not evade the question.

"Politically, yes. Fear creates demand for authority. The Minister will argue that Hogwarts requires closer supervision because its students were attacked."

"Outside Hogwarts."

"The distinction will not trouble him."

"It troubles me."

"As it should."

Harry waited for Dumbledore to say they would handle it. Adults would meet. The Order would act. Harry should sleep and concentrate upon school while safer people decided how much he was allowed to know. Dumbledore folded his hands.

"Tomorrow morning, Professor McGonagall will provide you, Hermione and Draconia with a copy of every directive which concerns your testimony, communication or organised activity. Sirius will receive the guardian copies. If operational information must be withheld because revealing it would endanger another person, I will tell you that directly rather than inventing a reason connected to your health."

Harry looked at him.

"You remembered."

Pain moved through Dumbledore's face.

"I gave you my word. Today does not make it less important."

"What if we disagree?"

"Then we will talk about why. I won't promise to agree with you, but I won't ask you to accept an answer you haven't been allowed to understand."

Sirius leaned back in his chair.

"Decent start."

Dumbledore accepted the judgement. Harry touched the edge of the directive.

"Will she teach Defence?"

"Professor Merrythought agreed to return for one year after the man impersonating Professor Moody was exposed," Dumbledore said. "This afternoon, the Ministry suspended her appointment pending a review of age, curriculum and adherence to approved safety standards."

"Because she would teach spells," Hermione said.

Dumbledore looked towards her bed. "That is my assessment."

"Who replaces her?" Draconia asked.

"Commissioner Umbridge has offered to supervise Defence until a suitable appointment is confirmed."

Harry stared at him.

"She attacked our letters, stole my badge and called the train an incident. Now she teaches us how to survive it?"

"Yes."

"No."

The word left before Harry decided to say it. Dumbledore's eyes held his.

"Tell me what you mean by that."

Not reprimand. A question. Harry looked at Hermione's boxes. Remus's homemade Defence book waited inside his trunk. Three supplemental texts lay beside it. Cedric had returned because he refused to let the graveyard become the only thing younger students learnt from him. Hermione understood how to organise. Draconia understood how rules could be used from both sides. Harry knew how close a Killing Curse could pass, and how little a shield could do once it was cast.

"I don't know yet," he admitted.

Dumbledore nodded.

"Whatever you decide to do, talk to us before you take risks with it. You have had more than enough of those today."

Harry almost smiled.

"That sounds like permission."

"It is advice."

"That still sounds a little like permission."

Dumbledore's expression softened. "For thinking, Harry? You have never needed mine."

Dumbledore rose. At the doors, he stopped.

"Harry."

"Yes?"

"You reached Hogwarts alive because the escort, the rescuers and the students kept helping one another after the train's protections failed. Too many of you were left without an adult beside you. That should never have been required. Nor will I describe everything you did to survive it only as disobedience."

Harry could not answer. Dumbledore inclined his head to the room and left. The directive remained upon Harry's blanket. Pink seal. Black ink. Rules written after the people they intended to stop had already begun.

At half past nine, applause finally travelled up from the Great Hall. It reached the hospital wing faintly, softened by staircases and stone. Harry could not hear the names or tell which House was cheering. He tried to guess anyway, finding Gryffindor in the loudest burst because he wanted to imagine Ron and Neville at the table below.

Between cheers, the ward was quiet. Mrs Granger helped Hermione undo her tangled hair. Narcissa held a glass while Draconia drank. Harry watched Sirius turn the watch over in his hand, rubbing at the blood dried on its face, then put it away when he caught Harry looking. The first-years were being welcomed at last. Harry was glad for them. He was also glad he did not have to stand beneath the ceiling and pretend that arriving alive had made the day a good one.

By midnight, Madam Pomfrey had removed everyone who was not injured, employed by Hogwarts or legally entitled to remain. The distinction caused several arguments. Mr and Mrs Granger were given rooms beside Professor McGonagall's office after Hermione promised to summon them if her headache worsened. Narcissa and Lucius left only after Madam Pomfrey promised to send for them if Draconia's condition changed. McGonagall had found them rooms nearby as well. Narcissa tucked the blanket around her daughter twice before Lucius gently held out his hand. Remus went to speak with Dumbledore and the Aurors guarding the grounds. Sirius produced his guardianship papers from inside the borrowed coat. Madam Pomfrey read the first page.

"The chair remains upon the floor," she said.

"I wasn't going to levitate it."

"You were considering transfiguring it into a bed."

Sirius lowered his wand.

"Invasive."

"Sit."

He sat. The lamps dimmed one row at a time. White curtains enclosed the other patients but remained open around the three central beds. Madam Pomfrey claimed this allowed her to monitor concussion symptoms and dark-curse residue simultaneously. Harry suspected she had noticed that Hermione's pulse accelerated when Draconia disappeared behind fabric, while Draconia woke every time Harry's bed stopped creaking. The castle settled beyond the windows.

Pipes knocked inside the walls. Suits of armour changed watch with distant metallic complaints. Doors closed below as the last students returned to their Houses. Hogwarts possessed a night voice Harry remembered better than any lullaby. Usually, it made him feel safe. Tonight, every ordinary sound became the beginning of another whistle. Harry lay upon his back and watched moonlight move across the ceiling.

The cup of Dreamless Sleep remained untouched beside him. Sirius had fallen asleep in the chair with his arms folded and his chin against his chest. The position would hurt when he woke. His wand rested loose within one hand. Hermione's breathing came evenly from the bed to Harry's left. Draconia had not moved for several minutes. Harry closed his eyes. Green light crossed the lids. He opened them again.

"You should drink it," Draconia whispered.

Harry turned his head. She lay facing him. Moonlight caught the pale line of her cheek and the edge of the dressing visible beneath her nightshirt.

"You're awake."

"An exceptional observation."

"You took yours."

"Pain potion. Not Dreamless Sleep."

"Why?"

"I keep remembering what happened when I couldn't move." She glanced at her hand on the blanket. "I know the potion's different. I still don't like the thought."

Harry looked at the cup beside him.

"Fair."

"You?"

"Same reason. Different someone."

Draconia's fingers moved over the blanket.

"The Portkey?"

"Everything after it."

The graveyard did not need naming. It had lived between them since June, sometimes small enough to sit beneath breakfast conversation and sometimes large enough to fill every room. Harry looked towards Hermione. Her eyes were open.

"Were either of you actually asleep?"

"I was trying," Hermione said.

"You breathe differently when you're trying," Draconia told her.

"That makes no sense."

"You get very still, as though that will persuade the rest of you."

Hermione pushed herself higher against the pillows. The warning charm had gone quiet after midnight. Madam Pomfrey had removed Hermione's quill and put the notebook on the cabinet beyond her reach. Hermione kept glancing towards it, but for once she left it there.

"You frightened me today," Hermione said.

Harry looked at Draconia. "She fell from a train."

"You."

He became still. Draconia's attention sharpened.

"What happened?"

"After you were Stunned," Hermione said, "he stopped responding."

"I was fighting."

"You were chasing."

"They were still attacking us."

"One surrendered."

Harry stared at the ceiling. The hospital wing felt too warm.

"I didn't hurt him after he surrendered."

"You weren't listening when I said he'd stopped fighting."

Hermione did not soften the words. They hurt less because of it. Draconia pushed one elbow beneath herself. Pain crossed her face before she could hide it.

"Lie down," Harry said.

"Do not change the subject."

"You're reopening the wound."

"Madam Pomfrey closed it."

"That isn't how bodies work."

"Then help me move the pillow. Don't use my side to avoid telling us what happened."

Harry looked away. Draconia waited. Hermione did too. They had always been dangerously good at silence when working together.

"The glass came off the floor," Harry said at last.

Draconia's expression did not change.

"Accidental magic?"

"Maybe."

"You do not believe that."

"I was angry."

"Harry," Hermione said. "What aren't you saying?"

He thought of Dumbledore sitting at eye level and promising not to turn concern into secrecy. Harry had made no corresponding promise. The omission waited inside him, familiar and useful. If he told them, they would worry. If he remained silent, the voice might belong only to a moment of rage. If it belonged to Voldemort, silence gave it somewhere private to live. Harry touched his scar.

"I heard something."

Hermione's blanket rustled. Draconia went completely still.

"A voice?" she asked.

"Not exactly. A thought."

"Whose?"

"I don't know."

The answer sounded weak. Harry forced himself to continue.

"It said to show them. To show them what happens."

Hermione's face lost colour.

"The attacker?"

"No. Inside my head."

"Did it sound like Voldemort?"

The name disturbed Sirius even in sleep. His fingers tightened around his wand, then loosened. Harry lowered his voice.

"It felt like him. But I was angry enough that it could have been me."

"It could be both," Draconia said.

Hermione looked at her.

"He made Harry see things in the graveyard. Perhaps he tried again."

"We don't know that," Hermione said. "But we should tell someone who can help us find out."

Harry listened to them turn the worst moment of his day into a problem with edges. It helped. It also made the problem real.

"We should tell Sirius tonight," Hermione said. "Dumbledore and Remus can help us work it out in the morning."

"Remus can sit with Harry when he tells Dumbledore," Draconia replied. "We already agreed he should be there if they're going to discuss his mind."

"And I get to say it myself," Harry said.

"Yes," Hermione said. "We can help if you want us to. We don't have to answer for you."

Harry frowned. "Thank you for arranging my confession."

"You are welcome," Draconia said.

"I was being sarcastic."

"So was I."

Hermione looked at him. "Were you going to tell anyone?"

"Eventually."

"When?"

"After I knew whether it happened again."

"Absolutely not."

Her voice rose. Sirius shifted in the chair. All three froze. He settled again. Hermione continued in a whisper which lost none of its force.

"You don't wait for it to happen again before telling someone. Even if we don't know whether it was him."

"I didn't invite him."

"I know."

"Do you? Because everyone keeps talking like I left a door open."

The anger surprised him. Hermione's expression changed.

"That isn't what I meant."

"It sounded like it."

"Then I said it badly."

Harry looked at her. She did not defend the intention until the words stopped mattering. She let the mistake remain visible.

"I'm sorry," she said.

His anger had nowhere to push.

"All right."

Draconia lay back slowly.

"You were angry before you heard it," she said. "You were already angry. If it was him, that doesn't mean every feeling belonged to him. You still have to tell them what you remember."

"What if I wanted the same thing?"

"You did."

Harry flinched.

"Draco," Hermione warned.

"He wanted to hurt the person who hurt us. Pretending otherwise will not make him safer."

Draconia held Harry's gaze.

"What did you do?"

"I followed them."

"Then?"

"Ron stopped me."

"How?"

"He said you were behind us."

"And you stopped."

"Because he caught my jacket."

"Did he restrain you?"

"No."

"Take your wand?"

"No."

"Stun you?"

"You know he didn't."

"Then he reminded you who you were protecting, and you chose to return."

Harry looked down at his hands. The cuts from Draconia's nails had been sealed, leaving four faint pink crescents upon his palm.

"After I forgot."

"For a few seconds," Hermione said.

"You said that was enough."

"Enough to frighten me," Hermione said. "I'm still frightened. But I saw you protect the children too. We don't have to forget that to talk about the part that went wrong."

The room remained quiet. Harry tried to hold both truths at once. He had nearly crossed a line. He had come back before crossing it. One did not erase the other. Perhaps that was what healing always asked of him: not choosing the kinder version of memory, but learning to survive the complete one.

"When I fell from the roof," Draconia said, "I was not afraid you would let go."

Harry looked at her.

"You should've been. My shoulder was failing."

"I knew that."

"Comforting."

"I was afraid you would hold on after you could not."

He said nothing.

"You would have followed me over the edge," she continued. "Not deliberately. You would have decided releasing me was impossible and allowed the decision to take both of us."

Harry remembered the roof beneath his chest, her wrist inside his hands and the track turning beneath her body. The tether had been fraying under her weight. He had watched its strands part and tightened his grip because he could think of nothing else to do. He could not say she was wrong.

"What was I meant to do?"

"I don't know," she said. "I was hanging off the train. I didn't have a better idea either."

Harry looked at her.

"Then why—"

"Because you keep talking as though you wouldn't matter if you'd fallen after me. You do. You would."

The words landed harder than anger. Harry's chest tightened.

"I saved you."

"Yes."

"That should count for something."

"It matters more than I can say."

Draconia's voice broke. She turned her face towards the dark window. Harry had seen her frightened, furious and possessed. He had watched her survive the Chamber and wake with another person's memories inside her. He had almost never seen her lose control of her own voice.

"I just wanted you alive too," she said. "I was hanging there and I couldn't help you pull."

Harry left his bed. Pain caught beneath his ribs. He ignored it for three steps and regretted all three. By the time he reached Draconia, Hermione had thrown back her blankets.

"Stay there," Harry told her.

"You are walking with cracked ribs."

"I have more practice."

Draconia wiped angrily at one eye.

"Both of you are intolerable."

Harry sat on the edge of her bed.

"So I've heard."

"Go back."

"No."

"You require rest."

"Apparently I don't obey medical instructions."

"That stopped being charming years ago."

"You thought it was charming?"

Draconia looked at him. Harry smiled. It was a poor attempt. It worked. A wet laugh escaped her before she could stop it. She covered her face with one hand. Harry touched her wrist.

"I didn't follow you over."

"Not this time."

"I listened to Ron."

"Eventually."

"I'm told that is progress."

Draconia lowered her hand. Her composure had gone. Without it, she looked younger. Fifteen instead of a Malfoy daughter trained to make fear appear impolite.

"When the curse struck," she said, "I thought I would die before we reached Hermione."

Harry's hand tightened around her wrist.

"You didn't."

"I know that now."

"I thought you knew I'd get you out."

"I wanted to. That isn't the same thing."

The honesty hurt. Draconia looked towards Hermione.

"I thought Granger would wake and I would not be there. I thought Mother would receive a Ministry letter. I thought Father would believe choosing us killed me."

Her eyes returned to Harry.

"And I was frightened about you. Afterwards. If I wasn't there."

Harry's breath caught.

"You can't know what I'd do."

"No. I can't." She wiped her cheek again, impatient with the wetness. "I'm not saying you would. I'm saying I was scared. I still am."

He had been preparing to argue. Her answer left him with nothing to argue against. After the Chamber, when Hermione had lain Petrified and Draconia had been pulled into the dark, Hogwarts became Privet Drive by another name. After the graveyard, Harry had begun measuring his life by how much pain other people would avoid if he managed it correctly. If Draconia had died in his arms, something inside him would have interpreted survival as accusation.

"I don't want to stop," he said.

The words came quietly.

"I know."

"Sometimes I don't know how to keep going without making it everyone else's problem."

Hermione crossed the space between beds. She moved more steadily than Harry and looked offended by the fact.

"You are already our problem," she said.

Harry stared at her.

"That sounded better in my head."

Draconia laughed again. Hermione sat on Draconia's other side.

"I mean you do not become a burden by telling us when you are hurt. If you don't tell us, we still notice. Then we guess, and usually I guess something worse than whatever you're trying to hide."

"You make friendship sound bureaucratic."

"It has procedures."

"Nine," Draconia said.

Hermione looked at her. "We abandoned rule nine after the second unauthorised amendment."

"You abandoned it."

"There was no valid adoption process."

Harry looked between them.

"I nearly killed someone today, and we've returned to rules."

Hermione's smile faded. "You could have hurt him much worse. I'm not pretending you couldn't."

"I know."

"But you stopped. We have to talk about that part too."

Her hand found his. Draconia's remained beneath his other palm. Harry felt her fingers shift until they fitted between his. She watched their hands instead of his face, and the little movement made him more aware of her than any of Ginny's teasing in the compartment had done. He wanted to stay beside her. He wanted to ask whether that meant what he hoped it meant. Instead he rubbed his thumb once over her knuckle. She held on a little tighter.

Hermione drew the blanket back across Draconia's knees. Harry let go of Hermione's hand long enough to help with the corner nearest him, then found her fingers again. For a minute they did only that, making room on the bed without making anyone leave.

"Remus can help with the rest in the morning," Hermione said. "We tell Sirius enough tonight so he knows what happened."

Harry nodded.

"Remus first in the morning. Before anyone starts asking to look inside my head."

"Agreed."

"And I get to explain it."

Draconia considered. "I'll try not to interrupt."

"That sounded painful."

"It will be. Don't make me regret it."

"You fell off a train. Your accuracy is questionable."

"Partly off."

Hermione looked from one to the other.

"There are degrees?"

Harry began laughing. He tried to stop because of his ribs. This made it worse. Draconia joined him, one arm held against her side. Hermione lasted several seconds before surrendering. Sirius woke with his wand raised. He looked at the three of them crowded upon one hospital bed, all injured and attempting not to laugh loudly enough to summon Madam Pomfrey.

"What happened?"

"Medical emergency," Harry said.

Sirius examined their faces.

"Do I need to get Pomfrey?"

"No," all three replied.

He lowered the wand.

"Terrifying."

Sirius helped Harry back to his own bed. Neither mentioned how slowly the three steps had to be taken this time. Before letting go, Harry looked back at Draconia; she was watching him, her hand still open on the blanket where his had been. Harry picked up the Dreamless Sleep potion, then put it down again.

"Before I drink it—there's something else."

He told Sirius about the thought he had heard among the floating glass. He kept the account brief, but did not call it a dream or leave out how much he had wanted to obey it. Hermione and Draconia stayed quiet while he spoke.

Sirius sat down without reaching for his wand. "I'm glad you told me. Is it happening now?"

Harry shook his head.

"If it does, wake me. Even if you think you've imagined it." He looked towards Pomfrey's office. "I'll tell her before you take that. We'll speak to Remus together in the morning."

Pomfrey came to the bedside and asked Harry a few quiet questions. The draught would help him sleep, she explained; it was not a defence against the scar connection. She made a note for Dumbledore and checked that Harry still wanted it before leaving them. He did. He was tired of being afraid to close his eyes.

"Will you stay?" he asked.

The question seemed unnecessary with Sirius already in the chair. It was not. Sirius's face softened.

"All night."

Harry drank. The potion tasted of mint and nothing. Warmth moved through his limbs, loosening muscles which had forgotten how to release. He placed the empty cup upon the table. Hermione reached across the gap and rested her hand upon the edge of his mattress. Draconia did the same from the other side.

Neither could reach him. They did not need to. Sirius remained at the foot of the bed. Tonight, Harry surrendered consciousness knowing who would be there when he returned.

He woke to sunlight and Sirius snoring. The sound was not especially loud. It only seemed so because the hospital wing had fallen quiet around it. Morning lay across the floor in long gold rectangles. Dust moved inside the light. Beyond the windows, the mountains had sharpened from shadow into stone and early mist covered the lake. Harry remained still. No graveyard followed him into waking.

The absence felt strange enough to examine. His scar ached. His shoulder hurt. One rib objected when he breathed too deeply. None of the pain belonged to a dream, and for once he could separate memory from the room without touching every wall. Sirius slept folded inside the chair with his arms crossed and one boot beneath Harry's bed. During the night, someone had covered him with a hospital blanket. It ended at his knees. Hermione lay curled towards Harry, one hand still upon the edge of her mattress. Draconia faced him from the opposite side. Her eyes were open.

"How long?" he whispered.

"Not long."

"That's unsettling."

"You did not move."

"I was asleep."

"Exactly."

Harry understood. After the Chamber, Draconia watched sleeping people because stillness had ceased to mean safety. Possession had taught her that a body could breathe, speak and walk while the person inside it disappeared. Harry moved his fingers above the blanket.

"Still me."

Her gaze met his.

"I know."

She sounded as though she did now. Madam Pomfrey entered from her office carrying three diagnostic charts. Sirius woke instantly, wand halfway raised before his eyes opened.

"Morning," Harry said.

Sirius looked at the sunlight. Then at Harry.

"You slept."

"Apparently."

"All of it?"

"How would I know?"

Sirius stood. The blanket fell from his lap. He grinned. Harry had not realised how badly Sirius needed him to sleep until seeing what relief did to his face. The borrowed coat hung from the chair behind him, one pocket turned inside out. His boots were muddy; there was a line from the chair cushion pressed into his cheek. He looked nothing like the fearless figure beside the train window. Harry liked him better here. He was close enough to touch, tired enough to stop performing, and still there when morning came.

"Tea," Sirius decided. "Breakfast. Possibly a national holiday."

"Examination first," Madam Pomfrey said.

"You ruin every celebration."

"That is why they pay me."

She checked Hermione's pupils, Draconia's wound and Harry's ribs. Hermione was permitted to read for intervals of fifteen minutes. Draconia could walk if accompanied and forbidden from stairs. Harry was informed that Quidditch practice would not occur for at least one week.

"We haven't scheduled trials," he said.

"Then the restriction should be easy to obey."

"You don't know him," Draconia murmured.

Madam Pomfrey did. She added a second week if the shoulder pain persisted. Remus arrived before breakfast with Dumbledore. Harry had hoped for more time. That was the reason he spoke immediately.

"I heard something on the train that felt like Voldemort. I don't know if it was him."

Sirius set down his cup. He had heard the short account during the night; this would be the first time Harry tried to explain all of it. Dumbledore closed the doors. Remus took the chair Sirius had left and placed it beside Harry's bed. He did not ask the girls to leave. Neither did Dumbledore. Harry told them.

He described Draconia taking the Stunner, Hermione falling and the magic lifting broken glass around him. He repeated the thought as precisely as he could. Show them what happens. He admitted that it felt like Voldemort and like himself at the same time. He did not omit the pleasure which followed when the attacker became afraid. Nobody interrupted. When Harry finished, Sirius stood at the window with his back to the room. Both hands were braced against the sill. Dumbledore's face had gone very old.

"Did he see through me?" Harry asked.

"I do not know," Dumbledore said.

The answer frightened Harry. It also made him trust the next one.

"The connection may have behaved differently since the ritual," Dumbledore continued. "Voldemort used your blood to rebuild his body and entered your mind directly through Legilimency. He may now recognise intense emotion more easily than before. It is also possible that the thought was an echo left by the graveyard rather than a conscious intrusion."

"How do we tell the difference?" Hermione asked.

"Carefully," Remus said.

Draconia frowned. "That word has become suspiciously popular."

Remus almost smiled. "Because we know what Harry heard, but not yet what caused it. I'd rather say that plainly than give him the wrong answer quickly."

Dumbledore drew his wand. Sirius turned sharply.

"Explain before casting."

"I intended to place a detection charm upon the room, not Harry. It will respond if external Legilimency touches anyone inside."

He waited. Harry nodded. Only then did Dumbledore cast. Fine blue threads spread across the windows, doors and ceiling. They disappeared into the stone after one pulse.

"This will warn us of ordinary Legilimency directed into the ward," Dumbledore said. "I cannot promise it will recognise an intrusion through your scar. That is part of what we must investigate."

Harry knew what came next.

"Occlumency."

"Yes."

"With Snape."

Sirius left the window. "No."

Harry looked at him.

"That was quick."

"He's skilled. He is also the last person who should be walking through your memories while calling it education."

Dumbledore did not defend Snape automatically.

"Professor Snape is the most accomplished living Occlumens available to us and understands Voldemort's methods better than any other teacher at Hogwarts. Sirius's concern is nevertheless valid."

Remus rested both hands upon his cane.

"Then the lessons require limits."

"What limits?" Harry asked.

"The limits we discussed at Hawthorn still apply," Remus said. "He explains each attempt before it begins. No deliberate probing into unrelated memories. We allow time to ground before and afterwards, and an observer can stop the lesson if you freeze or cannot answer."

He looked at Dumbledore. "Flitwick's barrier must be tested before we rely on it. Until then, there is no entry. Harry can ask to stop without having to defend the decision."

"And no reporting private memories to Dumbledore," Sirius added.

Dumbledore inclined his head. "Agreed. Professor Snape would report only evidence of outside intrusion or an immediate danger."

Harry considered the terms. Snape had seen him frightened and hurt before. Letting him see the memories behind that was another matter. The thought of allowing him near Privet Drive, the Chamber or the graveyard made Harry's skin tighten. Yet Snape had entered the ward with his robes fastened incorrectly because Draconia was hurt. He had told the truth about pain rather than promising it would not exist.

"One lesson," Harry said. "Remus stays. I decide after that."

"Harry," Sirius began.

"You said no because you hate him."

"I hate him for excellent reasons."

"Probably. I still want to decide."

Sirius's jaw tightened. For a moment, Harry expected protection to become authority. Then Sirius exhaled.

"One," he said. "Remus stays. If Snivellus uses your childhood as target practice, I turn his cauldron into a toilet."

"That safeguard is not necessary," Dumbledore said.

"It is to me."

Harry smiled.

"Agreed."

Dumbledore rose. "I will speak with Professor Snape. The first session will occur only after Madam Pomfrey clears you and after we have reviewed the train's magical residue."

"Tell him I asked," Harry said.

Dumbledore paused.

"You wish him to know that?"

"I don't want him thinking you forced me and resenting me for it. He can resent me for something accurate."

Remus looked down, hiding amusement.

"A promising beginning," Dumbledore said.

He left to prepare the morning assembly. Sirius remained near the bed. Harry waited.

"You're angry," he said.

"Terrified."

"That wasn't the option."

"It is the honest answer."

Sirius sat beside him.

"I hate that he can reach you somewhere I can't stand in front."

Harry looked at the hand Sirius had placed upon the blanket.

"You were behind me yesterday."

"And I couldn't stop whatever happened in your head."

"So did you."

Sirius's fingers closed around Harry's.

"One lesson," he repeated.

"One."

It was not certainty. It was consent small enough to withdraw. For now, that was enough.

The Daily Prophet reached Hogwarts before breakfast. Its headline was visible from the hospital wing doors.

MINISTRY FOILS HOGWARTS EXPRESS SABOTAGE

Beneath it, a photograph showed Aurors standing beside the stopped train after the fighting had ended. The angle excluded the Dark Mark, the missing carriage and every student who had flown between broken windows. The subheading praised a rapid coordinated Ministry response to an attempted abduction by masked extremists of presently unknown allegiance. Cedric carried the paper. He entered the ward wearing fresh Hufflepuff robes and a bandage around his left forearm. Cho walked beside him. Her burned palm had been wrapped from wrist to fingers. Cedric's gaze found Harry before it found the beds. The check passed over sling, bandages and scar with the efficiency of habit.

"You slept," he said.

"Apparently this is public information."

"Sirius told the corridor."

Harry turned towards his guardian. "Of course he did."

Sirius appeared unrepentant.

Harry faced Cedric again. "How many trips?"

"Six."

"Show-off."

"You were occupied."

The answer belonged to the train, when Cedric had taken the emergency broom because Harry could not be everywhere. Neither added gratitude to a debt already understood. Cedric held out the newspaper.

"Unknown allegiance," Cedric said.

Harry accepted the paper.

"Masks make it difficult."

"And the Mark?"

"Very unfortunate cloud formation."

Cho sat upon the edge of Hermione's bed. "Page four says the symbol may have been used by criminals hoping to exploit existing public anxiety."

Hermione held out her hand. Madam Pomfrey looked at the clock.

"Fifteen minutes."

Hermione took the newspaper. Her eyes moved rapidly down the columns.

"They name Lucius."

Draconia went still. Hermione read aloud.

"Sources within the Department are examining whether the attack may relate to former associates angered by Mr Lucius Malfoy's recent efforts to secure favourable treatment through controversial testimony."

"Favourable treatment," Draconia repeated.

"They've made your father the cause," Harry said.

"And themselves the rescue."

Cedric looked towards the Great Hall. "That isn't what the students remember."

Harry folded the paper.

"Umbridge plans to help with that."

"So do we," Cho said.

She handed Hermione a second publication. It consisted of four sheets of ordinary parchment folded through the centre. No photograph occupied the front. No moving headline attempted to catch the eye. Three words had been printed at the top.

WHAT WE SAW

Beneath them appeared a list.

12:27 — Three warning whistles. First masked rider reported beside carriage seven.

12:29 — Dark Mark visible above the fields. Accounts from each passenger carriage.

12:32 — Silver carriage wards reversed after impact beneath the rails.

12:36 — Attackers demand Harry Potter surrender by name.

The times continued through the derailment, evacuation and bridge. A note beneath the list explained that the times came from clocks and watches in different carriages. Where they disagreed, both readings had been kept; no student had been asked to supply a time they did not remember. No conclusion had been added. It did not call the attackers Death Eaters. It recorded masks, curses, words and the Mark. Witness numbers appeared beside every entry.

"Who made this?" Hermione asked.

"A lot of people," Cedric said. "Mostly the prefects who already had the accounts. Nobody tried to write the whole thing alone."

Harry looked at the witness numbers printed beside the first entry. There were too many for one person's story to stand in for all of them.

Cho explained. "Padma organised the Ravenclaw copies from her bed. She was telling people where to put the pages before the healer had finished with her ankle. Ernie and Hannah checked the Hufflepuff times. Pansy sent the Slytherin accounts through the prefect network before Umbridge's directive reached the dormitories."

"Ron and Ginny collected Gryffindor," Cedric added. "Luna supplied the title."

Hermione examined the pages.

"There are no names."

"Witness numbers only," Cho said. "Anyone wanting the original requires that student's permission."

"Good," Hermione murmured. "They should get to decide."

Pride entered her face so brightly that Harry expected the bandage to object.

"How many copies?"

Cedric smiled. "Enough to start with. We've kept a clean set for the teachers, and nobody's handing out an account unless its writer agreed."

Madam Pomfrey released them for breakfast under protest. Hermione was instructed to avoid the moving staircases. Draconia could walk no farther than the Great Hall without resting. Harry was forbidden from carrying anything heavier than a book. When he suggested taking several in separate trips, Madam Pomfrey looked at him until he withdrew the suggestion. They remained admitted to the ward. Breakfast meant a brief supervised visit, followed by another examination. Their beds would stay ready until she was satisfied.

Sirius levitated three ward chairs down the fixed staircase, moving slowly enough that Madam Pomfrey could supervise from the landing. Harry would ordinarily have objected to arriving anywhere in a chair; today he was too relieved not to use the stairs. At the bottom, Sirius left the chairs ready for their return and waited while each of them stood.

He left for Hogsmeade only after Harry promised to use the mirror at lunch. The Grangers walked with Hermione across the entrance hall before McGonagall escorted them towards the gates. Narcissa and Lucius departed through Dumbledore's Floo to begin answering the article. At the entrance to the Great Hall, the trio stopped. Hundreds of voices moved beyond the closed doors. Harry could face masked riders. He found the sound of breakfast more difficult. Draconia noticed.

"We can return to the ward."

"Pomfrey will think we disobeyed something."

"We almost certainly have."

Hermione adjusted the bandage beneath her hair. "If we go in separately, people may be less likely to stare."

Draconia looked at her.

"They'll still stare. They'll just have three chances."

Hermione sighed. "I know. I was hoping one of you would have a better idea."

"Together," Draconia said. "Then we don't have to cross it alone."

Harry offered his right arm, then glanced down at the sling holding his left.

"Medical assistance?"

Hermione looked at the sling on his left.

"You are also injured."

"Medical symmetry, then."

Draconia took his right arm. Her fingers rested lightly above his wrist rather than placing weight upon the shoulder. Hermione walked at his left, keeping a hand lightly against his elbow below the sling. Harry looked at the doors.

"This will reduce staring."

"Considerably," Draconia said.

Hermione smiled. They entered together. Conversation stopped from the doorway outwards. The silence crossed Gryffindor, Hufflepuff and Ravenclaw before reaching the Slytherin table. Heads turned. Forks lowered. First-years stared without pretending not to.

Harry felt every visible injury anew. The bandage upon Hermione's head. The careful way Draconia held her side. His sling. The cut upon his cheek. Their hands through his arms.

Proof arranged as spectacle. He nearly stepped backwards. Then a Hufflepuff second-year stood. Harry recognised her as the girl Cedric and Cho had carried from carriage six. She did not applaud. She lifted one folded copy of What We Saw above her head. Another student stood beside her. Then three Ravenclaws.

Pansy rose at the Slytherin table with her copy already open. Millicent stood beside her. Ginny and Neville rose among the Gryffindors. Ron remained seated only long enough to move three plates and make space upon the bench. Throughout the Hall, papers lifted. Hundreds of quiet pages. Not celebration. Recognition. Harry's lungs loosened. Draconia's hand tightened upon his arm. Hermione blinked rapidly.

The voices returned slowly as students sat. Harry and Hermione walked towards Gryffindor. Draconia's fingers lingered until the distance required release. Pansy met her halfway to Slytherin.

"You look terrible," she said.

"Good morning to you too."

Millicent took Draconia's other arm without asking.

"We saved porridge."

"How considerate."

"It was Pansy's idea."

"It was not."

Draconia looked back at Harry. He waited until she reached the bench before sitting beside Ron. Hermione took the place on his other side. Three plates.

Harry looked at the place he had imagined saving for Draconia, then at the table where Pansy was moving a bowl closer to her. She had eaten with them whenever she could, but on formal mornings she still belonged at the table across the Hall. Harry noticed it more than he expected. Ron pushed the toast towards him.

"Dietary supervision."

"You are enjoying this."

"Deeply."

At the staff table, Umbridge wore pink. Her plate remained empty. Commissioner Directive One lay beside it. Professor Snape sat two places away and looked as though the distance was insufficient. Dumbledore rose. The Hall quieted.

"Yesterday," he said, "the Hogwarts Express was attacked."

No euphemism. Harry remembered Mr Granger's voice in the hospital wing and the simpler word he had used.

"Masked assailants used the Dark Mark, curses associated with Voldemort's followers and Mr Potter's name while endangering every person aboard. The Ministry continues to investigate their identities. Hogwarts will not permit that investigation to erase what you experienced."

Umbridge's face remained pleasant.

"All eight hundred and twelve students have been accounted for. Twenty-three remain under medical observation. None were killed. This outcome was not fortune alone. Prefects maintained evacuations. Older students protected younger ones. Members of every House crossed danger for people whose names they did not know. Mr Diggory, Miss Chang, Miss Weasley and Mr Goldstein carried students between damaged carriages upon four broomsticks."

Attention moved towards Cedric. He lowered his gaze.

"Mr Potter, Miss Granger, Miss Malfoy and Mr Weasley held a breached corridor until it could be evacuated. Many others performed acts I have not yet learnt."

Harry felt Ron go still beside him. Dumbledore's gaze travelled over the tables.

"You should never have been required to save yourselves. Do not allow praise for your courage to become an excuse for the failure which made that courage necessary."

Professor McGonagall lowered her eyes. Several teachers did the same.

"Classes will begin on Monday. This weekend will be used for rest, medical review and the repair of communication with families. Any student may speak to their Head of House, Madam Pomfrey or a member of the counselling team arriving this afternoon. Attendance at the evening meal will not be compulsory."

He looked towards Umbridge.

"The Special Commissioner will now explain the Ministry measures introduced overnight."

Umbridge stood. She was shorter than the lectern. A small adjustment from her wand lowered its reading surface several inches.

"Thank you, Headmaster."

Her voice acquired the sweetness it used when turning restrictions into favours.

"Yesterday's unfortunate incident understandably caused fear and confusion. The Minister wishes each of you to know that your safety remains the highest priority of the Ministry for Magic. Additional Aurors have been assigned to Hogsmeade. Incoming and outgoing post related to the event will be examined to protect the integrity of the investigation."

Pages rustled throughout the Hall. Umbridge's eyes flicked towards them.

"Students are strongly discouraged from circulating unofficial claims, particularly those which attribute the attack to any individual or organisation before evidence has been properly reviewed. Commissioner Directive One requires approval for organised discussion groups, publications and gatherings concerning disputed events."

Luna raised her hand from the Ravenclaw table. Umbridge attempted not to see her. Luna kept it raised.

Dumbledore said, "Miss Lovegood has a question."

Umbridge's smile thinned.

"Yes?"

"Is something disputed because two people remember it differently, or because one person dislikes what everyone else remembers?"

Silence held for half a second. Then laughter escaped from the Hufflepuff table. It spread. Umbridge waited until it stopped.

"Memory is vulnerable to suggestion, Miss Lovegood."

"So are newspapers."

Harry covered his mouth. Hermione placed her elbow upon his good side in warning. It shook with suppressed laughter. Umbridge returned to her parchment.

"Until a permanent appointment is approved, I will personally supervise instruction in Defence Against the Dark Arts. The course will emphasise safe theoretical understanding, non-provocative conflict management and Ministry-approved defensive principles."

Harry looked at the copy of What We Saw beside his plate.

12:54 — Green curse passes through carriage eight after Potter ducks. Witnesses 71, 74, 76, 81; identification referred to escort report.

Non-provocative conflict management. The absurdity settled into something colder. Umbridge continued speaking about stability, healing and responsible education. Students began passing the papers beneath her words.

One copy travelled from Gryffindor to Hufflepuff beneath the table. Another crossed to Ravenclaw inside an empty cereal box. Pansy read the timeline aloud in a whisper to two younger Slytherins. Draconia listened, then asked whether the note about different clocks was included. Pansy showed her the bottom of the page. The registration requirement applied to gatherings of more than three students. Small clusters formed around the papers, each careful not to draw a fourth person in. It was not a safe answer to every restriction: Umbridge could still object to the publication itself. But she could not stop students looking at a page merely by counting the heads around it, and the copies were already in their hands. Harry caught Draconia's eye across the Hall. She lifted three fingers.

Hermione saw and did the same. Harry looked at the two of them separated by green and red, by tables, rules and every authority which found division easier to manage. He raised three fingers back. Umbridge spoke from the staff table beneath the enchanted ceiling.

At the end of the bench, a first-year pointed to one of the entries and whispered something to Neville. Neville listened, then turned the page over and offered him a quill. Harry watched the boy hesitate before beginning to write. Umbridge was still speaking when the quill reached the parchment.
