7

The last thing Harry remembered was Draconia holding on.

When consciousness returned beneath the white glare of the hospital-wing ceiling, his fingers closed upon nothing.

He opened his eyes too quickly. Light struck behind them, sharp enough to turn the rafters into pale streaks, and the room tilted when he attempted to sit. Pain followed from several directions at once. His chest ached where Draconia's Stunning Spell had thrown him backwards. One shoulder objected to the pipe, both knees remembered the Chamber floor, and every muscle seemed to have reached a separate conclusion concerning the wisdom of fighting a basilisk.

His glasses lay folded upon the bedside cabinet, one lens scratched but otherwise intact. Harry put them on and discovered that seeing his injuries more clearly did not improve them.

Harry ignored all of them.

The bed beside his was empty.

White sheets had been drawn smooth across the mattress. Morning sunlight lay over them without interruption. For one breathless instant, Harry saw Draconia beneath the statue again: silver hair spread through black water, skin cold beneath his hand and each weakening breath arriving farther apart than the last.

He tore the blankets aside.

"If you place one foot upon that floor, Mr Potter, I shall attach you to the bed."

Madam Pomfrey appeared from behind the nearest screen carrying a tray crowded with bottles. Her robes were creased, several pins had escaped her cap and the shadows beneath her eyes suggested that she had spent the night personally arguing with death and resented being made to repeat herself.

"Where is she?" Harry demanded.

"Recovering."

"Where?"

Madam Pomfrey pointed towards the far end of the ward. Three screens had been drawn around one bed, their white fabric leaving only the iron legs visible beneath them. A chair stood outside. Snape's black travelling cloak had been folded over its back.

Harry's lungs began working again.

"She woke twice during the night," Madam Pomfrey continued. "The first time, she asked whether you were alive. The second time, she attempted to leave her bed and find out for herself. You have both been equally tiresome."

"Can I see her?"

"No."

"You just said she tried to see me."

"And I prevented it. I am consistent."

Harry swung his legs towards the floor.

Madam Pomfrey lifted her wand.

He stopped. "You wouldn't actually attach me to the bed."

Silver restraints appeared around the bedposts.

"Right," Harry said, retreating beneath the blankets. "Good discussion."

Madam Pomfrey set the tray down with the controlled force of someone resisting the urge to throw it. She pressed two fingers against his wrist, examined his pupils and passed her wand over his ribs. Pale blue light followed the bruising beneath his pyjamas before disappearing into the fabric.

"Nothing is broken now," she said. "Professor Snape treated the worst of the curse damage. You're exhausted and concussed, and I have lost count of your cuts. You will recover, provided you stay in that bed."

Harry glanced towards the screens. "How did we get out?"

"Professor Flitwick levitated you through the tunnel. Professor McGonagall spent twenty minutes enlarging the entrance so the basilisk could be removed later. Professor Snape carried Miss Malfoy and has since threatened three portraits for repeating that information."

The image arrived before Harry could prevent it: Snape climbing through the filthy pipe with Draconia held against his chest and murder arranged across every line of his face.

"He carried her?"

"You will value your remaining school years by never mentioning it."

From behind the distant screens came a weak, familiar voice.

"I intend to mention it constantly."

Harry turned so sharply that Madam Pomfrey caught his shoulder before he left the mattress.

The screen shifted. Draconia stood within the opening, one hand wrapped around its edge and the other clutching a blanket closed over her hospital gown. She was almost colourless in the morning light. Her hair hung loose around her shoulders, still unevenly damp at the ends despite the cleaning charms, and bruises darkened the skin beneath both eyes. Without the severe arrangement of her robes or the green satchel crossing her chest, she appeared smaller than Harry remembered.

Alive, though.

Undeniably, impossibly alive.

"Draco."

Her mouth moved into something which tried to become a smile.

"You look dreadful."

Relief broke through Harry with enough force to hurt. "You said that already."

"I wanted to ensure you understood."

Her knees folded.

Madam Pomfrey reached her before Harry could. She caught Draconia beneath both arms and guided her into the chair outside the screens, scolding with the intense professionalism of someone whose anger existed mainly to conceal fear.

"What part of complete bed rest sounded optional?"

"The word 'bed' seemed unnecessarily specific."

"Your heart nearly stopped."

"I'm sitting down, aren't I?"

Draconia's voice was dry, but her hands betrayed her. They shook against the white blanket, the tremor worsening when Madam Pomfrey reached to check the pulse at her wrist. Draconia drew back before she could stop herself.

The movement silenced the ward.

Madam Pomfrey's hand remained suspended between them. Her expression changed, irritation falling away without leaving pity behind.

"May I?" she asked.

Draconia stared at the offered hand. A long second passed before she nodded.

The healer touched her wrist only after Draconia turned it deliberately into her palm.

Harry watched without pretending not to. Draconia noticed, because she noticed almost everything, but for once she did not order him to look elsewhere.

"Where's Hermione?" she asked.

Harry's relief narrowed.

Madam Pomfrey looked towards the bed nearest the windows. Its curtains had been left partly open. Hermione lay exactly as she had the previous evening, motionless beneath the blankets with her unseeing eyes fixed upon the canopy. New spectacles rested upon the cabinet where Harry had broken the old pair.

"Professor Sprout believes the Mandrakes are ready," Madam Pomfrey said. "She and Professor Snape have been brewing the Restorative Draught since midnight."

"Believes?" Harry repeated.

"Living ingredients do not consult timetables. The plants are younger than I would prefer, but the danger of waiting now outweighs the danger of using them early."

Draconia looked at Hermione. Her fingers tightened into the blanket.

"Did I—"

"No," Harry said.

She did not look at him. "You don't know what I was going to ask."

"Yes, I do. You didn't hurt her."

"I aimed my wand at her."

"He aimed it."

"It was my hand."

"Which kept moving away."

"Not enough."

Harry pushed himself higher against the pillows. The effort made the room pulse, but he waited until it steadied.

"You warned me," he said. "You made the first spell miss. You put the Stunner somewhere Madam Pomfrey could fix, and you stopped him killing anyone all year."

Draconia's face tightened. "He told you that?"

"He talked a lot."

"What else did he tell you?"

The question was carefully controlled. Fear lived beneath it.

Harry remembered Tom standing in the green light and reciting sentences Draconia had believed private. Harry forgot to eat. Harry looked tired at breakfast. Harry kept smiling today. Riddle had held each confession like a blade and expected Harry to use it too.

"Mostly that he was clever," Harry said. "He seemed very invested in the subject."

Draconia studied him.

"He read everything."

"I know."

"And he told you—"

"Nothing that belonged to him."

Her breath caught.

Harry looked towards Hermione because meeting Draconia's eyes had become unexpectedly difficult. "He was a thief. I'm not helping him keep anything he stole."

For several moments, the only sound came from the fire settling inside the iron stove. Draconia lowered her face. Her loose hair slipped forward, concealing most of her expression.

"Thank you," she whispered.

Madam Pomfrey pretended to rearrange bottles.

The hospital-wing doors opened before Harry could decide how to answer. Professor Sprout entered carrying a wooden case in both hands and accompanied by a smell powerful enough to suggest that several greenhouses had been boiled into a single potion. She wore dragon-hide gloves, and a fresh bite had torn through one sleeve.

"It is ready," Professor Sprout announced.

Madam Pomfrey became movement.

The occupied beds were uncovered. Bottles flew from the wooden case and arranged themselves upon a hovering tray. Behind the nearest curtains, Colin Creevey lay stiffly with his camera on the cabinet beside him. Penelope Clearwater occupied another bed, while Mrs Norris had been brought from the staff quarters in a wicker basket and placed at the foot of the ward.

Harry attempted to stand again.

Madam Pomfrey pointed at him without looking. "Remain."

"I want to be beside Hermione."

"You may be beside her when she is capable of complaining about it."

Draconia rose from the chair.

"That instruction applies to you as well."

"I was not addressed."

"Sit down, Miss Malfoy."

Draconia sat.

They watched as Madam Pomfrey measured a silver spoonful of the draught. It was thick and dark, almost black where it gathered inside the bottle, though green light moved across its surface whenever she tilted it. Professor Sprout supported Hermione's head while Madam Pomfrey placed the potion between her lips.

Nothing happened.

Harry counted one breath, then another.

Draconia's hand found the edge of his mattress. Her fingers closed around the iron frame rather than reaching for him.

Hermione inhaled.

The sound was small, no more than a startled intake of air, yet it seemed to travel through every corner of the ward. Her eyes closed for the first time in weeks. When they opened again, they moved.

She blinked at the canopy.

Then she sat up and began coughing.

Harry was out of bed before Madam Pomfrey completed the first syllable of his name. Draconia rose with him. They reached Hermione from opposite sides and stopped only because Professor Sprout stood between them holding an empty spoon.

Hermione's gaze crossed the ward without focus. It found Harry first, passed over the bandages around his chest and face, then fixed upon Draconia wrapped in a hospital blanket.

"What happened?" she croaked.

"We found the basilisk," Harry said.

Her eyes widened. "You found it?"

"Technically it found me."

"Harry."

"I killed it."

Hermione stared at him.

"There were mitigating circumstances."

"You fought a basilisk?"

"I kept my eyes closed for most of it."

"That does not improve the sentence!"

The force of her outrage exhausted what little strength the potion had restored. Hermione swayed. Harry caught one shoulder while Professor Sprout steadied the other.

"You've been awake for less than a minute," Harry said. "It's comforting that nothing important has changed."

Hermione's eyes filled.

She seized the front of his hospital robes and pulled him into an embrace which punished every injury he possessed. Harry made a small noise, but his arms closed around her before the pain could matter.

"You complete idiot," she whispered into his shoulder.

"That's nearly what Snape said."

"Then Professor Snape was right."

"You have been ill."

Hermione released him only when Madam Pomfrey threatened to separate them magically. She wiped both cheeks with the heel of one hand, accepted her new spectacles and put them on.

Then she looked at Draconia.

The joy left her face slowly as she took in the bruises, the trembling hands and the absence of the green satchel.

"Draco?"

Draconia stood very still.

"It was the notebook," she said.

Hermione glanced from her to Harry. Understanding formed through fragments: the warnings, the missing hours, the way Draconia had protected the satchel even while asking them to trust her.

"You opened the Chamber," Hermione said.

Harry stepped forward. "Riddle did."

Draconia did not defend herself. "He used me."

Hermione's face had gone pale, but her voice remained steady. "Are you still connected to it?"

"It's destroyed."

"Can you hear him?"

"No."

"Are you certain?"

Draconia swallowed. "No."

Harry felt that answer strike both of them.

Hermione pushed back her blankets.

Madam Pomfrey appeared between the beds. "Absolutely not."

"I need to stand."

"You need to sit down."

"Then move her chair closer."

Professor Sprout did so before the matron could object.

Draconia sat beside Hermione's bed. Her posture remained straight, though the blanket had begun slipping from one shoulder. Hermione reached towards her. Draconia flinched.

Hermione stopped immediately.

"May I?" she asked.

The same question Madam Pomfrey had used.

Draconia stared at her open hand.

"I attacked you."

"You warned me to stop investigating before I was attacked."

"Because I knew enough to be afraid and not enough to be useful."

"You were twelve."

"So were you."

Hermione's mouth tightened. "Yes, and I responded by tearing a page from a library book and walking towards a basilisk."

Harry sat on the edge of her bed. "I'm glad you finally recognise the recklessness of damaging school property."

"This is not about the book."

"Madam Pince may disagree."

Hermione ignored him. Her hand remained between herself and Draconia, offered but not imposed.

"Being controlled is not the same as choosing," she said.

Draconia's eyes dropped. "I chose to write in it."

"You did not choose what it was."

"I chose not to tell either of you when I became suspicious."

"That was a spectacularly bad decision."

"Hermione," Harry said.

"It was. She knows it was." Hermione's voice softened. "It still does not make what he did your fault."

Draconia looked at the offered hand again. Slowly, she placed her own inside it.

Hermione closed her fingers.

Harry watched their hands join across the white blanket. Something which had remained braced inside his chest since the corridor loosened.

"There," he said. "Now that we've all survived, perhaps one of you can explain why I'm the only person being called reckless."

Both girls turned towards him.

"You went into the Chamber alone," Hermione said.

"You jumped onto a basilisk," Draconia added.

"I had a sword."

"Eventually," Draconia said.

"Before that, you had poor judgement and defective eyewear," Hermione continued.

"This reunion has deteriorated."

Hermione's grip tightened around Draconia's hand. Draconia's mouth curved faintly.

For the first time since Halloween, all three of them were together and entirely themselves.

Peace lasted eleven minutes.

Madam Pomfrey returned Harry to his bed, Draconia to hers and Hermione to the pillows she had already begun arguing she no longer required. Professor Sprout moved along the ward administering the Restorative Draught to the remaining victims. Colin Creevey woke asking whether anyone had developed his film. Penelope Clearwater demanded the date before accepting water, while Mrs Norris came out of Petrification with a scream which sent Professor Sprout backwards into a cabinet.

Filch arrived seconds later.

He gathered the furious cat against his chest, buried his face in her fur and wept without shame. Mrs Norris endured this for nearly half a minute before sinking her claws into his shoulder. Filch cried harder.

The hospital-wing doors opened again while Madam Pomfrey was attempting to restore order.

Professor McGonagall entered first. She had changed from the torn robes Harry remembered seeing in the Chamber, though a narrow cut remained along one cheek and stone dust still silvered the edge of her hair. Flitwick followed with his left arm held inside a sling. Snape came behind them, immaculate except for the damp ends of his robes, his expression daring anyone to mention the journey through the pipe.

Dumbledore entered last.

He wore deep blue travelling robes beneath a cloak fastened incorrectly at one shoulder. Rain shone upon the fabric. He must have returned to the castle directly from London, but nothing in his manner appeared hurried until Harry saw his eyes.

Their usual brightness had disappeared.

"Headmaster," Madam Pomfrey said. "My patients have been conscious for less than a quarter of an hour."

"Then I shall attempt to keep my questions within the limits of their patience."

"Their patience is irrelevant. Mine is limited."

Dumbledore inclined his head. "A substantially more dangerous condition."

McGonagall did not smile. She came to stand at the foot of Harry's bed and examined him with an expression more severe than any he had faced in her classroom.

"Mr Potter."

"Professor."

"You disobeyed Madam Pomfrey, Professor Snape, Professor Flitwick and me in the course of approximately forty minutes."

Harry considered this. "Were all the portraits giving orders as well?"

"Do not improve the total."

"No, Professor."

"You entered an unknown passage alone while injured. You then engaged a basilisk without waiting for assistance which was already following you."

"I didn't know how far behind you were."

"That is not a defence."

"No, Professor."

"It is barely an explanation."

Harry looked down at the blankets. "The message said her body would be left there forever."

The anger in McGonagall's face altered.

"I thought if I waited," he continued, "there might not be anyone left to rescue."

No one answered immediately.

Snape stood near Draconia's bed, arms folded tightly across his chest. His gaze moved once towards her before returning to Harry.

"You had reason to fear for her," he said.

McGonagall turned. "Severus."

"I did not call it wise."

Harry looked at him. "That was nearly supportive."

"I can correct the error."

"No need."

Dumbledore approached the centre of the ward. Fawkes flew through the open doorway above him, scarlet wings filling the room with a sudden warm light. The phoenix circled once before landing upon Hermione's bedpost.

Hermione stared. "Was he really in the Chamber?"

"He was," Harry said. "He brought me the Sorting Hat."

"Of course he did."

"It made more sense at the time."

Fawkes released a mildly offended trill.

"You were extremely helpful," Harry assured him.

Dumbledore's attention moved between the three beds. "Before we discuss anything else, I owe each of you an apology."

The words removed the remaining humour.

"You warned me that danger had entered Hogwarts before the term began," he told Harry. "You brought forward evidence concerning Dobby, an object from Malfoy Manor and a threat none of us could yet name. I permitted the absence of certainty to become an excuse for delay."

Harry remembered the meetings, the careful reassurances and the protections which had always seemed to close after something passed through them.

"You believed me," he said.

"I should have done more."

Dumbledore turned towards Draconia.

"And you, Miss Malfoy, were left alone with an object designed by a gifted and profoundly cruel mind to make secrecy feel safer than trust."

Draconia held his gaze, though her hands had disappeared beneath the blankets.

"I concealed it."

"You did."

Harry stiffened at the answer. Dumbledore continued before he could object.

"You should have told someone. But the diary worked hard to make sure you did not. You made a mistake, Draconia. Riddle chose to hurt you. We must not confuse the two."

Draconia looked away.

Hermione pushed her spectacles higher. "What exactly was the diary?"

Dumbledore glanced at Madam Pomfrey.

She folded her arms. "Five minutes."

"You said that when their parents arrived," Harry told her. "They stayed nearly an hour."

"Their parents had not recently fought a basilisk."

"That sounds like favouritism."

"Four minutes."

Harry decided not to negotiate further.

The Headmaster drew a sealed glass case from inside his travelling cloak. The ruined diary rested within it, pierced through the centre and swollen with black water. Its false green covering had peeled almost entirely away. Beneath it lay the same scuffed black leather Harry had seen in the Chamber.

Draconia recoiled.

Her back struck the headboard. One hand rose as though to protect her face, while every trace of colour disappeared from it.

Harry was out of bed again.

He crossed the narrow aisle and placed himself between Draconia and the case before anybody could stop him. Hermione reached Fawkes first and turned the glass container away.

"Put it somewhere else," she said.

Dumbledore did so immediately.

He placed the case inside an empty cabinet and closed the wooden door across it. Snape's attention remained fixed upon Draconia. He did not approach until she saw him and gave the smallest nod.

"It cannot affect you," he said.

"I know."

"Knowing and believing are separate disciplines."

Her breathing slowly steadied.

Harry remained beside the bed. "What was it?"

Dumbledore's answer came without ornament.

"A fragment of Tom Riddle."

Hermione went motionless.

"A memory?" she asked.

"More deliberate than an ordinary memory and far more dangerous. It contained his mind as it existed when he was sixteen, preserved through exceptionally dark magic and capable of strengthening itself by consuming the life entrusted to its pages."

Draconia's fingers pressed into the blankets. "He called himself Lord Voldemort."

McGonagall inhaled sharply.

Snape's face did not change. The absence of reaction looked practised.

Hermione whispered, "Tom Riddle became Voldemort?"

"Yes," Dumbledore said.

"Did my father know?" Draconia asked.

"No," said a voice from the doorway.

Narcissa Malfoy stood there with one hand gripping the frame.

She had crossed the castle quickly enough to leave her hair coming loose from its pins. Her travelling cloak remained fastened over an evening dress, and the hem was dark with rain and snowmelt. Lucius stood behind her, one bare hand closed around the silver head of his cane. Mr and Mrs Granger waited farther back between two professors, their Muggle coats looking startlingly ordinary within the stone archway.

For an instant, none of the parents moved.

Then Narcissa saw Draconia.

She crossed the ward without removing her cloak. Draconia attempted to straighten, drawing every familiar defence around herself before her mother reached the bed.

"Mother, I—"

Narcissa gathered her into both arms.

The apology ended against her shoulder.

Draconia remained rigid. Harry saw the exact moment the effort failed. Her hands caught in the back of Narcissa's cloak, and she folded forwards, face hidden against her mother as the first broken sound escaped her.

Narcissa closed her eyes.

"No," she whispered, one hand cradling the back of Draconia's head. "You do not apologise for surviving."

Lucius stopped at the opposite side of the bed. He looked at his daughter, and the careful authority with which he ordinarily entered a room deserted him. His mouth opened without producing words.

Draconia reached one hand towards him.

Lucius took it.

That was all. Yet Harry had never seen him hold anything so carefully.

Across the ward, Hermione's mother ran.

Mrs Granger reached Hermione's bed and wrapped her daughter in an embrace fierce enough to push her spectacles crooked. Mr Granger followed more slowly, his face white beneath several hours of fear. He touched Hermione's hair, her cheek and both shoulders as though confirming separately that every part of her had returned.

"I'm all right," Hermione insisted through tears.

"You were turned to stone," Mr Granger said.

"Petrified."

"Hermione."

"Apparently I'm a different person."

Mrs Granger laughed once, badly, and hugged her again.

Harry returned to his bed before either family noticed he had been standing. He had become skilled at making himself smaller around other people's relief. Families closed naturally around their own; he understood the arrangement and felt no resentment towards it.

Mrs Granger saw him anyway.

She released Hermione, crossed the aisle and pulled Harry against her without asking. His body locked before he could conceal it. One of his hands remained suspended uselessly beside her shoulder.

Mrs Granger felt the reaction. Her hold loosened at once, giving him room to leave.

Harry did not.

After a moment, his hand found the back of her coat.

"Thank you," she said into his hair.

"I didn't un-Petrify her."

"You stayed with her."

"She was unconscious. It wasn't difficult."

Mrs Granger drew back far enough to examine him. Her eyes travelled over the bandages, bruises and the fading burn across his chest. Something inside her expression broke quietly.

"You have a very strange idea of what is difficult."

Harry looked towards Hermione. "She says that too."

"Frequently," Hermione confirmed.

Lucius had watched the exchange from Draconia's bedside. His gaze lingered upon the hand Harry had only just managed to lift, then moved towards the closed cabinet containing the diary.

"You said I did not know," he said to Dumbledore. "Know what?"

The Headmaster opened the cabinet.

Lucius saw the black leather.

Every part of him became still.

"Nott."

The name crossed the ward like a draught.

Narcissa lifted her head. Draconia's grip tightened in her cloak.

"You recognise it?" McGonagall asked.

"It was brought to my home during the summer." Lucius's voice had lost its smoothness. "Nott claimed it had belonged to Voldemort. He wanted it planted among Ginevra Weasley's possessions to obstruct her father's searches."

Hermione stared at him. "And you let him leave with it?"

Lucius's eyes cut towards her, then stopped.

The old offence appeared by instinct. What replaced it was worse.

"I refused him," he said. "I watched him carry it from my study and through the front doors."

"He didn't take it through the front doors," Draconia whispered.

Lucius looked at her.

"It was in my trunk."

Narcissa closed both arms around her daughter again. Lucius stared at the diary as though it had opened beneath his feet.

"You were warned that the object might be dangerous," Dumbledore said.

"I warned you an unidentified artefact had been brought to the Manor. I provided its description and intended target."

"You withheld the visitor's name."

"I lacked evidence."

"You possessed concern."

Lucius turned upon him. "Do not mistake this outcome for something I permitted."

"I do not." Dumbledore's voice remained quiet. "But Mr Nott relied upon your reluctance to expose the dealings of your former associates. He used that silence as surely as he used the diary."

The accusation landed because Lucius could not dismiss it.

Harry remembered Dumbledore's apology. Belief without action.

Lucius looked down at Draconia's hand inside his own.

"Then I will not be silent again," he said.

Dumbledore studied him. "Will you give the Ministry a complete account of the meeting?"

"Under oath, if necessary."

"Lucius," Narcissa said quietly.

He met her eyes across the bed. Whatever warning she meant to offer changed before it reached her mouth.

"Good," she said.

Hermione shifted against her pillows. "That proves Nott had the diary, but not how he put it in Draconia's trunk."

"No," Dumbledore agreed. "For that, we need someone who saw him."

Fawkes lifted his head.

The phoenix sang one clear note. It entered the air like sunlight passing through coloured glass, warm enough that Harry felt it behind his ribs. Space folded beside Draconia's bed with a soft crack.

Dobby appeared.

The house-elf wore the same stained pillowcase and woollen bandage Harry had seen after the Quidditch match. His ears drooped beneath the weight of every eye in the room. He looked first at Harry, then at Lucius and Narcissa.

Finally, he saw Draconia.

Dobby collapsed to his knees.

"Young Mistress is alive."

The words broke apart. He covered his face and began to sob, his narrow shoulders shaking so violently that the pillowcase slipped from one shoulder.

Draconia stared at him. "Dobby."

The elf pressed his forehead against the floor.

"Bad Dobby. Useless Dobby. Dobby knew and did not stop—"

His hand rose towards the stone.

"No," Harry said.

Dobby froze.

Harry had moved before anyone else recognised what the elf intended. Pain flared through his knees when he crouched, but he caught Dobby's wrist before it struck the floor.

"We discussed this."

Dobby looked at him through tears. "Dobby failed Harry Potter and Dobby's young mistress."

"You were wrong about most of your methods."

Hermione made a faint sound. Harry suspected she considered this insufficiently gentle.

"But you were right that something was coming," he continued. "So tell them now."

Dobby's eyes moved towards Lucius. His mouth opened. No sound emerged.

The bandage around his hand tightened by itself.

Narcissa rose.

"The restriction remains," she said.

Lucius's face hardened—not at Dobby, Harry realised, but at the magic preventing him from speaking.

He placed his cane beside Draconia's bed and stepped forwards.

"Dobby, attend."

The elf straightened instinctively.

"Every command forbidding you from discussing Nott's visits, his proposals or his movements within Malfoy Manor is withdrawn. Every obligation of hospitality which prevents you reporting danger to a member of this family is suspended. You will answer Professor Dumbledore truthfully, and you will not punish yourself for anything spoken under that order."

Magic moved through the room.

It did not produce light or sound. Harry felt it only as a pressure passing across his skin, old and cold and threaded through with the weight of generations. Dobby gasped. The woollen strip around his hand loosened.

"Tell us what you saw," Lucius said.

Dobby's voice trembled.

"Dobby served refreshments in Master Lucius's study when Mr Nott first brought the diary. Dobby felt bad magic inside it. Hungry magic."

Draconia pulled the blanket higher.

"Mr Nott returned twice. On the last evening, Master told him to leave. Mr Nott carried the black book into the corridor, but he did not go directly to the doors."

Lucius's eyes narrowed. "I watched him cross the entrance hall."

"Master saw what Mr Nott wished Master to see."

Dobby twisted his fingers, remembered the order and forced them apart again.

"Mr Nott sent an image ahead wearing his face and carrying a black book. Mr Nott himself used the servants' passage. Dobby followed."

"Why did the Manor wards permit it?" Narcissa asked.

"He was still a welcome guest. Old magic recognises old names."

Disgust crossed her face. The answer seemed to condemn more than one person.

"He went to Young Mistress's room," Dobby continued. "He opened her school trunk, wrapped the diary in green leather and marked it with her initials before placing it beneath her parchment. Before he closed the trunk, Mr Nott said Harry Potter would follow wherever Young Mistress led—and that Master Lucius would learn the cost of choosing him."

Harry's stomach turned.

Draconia made no sound. Narcissa sat beside her again and took both her hands.

"Dobby tried to remove it after Mr Nott left," the elf said. "The cover burnt Dobby. The diary had been accepted by the Manor as a gift belonging to its heir. Dobby could not destroy or steal Young Mistress's property, and Master's order would not allow Dobby to name the wizard who placed it."

"You could have shown us," Lucius said.

Dobby's ears fell. "Dobby tried. Dobby broke crystal outside the study. Dobby moved Young Mistress's trunk. Dobby hid every bottle of green ink."

Despite everything, Harry looked at Draconia. "Didn't you wonder about that?"

"I assumed Pansy was being irritating."

"Did you accuse her?" Hermione asked.

"Repeatedly."

"We owe Pansy an apology."

"Let us not become unreasonable."

The thin exchange faded without becoming laughter. Dobby continued.

"Dobby heard Mr Nott speak of Harry Potter. He said Young Mistress's friendship had weakened the Malfoys. He said the book would make Master choose between his daughter and the world he had betrayed."

Lucius went pale with fury.

"Dobby believed the book would use Young Mistress to reach Harry Potter. If Harry Potter did not return to Hogwarts, the plan could not be completed. Dobby stopped the letters so Harry Potter would forget. When Harry Potter came to school anyway, Dobby enchanted the Bludger."

Mrs Granger's expression grew steadily more appalled.

"You dropped an iron ball on him?"

"Only to send Harry Potter home, ma'am."

"You nearly killed him," Hermione said.

"I mentioned that at the time," Harry added.

Dobby bowed his head. "Dobby was wrong."

"Very," Draconia said.

The elf flinched.

Her face changed.

"But you were trying to protect us," she continued. "And our rules made it impossible for you to do so sensibly."

Dobby stared at her.

"I should have noticed what you were attempting to tell us during the summer."

"Young Mistress must not blame—"

"Then neither will you."

The command emerged with a trace of her old precision. Dobby's mouth opened and closed.

Draconia looked towards Lucius. "The bindings must change."

He had already reached the same conclusion. Harry saw it in the grim line of his mouth.

"They will," Lucius said.

Narcissa turned to Dobby. "From this moment, no command concerning privacy, hospitality or the reputation of this family will prevent you reporting a danger to a child. You may speak to Lucius, to me, to Draconia or to an appropriate authority outside the household if those within it refuse to listen."

Lucius glanced at her. "That wording is exceptionally broad."

"Good."

Hermione looked at Narcissa with something approaching astonished approval.

"Dobby will not be punished?" Harry asked.

"No," Narcissa said.

"For the Bludger either?"

Lucius exhaled through his nose. "The Bludger will be discussed."

"Discussed is not punished."

"Do you negotiate every promise offered to you?"

"The important ones."

Lucius regarded him for a long moment. "Dobby will not be punished."

The elf began crying again.

This time, Draconia lowered one hand towards him. She stopped before touching and waited. Dobby crossed the remaining distance himself, pressing his forehead against her fingers.

Harry looked away to give them privacy and found Lucius watching him.

The man crossed the ward after several seconds. His cane remained beside Draconia's bed, depriving him of the object he ordinarily used to establish distance. Without it, his hands looked uncertain.

"Harry."

The use of his first name drew every eye.

Lucius ignored them. "My failure to report Nott completely allowed him to place that object inside my home. You entered the Chamber because I did not finish what I began."

Harry frowned. "Nott planted it."

"I let him leave without searching him. I did not want to admit that he might turn against us."

That sounded painfully honest. Harry did not know what to do with it.

Lucius seemed equally unfamiliar with what came next.

"You saved my daughter," he said. "Nothing I offer can make that debt equal."

"She saved me first."

"From the Bludger?"

"From lots of things."

Draconia's face disappeared further behind Narcissa's shoulder.

Harry continued before she could object. "And she fought Riddle the whole year. I only reached the end."

Lucius's gaze shifted towards his daughter. Pride and pain occupied it together.

When he looked back, he extended his hand.

"Then accept my gratitude without attempting to diminish either part."

Harry took it.

Lucius's grip was firm and careful around his bandaged fingers.

"Thank you," he said.

The words contained no performance and no audience Lucius wished to impress. They unsettled Harry more than the elaborate gratitude of someone else might have done.

"You're welcome," he managed.

Lucius released him. "Do not take this as encouragement to enter any further ancient chambers."

"I wasn't planning to."

Hermione looked over her spectacles.

Harry amended, "I will make a strong effort."

"Deeply reassuring."

Narcissa approached next.

Harry prepared for another handshake. She took his face gently between both hands, examined the cut along his cheek and then drew him against her.

He froze again.

Narcissa did not let go, but neither did she tighten her hold. She simply remained, allowing him to decide what happened next.

Harry's arms rose slowly.

"Thank you for bringing her back," she whispered.

He closed his eyes. Her cloak smelt faintly of winter air and the perfume Draconia carried home from the Manor on her robes.

"She brought herself most of the way."

"Then thank you for meeting her there."

When Narcissa released him, she brushed his hair from his scar with the same unnecessary care she had shown at King's Cross. Harry had no answer for that either.

Lucius turned towards Hermione.

"Miss Granger."

She straightened instinctively.

"Your investigation identified the creature and prevented further deaths. Draconia would not have survived long enough to resist it without what you discovered."

Hermione glanced at her parents before answering. "Harry found the entrance."

"Can none of you just say 'thank you'?"

"We worked together," Hermione said.

Lucius looked from her to Harry and Draconia. "So I am beginning to understand."

He inclined his head to her.

"Thank you, Hermione."

Draconia stared at him.

Lucius noticed. "Do close your mouth."

"I'm checking for signs of possession."

"You are still ill enough for me to take that away."

"There he is," Draconia murmured.

Mr Granger coughed into one hand. Narcissa's lips trembled.

Even Snape looked briefly towards the window.

The laughter which followed was not loud. It entered the ward hesitantly, uncertain whether such a sound belonged there after the night they had survived.

Then Hermione laughed properly. Harry joined her, and Draconia surrendered soon afterwards, one hand pressed to her ribs as though happiness had found muscles she had forgotten how to use.

Madam Pomfrey permitted it for nearly ten seconds.

"Everyone who is not a patient," she announced, "out."

The room erupted in objections.

"Now."

Even Dumbledore obeyed.

The question of Privet Drive returned before Harry was permitted to leave the hospital wing.

It arrived the following afternoon disguised as a discussion among adults.

Harry had discovered that adults often lowered their voices when saying things they intended children to hear only after the decisions had been made. Dumbledore stood near the windows with Lucius, Narcissa and the Grangers, all five positioned far enough from the beds to suggest privacy and close enough that Hermione could distinguish every word.

She had begun repeating the important parts beneath her breath.

"Blood protection," she whispered. "Created by Lily's sacrifice. Anchored through her surviving relative."

Draconia sat propped against the next bed, turning a cup of tea between both hands. "We can hear him."

"I am organising the information."

"You are narrating."

Harry stared at the adults. "They're deciding where I live."

That ended the argument.

Mrs Granger was speaking. "He can stay with us. We have already discussed it with the school, and our guest room—"

"Would be entirely suitable," Dumbledore said. "As would Malfoy Manor, once its protections have been examined. Neither, unfortunately, can replace the enchantment surrounding Harry at his aunt's home."

Narcissa's face had acquired the cold stillness which made Draconia resemble her most strongly. "An enchantment which permitted him to be starved and locked behind bars."

"It was created to prevent Voldemort and those acting in his service from reaching him."

"Then perhaps it should have been created with greater ambition."

Dumbledore accepted the blow without defending himself.

"You knew about the cupboard?" Mr Granger asked.

The Headmaster looked older beneath the winter light. "I knew Harry was not wanted. I believed his physical safety and the protection offered by his mother's blood outweighed the harm of an unhappy childhood."

Harry's fingers became still around the blanket.

"An unhappy childhood," Lucius repeated.

The words were perfectly controlled. Contempt made them dangerous.

"I was wrong," Dumbledore said.

Harry had wanted to hear that for longer than he had known how to name what happened at Privet Drive. Now that the admission had arrived, it did not repair anything. It merely made the failure real enough to belong to someone other than him.

"Does he have to return?" Mrs Granger asked.

Dumbledore looked towards Harry's bed.

The pretence of privacy ended.

"For part of each summer," he said. "Until he comes of age, or until the danger against which the enchantment was raised changes sufficiently that the protection is no longer required."

Harry thought of Tom Riddle taking his wand in the Chamber. Your mother is dead. He thought of green light, a woman's scream and the future Voldemort which the diary had promised still existed somewhere beyond the memory.

"How long is part?" he asked.

"Long enough for the magic to continue recognising the house as your home."

"It isn't my home."

The answer crossed the ward more loudly than he intended.

Dumbledore's eyes remained upon him. "Magic is not always wise enough to understand the distinction."

"Then magic is stupid," Draconia said.

"On that point," Lucius replied, "we are united."

Harry pushed the blankets away and stood. Madam Pomfrey had permitted him to walk as far as the window that morning, though she had not imagined he would use the privilege to enter negotiations concerning his own custody.

"You said I might have to go back," he told Lucius.

"I did."

"You also said I wouldn't be left without a way to leave."

"You will not be."

Lucius turned towards Dumbledore. "The existing arrangement ends."

"The protection—"

"May continue. The isolation will not. Harry will remain there only as long as the magic strictly requires. He will spend the rest of each summer elsewhere."

"With us," Mrs Granger said.

"Or at the Manor," Narcissa added.

Mrs Granger looked at her. "Harry should decide."

Narcissa inclined her head. "Naturally."

Harry stared between them.

The existence of two places willing to argue over who received him felt more impossible than the Chamber.

"I can stay with both," he said carefully.

Hermione leaned towards Draconia. "He thinks diplomacy means agreeing with everyone."

"It has kept him alive this long."

"Has it?"

"Poorly."

The adults continued.

Owls would be permitted to pass through the protection without interference. Hermione's paired parchment would be tested and approved. The Floo connection nearest Privet Drive could not be extended into a Muggle home, but an emergency object could summon assistance without violating the Statute of Secrecy. Someone already living nearby would be asked to observe the house, although Dumbledore did not provide a name.

Lucius insisted upon scheduled collections. Mr Granger demanded a telephone contact which did not rely upon owls, fireplaces or anyone understanding the phrase magical emergency. Narcissa required written conditions forbidding locks upon Harry's bedroom, interference with food and the confiscation of his belongings.

Dumbledore agreed to all of it.

"And if they break the conditions?" Harry asked.

"You leave," Lucius said.

The simplicity of the answer made him look up.

"You do not need to ask. If they hurt you, or stop you writing to us, you use what we give you and leave."

Harry thought of Uncle Vernon counting tins of soup. "What if it ruins the protection?"

"Then we will deal with it," Narcissa said. "You must not stay somewhere you're being hurt just to keep an enchantment intact."

Something in Harry's chest tightened painfully.

He nodded because speaking had become unreliable.

Dumbledore watched him with grief held carefully behind his spectacles. "These safeguards should have existed before now."

"Yes," Harry said.

The Headmaster accepted that too.

Hermione raised her hand from the bed.

Mr Granger stared at her. "You do know this is not a lesson?"

"I have a question."

"You generally do," Harry said.

"What happens if his letters stop again?"

"We have nine procedures," Draconia replied.

Hermione turned towards her. "Eight. Your amendment procedure was never ratified."

"There was no ratification process, which was why an amendment procedure became necessary."

"You cannot use an unauthorised rule to authorise itself."

Harry lowered himself onto the end of Draconia's bed. "Perhaps the basilisk had the right idea."

Both girls glared at him.

"I withdraw the remark."

The adults looked at one another.

Mrs Granger was the first to laugh.

Hogwarts spent the following weeks learning how to become a school again.

The mirrors returned to the corridors one at a time. Rooster cages disappeared from the staircases, and the surviving birds went back to Hagrid's enclosure beneath enough protective enchantments to discourage anything short of a military campaign. Professors stopped escorting students between lessons. Classroom doors remained open, curtains were drawn back and the sound of unaccompanied footsteps ceased being treated as evidence of approaching death.

The castle recovered more quickly than the people inside it.

Harry left the hospital wing after four days. Hermione escaped after two and was returned by Professor McGonagall before reaching the library. Draconia remained for nearly a week, partly because the diary had weakened her magic and partly because Madam Pomfrey had discovered that threats became ineffective once a patient learnt she could not be physically carried back to bed without assistance.

Harry and Hermione visited every afternoon.

They brought class notes, food from the Great Hall and enough ordinary conversation to fill the places Draconia did not wish to examine. Hermione complained about the condition in which Professor Binns had returned their essays. Harry described Colin's increasingly dramatic accounts of awakening from Petrification. Draconia criticised both with such familiar precision that sometimes the hospital wing almost became an ordinary room.

Then a quill leaked across her blankets.

Black ink spread through the white fabric in a sudden branching stain.

Draconia stopped breathing.

Her cup slipped from her hand and struck the floor. She backed against the headboard, staring while the ink continued soaking through the fibres. Harry covered the stain with his sleeve. Hermione removed the bottle, corked it and placed it inside her bag without comment.

Neither told Draconia that there was nothing to fear.

Harry had learnt at Privet Drive that being informed a fear was foolish never made it smaller. He remained between her and the stain until Madam Pomfrey replaced the blankets. Hermione spoke steadily about their Charms homework. By the time clean white fabric covered the mattress, Draconia's breathing had returned to something close to normal.

"I know it was ink," she said.

Hermione adjusted the new blanket. "We know."

"You don't need to behave as though I'm fragile."

Harry retrieved the unbroken cup. "You threw this at the floor."

"I dropped it."

"Very aggressively."

Draconia looked at him.

"There she is," he said.

Her mouth tightened. The expression did not become a smile, but it came close.

The diary's destruction did not erase what had happened inside it. Draconia woke from dreams with words beneath her tongue and sometimes lost several minutes convincing herself that every thought in her head belonged to her. She stopped carrying notebooks. When teachers required written journals, she used loose parchment and left no page folded over another.

She also began telling them things.

Not everything, and never easily. But when a corridor felt familiar without explanation, she said so. When she could not remember whether she had eaten, she asked. Once, outside Potions, she stopped Harry and Hermione before either noticed anything wrong.

"I can hear scratching," she said.

Harry listened. Hermione pressed one ear against the wall.

The sound came from a first-year's escaped quill writing obscenities beneath a suit of armour.

They found the owner and returned it.

Draconia laughed afterwards, a short and startled sound which seemed to surprise her more than either of them.

The Ministry investigation proceeded less cleanly.

Nott denied entering Draconia's rooms. He denied creating an illusion, denied touching her trunk and denied knowing anything about the Chamber. Lucius provided a sworn memory of their meeting. Dobby submitted testimony under Dumbledore's protection. Several officials objected to treating a house-elf's recollection as evidence until Hermione learnt their names and began drafting letters.

Narcissa sent her better parchment.

By Easter, Nott had resigned from three Ministry committees and withdrawn from public life while the investigation continued. No formal conviction followed. Lucius's decision to testify publicly cost him invitations, alliances and the comfortable approval of people he had once considered indispensable.

He did not retract a word.

The first letter he sent Draconia after the hearing contained six pages concerning Ministry procedure and one sentence asking whether Harry was eating properly.

Draconia showed Harry only that sentence.

"Your father is becoming repetitive," he said.

"You're still too thin."

"That isn't the point."

"It's his point."

Hermione inspected Harry's abandoned breakfast. "A rare example of Mr Malfoy being correct."

"This alliance is becoming oppressive."

Spring entered the grounds gradually. Snow withdrew from the mountains, the lake changed from iron-grey to blue and clusters of white flowers appeared beside the path to Hagrid's hut. Students began carrying their books outside. Windows stood open during lessons, admitting sunlight, birdsong and insects with a poor understanding of Professor Snape's patience.

Harry's world widened with the weather.

Hermione's voice returned to every empty place it had occupied in his memory. Draconia became more reliable, although there were days when she withdrew without explanation and returned only after sending a note to say she was safe. The numbness which had followed Hermione's Petrification receded slowly enough that Harry noticed it mostly by absence. Food acquired taste. Sleep began offering rest. He laughed without feeling as though the sound belonged to somebody else.

He did not forget how easily Hogwarts had collapsed around him.

The castle remained his escape, but it was no longer perfect enough to carry the entire weight of being home. It had contained Tom Riddle. Its professors had failed to understand a danger until children named it. Its walls had held Hermione silent and allowed Draconia to disappear inside herself while everyone praised her composure.

Harry loved Hogwarts differently after the Chamber.

He loved it with the knowledge that safety was something people made for one another, not something stone promised merely by surrounding them.

The approaching summer disturbed that safety long before the train arrived.

Each warmer day became a subtraction. Two months until Privet Drive. Six weeks. Twenty-three days. Harry did not count aloud, but Hermione noticed him examining the calendar and Draconia began arranging increasingly elaborate reasons for him to spend August at the Manor.

Neither pretended he was not afraid.

The end-of-year feast arrived beneath a ceiling filled with summer stars.

All four House tables had been restored to their ordinary places. Gold plates shone beneath hundreds of floating candles, windows stood open to the warm evening and the banners suspended behind the staff table displayed Gryffindor red and gold. The points added after the Chamber had carried Harry's House narrowly past Slytherin, although nobody had informed the Slytherins that accepting the result graciously was required.

Professor Snape regarded the banners as a personal insult.

Draconia sat halfway down the green-and-silver table. Her uniform was immaculate, her hair arranged with its former precision and nothing in her posture invited concern. Harry could still identify the differences. The goblet nearest her remained empty because its polished surface caught too much light. She had chosen a seat from which she could see both doors. When Pansy dropped a bottle of ink, Draconia's hand closed around the table before she forced it to relax.

Pansy cleaned the spill without being asked.

She also moved the bottle to the far side of her plate and said nothing about it.

Harry decided that was probably an apology in Slytherin.

Hermione followed his gaze. "You're staring."

"I'm observing."

"For nearly a minute."

"You noticed rather a lot."

Across the Hall, Draconia glanced up and caught him.

One eyebrow lifted.

Harry turned towards his plate.

Hermione looked insufferably pleased. "Very subtle."

"I wasn't doing anything."

"You generally aren't."

Before Harry could request an explanation, Dumbledore rose.

The Hall quietened gradually. He waited until the final conversation diminished and the last fork had been lowered.

"This year," he began, "Hogwarts was threatened by something which depended upon division. It concealed itself behind fear of names, Houses and histories, trusting that suspicion would prevent the people endangered by it from comparing what they knew."

His gaze passed across the tables.

"It failed."

Harry felt hundreds of faces turn.

"Miss Hermione Granger identified the creature when those with greater age and authority had not. Mr Harry Potter found the Chamber and destroyed the object controlling it. Miss Draconia Malfoy resisted that object from within, preserving lives at considerable cost to herself."

The Slytherin table had gone completely still.

Dumbledore had asked Draconia's permission before saying this much. She had agreed only because silence would allow other people to invent something worse.

"Each showed courage," he continued. "More importantly, each trusted someone they had once been given reason to misunderstand."

His eyes settled briefly upon the space between the Gryffindor and Slytherin tables.

"Hogwarts is safest when those spaces can be crossed."

He awarded all three Special Awards for Services to the School.

Applause rose first from Gryffindor. Ravenclaw followed, then Hufflepuff. At the Slytherin table, Pansy Parkinson began clapping with the expression of someone challenging anybody nearby to object. Daphne Greengrass joined her. Within seconds, green sleeves moved along the entire table.

Draconia stared determinedly at the empty goblet before her.

Harry knew she was trying not to cry because her chin acquired exactly the same angle when Narcissa embraced her in public.

He applauded until his palms hurt.

The feast appeared a moment later. Food filled every platter, interrupted by the Weasley twins releasing a flock of enchanted paper basilisks which pursued one another above the Gryffindor table until McGonagall turned them into napkins. Conversation swelled. Dean attempted to persuade Harry to describe the sword for the fifth time that week. Neville wanted to know whether the Chamber contained rare plants. Percy delivered a speech concerning the importance of respecting staff instructions while Fred and George supplied increasingly detailed examples of occasions upon which he had failed to do so.

Across the Hall, Draconia finally looked at him.

She lifted her goblet, noticed the reflection and exchanged it for Pansy's without asking. Pansy opened her mouth, reconsidered and accepted the empty one.

Draconia raised the borrowed cup towards Harry.

He raised his own.

Hermione inserted her goblet between them from several seats away.

None of the three knew precisely what they were toasting.

Survival seemed sufficient.

The Hogwarts Express left the following morning beneath a sky too bright to suit anyone departing the castle.

Students crowded the platform among trunks, owls and parents' instructions already being repeated several hours too early. Steam softened the edges of Hogsmeade Station. Beyond it, the mountains rose green and enormous beneath the sun, free of the snow which had covered them when the Chamber opened.

Harry boarded with Hermione and Draconia.

They found the same compartment they had occupied during the journey north. Hermione claimed the forward-facing seat. Draconia objected on principle. Harry put the luggage overhead and declined to participate in a dispute whose result had been decided the previous September.

The train pulled away.

Hogwarts appeared between the hills one final time, distant towers catching the morning light. Harry watched until the castle passed from view.

His chest tightened.

Hermione noticed. She lowered the book she had opened before the platform disappeared.

"You're staying with us in August," she said.

It was not phrased as a question.

"Part of August," Draconia corrected. "He is also staying at the Manor."

"We haven't chosen dates."

"Mother has."

"Narcissa cannot allocate Harry's entire summer."

"She has left you twelve days."

"Twelve—" Hermione stopped. "Harry, tell her that is unreasonable."

Harry considered the two girls waiting for his judgement. "I think we should discuss the emergency procedures."

"Coward," Hermione said.

"Diplomat," he corrected.

Draconia's mouth curved. "A poor one."

The argument occupied them until the lunch trolley arrived.

They ate too many sweets, played Exploding Snap and attempted a game of wizard chess which Hermione abandoned when Draconia and Harry formed a temporary alliance against her. No one discussed why Draconia preferred cards which destroyed themselves to paper intended to preserve thoughts. They simply played another round.

In the corridor, a compartment door opened and Percy Weasley emerged on his hands and knees.

"Has either of you seen a rat?" he asked with as much dignity as the position allowed.

"Not recently," Harry said.

"Grey, unusually thin and almost offensively ancient." Percy pushed his glasses into place. "He escaped when the sweets trolley passed. Scabbers has belonged to our family for nearly twelve years, and apparently age has improved neither his judgement nor his appetite."

Something scratched beneath Hermione's seat.

All three looked down.

A narrow grey head appeared beside the wall. The rat froze when it saw them. Its whiskers trembled, while one front paw curled close against its chest.

Draconia lowered an empty Chocolate Frog box over it before it could flee.

Percy collected the box with visible relief. "Thank you."

"Twelve years is unusually old for a rat," Hermione said.

"He sleeps a great deal."

"That does not explain it."

"It explains most things about Scabbers."

Percy carried the box away. The rat's claws scraped once across the cardboard and then fell quiet.

Hermione watched him leave. "I should look up the average lifespan."

"You should not investigate the rat," Harry said.

She turned. "Why?"

"The last animal you investigated led to a basilisk."

"That is not a statistically meaningful pattern."

"I find it persuasive."

Draconia dealt the next hand. "For once, I support Potter's academic caution."

The rat disappeared from their thoughts before the train crossed the next bridge.

Afternoon settled warmly against the windows. Hermione read until the motion made her tired. Draconia rested her head against the corner and pretended she had not fallen asleep. Harry watched fields replace mountains and villages gather along the railway line.

He thought about Privet Drive.

The fear did not arrive with the crushing weight it had carried after his first year. It entered more carefully now, through remembered sounds: the bolt crossing his bedroom door, tins placed outside the cat-flap and Uncle Vernon's footsteps stopping beneath the stairs. He imagined the magical world thinning with every mile until the castle became something impossible he had once invented.

Then Hermione's foot pressed against his beneath the table.

Draconia's folded cloak supported his shoulder where he rested against the window.

The fear remained.

It did not have the compartment to itself.

King's Cross Station appeared beyond a cloud of steam.

Families crowded Platform Nine and Three-Quarters. Owls called above the noise, doors slammed and younger students leaned from windows to continue conversations which could have been concluded by walking three feet. Harry lowered the luggage while Hermione checked beneath every seat and Draconia denied having slept.

They stepped onto the platform together.

Mrs Granger reached Hermione first. Mr Granger waited for the initial embrace to end before drawing his daughter into another. Narcissa kissed Draconia's forehead in front of three Slytherins and ignored the expression of profound betrayal it produced.

Lucius stood beside them.

The past months had altered his public life without changing his appearance. His hair remained perfectly tied, his robes severe and his silver cane positioned at precisely the angle which discouraged strangers from approaching. Only the people waiting with him betrayed anything unusual. A Muggle dentist stood on either side of him, while Narcissa spoke quietly with Mrs Granger. Lucius had chosen to occupy their company where anyone passing through the platform could see.

Harry suspected the decision was deliberate.

"Mr Potter," Lucius said.

"Mr Malfoy."

"Your uncle has received the conditions concerning your return."

"How did he take them?"

"Poorly."

Harry smiled despite himself.

Lucius handed him a silver disc no larger than a Knut. One side bore the Malfoy crest. The other had been engraved with an otter and a lion beside the family serpent.

"Break it if you are confined, deprived of food or prevented from contacting anyone," Lucius said. "It will summon assistance. The charm is tied to the wards around the house and will activate only if those conditions have been breached."

Harry turned the disc between his fingers. "What if I lose it?"

"Granger has three."

Hermione lifted a small pouch. "One for me, one for Draconia and one for Mrs Figg."

Harry looked at her. "Mrs Figg?"

"The neighbour Dumbledore arranged to check on you," Hermione said. "Apparently she has experience with unusual situations."

Harry pictured cabbage, photographs of cats and a sitting room which always smelt faintly of stale cake.

"That explains nothing."

"It is more than he intended us to know," Draconia said.

Mrs Granger hugged Harry. She asked permission first, and this time his arms rose before she had to loosen her hold. Mr Granger gripped his shoulder and reminded him that telephones were faster than owls when used correctly.

Narcissa adjusted Harry's collar.

"Write when you arrive."

"It's only Surrey."

"Then the letter should be easy."

Draconia placed a folded sheet of parchment inside his coat. "The schedule."

Hermione stared. "You made a summer schedule without me?"

"You objected to Mother's allocation."

"Because it was unreasonable."

"I reduced the Manor's claim by three days."

"And gave them to whom?"

"Harry."

Harry unfolded the parchment. Two days near the end of July had been left entirely blank beneath the heading Potter may decide without supervision.

"Generous," he said.

"I thought so."

They passed through the barrier still arguing.

Vernon Dursley waited beyond it beside a luggage trolley, his face already purple from the inconvenience of standing among ordinary travellers. He saw Hermione and Draconia first. Then the Grangers emerged, followed by Narcissa and Lucius.

The colour drained from him.

"Boy," he said.

Harry's shoulders tightened.

Draconia's hand closed around his wrist.

Not tightly. Not to prevent him leaving. Her fingers rested there long enough for him to feel the deliberate steadiness of them before she let go.

Hermione pressed a folded square of paired parchment into his palm.

"Nine procedures," she said.

"Eight," Draconia replied.

"We are not restarting this."

"You restarted it."

Harry slipped it into his pocket beside the silver disc. "I'll write tonight."

"Tomorrow," Draconia said. "You are allowed to sleep."

Hermione considered. "Tomorrow morning."

"After breakfast."

"A proper breakfast."

"You realise neither of you will be there to inspect it?"

They answered together.

"We have procedures."

Harry laughed.

Uncle Vernon flinched at the sound as though it offended him. Harry placed Hedwig's cage upon the trolley and followed him towards the station exit.

He looked back once.

The Grangers remained beside the barrier. Narcissa had one arm around Draconia, while Lucius stood close enough to Hermione's father that their shoulders nearly aligned. All six watched him leave.

For most of Harry's life, departure had meant being forgotten by the place behind him.

This time, he knew they were counting the hours until his letter arrived.

Number Four waited at the end of Privet Drive beneath the summer sky, square, polished and determined to appear harmless. The familiar windows reflected the car as Vernon pulled into the drive. Harry's stomach tightened when he saw the front door.

He was still afraid of it closing behind him.

But Hermione's paired parchment lay inside his trunk. Draconia's schedule pressed against his chest, and Lucius's silver disc rested inside his pocket. Beyond the clipped hedges lived a woman with too many cats who, apparently, had been watching more than Harry ever knew.

The magical world had not disappeared merely because he could no longer see it.

And if Privet Drive tried to make him disappear again, someone would notice.
