21

Snape returned at dawn. The Ministry statement and the five newspapers gathered around it still covered the table beside Harry's bed. Beyond the high windows, owls crossed a sky turning slowly from black to blue, their wings briefly visible whenever they passed through the squares of light. Hermione had fallen asleep upright with one hand around an untouched cup of tea. Draconia remained awake in the opposite chair, watching the doors as though enough attention might prevent anything else from entering them.

Harry heard uneven footsteps beyond the doors and closed one hand over the blanket. For an instant, every approaching step still belonged to the graveyard. The residue beneath his skin tightened along the knife wound, answering fear with an ache. Draconia noticed his grip and rose before the footsteps reached the ward.

The doors swung inward. Snape entered alone, his travel cloak gone and only one glove remaining. His hair had been burned short along the left side, exposing reddened skin above his ear, and blood had dried in a dark stain across the collar of his robes. Mud reached both knees. He carried himself with such rigid precision that, for three steps, the injuries almost disappeared inside the habit of refusing to display them.

On the fourth, his knee failed.

Harry threw off the blanket. Hermione woke at the movement, her teacup overturning without spilling; a charm caught the liquid in mid-air while she stared past it towards Snape. Sirius woke in the neighbouring bed before Harry's feet reached the floor. He caught Harry around the waist with one arm and drew his wand with the other.

"Stay."

"He's hurt."

"And you're attached to two potion lines."

Snape braced one hand against the nearest bedframe. The fingers of his ungloved hand left a red print upon the white rail.

"An inspiring display of medical reasoning from both of you."

Madam Pomfrey emerged from her office carrying a tray. She saw Snape, and the tray dropped. Glass broke across the floor, the sharp sound sending Harry's magic hard against his ribs. Draconia caught the edge of his mattress but waited for the flare to pass before touching him.

"Bed," Madam Pomfrey said.

"I have information for the Headmaster."

"You have blood inside your left shoe."

Snape looked down as though the detail had escaped him.

"Then I shall avoid the carpet."

Madam Pomfrey pointed her wand. The empty bed beyond Sirius crossed the ward and struck Snape behind both knees, seating him involuntarily. Linen wrapped around him before pride supplied an objection. Harry almost laughed; the impulse hurt his healing ribs and frightened him with its normality.

Snape turned his head.

"If you tear those stitches, Potter, I will allow her to attach our beds."

Harry stopped moving. Sirius guided him back beneath the blankets while pretending the threat had not worked.

Dumbledore arrived moments later with Remus, Lucius and Moody, Narcissa following at her husband's side. The real Defence professor wore a new wooden leg that had not yet been shaped to his stride; its heel struck the stones a fraction too early with every second step. His surviving eye took in Snape, the burned hair and the single glove.

"Accepted?" Moody asked.

Snape leaned back while Madam Pomfrey cut open his sleeve. The Dark Mark had spread beyond its ordinary borders, black tendrils climbing towards his elbow, interrupted by fresh burns and the angry red imprint of fingers that had closed around his skin.

"In the sense that I remain alive."

Lucius's face tightened.

"Who spoke against you?"

"Nearly everyone."

"Who believed you?"

"The Dark Lord does not require belief where usefulness is available."

Snape met Dumbledore's eyes.

"He accepted that you confined me after Crouch's exposure. He considers my delayed arrival an insult, but not yet a betrayal. Nott argued for my death. Crouch argued that leaving me at Hogwarts would place a servant near Potter."

"Crouch defended you?" Harry asked.

"Crouch defended access to you."

The distinction entered the ward like cold air. Sirius moved closer to Harry's bed, his wand still in his hand, while Snape continued.

"Pettigrew remains with him. Nott has lost the use of one arm from the cauldron and whatever Lucius cast before leaving. The others were dismissed before dawn with instructions to wait."

"For what?" Remus asked.

"Positions. Information. Permission to be useful again."

Moody's mouth twisted.

"Names?"

Snape produced a narrow roll of parchment from inside his remaining sleeve. It had been written in blood. Some names were compressed between others, as though added while he walked; one had smeared where rain, or a hand, had crossed it. Harry watched Moody take the list and tried not to imagine how Snape had found the time to write it.

"Those I recognised," Snape said. "Those whose voices I recognised. Three I suspect entered under concealment charms."

Lucius did not reach for the parchment.

"Did he ask about me?"

Snape's attention moved to him.

"He asked where you would take your family."

Hermione and Draconia stood near Harry's bed. At the question, Draconia's hand found her mother's without either looking down.

"What did you say?" Narcissa asked.

"That you would choose the most expensive possible refuge, then complain about the curtains."

Lucius considered this.

"Credible."

"He thought so."

Snape's voice roughened as Madam Pomfrey poured a clear potion over the Mark. Smoke rose from his arm, and he waited for her to finish before speaking again.

"He knows the Manor is empty. He has forbidden anyone to enter until he decides whether to claim it, destroy it or turn the wards against you."

Draconia's fingers tightened around Narcissa's. Lucius looked towards the pale windows, loss passing through his expression once before becoming strategy.

"The lower archive is separated from the household wards. Narcissa?"

"Dobby emptied it."

"The ancestral vault?"

"Sealed from Gringotts."

"Portrait network?"

"Disconnected."

Lucius nodded. Home reduced itself to assets successfully removed: an archive, a vault, a disconnected collection of painted ancestors. Everything warm enough to miss vanished from the inventory. Harry understood the method. He had counted letters and possessions before allowing himself to think about the people attached to them. It still hurt to watch.

Dumbledore placed one hand upon the end of Snape's bed.

"Did Tom mention Harry?"

Every eye returned to him. Snape looked at the bandages visible above Harry's collar.

"Repeatedly."

"That sounds healthy," Harry said.

"His preoccupation has not improved with embodiment."

"What does he want?" Sirius asked.

"To understand why the wand connection occurred. To locate the protection which allowed Diggory to survive. To learn whether the blood curse retained a link after Harry returned."

"It didn't," Bill said from the doorway.

He carried three rolled ward diagrams and a mug of coffee large enough to suggest he had abandoned sleep permanently.

"The transport break cut its external instruction. What remains is damage without direction."

"And the connection through his scar?" Dumbledore asked.

Bill's expression became cautious.

"That is older magic. Cutting the blood curse's instructions does not prove that the scar has stopped connecting them. I would treat it as a separate danger until we understand it."

Harry thought of the scar dreams: the ruined house, the snake, Voldemort's hunger entering sleep as though his mind were another room with an unlocked door. He remembered waking with the taste of someone else's anger beneath his tongue, unable to prove it had been more than a nightmare. Around him, the ward remained bright with morning, but the cold beneath his skin had not changed.

"I don't want to use it."

"Good," Moody said. "Then we discuss how to defend it before somebody mistakes a connection for an invitation."

Dumbledore looked around the ward. The gathering had formed without invitation: former prisoners, spies, a scarred Auror, the eldest Weasley son, a family that had crossed the graveyard's circle in both directions, and three children already too central to every plan. Morning light reached the floor between them and exposed each exhausted face without kindness.

"Then we begin again," he said.

The Order of the Phoenix returned in a hospital wing. There was no ceremony. Fawkes left his perch, circled once above the beds and vanished in a column of flame. The fire folded after him, leaving a single red feather drifting above Harry's blanket before that vanished too.

The replies began before breakfast. Fawkes returned carrying a strip of parchment tied around one leg, then again with two names and an address. A silver fox entered through the window, spoke a woman's acceptance in a voice Harry did not recognise and dissolved over the medicine cabinet. Other answers took longer. Several red feathers returned blackened at the tips; Dumbledore placed those apart without explaining whether they meant refusal, danger or death.

He named no leader and requested no oath.

"We are not rebuilding precisely what existed," he said. "The first Order was formed for a different war. We failed to protect information, mistook secrecy for trust and asked too many young people to become symbols before allowing them to remain people."

Moody grunted.

"We also won."

"Lily won," Sirius said.

The room quieted. Dumbledore inclined his head.

"Yes."

The old brass key lay in Harry's palm. His mother's sacrifice had become a phrase adults used whenever they could not explain the magic it left behind: a protection, a ward, a reason he survived. In the graveyard, Voldemort had used Harry's blood to step through the part of it that once prevented touch. Lily had still won thirteen years. That mattered even if it had not lasted forever.

The new Order began with practical questions because those were the only kind anyone could bear. Hawthorn Cottage would serve as the first protected meeting place. Sirius held the lease, and its household wards remained under his control rather than the Ministry's. The Malfoys would stay until a second safe house could be prepared. Moody would audit every entrance, Bill would rebuild the boundary against blood-based tracking, and Remus would contact those who distrusted Dumbledore but might believe Cedric. Amelia would remain outside the Order and inside the Ministry, where her independence provided more use than membership.

Lucius offered names, vault routes and the habits of men who would begin pretending they had never answered a summons. Narcissa offered the social network those men's wives considered invisible.

"Women are informed of everything," she said. "It is merely described as gossip so men can continue believing they possess secrets."

Hermione wrote that down. Draconia noticed.

"You're making minutes?"

"Someone should."

"The organisation has existed for four minutes."

"And we still don't know who gets a vote."

Moody looked at Dumbledore.

"Recruit her."

"She is fifteen," four adults said together.

Hermione looked offended by the relevance. Harry listened until exhaustion blurred the edges of the meeting. Decisions crossed the foot of his bed as though it had become a conference table: which names could be trusted, which fireplaces were watched, which families might need moving before the Ministry admitted there was a reason to move them. He caught pieces as the voices moved around him—guard rotations, safe correspondence, the possibility that Crouch still possessed memories extracted from Moody.

Every subject eventually returned to summer. To Privet Drive.

Sirius objected before Dumbledore completed the first sentence.

"No."

"Sirius—"

"Voldemort used his blood. Whatever protection existed has been compromised."

"Changed," Dumbledore said. "Not extinguished."

Harry sat higher against the pillows.

"What does that mean?"

Dumbledore faced him. No one answered on his behalf.

"The protections placed upon your aunt's home still answer to your mother's blood and to your continued right to shelter there," he said. "Voldemort's ritual altered them. It did not extinguish them. He can touch you now, and we must assume defences which once rejected him are no longer sufficient by themselves."

Harry searched his face.

"Why didn't using my blood destroy them?"

"I have theories," Dumbledore said. "Not certainty. The ritual may have produced consequences Voldemort did not intend, but I will not turn an unproved hope into something upon which you are asked to depend."

There was more beneath the answer. Harry heard it without yet possessing the knowledge to name what was missing, and the effort of listening left him tired. He looked at the ward diagrams rather than the expressions gathering around his bed.

"The house hates me."

"Which is why we changed the arrangements last summer," Dumbledore said. "The conditions you and Sirius insisted upon remain in force. Nothing that happened in the graveyard gives us the right to set them aside."

Sirius's hand tightened upon the bedrail.

"Then tell me why he still has to go back."

"The twenty-one-night renewal was already part of the agreement," Dumbledore said. "What we must establish now is how the ritual has affected it. Bill believes the same stay beneath Petunia's roof will stabilise what was disturbed and allow us to carry part of the ward's warning function with Harry afterwards."

"Move it where?" Hermione asked.

Bill unrolled the largest diagram across the foot of the bed. Lines representing Privet Drive curved around Harry's name, with a second structure appearing beside them in blue ink: Hawthorn Cottage.

"Not the original blood ward," he said. "We cannot manufacture Lily Potter's magic or transplant the protection itself. But we may be able to teach a second household to recognise the danger it recognises and resist an intrusion long enough for help to arrive."

Harry's attention settled upon the blue house.

"Because I have a key?"

"Because you believe the key opens your home. Wards notice choices more often than people imagine."

Sirius turned away sharply, and Remus put one hand against his shoulder. Harry studied the diagram. Three weeks at Privet Drive meant twenty-one nights after a year that had ended in a graveyard. The number felt both survivable and impossible, too short to justify the dread beneath his ribs and too long to imagine enduring it.

"What stops them locking me in?" he asked.

Sirius turned back. He knew about the locked room; the Malfoys had seen it for themselves. Harry watched the memory return to their faces and wished he had found a less ordinary way to ask. Narcissa's fingers closed upon the back of Draconia's chair. Lucius became very still.

"They locked you in?" Moody asked.

Harry picked at the edge of his blanket.

"Before second year."

"Bars on the window," Draconia said, looking at Moody. "Locks outside the door. We were there when he came out."

Harry glanced at her. She remembered the rescue, the way he had treated escape as though everyone occasionally required one.

"The existing agreement forbids both," Lucius said. "I explained that to his uncle personally."

Lucius reached for his cane. Narcissa placed one hand over it.

"Later," she said.

"I said nothing."

"You were considering architecture."

"A modest alteration."

"Their house must remain standing for the ward."

"Most of it."

Harry turned to Sirius.

"Memorial crater?"

"Still available."

Despite himself, Harry smiled. Bill tapped the diagram, bringing their attention back to the lines surrounding number four.

"We keep the existing conditions," Bill said. "No exterior locks upon Harry's room. No bars. No restriction of food, communication, medical care or access to the garden. This time we strengthen the monitoring charms upon the threshold and windows. Any violation collapses their concealment from us and activates an extraction Portkey."

"The mirror calls continue," Hermione added.

"Still twice daily," Draconia said.

"Once in the morning and once at night."

"And after nightmares."

Harry glanced at Draconia.

"How would you know?"

"You will call."

It was not a prediction. Lucius took Hermione's notes and added one line beneath them, his writing precise despite the bandage upon his arm.

"The Dursleys will sign the updated agreement."

"They won't understand a magical agreement," Harry said.

"I will explain it without jargon."

"That sounds worse."

"It is."

Dumbledore folded his hands.

"You may refuse."

The room became quiet again. Harry met his eyes, certain for a moment that he had misunderstood.

"Refuse Privet Drive?"

"Yes."

"And lose the protection?"

"Possibly."

"That isn't much of a choice."

"No," Dumbledore said. "But it must remain yours."

Harry examined the blue lines around Hawthorn Cottage. Bill had drawn six small windows and a crooked chimney. Someone, almost certainly Sirius, had added a motorbike beside the garden in black ink. The ward would protect Sirius, Remus, Draconia and her parents while they remained there. Perhaps Hermione when she visited. The graveyard had taught Voldemort that Harry possessed separate places to injure, and strengthening one of those places mattered.

He hated that the logic worked. He hated more that returning frightened him. His fingers pressed into the blanket while he tried to picture the journey ending somewhere other than the locked room he remembered.

"Twenty-one nights," he said.

Sirius's jaw tightened.

"Harry—"

"Exact collection date. Exact time. If they break the agreement, I leave early."

"Immediately," Sirius said.

"Immediately," Bill agreed.

"Letters aren't intercepted."

"Never," Hermione said.

"Hedwig stays in my room without a cage."

"Naturally," Draconia said.

"And nobody tells me I should be grateful they let me remain."

Dumbledore's face held more sorrow than Harry wanted from him.

"No one will."

Harry touched the key beneath his hospital shirt.

"Then I'll go."

Sirius crossed to the bed. He did not argue again.

"Twenty-one," he said.

"Twenty-one."

He held out his hand, and Harry took it. The agreement did not make the fear disappear, but it gave that fear an ending date. Harry held on while Bill began adding clauses to the diagram and Hermione turned to a fresh page.

Sirius did not release him immediately.

"Your room at Hawthorn is waiting," he said. "The blue walls, both windows, the shelf you rearranged in March. Nothing has moved."

Harry glanced at the blue house upon Bill's parchment. The cottage was filling with people who needed somewhere safe to stay.

"You didn't need it for the Malfoys?"

"We needed the guest room and rather more patience than I expected. Remus has claimed the study. Your room is yours."

Harry looked down at the old key. Sirius had given it to him after his birthday party, when a door he could open for himself had seemed almost too much to keep. It still fitted the same lock.

"And the lamp?"

"Still downstairs. It has made no attempt to migrate."

The answer came without hesitation. Guests had changed the household; they had not changed his place in it.

Harry smiled. Sirius squeezed his hand once.

"Twenty-one nights," he said. "Then we go home."

Recovery refused to behave like victory. The cuts closed, but the black branches remained, fading from ink to smoke and leaving thin grey lines beneath Harry's skin. They darkened whenever his scar hurt or sleep became too difficult to escape. Snape called them residual channels. Madam Pomfrey called them one more reason he would remain in bed. Sirius called them evidence during several conversations no Ministry official enjoyed.

Harry called them nothing. Names made things easier to remember.

He woke fighting twice more. The first time, Hermione was asleep in a chair with her cheek against an open book. His sudden movement knocked it to the floor; she woke, said his name and remained where she was until he recognised the hospital wing. It took longer than either of them wanted, but she did not fill the silence with instructions.

The second time, Draconia occupied the chair. Harry opened his eyes to pale hair in moonlight and saw Lucius kneeling in the graveyard, the false image raising a wand towards Cedric. He seized the silver potion and shattered it against the bedframe before Draconia caught his hand.

She did not tell him to calm down.

"The Cup is in Ministry custody," she said. "My father is asleep at Hawthorn Cottage. Cedric is snoring three beds away. You are at Hogwarts."

Harry stared at her.

"Cedric doesn't snore."

"He does tonight."

A low, irregular sound came from behind the adjoining curtain. Harry listened, holding his breath long enough to hear it again, and the graveyard released him.

By the fourth day, Madam Pomfrey allowed him to stand. His legs shook before he reached the end of the bed. Sirius did not offer help until Harry asked, which required more effort from both of them than the walk itself. By the fifth day he crossed the ward. On the sixth, he reached the doors and found half the school waiting in the corridor.

Students had adopted a complicated system intended to satisfy Madam Pomfrey's restriction upon visitors. Only six stood directly outside at any time; the others occupied nearby staircases, classrooms and suits of armour, exchanging places every ten minutes. Hermione denied creating the rota. The handwriting proved otherwise.

Neville brought a small potted plant that folded its leaves whenever Harry's pulse rose.

"It's meant to help with grounding," he explained. "Professor Sprout says you name five things you can see and it changes colour if you're actually looking."

Harry examined the plant. It remained green.

"Stubborn," he said.

The leaves turned blue, and Neville smiled. Luna delivered the first five hundred letters forwarded by the Quibbler, separated into those expressing support, those containing potentially useful information and those likely to release insects.

"Dad says the Ministry asked him to stop printing Cedric's account," she said. "He has doubled the type size."

Colin brought photographs from the return. Harry could not look at most of them, and Colin put the envelope away without apology or disappointment as soon as he noticed.

"There's one you might want later," he said.

He left it face down upon the bedside table. Harry turned it over after everyone slept.

The photograph had been taken badly. Half the frame contained only dark grass. In the centre, Sirius knelt with Harry in his arms while Hermione and Draconia reached them from opposite sides. Cedric moved in the background, alive enough to speak. Narcissa stood beside the Cup, and beyond her the stands rose full of blurred faces. Every person in the image moved towards someone else.

Harry kept it.

Hagrid visited near sunset carrying a wooden box large enough to contain a moderately dangerous animal. Madam Pomfrey inspected it from behind her desk.

"What is inside?"

"Biscuits."

"Why is the box breathing?"

Hagrid looked down. The lid rose once.

"Fresh biscuits."

Madam Pomfrey confiscated the box. Hagrid sat beside Harry empty-handed, his beard combed in a way that only made it more obvious he had pulled at one side throughout the week.

"Knew yeh'd come back," he said.

Harry waited. Hagrid's eyes filled.

"Didn't know in what shape."

Harry reached across the gap between them. Hagrid enclosed his hand so carefully that the gentleness hurt more than pressure would have.

"I'm all right."

"No, yeh aren't."

Harry met his eyes. Hagrid sniffed and wiped his face with a handkerchief the size of a pillowcase.

"Yeh will be," he amended. "That's different."

Harry thought of Cedric saying nearly isn't, Sirius saying fear meant courage had not been accidental, Hermione telling him jokes could stop when they ceased to help. Everyone had apparently decided to become precise. Draconia would be pleased.

"Yeah," he said. "It is."

The Tournament judges met one week after the Third Task. Harry attended in school robes because the task uniform had been sealed as evidence, and because he never wanted to wear black-and-red dragon hide again. Cedric walked beside him on one crutch. Fleur and Krum waited inside the chamber adjoining the Great Hall.

The room had been dressed to resemble the beginning of the Tournament. Four chairs faced a long judges' table, blue fire burned in silver braziers, and the champion banners had been repaired and hung behind their places. Every tear had vanished so neatly that Harry found the perfection insulting. Only the Cup refused to look untouched.

No official chosen by Fudge occupied the table. Madame Maxime represented Beauxbatons; Karkaroff's chair stood empty, his flight before the graveyard summons ended leaving Professor Koppel to speak for Durmstrang. Dumbledore sat for Hogwarts. Amelia represented Magical Law Enforcement in place of Fudge, and Bagman looked as though he would prefer an angry dragon.

The Triwizard Cup rested inside a glass case. Every foreign enchantment had been removed, but its blue surface still showed scratches from the graveyard and a shallow dent where Lucius had sent it into a headstone. Two names glowed around the rim.

CEDRIC DIGGORY

HARRY POTTER

Harry stopped just inside the doors. His body remembered the Portkey before his mind could insist it had been dismantled. Cold pulled beneath his navel, and for half a second blue fire became moonlight over wet graves. The glass case seemed transparent only because a hand was about to reach through it.

Cedric's crutch struck the floor beside him, once: solid wood against stone. Harry breathed. Amelia noticed without announcing she had noticed.

"The case will remain sealed," she said. "No champion is required to touch the Cup."

"An unusually promising start to a meeting about it," Harry replied.

Bagman cleared his throat.

"The contract recorded simultaneous completion."

"We know," Cedric said.

"Yes. Quite. The difficulty concerns whether the Third Task may be considered valid after external interference."

Harry's attention returned to the Cup.

"It transported us to a ritual murder."

"Which argues against validity," Amelia said.

"Strongly," Fleur added.

Bagman wiped his forehead.

"However, voiding the final task would require the standings to revert to the conclusion of the second. Mr Diggory led by one point, followed by Mr Potter, Mr Krum and Miss Delacour."

"Then Cedric wins," Harry said.

"No," Cedric replied.

Harry turned.

"You led."

"You reached the Cup with me."

"Because you stopped."

"Because we agreed."

"You also accumulated more points," Harry said.

"After you were entered against your will."

"That is not the same argument."

"It's adjacent."

Fleur rested her chin upon one hand.

"Do they always do this?"

Krum nodded.

"Since lake."

Dumbledore's eyes moved towards the Cup.

"The object itself appears to have reached a conclusion."

The two names remained equally bright. Amelia opened the Tournament charter, turning past several marked pages before resting one finger upon a line.

"There is precedent for joint victory where champions complete the final condition together. Ravenna Tait and Mathieu Charpentier, 1792."

"Did their Cup also abduct them?" Harry asked.

"No. Their cockatrice escaped and injured all three judges."

"Tradition," Cedric said.

The vote required less time than the argument. They were declared joint champions, the prize divided evenly. The Ministry offered additional compensation, which Amelia advised both families to accept before Fudge remembered indignation. Fleur received recognition for helping an injured competitor during the Third Task before her own withdrawal; Krum received the highest individual score awarded for wand work in seventy years. All four names would remain upon the Tournament record.

Bagman produced the winner's parchment. Cedric signed first and handed Harry the quill, but Harry hesitated over the line. A bead of ink gathered at its point and fell beside his name. The word Champion had followed him all year as accusation, obligation and threat. It had cost sleep, friendships and blood. Accepting it now felt like agreeing the year had been worth what it took.

Cedric leaned nearer.

"It means we reached it," he said quietly. "Nothing else."

Harry studied the two names upon the Cup, then signed. The parchment warmed beneath his hand. Somewhere inside the case, the Cup gave one low metallic note, and his magic recoiled before settling again. Cedric remained beside him until it did.

Dumbledore asked him to stay. The others left the chamber slowly, Cedric touching Harry's shoulder on his way through the doors. Fleur collected Krum, who had begun examining the scratches upon the Cup for evidence the object possessed tactical opinions. Amelia took the signed parchment, and Bagman escaped before anyone could discover another administrative task.

Harry remained beside the glass case, watching the joined names glow around its rim. Dumbledore came to stand opposite him.

"Do you regret signing?"

Harry considered the question.

"I regret being entered."

"That was not what I asked."

"No." Harry touched his own name through the glass. "I think Cedric would have followed me around until I did."

"Hufflepuff persistence is frequently underestimated."

"That seems like a polite word for it."

Dumbledore smiled, though the expression faded when his gaze reached the thin scar crossing Harry's cheek.

"There are several things I should have said before this year ended."

Harry lowered his hand.

"That sounds ominous."

"It is intended as an apology."

"Those can be ominous."

"So I am learning."

Dumbledore drew out the chair Amelia had vacated and moved it beside Harry, lowering himself until they faced the Cup from the same side. He left the judges' table behind him, its neat arrangement of papers now slightly disturbed.

"Barty Crouch Junior taught you for most of a year," he said. "He observed your fears, rewarded specific forms of risk and arranged circumstances in which his guidance became useful. Discovering what he was may make every skill you learned beneath his face feel contaminated."

Harry thought of the false Moody praising speed, encouraging isolation and placing solutions where he could find them. Even now, useful movements sometimes arrived with Crouch's voice attached.

"Some of it was."

"Yes."

The agreement surprised him. Dumbledore folded his hands over the handle of his wand.

"It would be comforting to claim that Crouch's manipulation was entirely false and that no wisdom came from him. Unfortunately, effective manipulation often contains truth. You did need to learn to think under pressure, and to protect yourself as fiercely as you protect your friends. Constant vigilance, however crudely repeated, is not useless advice."

"He used all of that to send me to Voldemort."

"He did."

"And you didn't know."

Dumbledore looked towards an empty chair, his expression suggesting he was seeing another room entirely.

"No."

Harry waited for an explanation. None came.

"You trusted him," he said.

"I trusted Alastor. Crouch exploited the distinction before I understood one existed."

"Everyone keeps saying they should've noticed."

"Many of us should have."

"Hermione thinks she missed something. Draco thinks the diary gave Nott the information he used. Sirius thinks he should have taken me out of the Tournament. Cedric thinks he should've made me drop the shroud. Lucius thinks he should have changed sides earlier."

Harry's voice sharpened.

"There are too many people trying to become responsible for what Voldemort did."

Dumbledore's eyes returned to him.

"Including you."

Harry turned his face aside.

"That is less convenient."

"Truth often is."

The Cup reflected them together, an old man bent by consequence and a boy carrying injuries meant to make him feel smaller than he was. Harry watched their blurred shapes in the blue metal before trusting himself to speak again.

"I saw my parents."

Dumbledore became still.

"During the wand connection."

"Yes."

"They came out of his wand. Not ghosts. Something else."

"Echoes," Dumbledore said. "The wand was forced to reveal the spells it had performed. A Killing Curse takes a life, but magic remembers the shape of what it was made to destroy."

Harry remembered Lily reaching without being able to touch him, and James telling him when to run. Their voices remained clearer than some parts of the escape.

"Were they real?"

Dumbledore considered before answering.

"Not alive. Not whole. But I do not believe that makes what they gave you unreal."

Blue metal reflected firelight inside the glass.

"Mum told me to stay."

"A wise instruction."

"Dad said you all talk too much."

Dumbledore's eyebrows rose.

"Also plausible."

Harry smiled faintly. Then the question that had followed him from the graveyard returned, pushing its way through the brief warmth of remembering his father's voice.

"Voldemort knows they matter."

"Hermione and Draconia?"

Harry nodded.

"Sirius too. Remus. The Malfoys. He knows where to hurt me now."

"He knew that before he entered your mind. What he lacked was detail."

"That isn't reassuring."

"No." Dumbledore's voice remained gentle. "He may know what hurts you, Harry. That doesn't mean he understands the people who will come when you're hurt. They crossed a school for you, entered a graveyard, contradicted a government before breakfast. He still doesn't know what to do with that."

Harry thought of the handmade paper.

We heard them.

"You knew the scar might connect us," he said.

Dumbledore did not pretend uncertainty.

"I suspected."

"You didn't tell me everything."

"No."

"Next year will be worse."

"In some respects."

Harry faced him fully.

"Then no more discussing me in rooms I'm not allowed inside."

Dumbledore's gaze sharpened.

"Some information concerns the safety of others."

"Then keep their secrets. Not mine."

The refusal sat between them. Harry did not soften it. Across the table, the braziers burned with the same steady light as before, but Dumbledore looked older than he had at the start of the Tournament.

"I cannot promise never to make another mistake," he said. "I can promise to tell you what I know about your mind, your protection and your future, even when I have no easy answer."

It was not the absolute agreement Harry wanted. It was more honest than one, and he held on to the distinction before asking the next question.

"And if the connection gets worse?"

Dumbledore did not require him to define which connection.

"Then we teach you to defend your mind, and we study every change together."

"What if being near me becomes dangerous?"

The question was not entirely about magic. Harry heard that only after asking it, and Dumbledore seemed to hear it too.

"Distance may sometimes be necessary," he said. "Silence should not be. If I believe there is danger, I will tell you what I can and why I am acting. I will not ask you to interpret absence as care."

Harry thought of unanswered letters, empty chairs, adults leaving rooms before deciding he was old enough to enter them.

"Hermione will write that down," he said.

"I assumed she already had."

Dumbledore stood. Before leaving, Harry looked once more at the joined names upon the Cup.

"Professor?"

"Yes?"

"If Mum won the first war, what did I do?"

Dumbledore followed his gaze.

"You ensured the next did not begin with a funeral."

Harry thought of Cedric's pulse. For the first time, he allowed that to be enough.

The final feast took place beneath the colours of all three schools. Hogwarts banners hung beside the pale blue of Beauxbatons and the red of Durmstrang, though no decorations celebrated victory. The Triwizard Cup stood upon the staff table between Harry and Cedric's engraved shields, no longer a Portkey and not yet innocent enough for either boy to touch.

Harry entered the Great Hall between Hermione and Draconia, and conversation stopped. He had expected staring. Hogwarts had spent four years staring whenever his life became public property; he knew how to lower his head, choose a distant wall and cross a room as though whispers did not alter the air.

This silence was different. Neville stood, Susan Bones following. Then Luna, Pansy, Ginny and the Hufflepuff Quidditch team. Beauxbatons students rose in pale rows, Durmstrang beside them. Benches scraped across stone until everyone in the Hall was standing.

No one cheered. Harry could not have endured cheering. They simply remained on their feet while he walked to the Gryffindor table, his steps sounding too clearly in the quiet.

Draconia's place waited beside him despite the green upon her robes. Snape had arranged it without explanation, and Hermione sat on his other side. Only after all three were seated did the Hall follow. Harry found the photograph in his pocket and held its edge beneath the table, remembering every person in it moving towards someone else.

Dumbledore rose. The doors closed behind the last professor. Snape sat at the staff table, pale and expressionless, his burned hair hidden by shadow. Moody occupied the other end with both hands around a metal flask no one had dared inspect. Lucius and Narcissa sat near Sirius and Remus. The Grangers had remained for the final week and looked deeply uncomfortable upon raised chairs where an entire school could observe them.

"This year began," Dumbledore said, "with a promise of international cooperation. It ends with proof of why such cooperation matters."

His voice reached every corner without becoming loud.

"The Ministry has issued its account of what occurred during the final task. Many in this Hall have read it. Many have also read Cedric Diggory's testimony and the supporting accounts offered by Lucius Malfoy, Fleur Delacour and Viktor Krum."

Faint movement passed through the tables.

"I will not ask you to choose the version which causes the least fear. Fear is not evidence of falsehood."

Dumbledore looked directly at Harry.

"Voldemort has returned."

No one gasped this time. The name had already crossed the pitch in Cedric's voice, the school in handmade papers and several countries in print. Repetition did not make it harmless, but it made silence less available. Harry kept his fingers upon the photograph's edge.

"He returned through a ritual planned by Barty Crouch Junior and the elder Nott. Peter Pettigrew assisted him. Others answered his summons. Some have already chosen where they stand. Many will spend the coming months claiming no choice is required."

Lucius looked towards the Slytherin table. Several students lowered their eyes; Draconia did not.

"Choice is always required," Dumbledore said. "Refusal to name it does not make it absent."

His gaze moved across the four Houses.

"We will not discuss every injury endured in the graveyard. Those details belong first to the people who survived them. But you should know this: Cedric Diggory lives because Harry Potter acted before hatred could complete a sentence. Both returned because they refused, separately and together, to accept the death prepared for them."

Harry stared at the empty plate before him. The Hall blurred, candlelight spreading into indistinct gold above the polished wood.

Cedric stood from the Hufflepuff table.

"Professor."

Dumbledore stepped aside. Cedric remained among his House, one hand resting upon the shoulder of a first-year beside him.

"Harry doesn't like that version."

A few uncertain laughs moved through the Hall. Harry raised his head, and Cedric met his eyes.

"He thinks Voldemort returning means everything else which happened that night became smaller. It didn't."

The silence deepened.

"The first curse in the graveyard was meant to kill me. Harry stopped it. When the second struck, his magic hid my pulse beneath his and built a shroud which made them believe I was dead. He maintained it while they hurt him for refusing to let it fall."

Cedric's hand tightened upon the first-year's shoulder.

"When the shroud finally broke, they discovered I was alive and attacked me again. Nott Stunned me. Mr Malfoy has testified that Harry then had the Cup within reach and refused to leave me behind. I remember waking at Hogwarts with Harry's rope still around me. The part I could not witness does not make what he did any smaller."

His voice did not break. His mother's did; she sat beside Amos at the staff table, crying openly into one hand.

"You will hear people say Harry is confused," Cedric continued. "You will hear that he wanted attention, that the Tournament damaged his judgement or that adults know better because fear makes fourteen-year-olds unreliable. I am seventeen. I was there. I remember."

His gaze moved towards the enchanted ceiling.

"Harry saved my life. Voldemort returned. Both are true."

Cedric sat. For one second, no one moved. Then the Hufflepuff table began striking open palms against the wood in one steady rhythm, and Harry recognised what they were making before Gryffindor joined.

A heartbeat.

Ravenclaw found it. Slytherin entered last, led by Pansy and Daphne while Draconia struck the table beside Harry. Beauxbatons and Durmstrang followed until the sound filled the Hall. He felt it beneath his hands, through the bench and into the scars under his skin. For one disorienting instant, the rhythm resembled the second heartbeat he had carried through the graveyard. This one asked nothing from his magic. It was simply there.

Hermione leaned close enough that her shoulder met his. Draconia did the same from the other side. The rhythm continued until Dumbledore lowered his head, then stopped together, leaving the candles flickering in the sudden quiet.

Dinner appeared. Harry managed half a plate, and no one told him it was not enough. Hermione quietly added a bread roll to his pocket for later. Draconia added two.

"I can see both of you," Harry said.

"Then the head injury is improving," Draconia replied.

For the first time since the maze, Harry laughed without it hurting.

Hermione and Draconia collected him after the feast, though neither described it as collecting. Hermione asked whether he wanted to walk. Draconia appeared at the Entrance Hall doors carrying his coat, which suggested the answer had already been incorporated into the plan. They led him away from the crowd emptying towards the dormitories and onto the moonlit grounds.

The maze had been removed. Only a square of flattened earth remained upon the Quidditch pitch. The Ministry tents had disappeared that afternoon, leaving pale marks in the grass and a forgotten length of white rope near the boundary. From the castle steps, the field looked almost ordinary. Harry turned away first, and the girls followed him towards the lake.

Summer had warmed the stones along the shore. Moonlight crossed the water in a path broken by small waves, and far from the castle the giant squid lifted one tentacle, disturbed the reflection and sank again. Hermione walked on Harry's right, Draconia on his left. Neither touched him, though both adjusted their pace whenever his weakened leg shortened a step.

Harry became suspicious.

"Is this an intervention?"

"No," Hermione said too quickly.

"There aren't enough of us," Draconia added.

"You've managed with two before."

"Those were informal."

"There are categories?"

Hermione looked across him. "We should have made categories."

"Do not begin a list now."

"I wasn't."

"You had the expression."

Harry stopped near the beech tree where he and Sirius had walked before the task. The brass key rested beneath his shirt, its crooked tooth pressing against the newest scar upon his chest.

"What's wrong?"

Both girls answered.

"Nothing."

Harry waited. Hermione looked at Draconia; Draconia looked towards the lake. His suspicion became alarm.

"Did one of you commit a crime?"

"Not recently," Draconia said.

"Draco."

"You wanted reassurance."

Hermione sat beneath the tree and arranged her skirt over the grass. Draconia remained standing for another moment before lowering herself beside her. Harry took the place between them, leaning carefully against the trunk so the bark did not press upon the wounds on his back.

The silence that followed had edges. It was not the easy kind from the hospital, and Harry recognised it as the unfinished thing that had occupied the days before the maze. Leaves stirred overhead while both girls seemed to consider how best to begin.

"You were going to tell me something," he said.

Hermione drew her knees slightly closer.

"Yes."

"Before summer."

Draconia glanced at him. "You remember."

"I nearly died, Draco. I didn't lose the entire week."

She flinched, and Harry regretted the words.

"Sorry."

"Don't." Hermione's reply was immediate. "You don't have to say sorry every time we remember it."

"That sounds like a rule."

"It may become one."

"What number are we on?"

"Nine remains disputed," Draconia said.

"Nine established an amendment process."

"Without using an existing amendment process."

His gaze moved between them.

"Have you delayed this conversation for four years because you're still arguing about rule nine?"

They stopped. He had meant it as a joke, but Hermione was staring at a patch of clover near her shoe.

"Not four years."

"How long?"

"That," Draconia said, "is one of the disputed details."

Harry became aware of his heartbeat. It was not the dangerous rhythm that activated the hospital wards; this felt warmer and somehow more difficult to breathe through. Hermione folded and unfolded one corner of her sleeve before looking at him.

"At the Yule Ball, I was angry because you hadn't asked me."

Harry remembered blue fabric, Viktor's hand at her back and the staircase afterwards.

"You already told me that."

"I told you that you could have asked. I didn't tell you why I wanted you to."

He turned to Draconia.

"And you?"

"I spent most of December becoming offended whenever you failed to understand implications I had made deliberately impossible to identify."

"That does sound like you."

"Thank you."

"It wasn't—never mind."

Draconia's mouth curved, but the humour did not protect her for long.

"I wanted you to ask me too."

The lake moved quietly beside them. Harry understood each sentence individually; together, they became something his mind refused to approach directly.

"Both of you."

"Yes," Hermione said.

"At the same time."

"More or less."

"And you knew?"

"Not initially." Hermione looked at Draconia. "We suspected. Then we argued."

"Repeatedly," Draconia said.

"You were jealous."

Both girls examined him. Harry raised his hands.

"I'm injured, not dead."

Hermione laughed despite herself, and Draconia looked impressed.

"He has been paying attention."

"Intermittently," Hermione said.

"I pay attention."

"You thought I wanted Viktor to be my boyfriend because he sat near me in a library."

"He looked committed to the chair."

"And you believed Draconia attended the ball with Blaise because she admired his conversation."

"I didn't hear any of it."

"Exactly."

Harry pulled grass between his fingers.

"Luna knew," he said.

Hermione's expression changed. "Knew what?"

"That it wasn't romantic. She asked before she agreed. She also knew I spent most of the ball watching both of you."

Draconia's eyes narrowed, not at Harry but at a memory. "Lovegood asked whether I intended to continue glaring at her after midnight."

"What did you say?"

"That I was examining the ceiling behind her."

"There was no ceiling behind her," Hermione said. "There were windows."

"I was distressed."

Harry rubbed one hand over his face.

"She deserved a better evening."

"She appeared to enjoy herself," Hermione said.

"She did. I didn't treat her badly. But I wasn't entirely there either." Harry looked down at the shredded clover between his fingers. "By the time I asked her, I had spent an afternoon failing to ask either of you. Then I saw you with Viktor and Blaise and decided I had missed whatever chance I was meant to notice."

Draconia glanced towards Hermione, satisfaction flickering across her face before she caught it.

"You were jealous," she said.

"That sounded far too pleased."

"It answered a longstanding question."

"You have just admitted to spending most of the ball glaring at Luna."

"My jealousy was never in doubt."

Hermione laughed, but guilt followed it.

"Mine wasn't particularly dignified either."

"You told me I had the emotional perception of a teaspoon."

"You haven't done much to change my mind."

"A teaspoon would have avoided the staircase."

"Only by being carried."

Harry's smile faded more slowly this time. There was room in the evening for it to disappear without taking everything warm along with it.

"What happened when you argued?"

Hermione exchanged a glance with Draconia.

"We stopped pretending jealousy would make either of us disappear," she said.

Harry stared at her.

"You made rules."

The pause that followed was incriminating.

"They are primarily concerned with what we will not do to one another," Hermione said.

"And with the fact that neither of us intends to withdraw merely to simplify your life," Draconia added.

"That sounds less like a boundary."

"It is a clarification."

Hermione drew one knee closer. "If you eventually decide you want to be with one of us—or neither of us—the other does not make you feel guilty for it."

The sentence removed the humour from the night. Harry heard what it had cost them to agree to it. Hermione stared towards the lake; Draconia's composure had gone thin enough that moonlight showed the fear beneath it. Neither was frightened merely of losing him. They were frightened of losing one another in the attempt to keep him.

"Did you agree not to compete?" Harry asked.

Hermione pressed her lips together. Draconia lifted her chin.

"No."

Hermione gave her an exasperated look. "We agreed not to be cruel."

"That is not the same thing."

"I noticed."

"Are you objecting?" Draconia asked Harry.

He considered the question with what remained of his clover.

"I'm trying to decide whether I should be afraid."

"Probably," Hermione said.

Draconia appeared pleased by the answer. Harry opened his hand, finding nothing but green flecks against his palm.

"What are you telling me?"

Draconia closed her eyes for one brief second. When she opened them, every defence had gone.

"I like you, Harry," she said. "I keep thinking about you, and it's getting difficult to pretend I don't."

Harry forgot how breathing worked. Hermione turned towards Draconia.

"You said we would phrase it carefully."

"I avoided the word love."

"Saying you avoided it rather defeats the purpose."

"He asked a direct question."

"You could have allowed me to answer first."

"You have had four years."

"So have you."

Harry's pulse reached his throat. Hermione turned back to him, her eyes shining, but held his gaze.

"I like you too," she said. "Not only as my best friend. I think I have for longer than I knew what to call it."

The night became very quiet. Harry had imagined confessions before, not frequently and never well. In those vague versions, a single person spoke, he understood his own answer, and no one had recently been tortured by a resurrected Dark Lord. Reality had preserved none of the convenient details.

"Oh," he said.

Draconia's eyebrows rose. Hermione covered her face.

"Four years," she said through both hands, "and all you can say is oh."

"I haven't finished."

"You appeared to."

"I forgot the next word."

"Do you know it now?" Draconia asked.

"Several. None are organised."

That honesty quieted them. Harry looked towards the castle, where warm light filled hundreds of windows. Behind one, adults planned a war. Beyond the mountains, Voldemort possessed Harry's blood and enough knowledge to find the shape of his fear. Beside the lake, Hermione and Draconia waited without asking him to become less confused than he was.

"He found separate places," Harry said.

Hermione lowered her hands.

"You told us."

"I didn't know they were there before. Not properly." He struggled for accuracy. "I knew I felt different when each of you was with someone else. I knew I hated the ball. I knew when Hermione was Petrified it felt like the castle had stopped being real, and when the diary took Draco, I couldn't think about anything except getting her back."

His hands had begun shaking. He pressed them into the grass, feeling the cool earth beneath it.

"In the maze, everything kept separating us. In the graveyard, he found the places you occupied and tried to turn them into weapons. I knew the pictures were wrong because neither of you reached for the other. That was the mistake."

By the end, soil had gathered beneath his fingernails and the shaking had eased. Hermione reached for his left hand; Draconia took the right. They did it without discussion, and Harry looked down at their fingers before trusting himself with the next sentence.

"I think I like both of you."

Hermione's fingers went still. Draconia watched him with unnerving concentration.

"Not because I can't tell the difference," he continued. "Because I can. It's different with each of you, and I don't know what either difference is supposed to mean yet."

The admission left him more exposed than shouting defiance across a graveyard had. There was no enemy here to make courage simple, only two people capable of being hurt by an answer he did not yet possess.

"That is an answer," Hermione said softly.

"An incomplete one," Draconia said.

Hermione looked across Harry. "We agreed not to pressure him."

"I am describing its condition."

"You are grading it."

"You can do better."

Harry looked between them.

"This is beginning to sound less like affection and more like an essay."

"You would know if it were affection?" Hermione asked.

"Apparently not."

Draconia's mouth curved.

"Promising. Self-awareness."

Harry leaned his head against the tree. Happiness came carefully now, testing the ground before placing weight upon it. This one entered with fear attached: the knowledge that he possessed another future Voldemort might try to ruin. Harry let it enter anyway, holding both girls' hands while the leaves moved overhead.

"So what happens?" he asked.

"We stay friends," Hermione said. "I'm not losing either of you over this."

Draconia nodded once. "And stop assuming we only ask you places because we've made a timetable."

"Some of them are."

"Mine rarely are."

"You invited me to inspect your father's ward ledger."

"Alone."

"Because Hermione had Arithmancy."

Draconia turned to Hermione. "He may be beyond intervention."

"We knew the work was difficult."

Harry laughed, startling birds from the beech tree. It pulled at his ribs, but the pain arrived beside something lighter rather than replacing it. Hermione waited until the sound faded.

"We don't need you to decide tonight," she said. "Or because the graveyard frightened all of us into believing every feeling must be settled before the next danger."

"I would prefer progress before graduation," Draconia said.

"That is three years away."

"I've been patient."

"You glared at a perfectly innocent Ravenclaw for three hours."

"Patiently."

Harry looked down at their joined hands.

"There is a list, isn't there?"

Hermione looked guilty. Draconia looked proud.

"Can I see it?"

"No," both girls said.

"That seems unfair."

"You're in it," Hermione said. "Of course you're biased."

"It is also unfinished," Draconia said.

"Because you keep adding loopholes."

"Because you keep confusing courtesy with surrender."

Harry's eyes moved between them.

"You really didn't agree not to compete."

"We agreed not to make you a prize," Hermione said.

Draconia's thumb moved once across his knuckles.

"That does not prevent us making our intentions clearer."

The warmth in Harry's face returned. Hermione saw it, and a small, almost daring smile replaced her embarrassment.

"May I kiss you?" she asked.

Harry's mind emptied with impressive efficiency.

"Where?"

Draconia made a choked sound. Hermione turned scarlet.

"Your cheek, Harry."

"Right. Yes. That seems—yes."

She leaned close. Her hair brushed his jaw before her lips touched the uninjured place beside his cheekbone, warm and brief and careful enough that the moment became more intimate rather than less. Hermione drew back, and Harry stared at the lake because it appeared unlikely to comment.

Draconia released his hand.

"Potter."

He turned. For once, she asked without hiding the question inside anything sharper.

"May I?"

Harry nodded. Draconia straightened his glasses with two fingers and kissed his other cheek, then settled back upon the grass with perfect composure.

"You copied me," Hermione said.

"I improved the execution."

"You nearly displaced his glasses."

"They were crooked."

Both looked at Harry. He was fairly sure language remained available somewhere, but none reached him.

"Oh," he said again.

Hermione began laughing. Draconia lasted three seconds before joining her, and Harry did not mind being laughed at while Hermione's fingers found his again and Draconia leaned close enough for their shoulders to touch.

Nothing had been decided. No one had won or been rejected. The future remained untidy, and for once uncertainty did not resemble a threat. It resembled time. They sat beneath the tree with the castle behind them and the dark grounds open ahead, their bodies leaning together, warm and alive. No promise made what waited beyond Hogwarts safe. The lake carried the moon in broken pieces and returned it whole whenever the water stilled.

For now, that was enough.

The last morning arrived inside open trunks. Harry's possessions covered half the Gryffindor dormitory: books upon the bed, robes hanging from curtains, empty potion bottles, protective charms and notes from three different healers forming an untidy collection upon the floor. He stood in the middle of it and attempted to remember how packing worked.

His body had improved faster than his concentration. The cuts upon his face and arms had closed into narrow pink scars, while those beneath his robes remained tender. He could walk without help, though stairs left him tired and sudden noises still caused magic to collect painfully behind his ribs. Madam Pomfrey had released him with nine potions and seventeen restrictions. Hermione copied the restrictions twice, Draconia annotated both copies, and Sirius ignored them to create a third list titled THINGS WHICH ACTUALLY MATTER, every item of which already appeared on Hermione's.

Harry placed all three in his trunk. Neville entered carrying Trevor and stopped in the doorway.

"Are you moving rooms?"

"Packing."

Neville examined the bed.

"Have you considered putting things inside the trunk?"

"Recently."

Together, they folded robes badly. Neville rescued two plants from beneath a stack of books. Harry found the emergency whistle in a sock, three uneaten biscuits in separate pockets and a note from Draconia.

The green potion is for pain. The silver is for the curse residue. If you confuse them, the result is unlikely to be fatal but may make you briefly transparent.

Beneath it, Hermione had added:

The bottles are labelled, Harry.

He wrapped both potions in the note. At the bottom of the trunk, he placed Colin's photograph, the student newspaper and the joint champion's shield. The money remained inside a new Gringotts pouch. He had not decided what to do with it, and for once no one encouraged him to give it away quickly enough to avoid feeling he possessed something. The brass key stayed around his neck.

When the dormitory emptied, Harry looked once at the bed he had occupied for four years. Gryffindor Tower had never become home in the complete way Hogwarts itself had. Too much of his life happened elsewhere: libraries shared with girls from two Houses, Hagrid's hut, hospital beds, hidden corridors and the edges of a lake. Still, the red curtains had enclosed nightmares and mornings. The windows had shown him a world larger than Surrey. This bed had been the first place where no one objected to his existence taking up space.

Harry closed the curtains and went downstairs.

Farewells filled the Entrance Hall. Beauxbatons students carried pale luggage towards their carriage, where the winged horses stamped upon the drive, irritated by the number of people attempting to photograph them. Durmstrang trunks floated across the grounds towards the lake; the ship had risen through still water before dawn, its masts standing dark against the brightening sky.

Fleur found Harry near the marble staircase and embraced him with enough care to avoid his ribs and enough force to make the care largely theoretical.

"You will write," she said.

"Hermione has arranged it."

"To me, not only to 'Ermione."

"Then you should probably arrange it."

Fleur drew back and examined him.

"You are very difficult."

"I've heard."

Gabrielle stepped from behind her sister and held out a small parcel. Inside lay a blue ribbon charmed never to tear.

"For next time," she said.

Harry looked at Fleur.

"There won't be a next time," Fleur translated.

Gabrielle nodded solemnly. Harry accepted the ribbon, running it once between his fingers before folding it back into its wrapping.

"Good."

Krum's farewell contained fewer words. He shook Harry's hand, then Cedric's.

"Next competition," he said, "we choose chess."

"Hermione would win," Harry replied.

Krum considered her where she stood beside Draconia.

"Yes."

"You accepted that rather quickly," Cedric said.

"I have played her."

Hermione's smile followed him towards the lake. There was affection inside it, but Harry no longer mistook affection for an answer to a question no one had asked.

Cedric waited until the foreign students left. His crutch had been reduced to a cane, and the loop of grass from the graveyard circled one wrist beneath his sleeve. Harry noticed. Cedric followed his gaze.

"I'm keeping it."

"You said."

"You looked as though you might object again."

"I was considering it."

"Consider quietly."

Harry smiled. Cedric held out his hand. They had shaken hands before the Tournament, two Hogwarts champions agreeing that competition need not make them opponents. This time, Harry ignored the hand and hugged him. Cedric froze, then closed one arm hard around his back.

"You're crushing my injuries," Harry said into his shoulder.

Cedric released him immediately.

"Sorry."

"That was a joke."

"Bad one."

"They become worse when I'm frightened."

Cedric's expression softened.

"Write."

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"It's possible we know you."

Amos called his son from the doors. Cedric stepped away, then turned back before Harry could collect his trunk.

"Harry."

"Yeah?"

"You won too."

Before Harry could argue, Cedric left.

The Hogwarts Express carried them south beneath a bright, indifferent sky. The trio claimed a compartment near the middle, Hermione sitting facing forward while Draconia objected to the automatic assignment on principle, lost a coin toss and occupied the seat opposite her. Harry took the window, where he could see open fields rather than the corridor whenever footsteps passed.

For the first hour, everything felt different. Hermione's knee touched his beneath the table, and neither moved. Draconia watched, then stretched one polished shoe across the floor until it rested beside Harry's. He became aware of every glance and accidental contact as though the confession by the lake had altered gravity. It was unbearable, and also rather nice.

At eleven, Luna entered carrying a deck of Exploding Snap and three chocolate frogs.

"You look tense," she observed.

"We're not," all three said.

Luna sat. The first explosion singed Hermione's eyebrow; the second sent Draconia's cards beneath the seats. Harry laughed hard enough to lose his turn and spent five minutes insisting sabotage should qualify as a valid strategy. By lunch, Neville and Ginny had joined them. Pansy appeared in the doorway, announced she was only looking for Draconia, and remained through two games, a trolley visit and an argument about whether Chocolate Frog cards reflected institutional bias.

The children who had signed WE HEARD THEM spent the afternoon behaving like children. They traded sweets, insulted one another's card technique and watched villages pass. Ginny imitated Fudge's expression upon discovering the student newspaper and nearly fell from the seat. Neville's grounding plant turned purple when Pansy called it decorative cabbage. Harry ate a full lunch without anyone monitoring each bite. Hermione noticed and said nothing.

As the afternoon lengthened, the others left for their own compartments. Luna took the scorched cards. Pansy told Draconia she expected an explanation by September and departed before learning whether one would be provided. The compartment quieted, leaving the steady sound of the wheels beneath them.

Harry rested his head against the window. Fields became towns, and the farther they travelled from Hogwarts, the more his body prepared for Privet Drive. His shoulders tightened. Hunger vanished. He began calculating how much food Narcissa had placed in his trunk and whether the floorboard beneath his bed still lifted cleanly.

Hermione closed her book.

"You don't have to hide the food."

Harry kept watching the window.

"I know."

"You were planning where."

"The floorboard is reliable."

Draconia moved from the opposite bench and sat beside him, leaving no gap.

"The agreement forbids withholding meals."

"Agreements don't make people kind."

"No," Hermione said. "They make cruelty observable."

Harry looked at her. She held up a small mirror, its twin waiting inside his pocket. Sirius had rebuilt the pair with help from Bill. If either remained covered for longer than twelve hours, the other would sound an alarm. If Harry spoke the extraction phrase, every adult connected to the Hawthorn wards would be shown his location.

Draconia removed a green coin from her sleeve. Harry's matching coin warmed against his chest, pressing beside the key beneath his shirt.

"And this makes silence unlikely," she said.

"Unless you both spend three weeks asking whether I've eaten."

"Have you considered eating without supervision?"

"I did it at lunch."

"One lunch, Harry."

Harry looked between them.

"I've reconsidered the romantic development."

Hermione smiled.

"Too late."

"Was that in the rules?"

"Page two," Draconia said.

"There are pages?"

They refused to answer. Harry let his head fall sideways against Draconia's shoulder. She went completely still before relaxing beneath the weight, and Hermione moved to his other side, taking his hand upon the seat. No one tried to speak. The train continued south, and Harry slept without dreaming for nearly an hour.

King's Cross contained too many guardians. The Grangers waited nearest the barrier, Mrs Granger embracing Hermione before she cleared the steam. Mr Granger shook Harry's hand, then ignored the formality and pulled him close. Sirius and Remus stood beside them, the Malfoys occupying the space beyond with sufficient luggage to suggest Hawthorn Cottage had already surrendered.

Narcissa wore travelling robes the colour of smoke. Lucius leaned more heavily upon his cane than he had at Hogwarts, but had recovered enough strength to look threatening on purpose. Dobby balanced upon a trunk behind them, wearing three tea towels and one of Sirius's old waistcoats.

"Dobby has brought emergency sandwiches," he announced.

"How many?" Harry asked.

"Forty-seven."

"That seems reasonable."

"Thank you, Harry Potter."

Narcissa adjusted Harry's collar. It did not need adjusting.

"There is food in your trunk," she said.

"You mean the thousand kilos of sandwiches?"

"Those are for the journey to Surrey. There is separate food in the trunk."

"It's an hour at most."

Lucius looked at Harry over her shoulder.

"Just take care of yourself."

The familiarity of it nearly undid him. Harry smiled instead, keeping one hand against the handle of his trunk.

"I'm staying with Sirius now. You don't have to maintain the role of a guardian."

"It was never just a role, Harry."

They crossed to the Muggle concourse in twos and threes, Dobby vanishing ahead to attend to the sandwiches. Sirius guided the group towards a quieter place near the station exit. A trolley rattled across the tiles behind them. Vernon Dursley had arrived, Petunia walking beside him and Dudley following with both hands inside his pockets. He was larger than when Harry left and deeply interested in every direction except the group of witches and wizards waiting around his cousin.

Vernon saw Sirius first, and his face changed colour. Their meeting at this station the previous summer had ended with a signed agreement and a motorbike following his car all the way to Surrey. He had apparently hoped the experience would not become an annual event. Then he saw Lucius and the Grangers beside him, and stopped walking.

"No."

Petunia's eyes found Harry. They moved across the closed scars upon his face, the stiffness in his posture and the faint grey line disappearing beneath his collar. Her thin mouth opened.

"What happened?"

Harry had not expected the question.

"Voldemort."

Petunia flinched as though he had shouted.

"He's dead."

"Not anymore."

Colour left her face. Vernon looked around the crowded station, where people continued passing with luggage, children and lives that had nothing to do with what waited beyond their world.

"We are not discussing this here."

"An excellent suggestion," Lucius said. "We will discuss the updates to last summer's agreement instead." Remus drew them into a sheltered alcove beside the exit; a quiet privacy charm softened their voices before Lucius continued.

He produced a roll of parchment. Vernon stared at it.

"I'm not signing any of your unnatural—"

"Then Harry leaves with me," Sirius said.

The answer came so quickly that Vernon stopped. Harry looked at his godfather.

"The blood ward," he said quietly.

"Is not worth purchasing with you."

Sirius did not look away from Vernon.

"If they refuse one condition, we walk."

Petunia heard. Her gaze moved to the cord around Harry's neck, where the edge of the brass key showed above his shirt beside Draconia's green coin. Something complicated entered her expression, and she held out one hand.

"Give me the paper. We received the updated conditions."

Vernon turned upon her.

"Petunia—"

"Give it."

Lucius placed the parchment in her hands. The language altered as she read, translating magical terms into ordinary English. No confinement or restriction of food. No interference with mail, mirrors or medical supplies. No forced labour beyond ordinary household contributions shared by all children in residence. No verbal or physical intimidation concerning Harry's parents, magic or scars. Access to the garden and Hedwig, private calls twice each day, and extraction after twenty-one nights or immediately upon breach.

Petunia reached the final line.

"And if it's broken?"

"The agreement alerts us," Remus said. "Harry leaves. The protection upon your home may leave with him."

She looked at Dumbledore's signature beneath the clause. Vernon read over her shoulder, his moustache trembling as the words settled into a form he could no longer claim not to understand.

"This is blackmail."

Mr Granger stepped forwards.

"No. It's the minimum conditions under which you are being trusted with an injured child."

Vernon recognised him, and his expression soured.

"You again."

"Yes," Mr Granger said. "We went through this before. The conditions haven't become less reasonable since then."

Vernon looked away first. Mrs Granger offered a pen, and Petunia signed. Vernon objected for another minute, discovered every person present willing to let Harry leave rather than negotiate, and signed beneath her.

The parchment flashed once. One copy folded itself into Petunia's handbag, another entered Sirius's coat, and a third disappeared towards Dumbledore. Twenty-one nights began.

Goodbyes became more difficult once the decision was official. Remus checked Harry's medicine, Narcissa checked the food, and Lucius examined the Dursleys' car as though evaluating whether it deserved to transport anyone he knew. Sirius handed Harry the mirror.

"Nine each morning. Nine each night."

"I remember."

"If you wake from a nightmare—"

"Call."

"If the marks darken—"

"Silver potion, then call."

"If your uncle—"

"Extraction phrase."

Sirius's fingers closed around the back of Harry's neck.

"If you simply want to leave?"

Harry looked at him.

"Call."

"Good."

"Three weeks."

"Twenty-one nights, twelve hours and"—Sirius consulted his watch—"forty-three minutes."

"You're counting the drive?"

"I'm counting everything."

Harry hugged him. Sirius held on long enough that Vernon made an impatient noise near the car. Lucius looked towards him, and the noise did not repeat.

When Harry stepped back, Hermione waited. She put a packet of addressed envelopes into his hand, their edges lined up so neatly he knew she had prepared them long before this morning.

"They are already stamped."

"Owls don't require stamps."

"The Muggle post does, in case Hedwig needs rest."

"You've even thought about sending them by post."

"Obviously."

Harry smiled. Hermione embraced him, whispering near his ear.

"You don't have to sound all right in the letters."

His arms tightened around her.

"Neither do you."

She drew back, eyes bright, and Draconia took her place. No envelopes, no instructions. She straightened his glasses with the same careful touch she had used beneath the beech tree.

"Potter."

"Malfoy."

"If you attempt to protect us by becoming unreachable again—"

"You'll break through the front door and find me."

"No, ill  break in, then break you."

"Your father will object to the neighbourhood."

"He has survived Black's curtains. He is prepared for anything."

Harry hugged her. Draconia's control lasted until her face rested against his shoulder.

"Twenty-one nights," she whispered.

"Twenty-one."

When they separated, Hermione remained beside her. The girls exchanged a look neither offered to translate. Whatever clarity the beech tree had given their rivalry, it had not erased the friendship beneath it. They stood shoulder to shoulder as Harry turned away, united for the moment by the shared conviction that he could not be trusted unsupervised in Surrey.

He entered the car.

The journey to Privet Drive passed in silence. Dudley occupied the front seat beside Vernon, leaving Harry alone with Petunia in the back. His trunk had been expanded on the inside and strapped to the roof by magic Vernon refused to acknowledge. Hedwig travelled uncaged beside Harry, turning her head whenever Petunia moved.

Surrey became familiar through the windows: wide roads, trim hedges, identical houses carrying the late-afternoon sun upon identical windows. Harry's body remembered before number four appeared. His breathing shortened, one hand went to the key, and the grey lines beneath his collar began to darken. Petunia noticed. She said nothing.

At the house, Vernon opened the boot and discovered forty-seven sandwiches arranged where his golf clubs should have been.

"What is this?"

"Traffic," Harry said.

Dudley laughed, one startled sound immediately disguised as a cough. Harry carried Hedwig inside.

The hallway smelled of polish, carpet and dinner prepared too early. Every object stood where it had the previous summer. Family photographs covered the wall without one sign Harry had ever lived among them. Nothing had changed, but he felt different standing upon the familiar carpet, with a key to another house beneath his shirt and people waiting to hear from him.

Vernon pointed upstairs.

"Your things stay in your room. None of that funny business. You will not disturb Dudley, speak to the neighbours or—"

The agreement inside Petunia's handbag chimed. Vernon stopped, and Harry looked at him.

"Clause six."

"I know what it says."

"Then the reading lessons worked."

Vernon's face reddened. The parchment chimed again, and Harry went upstairs before it could become musical.

His bedroom door possessed no exterior lock. The window stood open, with no bars across it. The desk was still clear, the broken toys still in the attic. A clean glass of water waited beside the lamp. Harry paused in the doorway, checking that the conditions had survived his absence before entering. His hand stayed near the key until Hedwig flew past him and settled upon the wardrobe.

Once his belongings had been brought upstairs, he set her empty cage in the corner and left its door open. He unpacked slowly, placing potions upon the desk and food inside the wardrobe rather than beneath the floor. The champion's shield stayed in the trunk. Colin's photograph went beside the lamp, where the moving figures continued reaching for one another.

At nine o'clock, the mirror warmed. Harry uncovered it, and Sirius's face appeared too close.

"You're three seconds late."

"Hello to you too."

The mirror moved. Hawthorn Cottage existed in pieces behind him: Remus at the kitchen table with a book, Narcissa rearranging jars upon shelves Dobby had already arranged, Lucius beneath the floral curtains holding one between two fingers as though it had personally betrayed civilisation. Hermione's face entered from the left and Draconia's from the right.

"Did they keep to it?" Hermione asked.

"Yes."

"Did you eat?" Draconia asked.

"Four emergency sandwiches."

"Four?" Narcissa called from the background.

"Traffic."

Sirius laughed. Harry showed them the bedroom, the unlocked door, the open window and Hedwig asleep upon the wardrobe. He did not show the floorboard because admitting it still comforted him felt unnecessarily complicated.

"Marks?" Sirius asked.

Harry checked beneath his collar. The grey had faded again.

"Fine."

"That word is banned," Hermione said.

"Stable."

"Acceptable," Draconia decided.

They remained in the mirror until half past nine. Most of the conversation concerned nothing. Dobby had hidden Lucius's hair products because they occupied too much bathroom space. Remus believed the attic contained mice; Narcissa pointed out that mice did not usually mutter about wallpaper, and Dobby denied knowing anything. Sirius announced plans to build two additional bedrooms without determining where the walls would go.

Home sounded crowded, untidy and waiting. Harry sat upon the edge of the bed with the mirror balanced between his hands, letting their voices fill a room where he had once listened for footsteps and tried to make none of his own.

"Tomorrow," Sirius said at last.

"At nine," Harry replied.

Hermione and Draconia pressed their shoulders together to remain inside the mirror.

"Write to us okay." Hermione said.

"try and sleep more." Draconia added.

"You've become my parents."

Lucius appeared behind them.

"An absurd suggestion. Although your posture remains dreadful."

Harry smiled.

"Goodnight."
