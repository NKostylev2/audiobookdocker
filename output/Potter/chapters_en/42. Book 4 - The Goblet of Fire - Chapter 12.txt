Water improved the golden egg without making it useful.

The experiment occupied the centre of an abandoned Charms classroom, where the desks had been pushed against the walls and a ring of towels surrounded a borrowed washbasin. Frost silvered the tall windows. Beyond them, the December sky was fading towards an early blue dusk, turning the towers into black shapes above the snow. Inside, the last light caught the water trembling beneath Hermione's wand.

Harry's ribs still objected when he knelt. He ignored them and lowered the egg with both hands, careful not to let the metal strike the porcelain. Hermione held the lid open with a Levitation Charm, while Draconia knelt beside the basin with her wand ready in case the clue reacted by exploding.

The screaming began beneath the surface, muffled and strangely distant. Water reduced the murder of several ghosts to the unhappy singing of one. Harry had expected the noise, but his shoulders tightened anyway. Hermione's charm wavered for half a second before she corrected it, and Draconia's free hand moved towards him without quite reaching his arm.

He leaned closer.

"Better."

"Not language," Hermione said.

"No."

Draconia adjusted the resonance charm. Ripples crossed the basin, and for a second a low note separated from the shrieking before dissolving into it again.

"There." Hermione moved her wand. "Again."

They repeated the attempt with warmer water, colder water and a solution containing powdered moonstone. The egg wailed through all of them. Its voice changed, but whatever meaning lay beneath the noise remained beyond reach. A damp patch spread past the towels, and Harry shifted his knee away from it.

"Perhaps it requires complete immersion," Draconia said.

He looked at the egg beneath six inches of water. "It is completely immersed."

"Not the egg."

Hermione understood first. "The listener."

Harry considered the basin. It was barely deep enough to drown a determined kneazle.

"I'm not putting my head in that."

"Why not?" Draconia asked.

"The moonstone."

"Powdered moonstone is harmless in diluted solution."

"Then you do it."

Draconia examined the cloudy water. "We require a larger container."

Hermione wrote FULL IMMERSION beneath their list of possibilities. For a moment, the three of them remained around the basin, their reflections wavering together on its surface: Hermione bent over her notes, Draconia with one hand beside the rim and Harry between them, his sleeves rolled clear of the remaining dressing.

He could see all three faces without turning his head. It was easier than looking at either girl directly and remembering the conversations they had been avoiding. In the water, a movement of Hermione's quill blurred into the pale outline of Draconia's hand; the next ripple restored them, still close enough for their reflections to touch.

Hermione spoke about Viktor when questions required it. Draconia mentioned Blaise only while discussing arrangements. Harry had told them he would find a partner, a promise that became less convincing with every passing day.

"You need to ask someone," Hermione said without looking up.

"I've been informed."

"The rehearsal is in four days."

"Also informed."

Draconia dried the egg with a sweep of her wand. "Most remaining possibilities now believe they are being selected because everyone else refused."

"That's comforting."

"It was not intended to be."

Harry watched her polish the last water from the shell, gathering the question before he could decide against it.

"Are you both happy with who you're going with?"

Hermione's quill stopped, and Draconia lowered her wand.

"Viktor has been kind," Hermione said.

"Blaise is behaving properly," Draconia replied.

Neither had answered what Harry asked. He had no right to request a different answer, especially after making so much effort to avoid giving them one of his own.

"Good," he said.

The word closed the subject. They returned to the egg.

Harry asked Luna Lovegood two days later. He found her outside the library attempting to persuade a stone gargoyle to return one of her shoes. The gargoyle held the trainer between its teeth, its carved eyes fixed on the opposite wall with the determined innocence of badly behaved architecture. Beneath it, Luna stood wearing one shoe and a pair of radish-shaped earrings.

"Have you tried asking Professor Flitwick?" Harry said.

"He didn't take it."

"No. He might make it let go."

"That would teach the gargoyle that theft earns attention."

Harry looked at the stone jaws. "Do gargoyles learn?"

"This one learned where I keep my shoes."

Fair. Harry raised his wand.

"Accio trainer."

The shoe pulled against the gargoyle's teeth. Stone scraped rubber, and the gargoyle growled. Harry strengthened the spell until the trainer came free, bringing one of the fangs with it. He caught the shoe and ducked the stone, which struck the library door behind him and broke into three pieces.

Madam Pince shouted from inside. Luna accepted her trainer with an interested glance at the damage.

"It will be more aerodynamic now."

"The shoe?"

"The gargoyle."

She sat on a window ledge and put it on. Harry had spent three days rehearsing ways to ask her; none had begun with vandalism.

"Luna?"

She tied the laces unevenly. "Yes?"

"Would you like to come to the Yule Ball with me?"

Her hands paused. Students continued past the far end of the corridor, and snow reflected pale light through the window, revealing dust suspended between Harry and the ledge. He waited, trying to resist the urge to fill the silence before she answered.

Luna's eyes were light and wide, but not vacant. People often mistook the time she took to answer for a failure to understand. Harry had begun to suspect she simply considered parts of a question everyone else ignored.

"As your partner?" she asked.

"Yes."

"Romantically?"

Harry nearly dropped his wand.

"I—no. I mean, unless that matters. I didn't think—"

"It does not matter to me. I wanted to know which evening I was agreeing to."

"Right."

She finished tying her shoe. "You wanted to ask Hermione and Draconia."

Harry looked along the corridor. "Did everyone know?"

"Not everyone. Most people think you were deciding which one would improve the photographs."

"I wasn't."

"I know."

"How?"

"You look at photographs less than you look at them."

She said it without teasing, which made denial feel pointless.

"They're going with other people."

"Yes."

"And I don't want you to think I'm asking because there was nobody else."

"There are many other people."

"Nobody else I'd want to spend the evening with."

Luna considered that. "Do you want to spend it with me, or do you want to avoid spending it alone?"

Sirius's advice returned: ask someone whose company he wanted, without treating her as consolation. Harry sat beside Luna on the ledge, letting his wand rest across his knees.

"Both," he admitted. "I don't want to be alone. But I thought of you because you don't make silence feel like something I have to fix. And you're funny when you mean to be. I think I'd have a good time with you even if the Ball wasn't compulsory."

Luna smiled. "That sounds like a good reason."

"Is that yes?"

"Yes, Harry. I would like to go to the Ball with you."

Relief arrived first, followed by a warmth that had nothing uncertain about it. He had asked a question, told her what he meant and received an answer he could trust.

"Thank you."

"You don't have to thank people for agreeing to things they want."

"I do that too often."

"Yes."

Harry laughed. Madam Pince opened the library door and discovered the broken fang, ending the conversation with a detention on the first Saturday after Christmas. Luna offered to attend, and Madam Pince gave her one as well.

Hermione and Draconia received the news differently. Hermione blinked.

"Luna?"

"Yes."

Draconia lowered the book she had been reading. "Lovegood."

"Still yes."

They sat in the library alcove where most egg research took place. Beyond the windows, snow was darkening into evening. The egg rested beneath a folded blanket to prevent other students opening it out of curiosity.

"When did you ask her?" Hermione said.

"This afternoon."

"You asked her properly?" Draconia asked.

Harry looked at her. "I can finish some questions."

"Apparently."

The word had a sharpened edge. Hermione rearranged her parchment, aligning two sheets that had already been straight.

"Luna is nice."

"I know."

"A little unusual."

"So are we."

"I didn't say it was bad."

Draconia returned to her book. "She is younger."

"By one year."

"And a Ravenclaw."

"That sounded almost prejudiced."

"It was observational."

Harry looked between them. "Are you jealous?"

Both answered too quickly.

"No."

The identical response made him smile. Hermione saw it.

"Don't."

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"I've been informed I have several."

Draconia turned another page without reading it. "Lovegood will need to understand that you forget to eat when overstimulated."

"I can feed myself."

Both girls looked at him.

"Luna will be told," Hermione said.

Harry covered the golden egg with another blanket. "I regret telling you."

He did not. For the first time since the invitations, the tension contained something almost playful. The hurt remained, but they could speak around it without every sentence becoming another reason to fall silent.

The champions' rehearsal took place on the afternoon of the twenty-third. Professor McGonagall arranged the four couples inside the undecorated Great Hall and explained the entrance order with the gravity of a military campaign. Chalk lines marked the future dance floor, and a gramophone played the opening music from a chair near the staff table.

Cedric and Cho required no correction. Fleur corrected Roger before McGonagall could. Krum counted under his breath in Bulgarian while Hermione quietly translated the rhythm into numbers.

Luna studied the ceiling.

"We move on this beat," Harry told her.

"I know."

"You were looking up."

"The ceiling is nervous."

Harry glanced at the grey clouds overhead. "About dancing?"

"About decorations."

McGonagall called their names, and they began. The trophy-room lessons remained in Harry's body. His hand found Luna's waist, he counted the first three steps, and then the rhythm carried him without assistance. Luna followed lightly, though she occasionally introduced movements no formal dance contained.

"Miss Lovegood," McGonagall said after Luna turned beneath Harry's arm during a section that required no turn.

"Yes, Professor?"

"Kindly follow the prescribed sequence."

"It seemed lonely."

McGonagall removed her glasses, cleaned them and restored them to her face.

"Again."

They completed the second attempt without invention. As they finished, Harry became aware of Hermione watching from beside Krum. She was looking past his feet and the steps he had learned, watching the ease between him and Luna.

When the music stopped, she came over.

"You've improved."

"Draco is terrifying."

"Yes."

Hermione looked towards Luna, who was explaining the ceiling's anxiety to Cho.

"You seem comfortable together."

"I am."

The answer hurt her. Harry saw it and could not find a way to apologise without making Luna sound like someone whose company mattered less. Before he settled on anything, Krum called Hermione for their next attempt, and she returned to him.

Draconia waited outside when rehearsal ended, with Blaise beside her and the white corsage box beneath his arm. She had come to meet Hermione. Harry emerged between Luna and Cedric, and for several seconds they formed an awkward group beneath the doorway, unsure which conversation belonged to whom.

"This would be easier if everyone stopped pretending not to look at everyone else," Luna said.

Blaise laughed. Draconia did not. Hermione linked her arm through Draconia's and led her towards the staircase, leaving Harry to watch them go together.

Luna touched his sleeve. "You're doing it again."

"I know."

He turned back to her and walked her towards Ravenclaw Tower.

Christmas morning arrived beneath deep snow and a sky clear enough to turn every window gold. Harry woke to presents at the foot of his bed, their paper catching the light through the curtains.

Sirius had sent a leather case for the repaired Firebolt, with a note insisting that it was no replacement for being present. Remus gave Harry a book of defensive theory containing more handwritten comments than printed pages. Hermione's gift was a charmed bookmark that remembered the last passage read even when a book was closed unexpectedly. Draconia sent dragon-hide gloves dyed black, accompanied by a card in sharp silver ink.

For objects which ought not be touched without protection. This category includes dragons, cursed artefacts and certain journalists. — D.M.

Harry smiled, trying on one glove before placing the card carefully beside the others.

He had chosen his gifts before the Ball invitations turned every ordinary decision into evidence. Hermione received an out-of-print collection of defensive number puzzles, its previous owner's pencilled errors left intact for her to correct. Draconia received a silver quill knife with a folding blade and TACTICAL STATIONERY engraved discreetly along the handle. For Luna, he had found a small brass compass charmed to point towards whichever missing possession she named.

Luna's gift to him was a glass jar labelled EMERGENCY SILENCE. It appeared empty. When Harry opened it, every sound in the dormitory vanished for ten seconds. Neville's mouth continued moving through an enthusiastic explanation of his new plant, while Dean attempted to make the curtains sing. Neither reached Harry.

Noise returned all at once. He closed the jar reverently; it might be the most useful gift he had ever received.

Breakfast passed in a storm of paper, sweets and speculation about the Ball. Draconia said nothing about the quill knife, but Harry saw it clipped inside her satchel, which pleased him more than it should have. Luna's thank-you card reported that the compass had already located two shoes, a scarf and somebody else's Transfiguration essay.

Younger students were excluded from the evening preparations and reacted as though Hogwarts had denied them food. Throughout the afternoon, older students disappeared to bathe, argue over mirrors and transform minor clothing problems into emergencies. Harry postponed his own preparation until Sirius called, the mirror brightening at four.

"Show me the robes," Sirius demanded.

Harry turned the glass towards the dark green dress robes hanging from his bedpost. Narcissa had selected them during the summer after declaring the maroon set originally offered by the shopkeeper an act of hostility. The fabric appeared almost black until the light touched it; then fine green thread emerged along the cuffs and collar.

"Too respectable," Sirius said.

"Narcissa chose them."

"Perfect. No changes."

Remus appeared over his shoulder. "Have you polished your shoes?"

Harry looked towards them. "They're shoes."

"That means no," Sirius said.

For the next hour, Harry dressed under the direction of two men who disagreed about everything. Remus demonstrated how to tie the formal cravat, waiting while Harry followed each movement in the mirror. Sirius then taught him how to loosen it without appearing to be strangled. Remus insisted his hair required only a comb. Sirius attempted to demonstrate a charm and caused his own hair to stand upright.

Harry laughed until his sides hurt. When he finished dressing, the mirror showed a version of himself he recognised only gradually. The robes fitted his shoulders, the green brightened his eyes, and the remaining pink line on his cheek stood out against skin that had finally lost its soot. His hair had refused order but accepted negotiation. There was no school crest or champion's badge, nothing telling everyone who looked at him what they were supposed to see.

"You look like James," Sirius said.

Harry's stomach tightened. Sirius saw it.

"Around the eyes," he amended. "The rest is yours."

Harry studied the reflection again, finding himself beneath the resemblance.

"Good."

Sirius smiled. "Have fun tonight."

"I'll try."

"That is not the same as promising."

"You accept distinctions."

"Only when they inconvenience me."

The mirror remained bright until Harry was ready to leave.

Hermione prepared in a classroom Professor McGonagall had converted into a dressing room for champions and their partners. Mirrors occupied one wall, temporary wardrobes another. The desks had been replaced with screens, chairs and tables crowded with pins, cosmetics and enough hair charms to alter the appearance of a small army.

Mrs Granger stood behind her with several pins between her lips.

"Hold still."

"I am."

"Your hair isn't."

"It never is."

Hermione watched their reflection. Her mother wore simple burgundy robes borrowed from McGonagall and appeared more comfortable holding hairpins than a wand. She had spent the afternoon asking questions about moving staircases, enchanted ceilings and why formal magical clothing resisted ordinary irons. Hermione answered each one until the questions turned towards Viktor. Then she became less informative.

"He seems polite," Mrs Granger said.

"He is."

"And very interested in you."

"Mum."

"I'm making an observation."

"You sound like Draconia."

The door opened, and Narcissa entered with Draconia. Hermione stopped breathing for a second. She had seen the black dress robes laid across a bed the previous evening, elegant even when empty, but the changing green and silver looked different when Draconia moved. Lake-light travelled through the fabric, and the bare line of her shoulders made her seem older and less protected than she usually allowed herself to be.

Draconia saw Hermione, and her steps slowed.

"You look beautiful," she said.

There was no qualification and no cleverness. Hermione looked down at her blue robes, suddenly conscious of the hours she and her mother had spent choosing them.

"So do you."

"I know."

The answer restored enough normality for Hermione to smile. Narcissa crossed behind Draconia and corrected the fall of her hair, meeting Mrs Granger's gaze in the mirror.

"Your daughter has excellent taste in colour."

"She chose it herself," Mrs Granger said.

"Even better."

Hermione watched Narcissa adjust a silver leaf at Draconia's throat. The motion was precise and gentle, familiar from the way she corrected Harry's collars. Draconia endured it with the particular patience reserved for someone whose touch was welcome even when the attention was not.

"Harry will forget how to speak," Mrs Granger said.

Both girls looked at her.

"What?"

"Nothing." Hermione turned towards the mirror. "He has a date."

Her mother exchanged another look with Narcissa. Adults communicated far too effectively when children required them not to.

The mothers left to join the guest tables once every clasp, pin and hem had been inspected. Narcissa kissed Draconia's forehead. Mrs Granger kissed Hermione's cheek and whispered that she was proud of her for reasons unrelated to appearance. Hermione held her hand briefly before letting her go.

When the door closed, the room seemed quieter than before. Warm scents lingered above the dressing tables: cosmetic charms, pressed fabric and the lavender water Mrs Granger had used on Hermione's hair. Somewhere beyond the classroom, a cello began tuning in the Great Hall.

Draconia noticed a bronze pin slipping above Hermione's ear. She stepped behind her and secured it without comment, then loosened a second curl so the arrangement looked intentional rather than repaired. Hermione met her eyes in the glass.

"Turn around."

Draconia obeyed. The clasp at the back of her silver collar had caught two pale strands. Hermione freed them carefully and smoothed the fabric between her shoulders.

"There."

"Thank you."

They regarded the finished result together. Hermione could remember too many nights when looking after one another had meant checking a locked door or finding a book that might help Harry survive something. Repairing a clasp ought to have felt much simpler.

"We are not going to spend the entire evening watching him," she said.

Draconia selected a pair of gloves. "I had no such intention."

"You watched him through lunch yesterday."

"He had not eaten."

"Luna will make sure he does."

The thought dissatisfied both of them. Draconia put the gloves down.

"Blaise asked me properly."

"I know."

"He deserves an evening in which I am present."

"So does Viktor."

They looked at each other through the mirror, neither able to avoid how much effort the promise already required.

"No punishing them because Harry panicked," Hermione said.

"Obviously."

"No interrogating Luna."

"I would not interrogate her."

"You asked whether she understood Harry forgets to eat when overstimulated."

"That was essential information."

Hermione turned. "Draco."

Draconia sighed. "Fine. No interrogation."

They stood in their beautiful robes, each about to attend with a boy who had wanted her clearly enough to ask. Hermione suspected neither felt chosen in quite the way she had hoped. She held out her hand.

Draconia looked at it. "What is this?"

"A truce."

"We are not at war."

"Then reassurance."

Draconia placed her hand in Hermione's.

"Whatever happens tonight," Hermione said, "we do not hurt each other over him."

Saying it made the possibility real enough to frighten her. Draconia's fingers tightened.

"Agreed."

They left the classroom separately so no one could ask why they had been holding hands.

The Entrance Hall had become a room Harry knew only by shape. Garlands of winter greenery climbed the walls, and ice sculptures stood between the doors, each containing a tiny scene moving beneath its frozen surface: skaters crossing a lake, silver stags passing through a forest, miniature dragons sleeping harmlessly on piles of gold. Hundreds of lanterns floated beneath the ceiling, their light multiplied by charmed snow that vanished before it reached the floor.

Students descended the staircases transformed. School robes had hidden more individuality than Harry realised. Familiar people arrived in colours he had never seen them wear, their hair arranged differently, jewellery appearing at throats and wrists. Neville wore deep blue and looked terrified of his sleeves. Gold stitching moved on Dean's robes whenever he laughed. Ginny descended beside a Ravenclaw boy, her robes the colour of plum skins.

Harry waited near the marble staircase. He was early. Sirius had advised against it; Remus had advised for it. Apparently, arriving early merely provided more time to wonder whether Luna had reconsidered.

"You look worried."

She stood behind him in pale silver-blue robes, falling in soft layers that became almost transparent at the edges. Her long blonde hair had been woven into a loose braid threaded with tiny glass stars, brightening and dimming like distant lights. The radish earrings remained.

Harry smiled. "You look—"

He searched for a word that did not sound borrowed.

"Like moonlight."

Luna considered her sleeves. "Moonlight is reflected sunlight."

"You look like reflected sunlight."

"Thank you."

She touched the collar of his dress robes. "You look like a forest at night."

"That sounds good."

"Forests are often full of parasites."

"Less good."

"I did not mean those."

Harry offered his arm, and Luna took it. They joined the champions gathering beside Professor McGonagall.

Cedric arrived with Cho Chang, whose red robes lay beneath a black outer layer embroidered with small golden birds. Whenever she moved, they rose from the fabric and circled her shoulders. Cedric looked so happy that Harry forgave him for every piece of sensible advice. Fleur descended with Roger Davies, silver fabric following her like water while Roger appeared to have forgotten the purpose of speech.

Krum came last, with Hermione beside him. Harry forgot the Entrance Hall.

Her robes were the deep blue of evening immediately after sunset. Fine bronze beading crossed the sleeves and gathered at her waist before scattering down the skirt like numbers escaping a pattern. Her hair remained itself, thick and brown, gathered away from her face in a twist that released curls down her back. She wore no necklace, and Harry found himself looking at the familiar face above the unfamiliar robes, unable to decide why seeing both together felt so different.

Hermione saw him staring. Her step faltered, and Krum steadied her with one hand, removing it as soon as her balance returned.

"You look beautiful," Harry said.

The words came without preparation. Hermione's face softened.

"Thank you." Her gaze moved across his robes. "You look very handsome."

Harry became aware of Luna's hand on his arm and Krum standing beside Hermione.

"Thanks."

Krum inclined his head.

"Potter."

"Krum."

They had fought dragons more comfortably than they regarded each other now. Luna rescued them.

"Your hair is lovely," she told Hermione. "It looks like it would remember being touched."

Hermione blinked. "Thank you, Luna."

Harry understood the compliment without understanding how. McGonagall arranged the champions into order: Cedric and Cho first as Hogwarts's official pair, then Fleur and Roger, followed by Krum and Hermione. Harry and Luna took the final position.

"Where is Draconia?" he asked.

Hermione looked towards the dungeon stairs. "She'll come."

The reassurance seemed intended for both of them. Music began inside the Great Hall, though the doors remained closed, and students gathered behind the champions beneath the lanterns. Blaise emerged from the dungeon corridor alone, black robes showing burgundy silk at the collar. He took his place near the doors and waited.

Harry looked towards the stairs again. Draconia appeared.

She wore black, with shades of green and silver surfacing through the fabric as she descended, changing with each movement like light beneath the lake. The robes fitted closely through the waist before falling in long, clean lines to the floor. A narrow collar of interlocking silver leaves lay above her bare shoulders. Her pale hair had been drawn back from her face and allowed to fall down her back, secured by the green stone from the paired ward bracelet.

She wore no family crest, but Harry doubted anyone needed one to recognise her. Conversation shifted as she passed. Draconia ignored it, finding Blaise first as he stepped forward and offered his hand.

She placed her fingers in his. The gesture was perfectly ordinary. He had asked, she had accepted, and he stood where an invited partner ought to stand, offering the courtesy Harry had spent weeks failing to offer.

It still looked wrong.

The certainty arrived beneath every sensible thought Harry possessed. He knew that place beside Draconia: close enough to hear the dry remarks she refused to share with a room, near enough for her hand to find his without anyone having to ask. He had surrendered it without ever finding out whether it might have been his in another way.

Then she looked at him. Harry had prepared compliments after failing to answer well in the trophy room. Every one disappeared.

"Oh," he said.

Draconia's eyebrows lifted. "Compelling."

"You look—"

Hermione watched him. Luna waited. Blaise's mouth showed the beginning of amusement.

"Beautiful," Harry finished.

Draconia became still, the green stone catching a lantern's light as she inclined her head.

"Thank you. You appear presentable."

"High praise."

"Do not become accustomed to it."

The Great Hall doors opened, pouring warmth and golden light across the Entrance Hall. Applause began within. For half a second, Harry was back in the arena: heat, splintering wood and the Horntail's scream arriving before his mind restored the music, lanterns and cool stone beneath his shoes.

Luna's hand tightened once on his arm. She did not ask what had happened, simply waiting until he breathed again. Then McGonagall directed the champions forward, and Harry entered with Luna while Hermione walked ahead with Krum and Draconia remained behind with Blaise.

Luna's arm felt warm and friendly through his sleeve. Ahead, Krum bent his head when Hermione spoke. Behind, Blaise murmured something that softened Draconia's mouth despite herself. Harry had wanted to avoid making either girl feel left behind. Somehow, he had managed to enter the Hall without either beside him at all.

Winter occupied the Great Hall. Frosted branches arched between the walls, their leaves made from silver and glass. The House tables had vanished, replaced by smaller round tables around a polished dance floor. Stars shone between slow-moving clouds on the enchanted ceiling, and icicles hung along the walls without melting. At the front, a raised platform held instruments beneath curtains of falling light.

Harry saw Sirius and Remus at a guest table. Dumbledore's definition of Tournament guests had proved remarkably elastic: the champions could nominate family, their partners had received the same courtesy, and several further names appeared after McGonagall decided that leaving Sirius unsupervised in Hogsmeade on Christmas night presented the greater administrative risk. Sirius maintained that the argument was insulting. He had still accepted before she finished making it.

He wore formal robes with the collar already open. Remus had chosen brown and looked resigned to supervising him. Narcissa and Lucius sat at the same table beside the Grangers, where Mr Granger adjusted an unfamiliar formal cloak while Lucius explained the correct fork with excessive seriousness.

Harry waved. Mrs Granger waved back with both hands, Narcissa smiled, and Sirius raised his glass until McGonagall frowned at him.

The champions took their places at the central table. Dinner appeared in courses rather than all at once, which Harry found inefficient, although he could not complain about the food. Luna identified ingredients by scent and invented increasingly unlikely reasons for their selection. Krum spoke quietly with Hermione about Durmstrang's winter term. Cedric and Cho laughed often enough that Fleur glanced towards them with interest.

Harry ate under supervision. Luna admitted that she had received instructions after placing bread on his plate for the third time.

"They both spoke to you?"

"Separately. They gave similar information."

"That doesn't concern you?"

"It means the information is probably reliable."

Harry looked down the table. Hermione was pretending not to listen. Draconia sat with Blaise at another table, her attention apparently on Narcissa; she could not possibly have heard, but she looked towards him anyway.

Luna followed his gaze.

"They're both watching you."

"They think I'll forget to eat."

"That isn't why."

Harry drank his water rather than answer. At the judges' table, Percy Weasley occupied Crouch's place, dressed in new Ministry robes and consulting a parchment beside his plate. The empty chair that might have acknowledged Crouch's absence had been removed.

An unfamiliar brown owl arrived during the third course. It dropped a sealed message beside Percy, who recognised the seal at once and shielded the parchment with one hand before breaking it. Harry recognised the angular B.C. pressed into the red wax.

Crouch.

Percy read the message twice, his expression moving from pride to confusion and back again. He carried it to Dumbledore, who read without touching the parchment, then floated it towards Moody. The magical eye examined the seal. Moody said something too quiet to carry across the Hall, and Percy folded the letter into his robes.

No reply was sent. Harry looked towards Hermione and found that she had noticed. Across the room, Draconia was watching too. Even here, with the egg and their notes put aside, the same questions followed all three of them.

Moody sat beyond Dumbledore. His magical eye travelled over the Hall independently, passing across the champions, the exits and the staff before stopping on Harry. Harry looked back. Moody raised his cup, but his ordinary eye did not smile.

Then the final plates disappeared, McGonagall stood, and the opening dance arrived.

Harry's stomach tightened. Beneath the table, Luna offered her hand.

"You have already fought the larger dragon."

"Draconia said something similar."

"She taught you."

"How did you know?"

"You keep checking whether she is watching."

Harry looked towards Draconia. She was watching.

"Right."

The champions entered the floor as low strings gathered around them, joined by piano and woodwind. Harry placed a hand at Luna's waist and took hers in the other. She rested her palm on his shoulder. There was no question hidden in the contact, no need to search her face for an answer he was frightened to hear. They had agreed to be friends entering a dance together, and the certainty made touching her easy.

"I may step on you," he warned.

"I may step on you too. That makes it balanced."

They began. Harry found the first beat, and Draconia's voice returned with the movement: left foot, turn, lift his eyes. Trust the person following him enough to lead without dragging. Every instruction had first existed with her hand in his, her body close enough to correct his balance. Remembering her made him notice the absence more sharply.

Luna moved lightly, unconcerned with perfection. When Harry missed a step, she adjusted as though the mistake belonged to the dance. They crossed beneath the lights, and he allowed the applause to remain beyond the edge of the music.

"You dance well," Luna said.

"I had an unforgiving teacher."

"She looks pleased."

Harry glanced towards the edge of the floor. Draconia's expression was controlled, but pride escaped anyway. Blaise noticed it beside her.

The opening dance ended in applause. Other couples entered the floor while the champions separated. Krum offered Hermione his arm again, Cedric spun Cho before the next music began, and Fleur and Roger remained close enough to make speaking unnecessary.

Harry returned Luna to their table.

"Do you want to dance again?"

"Later. You want to ask them."

He looked at her. "I came with you."

"Yes. Not into captivity."

"Luna—"

"One dance each will not make me lonely. I intend to speak with Professor Sinistra about lunar moths."

She walked towards the staff tables before Harry could argue. Across the Hall, Sirius lifted both eyebrows and pointed discreetly towards Hermione and Draconia. Remus lowered his hand.

Harry remained beside the floor as the music changed. Krum and Hermione were already dancing, and Blaise led Draconia towards them. Harry had spent weeks fearing the moment he chose one; now he watched both choose someone else.

He waited through a dance that lasted approximately a year.

Krum moved with more care than grace. His size made each turn deliberate, but Hermione followed easily. She looked nervous during the first circuit and was smiling by the second. He bent his head to hear her above the music, his attention remaining on whatever she said instead of the people watching him.

That was what Harry resented most. Hermione spoke, and Krum listened as though no quicker, louder or more convenient person existed elsewhere in the room. Harry had received that attention from her for years, and its constancy had made it easy to forget that she might want the same thing in return.

Blaise danced as though he had been taught by the same severe relatives as Draconia. Their posture matched, his hand rested properly at her waist, and her pale hair moved across the black fabric whenever he turned her. Harry knew exactly where that hand lay because his own had occupied the same place in the trophy room. He remembered her fingers on his shoulder, the shift of her weight before a turn and the instant her gaze had dropped towards his mouth.

Blaise had done nothing except arrive when invited. Harry hated how completely that was enough. Both boys became hardest to dislike when they behaved well, which only made him resent them more.

The music ended. Krum escorted Hermione towards the refreshments, and Harry intercepted them before caution returned.

"Hermione?"

She looked at him.

"Would you dance with me?"

The question came out whole. Surprise widened her eyes, then she looked at Krum. Harry remembered, belatedly, that asking someone else's partner while he stood beside her might be rude.

Krum's expression revealed nothing. He looked at Hermione rather than Harry.

"If you vant."

The choice remained hers. She placed her untouched drink on the table.

"Yes," she said. "I want to."

Harry offered his hand, and her fingers settled inside it. They entered the floor as the next music began, slower strings stretching beneath the stars overhead. He placed his hand at her waist, the bronze beads cool beneath his palm, and she rested hers on his shoulder.

For the first few steps, both watched their feet.

"We look ridiculous," Harry said.

"Looking down makes it worse."

"Draco said that."

Hermione's gaze lifted. "She taught you well."

"She insulted me until I improved."

"Her preferred method."

They turned. Harry remembered the rhythm, and Hermione adapted quickly, her movements becoming certain once she understood the pattern.

"You could have taught me," he said.

"You didn't ask."

The answer contained the entire month. Harry looked at her, at the light catching the bronze on her sleeves and the curl that had escaped beside her face. He wanted to tuck it back, but could not decide whether that was something he was allowed to do.

"Luna said yes," Hermione said.

"You know that."

"I know she came with you. That isn't the same."

"We're friends."

"Only friends?"

"Yes."

Her fingers shifted on his shoulder. "She is very pretty."

"She said I look like a forest."

"Romantic."

"Then she mentioned parasites."

Hermione laughed, loosening the tension between them. Harry guided her through a turn, her skirt moving around his legs like evening water.

"You look beautiful," he said again.

Her smile faded into something more vulnerable.

"You told me."

"I didn't say it properly."

"How do you say it properly?"

Harry thought. "You look like yourself. But like everyone else finally noticed."

Her breath caught. He wondered whether that had been better or considerably worse. She stepped closer, her hand sliding from his shoulder towards the back of his neck. The movement might have belonged to the dance, but it made him aware of the faint lavender scent she had brought from home, familiar beneath everything changed about the evening.

"Why didn't you ask me?" she said.

There was no anger, which was harder. Harry's hand tightened slightly at her waist.

"I tried."

"Twice."

He stared. "You knew?"

"Yes."

"Both times?"

"Harry, you asked whether I wanted to go somewhere warm while looking as though you were about to be executed."

"I thought I hid it."

"You did not."

They turned slowly beneath the enchanted ceiling.

"Why didn't you let me finish?" he asked.

Hermione drew back enough to look at him. "I waited."

The truth stopped him. She had waited on the staircase, on the landing, after Krum asked and before she answered. Harry had filled every silence with something safer.

"I would have said yes," she said.

The music continued around them, indifferent to the month collapsing into five quiet words. Harry imagined a Christmas in which he had finished one sentence. Hermione would still be wearing blue, and her hand would still be warm against his neck, but neither would be borrowing these minutes from someone who had been braver about asking.

"You could have asked me," he said.

Her eyes flashed. "I wanted you to choose me."

Harry missed a step. Hermione corrected them before anyone fell.

"That was unfair," she said quietly. "I know it was. But I spend so much time telling you where to be, what to eat and what danger you've overlooked. I didn't want to arrange this too. I wanted to know you wanted me there without requiring instructions."

"I did."

"You never said it."

"I'm saying it now."

"While I am here with Viktor."

Harry looked over her shoulder. Krum stood beside the refreshments, deliberately speaking with Fleur instead of watching them.

"He treats you well," Harry said.

"Yes."

"I'm glad."

"No, you aren't."

Harry met her gaze. "No," he admitted.

Her expression softened and hurt at once. The music ended before either found the next words, and Harry returned with her to Krum.

"Thank you," Krum said.

Harry did not think he meant the dance. He nodded, and Hermione looked back once as Krum led her away.

Blaise spared Harry the difficulty of asking. He approached near the band platform while Draconia spoke with Narcissa.

"Potter."

"Zabini."

Blaise watched Draconia adjust a silver leaf at her collar while her mother murmured something.

"She taught you to dance."

It was the second time someone had identified the fact without being told.

"Yes."

"You should ask her."

Harry looked at him. "You're her partner."

"For the evening, not the duration of her natural life."

"Lucius might disagree."

"Lucius has considered me unsuitable since I spilled juice on an heirloom carpet at seven."

Harry almost liked him. That was becoming inconvenient.

"Why are you helping me?"

Blaise regarded him with calm assessment. "I am helping Draconia. She has watched you during every dance, including the one she was participating in."

"Doesn't that bother you?"

"It informs me."

The answer sounded exactly like a Slytherin and nothing like cruelty. Narcissa moved towards Lucius, leaving Draconia beside the platform. Blaise bowed his head in her direction.

"Go."

Harry went. Draconia saw him approach and glanced behind him, where Blaise had begun speaking with Daphne Greengrass.

"Has something happened?"

"Would you dance with me?"

Her composure shifted. "Now?"

"Ideally before the music ends."

She placed her hand in his. "Your timing remains poor."

"I've heard."

They joined the dancers. Harry knew the first movement before the music instructed him. His hand found the place at her waist where she had taught him to hold it, and her palm rested on his shoulder with the same careful avoidance of the healing burn.

Familiarity entered at once, followed by relief. Harry had not understood how much he remembered until Draconia fitted the space without adjustment. Her next movement reached him through his palm before he thought about the count. For several dangerous seconds, dancing with anyone else seemed like practice for this.

"You remembered," she said.

"Every step."

Draconia looked at him, and he realised the answer had said more than he intended. He led her through the first turn, the black fabric revealing green beneath his hand and the silver leaves catching the lights. Her ease made it possible to forget the rest of the floor.

"Luna looks lovely," she said.

"So does Blaise."

Her eyebrows rose. "Do you find him attractive?"

"I find his hand irritating."

"His hand."

"When it's on you."

Draconia drew a slow breath. "That sounds like jealousy."

"Hermione said I wasn't glad she came with Krum."

"Are you?"

"No."

"And Blaise?"

Harry looked at her. "No."

The honesty frightened him less the second time. Draconia's fingers moved against his shoulder.

"You told me to accept."

"I know."

"You did not ask me."

"I know."

"Then you do not possess the right to resent him for doing so."

"I know that too."

She searched his face. "That is inconveniently reasonable."

"I'm trying something new."

The corner of her mouth lifted. They completed another turn, and Harry felt the trophy-room lesson beneath this one: the missed step, the glass against Draconia's back and her breath close to his face. She remembered too. He saw it when her gaze lowered briefly towards his mouth before returning to his eyes.

His hand shifted at her waist, and she moved closer. The Hall blurred around them, lights and colours passing beyond a space that suddenly seemed much smaller.

"Why didn't you ask?" she said.

"Why didn't you?"

Her step faltered so slightly that anyone else might have blamed the music.

"I nearly did."

Harry forgot the next count. "When?"

"In the trophy room." Her voice remained controlled, which made the answer harder to hear. "You were holding me. I believed—"

She stopped, recovered the step and made herself finish.

"Then you said Hermione's name."

Harry looked towards Hermione without meaning to. She was dancing with Krum on the opposite side of the floor. Draconia followed his gaze, and understanding sharpened her face.

"Of course."

She began to step away. Harry kept her hand.

"That isn't—"

"You were deciding."

"No."

"You looked at Hermione while I asked why you did not choose me."

"Because she is the reason I couldn't."

Draconia's expression emptied. Harry heard the cruelty only after speaking.

"Not because I wanted her instead."

"An important distinction you might have led with."

"I meant—"

The music ended. Draconia removed her hand.

"Thank you for the dance."

She returned to Blaise before Harry could finish. He remained on the edge of the floor, watching the space she had left and wondering how a sentence could become so much worse while he was still trying to explain it.

Luna found him beside an ice sculpture, watching the silver stag move between frozen trees and considering whether he could fit inside the block.

"Your dances went badly," she said.

"How could you tell?"

"Hermione danced very fast afterwards, and Draconia has explained the properties of her corsage to three people who did not ask."

Harry rested his forehead against the cold ice. "I should stop talking."

"That would make the next dance difficult."

"You still want another?"

"Yes."

The band abandoned formal music for something faster. Students cheered, couples dissolved into groups, and Fred and George climbed onto two chairs until McGonagall removed the chairs and allowed them to continue from the floor.

Luna took Harry's hand.

"I don't know how to dance to this," he said.

"Neither does anyone else."

She pulled him into the crowd. There was no pattern to fail, and Harry moved because the music demanded it. Luna spun beneath his arm, silver-blue robes lifting around her. Neville collided with Dean, Ginny formed a circle with several Ravenclaws, and Cedric and Cho abandoned dignity completely.

For three songs, Harry stopped watching the edge of the room. He laughed, and Luna laughed too, quieter and stranger. When he attempted one of Draconia's turns at twice the correct speed, they nearly fell into Hagrid, who caught them with one hand each.

"Careful!"

Harry straightened. "Sorry."

Hagrid wore an enormous brown suit and a flower larger than Luna's head on one lapel. Madame Maxime stood beside him in violet silk.

"Yeh look happy," he said.

Harry looked at Luna. "I am. Briefly."

"Briefly counts."

Hagrid pushed them gently back towards the dancers, his booming laughter following them. When Harry glanced towards the edge of the crowd, Hermione was watching beside Krum. Draconia stood near Blaise with the same arrested expression. Harry's laughter caught for half a beat, but Luna seized his hand and pulled him beneath Cedric's raised arm before he could begin interpreting either face.

By eleven, formal order had surrendered. The band played without pause, shoes accumulated beneath tables, ties loosened and carefully arranged hair escaped. Enchanted snow began falling inside the Hall after Fred and George altered a ceiling charm. Flitwick corrected the temperature but allowed the flakes to remain, settling in students' hair without melting.

Harry and Luna joined Sirius's table to recover. Mrs Granger moved at once to make room, her eyes following Hermione across the floor with open pride. Mr Granger had abandoned the formal cloak and was listening while Lucius explained Quidditch through the language of cricket.

"But if the batsman—" Mr Granger began.

"He is not a batsman."

"He has a bat."

"That is incidental."

"To hitting the ball?"

Lucius looked towards Narcissa for assistance. She had chosen to ignore both of them.

Harry sat beside Sirius. Luna complimented Remus on the moonstone pin at his collar; he thanked her before realising that she knew exactly what it referenced. She smiled and began speaking with Mrs Granger about migrating moon frogs.

Sirius leaned towards Harry. "How bad?"

"Why does everyone know?"

"You danced with Hermione like the room was empty, then Draconia returned to her partner looking prepared to poison someone."

"She usually looks like that."

"Not at Christmas."

Harry watched Draconia dance with Blaise again. Whatever anger she had brought from their conversation was hidden behind her composure. Blaise did not demand that it disappear. He spoke quietly, waited when she withdrew into thought and changed the subject when she seemed to need him to.

"He's decent," Harry said.

Sirius followed his gaze. "How unfortunate."

"Krum too."

"Tragic."

Harry looked at him. "You aren't helping."

"I advised you before they acquired decent partners."

"I tried."

"You discussed heated eggs."

Harry rested his forehead on the table. Sirius laughed and placed an arm across his shoulders.

"Stop trying to solve the whole thing tonight."

"It feels like everyone else already has."

"They chose dance partners. Not lives."

"You said that before."

"You remain unconvinced."

Harry turned his head against his folded arms. Hermione stood at the edge of the floor speaking with Krum. She looked older in the blue robes, partly because he listened as though everything she said deserved space. Harry was glad someone gave her that. He hated being glad.

"Am I awful?" he asked.

Sirius's arm tightened slightly. "No."

"I'm jealous of people who haven't done anything wrong."

"Feelings aren't offences."

"What you do because of them can be."

Sirius looked at him with quiet pride.

"Remus?" Harry asked.

"Mostly Lily."

"Thought so."

"You're allowed to feel miserable while behaving well."

"Another excellent Christmas tradition."

Sirius kissed the top of his hair before he could evade it.

"Go enjoy the part of the night you still have."

Luna returned from her discussion with Mrs Granger.

"There are no moon frogs," Harry told her.

"Not in Britain this time of year."

"Right."

She offered her hand. Harry took it, and they danced until the Hall became too warm. Then Luna led him outside.

The rose garden had been transformed beneath domes of heated air. Frost covered the hedges beyond them, while clear paths wound between flowering arches. Fairies slept inside lanterns. Water rose from the fountains in silver ribbons, freezing into changing shapes before falling again. Music reached the garden through the open doors, softened by distance.

Harry and Luna walked without speaking. Their silence felt as she had promised: comfortable enough that he did not need to find a way to fill it.

Near the far fountain, Hagrid sat beside Madame Maxime on a reinforced bench. The flower on his lapel had lost several petals. Her laughter carried across the garden, deep and surprised, while Hagrid attempted to smooth his hair and made it larger.

Harry stopped behind a hedge.

"We should leave them alone," he whispered.

Luna watched Hagrid offer Maxime what appeared to be a dragon tooth wrapped in ribbon.

"That is romantic."

"Is it?"

"For Hagrid."

Maxime accepted the tooth, and his whole face brightened. Harry and Luna retreated before he noticed them, finding an empty bench beneath an arch of winter roses. Luna removed her shoes and tucked her feet beneath her robes.

"Do gargoyles steal those often?" Harry asked.

"No. Usually people do."

The answer made him look at her. She examined a glass star that had fallen from her braid, turning it between her fingers.

"People hide my things because they think confusion is funny. I usually find them."

"Why didn't you tell someone?"

"I did. They asked whether I was certain I had not misplaced them."

Harry knew that kind of question. It made the harm sound like a misunderstanding, something that could be settled by asking the person hurt to look more carefully.

"Tell me next time."

Luna tilted her head. "Will you threaten the gargoyle?"

"Sirius can. He enjoys it."

She smiled. Harry looked towards the lighted windows of the Hall, where figures moved beyond the glass. He could not distinguish Hermione or Draconia among them.

"Thank you for coming with me."

"I wanted to."

"I know."

The knowledge still felt unusual. Luna placed the fallen star in his palm, its points small and cool against his skin.

"You aren't in love with me."

Harry swallowed hard. "We discussed that."

"You keep being afraid I will forget."

"I don't want to hurt you."

"That is not the same as being responsible for every feeling I might have."

He turned the star between his fingers. "Sirius said something similar."

"He is less unstable than the newspapers suggest."

"High praise."

Luna looked through the arch towards the Hall. "You love them."

"Everyone is very interested in telling me that tonight."

"Have they?"

"Sirius used the word once."

"And you?"

Harry thought of Hermione on the moonlit landing, Draconia between him and the trophy cabinet, both girls at the arena rail. He thought of the way home had stopped being one place and become the certainty that they would look for him.

"They're my best friends."

"Those statements are not opposites."

"I know." He closed his fingers around the star. "I don't know if I'm in love with either of them. Or both of them. I don't know what it's meant to feel like, and everyone keeps speaking as though I've missed a lesson they attended."

"Perhaps they are trying to name it because uncertainty makes them uncomfortable."

"Doesn't it make you uncomfortable?"

"Often. I don't think that makes the uncertain thing less true."

Harry watched the glass catch the lantern light. "What am I supposed to do?"

"Not decide because other people are impatient."

He looked at her. "Where do you get these things?"

"My father subscribes to several excellent magazines."

Harry laughed. Luna rested her head briefly against his shoulder, and he let his head touch hers. The warmth was real, but it carried none of the breathless uncertainty Hermione's hand against his neck had created, none of the pull he felt when Draconia's grey eyes dropped towards his mouth. Luna was his friend. Knowing that made it easy to remain close without wondering what ought to happen next.

Hermione found Viktor beside the dance floor. He had waited while she watched the garden windows, where reflected snow reduced Harry and Luna to two pale shapes beneath the roses.

She knew Luna was not replacing her. Harry had said they were friends, and Luna showed none of the guarded hope Hermione recognised in herself. Knowing that did not prevent the jealousy; it made its cause harder to ignore. Harry had asked Luna an honest question and trusted her with the honest reason. He had accepted her hand, her silence and now the weight of her head without retreating from any of it.

Hermione envied how safely he gave that affection. She could remember him looking at her on the landing, beginning to ask, and the care with which he had put the question away again.

"You vant to go after him," Viktor said.

She turned. "No."

The lie sounded poor even to her. He looked towards the window.

"You like him."

Hermione folded her hands at her waist. "He is my best friend."

"This is not answer."

His directness had none of Draconia's sharpness. He stated facts as though saving everyone the labour of pretending.

"I'm sorry," Hermione said.

"For vhat?"

"You asked me here. I should not spend the evening looking for someone else."

Viktor considered her. "I ask because you are clever, and kind when no one is looking. You argue vith books. You do not become less those things because you look for Potter."

Tears threatened for the second time that evening. Hermione had spent so long trying not to disappoint him that she had scarcely considered the possibility that he might understand.

"That is a surprisingly nice thing to say."

"People expect less because I do not speak much."

"People often confuse silence with having nothing to say."

"Yes."

He offered his hand. "One more dance?"

Hermione took it. This time, she gave him the attention he had offered her all evening. Whatever else the Ball had held, she did not regret accepting his invitation.

Blaise found Draconia near the frozen fountain. She had left the dance floor after seeing Harry and Luna disappear into the garden. The corsage remained fresh at her wrist, white petals opening and closing beneath the lights.

She did not dislike Luna Lovegood. Dislike would have made the feeling easier to defend. Luna had done nothing except accept the evening Harry freely offered her, and that was what hurt. He had danced with her before the school, laughed without checking who witnessed it and followed her outside without recoiling from what people might assume. Whenever the same possibility arose between him and Draconia, he looked towards Hermione and stepped away.

"You may follow him," Blaise said.

Draconia looked at him. "I have no intention of following anyone."

"Then you may continue staring at the door. It is an excellent door."

"Your concern is unnecessary."

"My concern is primarily for the evening becoming tedious."

Her mouth tightened. He leaned beside her against the fountain, his shoulder almost touching hers.

"You wanted him to ask."

"Everyone has developed an exhausting interest in that subject."

"Most people recognised it before you did."

"I recognised it."

"And accepted me."

Guilt interrupted her irritation.

"Blaise—"

"I knew."

She became still. "Why ask?"

He looked through the open doors, where his mother spoke with Narcissa beneath the Hall's silver branches.

"Because I enjoy your company. Because our mothers would have arranged it if we delayed another week. Because I thought Potter might discover courage when presented with competition."

"That was manipulative."

"I am a Slytherin."

"It did not work."

"Yes. That has been difficult."

Draconia looked at him properly. His expression remained composed, but there was disappointment beneath it: the knowledge that he could behave perfectly and still occupy the wrong place in someone's attention. She recognised more of it than she wanted to.

"I am sorry," she said.

"Do not be. You did not promise more than an evening."

He offered his hand. "We still have twelve minutes of it."

Draconia accepted. They returned to the floor, and for the final song she stopped searching the room.

The Ball ended at midnight. The last song slowed until conversation rose above it, lanterns dimmed, and McGonagall began directing students towards their common rooms. On the platform, the band packed its instruments beneath shrinking charms.

Viktor escorted Hermione into the Entrance Hall. He thanked her for the evening, asked whether she would write during the remainder of the Tournament, and waited until she agreed before kissing the back of her hand.

"Goodnight, Hermione."

He pronounced it correctly.

"Goodnight, Viktor."

Draconia watched Hermione follow his departure with her eyes as he joined the students returning to the Durmstrang ship. When Blaise offered to walk Draconia to the dungeons, she looked towards Hermione, alone beneath the last drifting snowflakes.

"I need to speak with her."

He followed her gaze. "Of course."

He removed the corsage before its fastening could catch on her sleeve and returned it to the black box.

"My mother will want evidence that the flowers survived."

"Tell her they behaved impeccably."

"Unlike the evening."

Draconia almost smiled. Blaise bowed over her hand without touching his lips to it.

"Goodnight, Draconia."

"Goodnight, Blaise."

He descended towards the Slytherin common room. Draconia joined Hermione, and together they looked towards the staircase Harry had taken with Luna.

Harry walked Luna towards Ravenclaw Tower. She carried her shoes in one hand, their straps swinging with each step.

"You know those work better on your feet."

"They wanted to see the castle."

At the first landing, she stopped. "I had a lovely time."

"So did I."

"Even the difficult parts?"

"Not those."

"They were still part of the time."

Harry considered it. "Then mostly."

Luna hugged him, putting her arms around his middle without warning. He returned the embrace carefully, reminded of the healing burns and the strange tenderness of being trusted by someone who expected nothing unspoken. She kissed his cheek.

"Goodnight, Harry."

Before he found an answer, she climbed the stairs barefoot and disappeared around the turn. Harry touched his cheek, more startled by the gesture than moved by it.

"That looked romantic."

Hermione stood at the bottom of the landing. Draconia was beside her, one hand closed around the other wrist. Most students had gone, and without the crowd the Entrance Hall seemed too large for the three of them. Every footstep and dying note of music lingered beneath the ceiling.

Neither girl sounded merely curious. Harry lowered his hand.

"It wasn't."

"She kissed you," Draconia said.

"On the cheek."

"Her head was on your shoulder in the garden," Hermione added.

"Were you watching us?"

"We went outside for air."

"Together?"

Draconia folded her arms, hiding the hand she had been holding, though Harry saw her fingers tremble before they disappeared beneath her sleeve.

"Is that relevant?"

"Apparently who stands near whom is very relevant tonight."

The defensiveness arrived before he could stop it. He heard the accusation in his own voice and resented it, which only made him sound guiltier.

Hermione's expression tightened. "We asked a question."

"No. You told me what it looked like."

"It looked romantic," Draconia repeated.

"Luna did nothing wrong."

"We know," Hermione said at once.

The agreement should have helped. It did not. She came one step higher, blue skirts whispering across the stone.

"That almost makes it worse."

Harry stared at her. "How?"

"Because Luna isn't what we're asking about." Hermione's voice was controlled, but only just. "You asked her an honest question. You told her why you were asking. You let her answer without trying to guess what everyone else might think."

"You danced with her in front of the entire school," Draconia said. "You let her take your arm. You followed her into the garden. You did not retreat the instant somebody might notice that you cared for her."

"She's my friend."

"So are we," Hermione said.

The simple answer struck harder than the jealousy.

"It's different with Luna."

"Yes," Draconia said. "We had noticed."

"That isn't what I meant." Harry glanced towards the staircase, but Luna had vanished beyond the turn. "I knew what asking her meant. I knew what she was to me."

Hermione did not look away. "And what is that?"

"My friend. Exactly that. There was no other question hidden underneath it. I wasn't afraid that one wrong sentence would turn us into strangers."

Silence opened between them. Draconia's arms loosened, revealing something less protected beneath the anger in her face.

"And with us?" Hermione asked.

Harry had no answer that did not expose too much. His failure to speak became one anyway, and Draconia looked away first.

His own hurt rose to meet theirs, sudden and unreasonable.

"You were both with other people all night."

Hermione gave a small, disbelieving laugh. "There it is."

"What?"

"The part where you make this our fault."

"I didn't."

"You looked at Viktor as though he had personally arranged the Tournament."

"And you watched Blaise's hand more closely than you watched half the opening dance," Draconia added.

Heat climbed Harry's neck. "I wasn't watching his hand."

"You nearly walked into a Christmas tree."

"It moved."

"The tree was rooted to the floor."

"Then someone put it in a stupid place."

For a dangerous second, Hermione looked as though she might laugh. The expression collapsed before it reached her mouth.

"Viktor listened to me," she said.

Harry stopped defending himself. Her chin lifted, although her voice grew quieter.

"He asked about my work. He remembered what I said. When I spoke, he did not look across the room to see whether someone more important required him."

"I listen to you."

"Usually while attempting the exact thing I have asked you not to do."

"That still requires listening."

"Harry."

The tiredness in his name removed the last of the joke. He looked at the blue ribbon in her hair and remembered Krum bending close to hear her over the music. Hermione had smiled because somebody gave her his whole attention without appearing frightened by how much of it she deserved.

"I hated it," he admitted.

She went still.

"Not him," Harry said, then stopped because it was not entirely true and Krum did not deserve the lie. "Maybe a little bit him. I hated how easily he made you smile. I hated that he knew how to stand beside you without making it seem complicated. And I hated knowing I had no right to be angry about any of it."

Hermione's anger wavered. Her fingers closed around the folds of her skirt.

Harry turned to Draconia. The green stone securing her hair caught the remaining candlelight, and a pale strand had slipped near her temple. Blaise had probably stood close enough to notice it. The thought tightened something beneath Harry's ribs again.

"He put his hand where mine had been," he said.

Draconia's lips parted.

"In the trophy room. When we practised. I knew exactly where Blaise was touching you because I could remember what it felt like to hold you there. Then he offered you his hand at the top of the stairs, and it looked as though he had stepped into my place."

Her face changed with painful slowness.

"You gave him that place."

"I know."

"You told me to accept him."

"I know."

"Stop saying that as if it makes the evening less humiliating."

Her voice broke on the final word. She turned aside at once, furious with the sound. Harry had seen her bloodied, possessed and barely conscious. He had seen her wake from nightmares with her wand drawn. The loss of control in her voice still startled him, precisely because he knew how hard she worked to prevent anyone hearing it.

"I'm not trying to humiliate you."

"No," she said. "You merely looked at me as though you wanted something, stepped away whenever I believed you might ask for it and then watched another boy provide what you had refused."

"Because he asked," Hermione said. "That is the entire difference, Harry. They asked."

"And you said yes."

"You told us to."

"I didn't make you go with them."

"No," Hermione said. "You made it clear that waiting for you would leave us standing alone."

That was unfair. It was also close enough to the truth that Harry could not dismiss it. The last guests crossed the distant corridor in twos and threes, their laughter softened by the walls. Somewhere behind the closed doors, an instrument struck a final uncertain chord.

Three staircases waited to carry them in different directions.

"You could have asked me," Harry said.

Neither girl moved. There was the sentence he had carried since Hermione admitted she wanted him to choose her. Spoken aloud, it sounded smaller than the weeks of silence built around it, and much more wounded.

Hermione's face changed first. "You're serious."

"Why wouldn't I be?"

"Because you were the champion," Draconia said. "You were required to bring a partner."

"That doesn't mean I wanted to be treated like an assignment."

Hermione flinched. Harry regretted the phrasing, but not the truth beneath it.

"I spend half my life being told where I'm supposed to stand and what everyone expects me to do. The school decided I would ask one of you before I had worked out what asking meant. Rita wrote about it. People placed bets. Even you two kept watching me as though there was a correct answer I was meant to know."

"There wasn't a correct answer," Hermione said.

"Then why did it feel as if one wrong choice would ruin everything?"

Neither answered. Harry pressed on, afraid that stopping would return them to everything they had failed to say.

"Maybe I wanted to know you wanted me there too. Not because I was champion. Not because everyone assumed it. Me."

"We did," Hermione whispered.

"You never asked."

"Neither did you."

"I tried."

"You stopped."

"So did both of you."

That landed. Hermione looked down at the step between them. Draconia drew herself straighter, but without her usual certainty.

"I nearly asked you in the trophy room."

"I know. You told me."

"You said Hermione's name while you were holding me."

Hermione's eyes lifted. "And I waited. Because I wanted one evening when I did not have to arrange everything for you. I wanted you to look at me and know."

"I did look at you."

"You looked. You almost spoke. Then every time it mattered, you found a reason to leave."

Harry remembered the library table, the staircase, Hermione waiting while he gathered courage and spent it on nothing. He remembered Draconia beneath the trophy-room lights, close enough to feel each breath, watching his attention break away from her.

"So all three of us waited," he said.

No one contradicted him. Understanding did not absolve any of them; it made the waste visible. They had stood within arm's reach for weeks and allowed fear to escort them separately into an evening each had wanted to share.

"Why?" Hermione asked.

Harry knew she meant more than the invitation. The familiar explanations waited: the Tournament, the Prophet, the school, nerves. All were true, and none reached the centre.

"Because every time I looked at one of you, I thought about the other."

The Hall became quiet enough for Harry to hear the soft disappearance of enchanted snow against the floor. Draconia unfolded her arms.

"Explain."

It sounded like an order issued because the alternative was fear. Harry's heart beat painfully beneath his formal robes.

"When I tried to ask Hermione, I pictured you watching us open the Ball."

Draconia did not move. He turned towards Hermione.

"When I tried to ask Draco, I remembered your face on the staircase. I knew what choosing her might say to you."

Hermione's eyes shone. "So you chose neither."

"I thought nothing would change."

"And how did that work?"

Harry almost laughed, but nothing about her face permitted it.

"Badly."

Draconia watched him with unnerving stillness. "Which of us did you want?"

The question demanded a singular answer. Harry had spent weeks trying to force one into existence. Watching Hermione with Viktor had not erased the wrongness of Blaise's hand on Draconia. Dancing with Draconia had not dimmed the memory of Hermione against him. Everything he felt for one remained when he looked at the other.

"Both," he said.

Hermione drew a sharp breath. Draconia's expression emptied so completely that he might have preferred fury.

"And I know that isn't an answer either of you can do anything with," Harry continued. "I can't do anything with it. I wanted each of you there, and every version in which I chose one ended with the other standing somewhere else. Asking one felt like telling the other she mattered less." He swallowed. "That would have been a lie. So I said nothing, which apparently communicated something worse."

"It did," Hermione said.

Her voice was small now, frightening him more than shouting would have.

"You made both of us believe you did not want us enough to ask."

"I know."

"Stop saying that."

This time, her voice broke. Harry stopped. She wiped beneath one eye before the tear could mark the makeup her mother had helped her apply, but another gathered anyway.

Seeing it rearranged the Ball in his memory: Hermione at the top of the staircase, searching the room for him; Hermione giving him the dance she had hoped would belong to them; Hermione saying, as plainly as she could, that she had waited. He had hurt her before the evening began, then again as it ended.

"You keep saying you know as though understanding the mistake means we are no longer hurt by it," she said.

"I don't know what else to say."

"The truth would have been useful three weeks ago."

"I didn't understand it three weeks ago."

Draconia looked towards the staircase Luna had climbed.

"But you understood what you wanted from Lovegood."

"Because it was different."

"Safer," Hermione said.

Harry looked at her. "Yes."

The admission seemed to take the remaining warmth from the Hall.

"I wasn't afraid of losing everything if Luna said no. I wasn't afraid that asking her would change the shape of my whole life."

He heard what he had admitted only after the words were beyond recall. Hermione looked away, and Draconia's anger thinned enough to reveal the wound beneath it.

"You were honest with her because she mattered less."

"No." Harry answered too quickly for doubt. "Luna mattered enough for me not to lie to her. She is my friend, and I care about her. But there was no almost with Luna. No moment when I thought she might—"

He stopped, unable to finish under both girls' attention.

"You two are the part I don't know how to risk."

Hermione's eyes returned to him. "We are not something you can preserve by refusing to touch it."

"I know."

She closed her eyes.

"Sorry," he said.

Draconia's fingers tightened against her sleeves. "When you danced with Hermione, you forgot the room."

Harry's gaze fell.

"When you danced with me, you looked for her."

"With Hermione, I still knew exactly where you were. With you, I looked for her because I already knew I had hurt her. Neither meant I wanted the person in my arms less."

Draconia breathed in carefully. "That distinction is not especially comforting."

"It wasn't meant to be. It was true."

The words landed differently from his earlier explanations. For a second, anger gave way to something stranger. Harry saw the same bewildered possibility cross both faces before caution returned. Naming one part of what existed between them had not made it simpler, but they could no longer pretend it was absent.

Then Draconia stepped back.

"You do not get to discover this while we are standing in dress robes after attending with other people."

"I didn't plan to."

"No. Planning was not the issue."

Hermione almost smiled and failed. Harry looked at her.

"What happens now?"

She folded her arms around herself. "I don't know."

"Eventually," Draconia said, "you will require a better answer than both."

Hermione's gaze remained on him. "Not tonight."

"No," Draconia agreed. "Neither do we."

"That seems fair."

"Do not make a joke."

"I wasn't."

That was worse. Hermione glanced towards Draconia, and a conversation passed between them without Harry. Hurt had not erased their alliance; it made the connection more visible. They had spent the evening comparing themselves to each other and still ended it on the same step.

"We need time," Hermione said.

Panic moved through him. Time meant distance. Distance became unanswered letters, locked doors and discovering too late that everyone had learned to live without him.

"How much?"

Draconia heard the fear. Her expression softened by a fraction.

"Not forever."

"That isn't a measurement."

"No," Hermione said. "It's a promise."

Harry looked at her. They had asked for promises before, and he knew how much weight each of them placed on the word. He made himself trust this one.

"All right."

Draconia turned towards the dungeon staircase, then stopped before taking the first step.

"For what it is worth," she said without looking back, "I wanted you to ask."

The green stone in her hair gleamed once more before the turn of the staircase hid her. Harry listened to the measured tread of her shoes fade below them, fighting the impulse to follow before she could get any farther away. Hermione remained.

"And I did want to ask you," she said quietly.

"Why didn't you?"

"For some of the same reasons you didn't ask us." Her gaze followed Draconia's retreat. "I was frightened of the answer. I was frightened of what it would mean for her. And I wanted you to choose me without having to be told."

The honesty left no one innocent. Harry looked towards the empty staircase, then back at Hermione in the robes he had not known she would wear to a Ball he had never asked her to attend.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Her mouth trembled. "So am I."

She followed Draconia downstairs. Harry remained alone on the landing while enchanted snow fell through the air, vanishing before it could settle.

He did not sleep. He lay in the dark wearing his shirt and formal trousers because removing them required movement he could not imagine beginning. The green dress robes hung from the bedpost, beautiful and empty. Beyond the curtains, the other boys returned, talked quietly and finally fell silent, leaving Harry with every version of the confrontation.

Both.

The word seemed impossible beneath the canopy: greedy, honest, too large for fourteen and too simple for the weeks he had spent avoiding it.

He had wanted proof that they wanted him. They had arrived beneath his barred window, read his memories, followed him into danger, faced Sirius and challenged professors. They found him in empty corridors and knew the difference between every lie he told about being fine. Apparently, none of that had counted because he wanted one perfectly phrased invitation.

Shame settled heavily beneath his ribs. Then anger answered. They could have asked. They had wanted him to take the risk because they were afraid to do it themselves, waiting for him to choose while knowing that choice was the thing most likely to frighten him.

Their fault did not remove his, and his did not remove theirs. Lying awake with both truths made the night harder rather than easier.

The mirror brightened twice on his table. Sirius. Harry turned it facedown; he could not explain without hearing the disappointment in his own voice.

At four, he took the robes from the bedpost and folded them into his trunk. At five, he polished Luna's glass star where his palm had dulled it and placed it beside the mirror. At six, he took the golden egg from beneath his bed and left Gryffindor Tower.

Hermione waited at the foot of the marble staircase with Draconia beside her. Both had changed into ordinary clothes, but Hermione's hair remained partly pinned from the Ball. Draconia had removed the silver leaves and missed one near the back of her head. The shadows beneath her eyes suggested she had slept no more than Hermione.

They looked towards the staircase as students began descending for breakfast. Harry did not appear. After twenty-five minutes, Hermione reached inside her bag for the Marauder's Map, but Draconia stopped her.

"We promised him time."

"We promised not forever."

"It has been six hours."

"He didn't answer Sirius."

Draconia looked at her. "How do you know?"

"Sirius called me."

They stood beneath the Christmas garlands while students passed around them, carrying yesterday's presents and talking about the evening as though it had ended when the music stopped. Hermione kept her hand inside her bag.

"He should have been here fifteen minutes ago."

Draconia looked towards the empty staircase. "Five more."

They waited. Five minutes passed, and Hermione unfolded the map.

Thousands of ink marks travelled through the castle. Students gathered in the Hall, drifted through corridors and climbed staircases after too little sleep. Harry's dot sat motionless inside an unused classroom on the fifth floor. Hermione found it quickly, then checked the label again as though she might have misread it.

Draconia looked over her shoulder.

"He is safe."

"He is alone."

"That is what he chose."

"Those are not the same thing."

Draconia closed her eyes briefly. "No."

Hermione began folding the map, keeping Harry's name in sight until the last crease.

"We said time, not abandonment. We don't enter. We leave food outside the door and tell Sirius where he is."

"He will know we found him."

"Good."

Draconia looked towards the stairs. "Tea as well."

"Without milk. It will go cold."

"He drinks it cold when pretending not to notice."

They went to the kitchens together.

Harry sat alone in the unused Charms classroom. He had taken the route behind the tapestry on the fifth floor, avoiding the Great Hall and every staircase Hermione expected him to use. Snow filled the windows, and the room remained grey without lamps. Desks stood where they had been pushed during earlier experiments, leaving an empty space on either side of him.

The golden egg rested before him, its dent turned towards the window. He ran a thumb along the shallow mark left by the arena, then found the handles and opened it. Screaming filled the classroom. He cast no recording charm, compared no languages and made no list of environments. He listened until the noise was large enough to occupy every place their voices had been.

Then he closed the shell. Silence returned.

For the first time since arriving at Hogwarts, silence felt more familiar than home.
