11

On the first Monday of October, Montague was waiting between Harry and the toast. He had acquired a clipboard to go with his silver badge, and appeared to regard both as a reason for everyone else to miss breakfast. By then Hogwarts had developed two separate governments. The first operated through decrees, silver badges and the authority to turn out a student's pockets between breakfast and Transfiguration. The second operated through counterfeit Galleons, moving staircases and the collective decision that Montague should never be allowed to walk through a doorway without checking the lintel for glue. Neither appeared capable of leaving Harry out of its business. One blamed him for disorder; the other wanted to know whether he had found a better way to hide their coins.

"Bag," Montague said.

He stood at the entrance to the marble staircase with Warrington and a Ravenclaw prefect named Chambers. Each wore the silver knot of the Inquisitorial Patrol. Behind them, students formed an untidy queue while breakfast cooled inside the Great Hall. A second-year near the back kept checking the clock over the doors, clutching a roll of homework so tightly that its edges had begun to crease. Somewhere behind Harry, someone complained that they would be late for Herbology. Montague did not look away from Harry long enough to answer. Harry had hoped to go back for another piece of toast before Transfiguration. He could still smell the breakfast from here. The ordinary wish made the hand barring his way more irritating, not less.

Harry looked at the hand Montague held out.

"dont you already have one."

"Your bag, Potter."

"geez, getting abit greedy are you?"

Warrington moved to Harry's left. Chambers had the decency to appear embarrassed.

"Watch your snark, its random inspection," Montague said.

"You searched me yesterday."

"Randomly."

"And Saturday."

"Also random."

"Remarkable how chance keeps deciding on me."

Ron made a choking sound behind him. Harry handed over the bag. It contained three textbooks, two rolls of parchment, a half-finished essay, Remus's homemade Defence manual and a plain Galleon nestled among six ordinary coins. Hermione had altered the communication charm after the first meeting. The coins now required their owner's touch and a quiet revealing charm before showing a message. Everyone else saw the copied minting date and a mildly disappointing prediction concerning turnip prices. Hermione had made them practise closing the message again before putting a coin away. Harry had left his hidden; the familiar weight was more reassuring than anything written on it. Montague emptied everything onto the floor. The Galleons scattered across the stone.

Harry's heart struck once against his ribs. Chambers crouched to gather them. His fingers closed around the charmed coin.

"Money isn't prohibited," he said.

"Check it," Montague ordered.

Chambers turned the coin beneath the morning light. The edge read TURNIP FUTURES EXPECTED TO REMAIN STABLE beneath the copied date. Chambers squinted at it, turned it once more and appeared to decide that the joke did not warrant further investigation.

"It's a Galleon."

"Where did Potter get seven Galleons?"

Harry looked at him. "I have a vault, Montague. They didn't check your finances before giving you the badge, did they?"

"Everyone knows your relatives never sent you anything," Montague said. He glanced towards the queue to make sure they had heard.

The corridor changed. Not visibly. Students still whispered. Cutlery moved beyond the doors. A suit of armour near the staircase adjusted one metal glove. For a moment Harry heard Vernon's voice rather than Montague's: Vernon's locked cupboard, Dudley receiving gifts while Harry learnt not to look interested, every empty hand used as evidence that he deserved nothing inside it. Montague smiled, watching for a reaction. Harry bent, picked up the coin and placed it in his pocket.

"Neither did yours," he said, "but they seem to have bought you a badge."

Ron laughed aloud. Several people in the queue followed. Montague's face darkened.

"Ten points from Gryffindor."

"For receiving financial advice?"

"Twenty."

"Turnips are controversial."

"Thirty." Montague wrote the recommendation on his clipboard, underlining it twice.

"Mr Montague."

McGonagall's voice descended the staircase before she did. The professor approached with a stack of marked essays beneath one arm. Her gaze moved from Harry's possessions across the floor to Montague's badge.

"On what grounds are you recommending thirty points, Mr Montague?"

"Disrespect towards the Inquisitorial Patrol."

"The patrol may recommend a deduction to the supervising professor," McGonagall said. "It may not award itself permission to remove points. I see no professor supervising this search."

Montague touched the badge. "Professor Umbridge said—"

"Then you may ask her to supervise. Until she does, strike out that recommendation. I will not approve a penalty for objecting to a search you were not authorised to conduct alone."

"But—"

McGonagall watched until Montague drew a line through the entry. The red hourglass beside the doors had not moved; he had managed to frighten the second-year near the back without costing Gryffindor a single gem.

"And in future," she added, "you will wait for a member of staff before emptying anyone's possessions onto the floor."

Harry looked up from his scattered books. "Does that mean I get to breakfast?"

"It means you have four minutes to finish breakfast before Transfiguration. The first bell has rung while you have been discussing root vegetables."

"I had rather less choice about the inspection."

"Which I shall remember if you are late. Now collect your books, Potter."

Montague stepped aside. Harry packed slowly enough to irritate him and quickly enough not to test McGonagall's patience. Ron helped, placing the Defence manual inside before Montague could read its title. They entered the Great Hall together.

"I can't decide whether that was brilliant or suicidal," Ron said.

"Those aren't opposites."

"You sound like the twins."

"I am going to take that as an insult."

"You should."

They reached the Gryffindor table. Hermione had saved two seats. Draconia sat at the end of the Slytherin table across the room, Pansy beside her and an empty place on her other side. She looked at Harry's bag. He tapped the pocket holding the coin. Safe. Her shoulders lowered by a degree. The movement was almost invisible. Harry noticed it anyway.

The D.A. continued to meet every Thursday. The first week's coins carried reminders and nervous questions; by the next meeting, they carried complaints about essays, requests for spare parchment and Fred's persistent campaign to give them a more interesting warning sound. Hermione refused the last request so many times that George began numbering the refusals.

Through September and into October, they learnt Disarming Charms, began Stunning practice and worked on moving a willing partner who was pretending to be injured. Hermione made them use padded straps and demonstrate how they would keep the person's head supported before allowing anyone to lift a classmate. Neville volunteered to play the casualty, discovered he disliked being carried upside down and became an enthusiastic critic of Ron's first attempt. Hermione kept the structure. Cedric taught technique. Draconia built complications into every exercise until even Fred complained she possessed an unhealthy relationship with ambushes.

Harry taught what happened when the room stopped being quiet. He practised every exercise with Cedric first, partly to check the spells and partly because he did not want to discover halfway through a meeting that he had asked everyone to do something he could not explain. More than once, Cedric stopped him and made him describe the easy version. Harry was beginning to understand how much he had learnt by having no choice, and how little use that was to someone holding a wand for their first attempt.

He taught students to cast while alarms sounded, lights failed or a partner shouted the wrong instruction. Nothing changed without warning. Anyone could refuse an exercise or leave for the quiet room. The difficulty came from choice rather than surprise. The distinction mattered more than Harry expected. Students attempted harder things when stopping belonged to them. Neville's shield grew wider. Ron learnt to cast while moving backwards and discovered that protecting another person produced stronger magic than protecting himself. Pansy became excellent at breaking sightlines with smoke and reflected light. Luna's shield remained shaped like an egg, but it could now roll slowly across the floor with her inside it. Nobody knew how to reproduce that.

"It feels safer when it is round," she explained.

Hermione wrote three pages attempting to determine whether feelings had altered the spell geometry. Theodore attended every meeting. He never arrived by the same route twice. His reports to Umbridge continued, carefully accurate about things which no longer mattered. Draconia had complained about her Potions mark. Pansy considered the new school cloaks badly cut. Harry argued with Ron during Quidditch practice. The details were true, harmless and sufficiently personal to make Umbridge believe access was increasing. Trust developed around Theodore without anyone naming it. It did not make his appointments harmless. Snape knew about the compulsory notices now, and Theodore could bring a question to him without first explaining which of his friends he feared it might endanger. He began standing further from the exits. At their seventh meeting, on the last Thursday of October, Harry began the evening with paired Stunning before they returned to the evacuation exercise.

The room warned them before producing red practice light along the walls. It was meant to make the spell's colour easier to track. Harry saw the first flash and returned to the graveyard. Stone beneath his knees. Cedric hidden by roots beside another grave. A circle of masked faces. Voldemort lifting his wand with something almost intimate in the angle of his wrist. Harry's shield rose before the spell was cast. It struck the ceiling. Gold light expanded across the room in a dome, knocking dust from beams which had not existed until that second. Every student stopped. Harry stood beneath the centre with his wand raised and breath trapped behind his ribs. The red practice lights vanished. Nobody had told the room to remove them.

Harry had stopped asking it for the practice lights. He was glad they had gone, even if he could not say whether the room had understood his fear or simply responded to the change in what he wanted.

"Harry," Hermione said.

"I'm here."

His voice sounded wrong. Draconia moved into his line of sight. Not closer. Visible.

"Where?"

"Seventh floor. Thursday."

"Who cast?"

Harry looked around. Neville held a wand but had not released the spell. Cedric stood beside Hannah with his own lowered. Every red light had belonged to the room.

"Nobody."

"Good. Lower the shield when you choose."

Harry became aware of the strain running through his arm. He lowered his wand. Gold light dissolved. Twenty people pretended not to stare. Zacharias failed worst.

"Was that Voldemort?"

Cedric crossed the floor.

"It felt like a memory," Harry said. "I know where I am now. I'll report anything different to Remus."

"How do you know it wasn't—"

"I'm not going to work that out in front of everyone, Zacharias."

"You looked like—"

"He said no," Cedric interrupted.

His tone closed the subject. Harry looked at the quiet-room door. Every instinct encouraged him to keep teaching. If he stopped, twenty students would remember the shield and the fear which produced it. If he continued, they might remember something useful instead. Cedric recognised the calculation.

"My section," he said.

"I'm all right."

"That wasn't one of the conditions."

"I can still—"

"Harry."

Cedric held his gaze.

If you are injured, exhausted or having a bad day, you say so before you begin.

Harry had agreed. He hated that promises continued existing after they became inconvenient.

"Your section," he said.

Cedric nodded once. Harry went to the quiet room with Hermione and Draconia while Cedric reorganised the pairs. No one applauded Harry for stepping away. No one treated it as collapse. Through the closed door, Cedric's voice carried clearly.

"We'll leave the lights ordinary for this section," Cedric said. "The same colour can belong to different spells. Watch the wand movement, listen to the incantation and keep enough space to move. We're practising that slowly first."

Harry sat between the girls upon a low sofa.

"Everyone knows why he changed it," Harry said.

"Everyone also knows where they're meant to stand now," Draconia replied. "They have something to do besides watch us."

Hermione gave him water.

"You stopped."

"Cedric stopped me."

"You let him."

Harry drank. Outside, spells struck shields. Students shouted warnings, laughed at mistakes and tried again. The lesson continued without him. Harry listened until he could tell that the voices were absorbed in the exercise. He had wanted the lesson to work without depending on him. Now that it did, he needed a little time to enjoy the proof. Before leaving that evening, he used the mirror to give Remus a short account of the trigger. They could discuss the rest after Harry had slept.

The first Hogsmeade weekend was finally announced on Friday, the morning after their seventh meeting. The post-attack restrictions had kept the earlier weekends inside the castle; the notice arrived too late to make Saturday feel entirely planned. Notices appeared inside every common room and beside the entrance hall, listing permission requirements, departure times and seventeen new Ministry conditions. Bags would be searched when students left and returned. Purchases might be inspected. Groups larger than four were prohibited from remaining inside one establishment for more than thirty minutes unless registered as a social club.

"How does one register friendship?" Hermione asked.

"In triplicate," Harry said. "With evidence of responsible conversation."

They stood outside Charms reading the notice while autumn sunlight crossed the courtyard. The day was cold enough that breath showed briefly in the shadows. Red leaves had gathered along the cloisters, scraping over stone whenever the wind passed through.

"Saturday," Hermione said. "We should inspect whether the patrol extends into the village."

"We could also enjoy ourselves," Harry suggested.

She looked at him as though he had introduced an untested theory.

"While inspecting."

"Naturally."

Harry had been thinking about Saturday since the notice appeared at breakfast. He had not decided what to do with the thought. Last year's Yule Ball had taught him that waiting for certainty was an efficient way to discover everyone else possessed plans. He did not want to repeat it. Asking Hermione and Draconia to Hogsmeade together felt too much like announcing a decision he had not made. Asking one felt worse. Asking whether the three of them wanted to spend the day together sounded exactly like every ordinary weekend and nothing like what Harry meant.

He had spent the last twenty minutes constructing possible sentences. Every one became ridiculous before reaching a verb. He could ask Draconia to walk with him after they had all been to Honeydukes. That sounded harmless until he considered the way her fingers had closed around his in the passage after the first D.A. meeting. He could ask Hermione what she wanted to do and then stop looking for a meaning hidden behind her answer. That sounded sensible until he tried to imagine doing it.

He wanted the easy part back: both girls beside him, something funny happening which did not require him to decide what it meant. The trouble was that he wanted more than that too. Pretending the second wish had not arrived was beginning to spoil the first.

"We could—" he began.

"Hermione?"

Anthony Goldstein approached from the opposite corridor. His blue-and-bronze tie sat perfectly straight. He carried two books, one of which Harry recognised as the Arithmancy text Hermione had been searching for since Diagon Alley.

"I found the revised tables," Anthony said, handing it to her. "Scrivenshaft's has the companion volume. I thought we might look on Saturday, if you haven't made plans."

Hermione glanced at Harry. Only for a second. Long enough for him to understand that an answer had been possible before he remained silent.

"I'd like that," she said.

Anthony smiled.

"Ten at the gates?"

"All right."

He left for Charms. Hermione examined the book without opening it.

"You were saying?"

Harry looked at the sentence he had failed to reach.

"Nothing."

Her expression changed. Not enough to become anger. Enough to reveal she knew he was lying and had hoped he might stop.

"Of course," she said.

The classroom door opened. Students moved around them. Hermione entered with the book held too tightly against her chest. Harry turned towards Draconia.

"Don't," she said.

"Don't what?"

"Ask me because Granger is going with someone else."

Heat entered his face. "I wasn't."

"Good."

Draconia followed Hermione inside. Harry remained in the corridor. Ron arrived two minutes later and found him still there.

"Class started."

"I know."

"Are you protesting education?"

"Considering it."

Ron looked through the doorway. Hermione sat beside Lavender near the front. Draconia occupied the desk across the aisle. Neither had saved his usual place beside Hermione. The chair was occupied by an open bag, and Draconia was looking down at her notes across the aisle.

"Oh," Ron said.

Harry disliked how quickly everyone understood his life except him.

Blaise Zabini asked Draconia during lunch. Harry did not hear the beginning. He had been listening to Ron explain why Gryffindor's Chaser formation required revision and attempting not to look towards the Slytherin table more than once every thirty seconds. When he looked for the fifth time, Blaise had taken the empty place beside Draconia. He was speaking quietly. One elbow rested upon the table. His dark robes were immaculate, his posture easy in the way of people who had never been required to apologise for occupying space. Draconia listened. Then she looked directly at Harry.

"You're doing it again," Ron said.

"Doing what?"

"Trying to see through the back of somebody's head."

"I'm looking at the Slytherin table."

"Through Zabini's head."

Across the hall, Draconia answered. Blaise smiled. Pansy covered her mouth with one hand and said something which made Draconia kick her beneath the table.

"Looks as though she said yes," Ron observed.

Harry returned to his potatoes. He had eaten the fish without noticing it and now found the rest of the plate absurdly difficult to deal with. Across the Hall, a first-year was trying to persuade a spoon to balance on his nose. Somebody cheered when it stayed there. Harry watched for a moment, grateful for a thing which did not need interpreting, then ruined the relief by looking at Draconia again.

"You don't know what he asked."

"Neither do you."

"Exactly."

"But you've stabbed that potato four times since he sat down."

Harry looked at his fork. The potato had suffered.

"It was undercooked."

Ron glanced towards Hermione, who was discussing the Arithmancy book with Anthony at the Ravenclaw table.

"The carrots are terrible too," he said.

Harry followed his gaze.

"Oh."

"Don't."

"Don't what?"

"Understand."

"You understood first."

"That was different."

"How?"

Ron pushed the vegetables around his plate.

"I don't know. It just was."

They sat without speaking for a while. Harry knew the girls had every right to make their own plans; knowing it did very little for the unpleasant feeling beneath his ribs. Ron kept rearranging the vegetables as if an acceptable answer might be hidden under them.

"Saturday," Ron said eventually. "Want to go to Hogsmeade?"

Harry looked at him.

"Not like that."

"I didn't think—"

"Good."

"You asked very quickly."

"Forget it."

"No, I'll go."

Ron frowned. "You make friendship feel humiliating."

"I'm learning from you."

That surprised a laugh from him. Harry smiled despite himself. Across the hall, Draconia was no longer looking at Blaise. She was looking at Harry and Ron. Her face gave nothing away. Pansy, beside her, looked delighted.

Quidditch practice that evening was violent enough to concern Angelina. Harry flew as though speed might remove thought. The Firebolt cut across the pitch in steep turns, broom vibrating beneath his hands whenever he changed direction too quickly. Wind tore tears from his eyes. Below, Gryffindor robes moved between hoops while the late sun turned every window in the castle to copper.

"Potter!" Angelina shouted. "The Snitch is not personally responsible for your mood!"

Harry dived after it. The small gold ball dropped towards the stands. He followed until grass filled his vision, pulled out two feet above the ground and closed his fingers around the Snitch before momentum carried him between the lower rows of seats. His shoulder struck a bench. Pain flashed down his arm. The broom stopped sideways. Harry hung upside down with his knees hooked around the handle, one hand gripping the broom while the other held the Snitch. His robes fell across his face. He could hear Angelina land before he managed to push the fabric aside. Silence covered the pitch.

"Caught it," he said.

Angelina landed hard enough to throw dirt across his hair.

"Get down."

"I'm technically below the broom."

"Now."

Harry released his legs from the handle and dropped onto the grass. Ron descended from the centre hoop. The fastening on one Keeper glove had come loose, leaving a strap flapping against his wrist. He tightened it as soon as he landed.

"That was brilliant," he said.

Angelina turned upon him.

"Do not encourage him."

"It was also stupid."

"Better."

Harry opened his hand. The Snitch fluttered indignantly.

"Can I be encouraged a little?"

"No," both replied.

Angelina made him sit beside the stands for the remaining drills. She did not accept his attempt to rotate the shoulder as evidence that it was all right; she told him to have Madam Pomfrey examine it before he flew again. Harry considered arguing, caught sight of the scraped bench and decided he had already supplied enough evidence against himself.

The ache settled into a bruise rather than the sharp pain of the first impact. Harry set the Firebolt across his knees and listened to the calls overhead. From the ground he could see how far Ron moved before each shot and how quickly he recovered from a miss. He had been too busy trying to outrun his own thoughts to notice any of that in the air. Ron saved seven of ten shots during the final drill. He missed the eighth because Montague and Warrington appeared beside the pitch wearing patrol badges. Neither played for Gryffindor. Neither possessed a reason to inspect a closed team practice. Angelina flew directly towards them.

"Out."

Montague held up a parchment. "Inquisitorial safety inspection."

"This is a scheduled sports activity approved by Professor McGonagall."

"We received a report of dangerous spellwork."

"Those are Quaffles."

"One struck a student."

Katie Bell raised her hand. "That is generally the Keeper's purpose."

Montague's gaze moved towards Harry.

"We need to inspect the changing room."

The D.A. coin lay inside Harry's bag. Ron saw him glance towards it.

"I've got this," he said.

Before Harry could ask what this meant, Ron flew towards the centre hoop and deliberately mishandled the Quaffle. It struck the rim, rebounded across the pitch and caught Warrington between the shoulders. Warrington fell face-first into the mud. Every Gryffindor player became silent. Ron stared down from his broom.

"Sorry," he called. "Still learning."

Montague abandoned the inspection to help Warrington. Angelina landed beside Warrington to check that he could stand. He was furious rather than badly hurt. Once Montague had helped him off the pitch, she turned on Ron. "Keep it inside the practice area. I mean it." Ron looked suitably chastened for nearly five seconds. When Angelina ended the drill, Ron landed beside Harry near the goalposts. Harry stood carefully, keeping the Firebolt in his uninjured hand.

"You could have hurt him," Harry said, though he had to fight not to smile at the mud on Montague's robes.

Ron glanced back at Angelina. "I was aiming lower."

"That isn't quite the defence you think it is."

"I know. She explained it." He picked up his broom. "Still gave you time to get your bag."

They left the pitch after dark, brooms balanced over their shoulders. The path to the castle shone pale beneath a rising moon. Students moved behind lit windows, distant and golden.

"Did you want to ask both of them?" Ron asked.

Harry nearly walked into a shrub.

"What?"

"Hermione and Malfoy."

"I don't know."

"Useful."

"I wanted Saturday with them. That doesn't mean—"

"That you wanted Saturday with them?"

"It doesn't mean I know what to call it."

Ron kicked a loose stone from the path.

"Hermione would've said yes if you'd asked before Goldstein."

Harry looked at him.

"Does that bother you?"

"Does Zabini bother you?"

"That isn't an answer."

"Neither was yours."

They walked further.

"Yes," both said at once.

Ron laughed first. Harry joined him.

"We're pathetic," Ron said.

"You are. I remain complicated."

"You stared at a potato because Malfoy spoke to somebody."

"It knew what it did."

By the time they reached the doors, Saturday no longer felt entirely like being abandoned. Ron was still muttering about Anthony's perfect tie; Harry had contributed a lengthy and unconvincing objection to Blaise's gloves. It was easier to laugh at each other than admit how much either thing mattered.

Ron walked him to the hospital wing. Madam Pomfrey examined the bruised shoulder, reminded Harry that healing from one accident did not oblige him to arrange another, and made him demonstrate that he could lift the arm without pain before she let him leave. No bones were broken. Harry was given a salve, an evening off flying and a warning she required him to repeat back. Ron waited outside with both brooms and, for once, made no attempt to improve the story.

Frost still edged the lawn when Harry reached the gates on Saturday, the twenty-eighth of October. He had spent too long choosing a scarf for an outing he kept insisting was ordinary. The Inquisitorial Patrol searched every student at the gates. Harry's bag contained gloves, a scarf, twelve ordinary Galleons and the mirror linked to Sirius. Montague examined the mirror twice, read the guardianship registration engraved around its edge and attempted to confiscate it anyway.

"External communications are subject to inspection."

"Then inspect your reflection," Harry said. "The mirror stays with me."

"Failure to cooperate—"

"Would you like me to activate the alarm again?"

McGonagall looked up from the permission slips. "The registration is valid, Mr Montague. Return it." He handed it back. Harry put the mirror away before allowing himself a small smile. Hermione waited near the gates in a dark blue coat. Anthony stood beside her carrying a folded village map and attempting to conceal that he had planned their route. She had pinned her hair back from her face with a small silver clasp. Harry was used to seeing quills disappear into that same knot while she worked, then having to help her find them when she wanted to write. This morning no stray ends had escaped around her cheeks. She had clearly taken time over it. He wished he had said something before Anthony noticed everything first. She noticed him looking.

Her hand rose to the clasp. Then Anthony said something, and she turned away. Draconia arrived with Blaise ten minutes later. She wore green wool beneath a fitted black cloak. Her hair fell loose instead of being tied at the nape of her neck, pale enough to catch every piece of sunlight. Blaise offered his arm at the muddy section beyond the gate. Draconia took it. Harry walked into Ron.

"Ow."

"Sorry."

"You were looking the other way."

"I noticed."

Ron followed his gaze.

"We're not staring today."

"I wasn't."

"You changed direction without moving your feet."

"The path turned."

"It didn't."

They allowed the couples to go ahead. Hogsmeade waited beneath a sky scrubbed blue by the wind. Smoke rose from crooked chimneys. Shop signs swung above crowded pavements while students hurried between windows bright with sweets, quills and joke objects. The first frost remained in shadows between buildings. Somewhere near Zonko's, a firework released a flock of purple birds which circled the post office and vanished into sparks. Harry had visited the village in secret, beneath a cloak and without permission. Walking down the centre of the road still felt faintly illegal. Ron appeared to feel similarly about spending the day with one other boy.

"We could go to Honeydukes," he said.

"We always go to Honeydukes."

"That's why it's safe."

"From what?"

Ron looked towards Madam Puddifoot's, where lace curtains concealed tables designed for people who had successfully asked one another questions.

"Conversation."

They went to Honeydukes. A bell above the door shook vigorously as they entered, and heat met Harry's face before he had unwound his scarf. Someone was trying to persuade a box of Chocolate Frogs to stop hopping along the counter. Behind the till, Mrs Flume counted change into a Hufflepuff's palm while insisting that a sweet which had bitten him remained a sweet he had ordered. Ron immediately found this a more promising conversation than anything waiting outside.

The shop was too crowded to permit much private thought. Warm sugar filled the air. Shelves rose to the ceiling beneath jars of sweets in impossible colours, and every basket seemed to contain one object which moved when nobody watched it. Ron bought enough chocolate to suggest preparation for winter. Harry selected peppermint toads for Hermione, crystallised pineapple for Draconia and then stood holding both parcels. Ron saw them.

"You're buying gifts for their dates?"

"Not for their dates."

"While they're on dates."

"It isn't the same thing."

"It may feel like it from here."

Harry put the sweets back. Then he picked them up again.

"They're still my friends."

Ron nodded slowly. "Fair."

He added a packet of sugar quills to his own basket and refused to explain who they were for. They had just paid when Fred emerged from behind a display of Fizzing Whizzbees.

"Excellent," he said. "Unattached labour."

George appeared beside a pyramid of Chocolate Frogs. "Our favourite kind."

"No," Ron said.

"We haven't asked anything."

"Still no."

Fred draped an arm around Harry. "How would you feel about advancing practical student safety?"

"Suspicious."

"A healthy response."

The twins led them to the rear of the shop where six narrow boxes waited beneath a sackcloth cover. Mr Flume had agreed to hold the delivery for Zonko's while the paperwork was disputed; he had not agreed to become involved in whatever the twins were planning next. Each contained twelve knitted hats in colours nobody would voluntarily wear.

"Shield Hats," George said. "Prototype three."

"Prototype two set people's hair on fire," Fred added.

Ron stepped away from the boxes.

"Only briefly," Fred said. "And we changed the heat-dispersal charm before testing this batch on dummies. Nobody is being asked to repeat the hair experiment."

"Why are they here?" Harry asked.

"Zonko agreed to stock them. The patrol classified automatic shields as unauthorised defensive instruction before we moved the first shipment."

"Because students might use them to defend themselves," Harry said.

"Exactly. Deeply unsafe."

George lifted the sackcloth. "We need them inside Hogwarts. You possess a cloak, a map and the moral flexibility produced by persecution."

"Compliments won't work," Harry said.

"Ron also possesses broad shoulders."

Ron looked down at himself.

"How broad?"

"Traitor," Harry said.

They agreed to move two boxes after lunch, provided the twins could show them the test notes and explain how to deactivate the hats. Harry refused to let an automatic shield count as an excuse to stop practising with a wand. The Three Broomsticks was full enough to steam its windows. Students occupied nearly every table. The air smelled of butterbeer, woodsmoke and wet wool. Madam Rosmerta moved between chairs carrying five tankards in each hand and correcting orders before customers made them. Sirius had reserved a table near the back.

He stood when Harry entered. Harry had spoken to him through the mirror the previous evening and somehow found that it did not count for very much now. Here was the crooked smile properly in front of him, the chair being pulled out and a coat thrown across its back because Sirius had evidently decided that reserving a table also meant occupying as much of it as possible.

"You came," Harry said.

"I said I would."

Sirius caught the front of Harry's scarf and retied it before Harry could object.

"It was fine."

"It was decorative. There is a difference."

"You don't wear scarves."

"I have hair."

"That is not how warmth works."

"You have become argumentative at school."

"Educational progress."

Sirius pulled him into a brief hug. Harry allowed himself to lean into it. Remus sat at the table with tea, a plate of sandwiches and three books he claimed not to have brought intentionally. His smile included Ron without making an event of the inclusion.

"Mr Weasley."

"Professor."

"Not presently."

Ron took the offered chair. "That feels wrong."

"It usually does," Harry said.

Lunch became easy after the first few minutes. "Why?"

"The red practice light triggered the graveyard. Cedric took over."

"Eventually."

"Yes."

"Good."

Remus lifted his cup. "He has made significant progress."

"I loathe it."

"Naturally."

She carried two new books tied together with blue ribbon, and she was laughing at something Anthony had said. Not politely. Fully, head tilted and eyes bright. Ron stopped eating. Sirius followed both boys' gaze.

"Ah," he said.

"Don't," Harry and Ron replied.

"I said nothing."

"Your face did," Harry said.

Remus looked towards Hermione, then at Harry. "Complicated?"

"Apparently."

"We speak constantly," Harry said.

"About what you want."

"That seems excessive."

"James agreed with you for years."

"Did you help?"

Remus laughed into his tea.

Sirius appeared offended. "I was fifteen. My advice was excellent."

"You told him to make her jealous," Remus said.

Sirius looked at Remus. Remus examined his tea.

The question escaped before Harry decided whether it was his to ask. Sirius's fingers tightened around his tankard.

"Sometimes," he said.

"What happened?"

"War."

The single word closed around the table. Sirius looked towards the fire.

Remus's expression softened with pain. Harry understood that the story was not finished and that today was not the day to demand its ending. Sirius faced him again.

"The truth. Preferably before one of them throws you into a lake."

Ron looked towards Hermione.

"Does that advice apply generally?"

"Painfully," Remus said.

Blaise bent to say something near her ear while the pub noise surrounded them. Harry's grip tightened around his tankard. Sirius looked at his hand.

"That," he said, "is not speaking."

"I'm aware."

Draconia saw Harry. Her gaze moved from him to Sirius, then to Ron and the empty plates upon the table. She removed her hand from Blaise's arm. The movement should have helped. It did not help enough. Blaise followed her attention. His smile was subtle and entirely intolerable.

"Do you know him?" Remus asked.

"No."

"Then at least you have avoided bias through evidence."

Ron nodded towards Anthony. "I dislike him too."

"Anthony corrected your Shield Charm twice," Harry said.

"Exactly. Arrogant."

For the first time that day, both Harry and Ron understood why other people found their situation entertaining. They disliked those people as well.

PATROL SEARCHING STUDENTS. SIX OFFICIALS. MAIN ROAD BLOCKED. MONTAGUE HAS MEMBER DESCRIPTIONS. — T.N.

"Doesn't matter yet."

Enough members had come to Hogsmeade independently that a search might reveal identical coins. The copied Galleons might survive a quick glance. "I need the Map."

"Getting people back without letting Montague touch the coins."

Sirius went still.

"You know that passage?"

Remus pressed his lips together.

Sirius looked delighted despite the danger. "Prongs would be unbearable."

"Harry—"

Sirius considered. The pride which appeared was as worrying as the resemblance it recognised.

"Five minutes," he said. "Then you contact me."

"Agreed."

"Good."

Remus looked at Harry. "What do you need from me?"

"The path between here and Honeydukes kept clear. No spells unless they stop someone being hurt."

Remus nodded. Harry crossed the pub. He did not ask Hermione or Draconia to follow.

Hermione folded her map. "Who has the member descriptions?"

"We don't know."

"Then we keep both possibilities in mind. A description is enough for somebody who already knows us."

Draconia joined them. Blaise remained one step behind her.

"Theodore says Montague," she said. "If Umbridge possessed names, she would be here herself."

"Possibly," Hermione replied. "She might have names and still send somebody else. We don't assume she knows less than she does."

No rivalry entered their voices. The danger rearranged priorities without erasing anything beneath it.

Anthony looked towards the window. "Chambers is outside. He's with two Ministry clerks."

Blaise adjusted his gloves. "The back lane reaches Honeydukes through the apothecary yard."

Harry looked at him.

"How do you know?"

"I visit shops even when you are not observing me."

Harry disliked him more. Draconia noticed.

"Later," she said.

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face has become eloquent."

They separated. Sirius left through the front door with the controlled outrage of a guardian who had spent years waiting for legal authority and intended to use every inch of it. Remus followed more quietly. Within thirty seconds, Sirius's voice carried along the main road.

"Under which statute are you searching students' personal effects? No, not the decree. The statute. I can wait while you remember the difference."

Every Ministry official turned towards him. Harry and Ron left through the kitchen. Their day finally had a purpose neither needed to discuss.

"Shop's closed," he called.

"We need the cellar," Harry said.

The man stared at him.

"That is not generally where customers begin."

Fred's head appeared through the storeroom curtain.

"They're with us."

"That makes it worse."

George pulled Harry inside while Fred explained to the proprietor that a temporary student emergency required access to historically significant architecture. The man demanded to know which architecture. Fred glanced at Harry, then abandoned the joke. "There's a passage to Hogwarts. His guardian knows we're using it to get the pupils back. We need a few minutes." Mr Flume looked from him to the shuttered shopfront, then stepped aside without pretending he was pleased. They reached the cellar while he went to watch the shopfront. The trapdoor waited behind stacked crates of peppermint humbugs. Beneath it, stone steps descended into darkness.

"We need to move D.A. members through in groups of four," Harry said. "No coins visible. Anyone who isn't part of the group can use the main road."

George looked at the six boxes of Shield Hats.

"And our endangered educational materials?"

"Leave them."

Both twins recoiled.

"Harry," Fred said, "these hats have dreams."

"Their dreams are bright orange."

"Safety must be visible."

Ron lifted one box. "We can carry two."

Harry stared at him.

"Broad shoulders," Ron said.

"How long?" Ron asked.

"About twenty minutes from here if we keep moving. It's not a straight tunnel."

Ron looked towards the dark bend below. "Brilliant. A scenic route."

"Try to appreciate it without stopping."

They left George at the cellar entrance to direct arrivals. Fred set one crate beside the open trapdoor, ready for the last group to carry down. Ron took the second and waited beside him. Harry went back towards the shop stairs to check the route. He had barely reached them when the shop bell rang. He retreated into the cellar. Everyone froze. Footsteps crossed the floorboards above.

"Ministry inspection," a voice announced. "Open the shutters."

George looked towards the cellar ceiling.

"Earlier than expected."

Harry opened the Map. It showed the Hogwarts end of the passage, where no one was waiting beside the one-eyed witch. It could not show him the streets outside Honeydukes. Draconia and Blaise came behind them. Cho, Hannah and Susan followed through the apothecary yard.

"The others are waiting behind Scrivenshaft's," Hermione said. "Cedric's bringing them round. Some of the patrol have left the main road."

"The shop is being searched," Harry replied.

Anthony drew his wand. "Can we seal the cellar?"

"That tells them there's something inside it," Draconia said.

"Then we hide the entrance."

Hermione looked at the peppermint crates. "Not enough time."

The floorboards creaked above them. The cellar door opened. Mr Flume descended two steps, face pale.

"They want access to the storeroom."

"Tell them there are unstable sugar cauldrons," Fred whispered.

"There are unstable sugar cauldrons."

"Excellent."

"They know."

Mr Flume looked towards the back lane, then hurried upstairs and pulled the cellar door shut behind him. The latch caught. It would not hold against a determined spell, but for the moment it kept the voices above from entering quite so clearly. Cedric appeared at the back entrance with Pansy and Luna. Padma followed, then Neville and Ginny, Ernie and Zacharias. Angelina and Alicia came in last, bringing the news that Warrington was questioning a customer near the corner. Harry checked the faces nearest the door once more and nodded. He looked at the students, the narrow opening and the boxes. Exit. Distance. Shields. They had taught this.

"Hermione, take Hannah and Susan through," he said. "Anthony goes last in your group. If anyone follows, he shields the tunnel while you move."

Hermione hesitated. "What about you?"

"Once the others are through. Draco stays with me."

"No heroic last stands."

"No standing at all. Go."

She held his gaze for one second, judging whether he meant it. Then she went. Anthony followed after the Hufflepuffs. Cedric sent Neville and Ginny next, then Pansy with Luna and Padma close behind. Ernie, Zacharias, Angelina and Alicia followed them. Cedric waited until the last pair reached the bottom of the steps before going down himself.

Blaise looked at the orange hats. "I would rather be arrested."

"Then Montague can search your hair for counterfeit coins."

Blaise took the box.

"I understand the attraction," he told Draconia.

Harry nearly dropped the Map. Draconia's eyes widened.

"Go," she said.

Blaise smiled and entered the tunnel. Ron followed carrying the other side. Cho went behind them. Footsteps entered the storeroom above.

"I need to keep the shield up if they open the door."

"I wasn't planning—"

"If anybody photographs this," he said, "destroy the evidence."

"There will be songs," Fred promised, selecting orange. He showed Harry the seam that switched the charm off before pulling it over his hair.

"New plan."

The cellar door opened. Chambers descended with his wand raised. Montague appeared behind him. A Ministry clerk in brown robes crowded the top of the stairs. They saw an empty cellar. Crates lined the walls. A barrel of Sugar Mice scratched faintly beneath its lid. Peppermint humbugs occupied the centre of the floor. Harry, Draconia, Fred and George crouched beneath the cloak behind them. Montague entered.

"Search it."

Chambers lowered his wand. "For what?"

"Passages. Contraband. Students."

"There are no students."

George's tassel protruded beneath the cloak. Harry caught it beneath his shoe. George clapped a hand over his own mouth before the protest could escape. The brown-robed clerk cast Homenum Revelio. White light filled the cellar, and four points of light gathered above the crates. The Cloak still hid their bodies; it had not stopped the charm detecting that people were present.

"There," Montague shouted.

Harry threw the cloak aside.

"Protego!"

His shield filled the narrow cellar. A Binding Curse struck it and scattered into the ceiling. Sugar rained from the beams. The twins stood. Both hats flared. Transparent shields snapped around their heads, expanded downwards and collided in the confined space. Fred bounced into a barrel. George spun sideways and struck Montague with the purple shield surrounding him. Montague toppled into the Sugar Mice. The barrel burst. Hundreds of sweets escaped. They swarmed up his robes. "Useful data!" George replied.

Draconia sent a cloud of black smoke up the stairs.

"Tunnel. Now."

"Potter."

"Go."

"Together."

Fred, waiting below, shoved the trapdoor closed with his wand.

"Colloportus!" Draconia shouted.

The hatch sealed. Spells struck it from above.

"Move," Harry said.

They ran. The tunnel twisted beneath Hogsmeade. Earth shook from the ceiling as curses followed through the first stretch. Harry kept the Map folded inside his coat. It would help when they neared the castle; until then, he needed his wand, the uneven ground in front of him and the sound of the others keeping pace. Fred and George reached the others near a narrow bend. Hermione had stopped the first groups instead of continuing to Hogwarts.

"I told you to go," Harry said.

"And we agreed nobody gets left behind," she replied.

"The tunnel only has one direction. We have to keep that direction clear."

"We stopped long enough to count," Anthony said. "Nobody was going back up the steps. The next group is already moving. We can go now."

"I liked him better when he was arrogant," Ron muttered.

The sealed trapdoor broke behind them. Sound travelled through the passage: stone cracking, Montague shouting, the Ministry clerk ordering everyone to stop. Twenty students began to panic in a tunnel without exits. Harry climbed onto one of the Shield Hat boxes.

"Listen!"

Faces turned.

"They have one way to reach us," Harry said. "Keep a shield across it and keep moving. Pairs at the back, ten steps each, then change. Nobody runs ahead alone. Cedric, take the front. Hermione, count the steps and the people."

He stepped down from the box before anyone had to ask him to move too. Draconia caught the handle, and together they passed the crate forward to Fred. The first pursuing light turned the bend behind them. Harry heard its impact against Ron's shield and made himself look towards the next patch of clear ground. The plan moved through the group. Cedric led. Students advanced four abreast where the passage widened and two where it narrowed. At every count of ten, the rear pair changed. Silver, blue and gold shields overlapped behind them.

The first Stunning Spell struck Pansy's barrier. It shattered into pale sparks. Ron's shield caught the remaining force before it reached her, and Pansy stumbled one step against Luna. Luna steadied her without looking back.

"Keep going," Harry called. "You're clear."

The tunnel carried every sound farther than it should have travelled: shoes scraping, Hermione's count, the crack of another curse against stone. Dust fell into Harry's hair. He could not see the people pursuing them clearly enough to count wands. He could see the light their spells threw around each bend, and that was enough to keep him from pretending the danger had ended.

"Ten!" Hermione called.

Ron and Neville moved forward. Anthony and Padma took the rear. Another curse struck. They kept walking. No one attempted to defeat the people following them. No one remained to prove courage. Fred and George distributed Shield Hats to anyone whose magic began failing. The prototypes worked poorly, looked appalling and stopped three spells which would otherwise have struck students in the back. The tunnel rose. "George, then Harry and Draco," Ron said.

The three of them remained at the rear.

"Go," Harry told him.

George looked at Draconia. "Will he?"

"Eventually."

"Comforting."

Twenty-one students filled the corridor. Nobody had been captured. Nobody had won. Harry thought Remus might have called that the point. He had just enough breath left to say so, but Hermione had already caught his sleeve and pointed towards the stairs.

"Seventh floor. Separate routes. No running once we're clear of this corridor."

McGonagall sent a message to Sirius confirming their arrival, then asked those who had fallen or taken a hit to wait for Pomfrey near the hospital wing. The others could rest together for a short while; she would come for their accounts when they were ready. Cedric checked the junction while they divided into smaller groups. Harry put the Cloak over Draconia and himself and moved. He knew McGonagall stood between them and the pursuers now. His pulse had not caught up with the fact that they were inside Hogwarts. The room offered water, chairs and a floor clear of practice targets. Fred and Pansy joined them after Pomfrey had checked their knocks. Draconia had asked Harry whether Blaise could rest with them before they separated in the corridor; Harry agreed. It was not an invitation to a lesson. "I had a good time," he said.

"Pity. My mother would have admired the political possibilities."

Blaise inclined his head to Harry as he passed.

Draconia turned. "You were glaring?"

"The bubble makes me fearless."

Hermione deactivated the hat. Ron left with Ginny and Neville before Harry could retaliate. Cedric took the Hufflepuffs. Hermione remained to help Pansy repair the torn lining of her sleeve. "A peace offering?"

"I bought it before there was a war."

"Then what is it?"

"I saw it and thought of you."

The simplicity of the truth frightened him after he said it. Draconia accepted the parcel.

"Why?"

"You took all the pineapple from your mother's Christmas parcel last year."

"I did not take all of it."

"You left me the sugar at the bottom."

"I had reservations about your ability to appreciate the rest."

Harry smiled. Draconia untied the string. She did not eat a piece.

"You bought Granger peppermint toads," she said.

"They're her favourite."

"I know."

The parcel became heavier between them. Hermione's movements slowed at the other end of the room. Pansy saw all three of them and completed the sleeve repair with unexpected speed.

"We should go," she told Hermione.

Hermione looked at Harry. He wanted to ask her to stay. He wanted the conversation with Draconia. Both things were true, and for once Harry understood that pretending otherwise would not make either kinder.

"Can we talk later?" he asked Hermione.

Pain crossed her face before she hid it.

"Yes."

She left with Pansy through the main door. Harry watched the stone close behind them.

"You should not have done that," Draconia said.

"I know."

"Then go after her."

"If I do, I'm avoiding this."

"This may deserve avoidance."

"It has had four years."

Draconia went still. Harry's heartbeat became suddenly loud. He had faced a basilisk he could not look in the eyes, Dementors beside a lake and Voldemort inside a circle of people who wanted him dead. None of those experiences provided useful instruction for standing in an empty room with Draconia holding a parcel of pineapple.

"Was today a date?" he asked.

"Which part?"

"Blaise."

"He intended it to be one. I intended to learn which families were reconsidering their support for the Ministry."

Relief came first. Shame followed because it should not have felt like victory that her day had been something other than what she wanted.

"Why did you say yes?"

"I just told you."

"Not all of it."

Draconia retied the parcel with precise movements.

"He asked."

The two words hurt more than Harry expected.

"I was going to."

"You were going to ask Granger."

"I was going to ask both of you."

Draconia looked up.

"Together?"

"I hadn't worked out that part."

"That appears to be the governing feature of your romantic strategy."

"I don't have a strategy."

"Yes."

Harry pushed one hand through his hair.

"I didn't know what asking meant."

"It meant you wanted to spend Saturday with us."

"I always want to spend Saturday with you."

The answer escaped without permission. Draconia's composure broke. Not dramatically. Her lips parted. The parcel lowered in her hands.

"Both of us," she said.

She was not raising her voice. Harry found he would almost have preferred that. He could answer an accusation; he did not know how to answer the way she had folded her hands around a little paper parcel to keep them still.

"Yes," he said. "But I don't think it's the same. I'm trying to work out the difference, and I'm making a mess of it."

"And when Blaise asked me?"

"I hated it."

"Anthony and Granger?"

Harry thought of Hermione laughing by the window. "That too. Differently."

"Differently how?"

"I don't know yet."

Draconia looked away.

"You don't have to know everything," she said. "But you can't keep us waiting because you're frightened of saying the wrong thing. We're frightened too."

Sirius had said almost the same thing. Harry wondered how many people had been waiting for him to learn it.

"What do you want?" he asked.

Draconia laughed once, without humour.

"Now you ask."

"Yes."

"After I spent the day with someone else."

"Yes."

"After we were chased through a sweetshop."

"The timing could improve."

"Considerably."

Harry stepped closer. Draconia did not step away.

"What do you want?" he repeated.

She examined him as though searching for the trap beneath the question.

"I want you to stop behaving as if noticing me is something you must apologise for," she said. "I want you to ask instead of waiting until another person makes the decision dangerous. I want—"

Her voice failed. Harry stopped trying to prepare an answer. Whatever she said next deserved more than the words he had practised on the way here. Draconia closed her eyes.

"I want to kiss you," she said.

The room seemed to hold its breath. Harry's fear disappeared beneath something warmer. Not certainty about the future. Not an answer to Hermione. Only certainty about this.

"Can I kiss you?" he asked.

Draconia opened her eyes.

"Yes," she said. "You have taken an astonishing amount of time to ask."

Harry touched her face. His fingers rested against the line of her jaw. Her skin was cool from the village, warmer near her ear. He gave her time to move. For once there was no alarm to listen for, no spell waiting behind him and no friend calling them away from the doorway. The room had gone quiet except for the faint settling of its wooden floor. Harry could see a loose thread near the collar of her cloak and the place where her careful parting had come undone during the run through the tunnel. She closed the distance herself.

The first touch of her mouth was careful, almost questioning. Harry had imagined kissing as a moment in which instinct would provide competence. It did not. Their noses struck lightly, his glasses slipped, and Draconia laughed against his lips. He drew back just enough to look at her. The sound had startled him more than the collision. She was smiling without trying to make anything of it, one hand still caught in the front of his coat. He could see the small crease beside her mouth which appeared when she was trying not to laugh at him in class. He had spent an unreasonable amount of time hoping to cause it.

"Wait," she whispered.

For half a dreadful second he thought she had changed her mind. Then she pushed his glasses up with one finger, her own hand unsteady, and tilted her face towards his again. He followed the slight tilt of her head, and the kiss continued.

Now he knew to turn his head a little. Her fingers loosened upon his coat before closing again, and he felt the change through the thick cloth. He kept his thumb beside her cheekbone, concentrating so hard on being gentle that it took him a moment to realise she was leaning into his hand. Beyond the window, the last daylight lay across a strip of bare floor. Somebody's dropped quill rolled slowly towards a chair and stopped. Harry noticed these things with an absurd clarity while everything he had intended to say became impossible to remember.

When they separated, her forehead rested against his. She was still smiling. So was he; he could feel it, could not have stopped it if she had asked, and found he wanted to look at her rather than hide the evidence.

"What?" she asked softly.

"I'm trying to work out whether I can blame the glasses."

"For your entire performance?"

"The opening was difficult."

Her thumb moved once against his coat. Harry had often been close to her, squeezed together beneath the Cloak or sitting shoulder to shoulder over an essay. He had never stood this close with nowhere else they needed to go. For another few seconds, he let himself stay there.

"That was less terrible than your asking," she murmured.

"You hit my nose."

"You approached at an angle."

"I thought there was a technique."

"There is. You failed it."

"Do you want to demonstrate?"

Her smile changed. She kissed him again. The second was less careful. It remained sweet rather than urgent, but Harry felt the years beneath it: Madam Malkin's, letters, a hand around his wrist beneath words written on a wall, the Chamber, the lake, the Yule Ball, the graveyard and every moment in which one of them had nearly said something before danger spoke louder. When it ended, Draconia did not move away.

"Hermione," Harry said.

Draconia's eyes closed.

"Yes."

"We have to tell her."

"Yes."

Neither sounded pleased. That did not make the decision wrong. Harry picked up both sweet parcels. Draconia took his hand. They left the hidden room together.

Hermione was in the library, at their usual table beneath the western windows. Harry had expected to find her there; seeing the familiar place occupied did nothing to make the approach easier. Anthony's blue ribbon lay beside her ink bottle. Three books stood open around a blank sheet of parchment. She had positioned her quill above it, but there was no ink on the nib. Draconia's fingers tightened once around Harry's. Madam Pince looked at their hands, made a disapproving sound and retreated between the shelves. Hermione looked up. Her gaze dropped to the space between them, and Harry watched the expression she had prepared give way to another one.

He sat opposite her. Draconia took the chair beside him. Somewhere behind the shelves, a student turned a page with an enormous, deliberate rustle. Harry wished he could be furious about something as simple as noise. He put the peppermint toads near Hermione's hand.

"Please don't make sweets do this conversation for you," she said.

Harry drew the packet back. "I wasn't trying to. I didn't know where to start."

Draconia let go of his hand beneath the table. Hermione noticed the movement. Harry could tell from the way she looked from one of them to the other without speaking.

"We kissed," he said.

There it was. He had expected saying it to require more breath.

Hermione looked at Draconia. "Once?"

"Twice."

"Draco."

"She asked."

"I did," Hermione said. She straightened the ribbon beside her book. "I wish I hadn't."

Harry lowered his eyes to the table. There was a small ink stain near his wrist, left from an essay they had worked on together weeks ago. Hermione had laughed when he blamed the bottle. He remembered it with such force that for a moment he wanted to talk about that instead.

"I'm sorry," he said.

"For kissing her?"

"For hurting you."

Hermione looked at him properly. "Do you regret it?"

Draconia stayed beside him. She had not reached for his hand again or given him a look which offered an easier answer.

"No," Harry said.

Hermione pressed her lips together. He could see the effort it took to keep her face still.

"Then please don't make it sound as though you do. I can't work out what you're saying if you apologise for all of it at once."

"I don't know what to say."

"Neither do I. That doesn't usually happen."

She gave a small, unsuccessful laugh and stood. For a second Harry thought she was leaving. Instead she went to the window, placing one hand on the sill while the other remained curled around the end of the ribbon. Outside, the grounds had darkened to purple. The lake reflected a narrow band of sky, and lamps were beginning to glow along the distant path.

"I went with Anthony because he asked," she said. "He's kind. I liked being with him. And part of me wanted you to see us and be jealous."

Harry did not tell her that he had been.

"That was unfair to him," she added. "He didn't ask to be part of this."

Draconia looked down at her own hands. "Neither did Blaise."

Hermione turned. "I know."

There was a long pause. Harry could hear movement near Madam Pince's desk, a chair being pushed in and the soft knock of books placed on the return trolley. He could not understand how people continued finishing ordinary evenings while this one sat between them.

"I knew it might happen," Hermione said. "The two of you. I've been watching it for years. I thought knowing would make me less stupid about it when it did."

"You aren't stupid," Draconia said.

"Please don't."

Draconia fell silent.

"I can't have you understanding everything at me right now," Hermione said, and her voice broke on the last few words. She brushed a tear away angrily. "You could just be happy."

"I am."

Hermione flinched. Draconia did too, as though the answer had hurt on its way out.

"And I care about you," Draconia added. "That hasn't stopped. I don't know how to do this without hurting you, but pretending I don't want him won't help either."

Harry looked between them. He had spent so long imagining a decision he might have to make that he had almost forgotten they could have this conversation without asking him to settle it. Hermione and Draconia had their own friendship to protect. He could hurt it. He could not repair it by promising each of them an equal portion of himself. Hermione came back to the table but did not sit.

"Does this mean you've chosen?"

Harry caught the edge of his sleeve between his fingers. The answer she wanted would make the next minute easier. That was a terrible reason to give it.

"I know I wanted to kiss Draco," he said. "I know I want you in my life. I haven't worked out everything between those two things."

"Do you want to kiss me?"

Draconia went still beside him. Harry looked at Hermione, at the wetness she had been unable to hide and the way she held herself upright as though sitting down would make the question less brave.

"Yes," he said quietly. "But I don't want to do it now because you're hurt, or because I think kissing both of you would make it fair. It wouldn't."

Hermione covered her face. For a dreadful second Harry thought he had made everything worse. Then she gave a sound which was almost a laugh.

"You finally say something sensible," she murmured, "and I hate the timing."

"I'm sorry."

"Harry."

"Right. I'll stop saying that."

Draconia let out a small, unsteady breath. It was close enough to laughter that Hermione lowered her hands and looked at her. Neither smiled for long.

"I need time," Hermione said.

Harry nodded. His first instinct was to ask how much, then arrange himself somewhere she would have to pass so he could check whether she had finished needing it. He recognised the thought and kept it to himself.

"Don't follow me," she added.

"All right."

Hermione gathered her books and wound the blue ribbon around them again. Her fingers slipped once on the knot. Draconia moved as if to help, then stopped before reaching across the table.

"And don't hide it from me," Hermione said to her.

"I wasn't—"

"You let go of his hand. You thought I hadn't seen."

Draconia looked at the gap between her chair and Harry's. "I didn't want to make you watch."

"I already saw. It won't hurt less because you pretend nothing happened."

"I'll try," Draconia said.

Hermione nodded, accepting an answer which was less certain than either of them wanted. She lifted the books against her chest and walked towards the aisle. Harry remained seated, though every step she took made the urge to follow harder. At the end of the table, she stopped.

"I'm coming to breakfast," she said. "I need time. I'm not leaving."

Then she went. Harry watched until the shelves hid her. He had kept his promise. It felt worse than following would have felt for him, and perhaps that was why it mattered. Hermione had asked him to do something for her without making him responsible for changing how she felt. Draconia took his hand again, resting their joined fingers on the table. Harry looked down at them. The happiness returned before he could decide whether he was allowed it. He remembered her laugh against his mouth, and the memory did not become less precious because the evening had hurt someone else.

"She'll be there tomorrow," he said.

"She told us she would."

He looked towards the aisle again. Trusting Hermione's answer felt harder than going to check it. He stayed. Draconia stayed with him.

Hermione did not go directly to Gryffindor Tower. She walked until the castle became unfamiliar. Not lost. Hermione did not get lost. She simply passed the staircase she required, continued beyond the library corridor and descended towards a courtyard nobody used after dark. The cloisters enclosed a square of silver grass. Rainwater remained in the stone gutter, reflecting fragments of moon between moving clouds. Hermione sat upon the cold ledge beneath an arch. The stone caught through her coat at once. She set the books beside her, then put one hand over them because the ribbon had loosened during the walk and she could not bear to watch anything else come apart.

Only then did she cry. She tried to keep it quiet, swallowing between breaths until that made it worse. No one was asking her a question here. No one needed an answer she could arrange into a sensible order. She hated crying outside bathrooms. Bathrooms had locks, running water and mirrors which could be avoided by looking down. The courtyard offered no useful privacy beyond the hour and the cold. Each breath became visible before her, announcing distress to empty air. Draconia and Harry had kissed. Twice. The number should not have mattered. Hermione had asked because knowledge usually reduced uncertainty. Instead it gave the pain edges.

She imagined the hidden room after she left: Draconia's hand against Harry's coat, Harry touching her face with the careful attention he gave injured things and people he loved. The image was not memory. Hermione's mind constructed it anyway, using four years of observations she had pretended were evidence for a question nobody needed to answer yet. Part of her had always known. That did not make the knowing noble. Hermione pressed both palms against her eyes. She loved Draconia.

That was the ugliest part of jealousy. It could not simplify the other girl into an enemy. Hermione knew how Draconia watched Harry eat without appearing to count. She knew the exact change in Draconia's voice when fear became too large for composure. She knew Draconia would abandon status, safety and every inherited certainty before allowing Harry to believe he was unwanted. Hermione understood why Harry loved her. She resented understanding. Footsteps crossed the cloister. Hermione lowered her hands and reached for her wand. Luna Lovegood appeared beneath the opposite arch carrying two mugs.

"How did you find me?" Hermione asked.

"I saw you go past the Ravenclaw stairs," Luna said. "I thought you might like tea."

She looked at the gutter, where the clouds had broken the moon's reflection into pieces.

"I was also hoping the moon would fit inside the water. It doesn't fit very well here."

She offered one mug. Hermione accepted it. The tea smelled of chamomile and something faintly smoky. Luna sat beside her without asking what had happened. They watched clouds move across the reflected moon.

"Harry and Draco kissed," Hermione said.

Luna blew upon her tea. "That seems likely."

"You knew?"

"No. But it has seemed likely for a long time."

"That is functionally the same thing."

"Not to Harry."

Despite herself, Hermione laughed. The sound hurt and helped.

"He said he wants to kiss me too."

Luna considered this. "Do you want him to?"

"Yes."

The answer was immediate.

"Tonight?"

Hermione looked into the tea.

"No. Not because he feels guilty."

"Then it was good he didn't."

"It doesn't feel good."

"Good decisions often have very poor manners."

Hermione glanced at her. Luna's expression remained serene, but not distant. Her friendship with Harry had taught Hermione not to mistake unusual language for an absence of feeling.

"Anthony was kind," Hermione said.

"He usually is."

"I used him."

"A little."

"You don't have to agree so quickly."

"Would you prefer me to lie slowly?"

Hermione drank the tea. It was too bitter. She drank again.

"I did enjoy being with him," she said. "That makes it worse. He deserves not to be measured against somebody who wasn't there."

"You can tell him."

"It might hurt him."

"Not telling him may also hurt him."

Hermione thought of Harry in the library asking what she wanted him to say. They were all so afraid of choosing the wrong words that silence had begun choosing for them.

"I hate this," she admitted.

"Yes."

"I don't want to lose either of them."

"You can be upset with them and still see them tomorrow," Luna said. "You don't have to decide everything tonight."

The simplicity made Hermione angry. It also made sense. They finished the tea. Luna left first, taking both mugs and promising not to tell anyone Hermione had cried unless someone required evidence that she possessed functioning tear ducts. Hermione remained until the cold entered her fingers. Before going back to Gryffindor Tower, Hermione paused outside an empty classroom near the entrance hall. There was still time before curfew. She sent a small silver otter towards the dungeons with a quiet message for Draconia: could she come and speak for a few minutes?

Hermione sat on the edge of a desk with the books beside her. When Draconia arrived, she had changed into a jumper and brought her cloak over one arm. She stopped just inside the open door. Neither spoke at first.

"I said I needed time," Hermione said quietly.

"You did."

"This counts as time."

"I wasn't going to tell you how long it should take."

"Do not be unbearable."

"I'm frightened, Hermione. Being unbearable is easier than admitting it."

Hermione looked at her. Draconia had removed every trace of happiness from her face again. Not because the kiss mattered less. Because Hermione had been hurt and Draconia believed visible joy would become cruelty.

"You can be happy," Hermione said.

Draconia's throat moved.

"I want to be happy. I just don't want to look up tomorrow and find you've stopped coming to sit with us."

"Then let me decide when I need some space. You don't have to disappear every time I'm upset."

"I won't."

Hermione shifted along the desk. Draconia sat beside her, leaving a little space between them.

"I am still angry."

"I know."

"I might be jealous tomorrow."

"You can tell me. I'd rather know than spend the whole day trying to guess."

"And I am not going to pretend I stopped caring overnight just because that would be easier for everyone."

For the first time, Draconia smiled.

"Good."

Hermione's chest still hurt. The hurt no longer felt like a locked door.

"Breakfast," she said.

"Seven thirty."

"I may be late."

"Potter will watch the doors."

"I know."

They left the classroom before curfew and parted at the staircase, each taking the route to her own House. Back in the dormitory, Hermione changed without waking Lavender or Parvati. She untied Anthony's blue ribbon and put it carefully in the drawer beside Harry's letters and Draconia's first Christmas card. Lavender turned over behind her curtains, muttering at something in her sleep. The familiar sound made the room feel like a place she had returned to rather than a place she had retreated into. She would have to speak to Anthony. She did not know yet whether that meant another afternoon together or an apology with no invitation attached. For once she left the question unanswered beside the things in her drawer. There were people she wanted to see tomorrow, even if seeing them would hurt. She slept badly, waking once to reach for her wand before remembering that nobody had gone missing. After a while, she slept again.

Hermione arrived at breakfast seven minutes late. Harry knew because he had watched the doors for all seven. She crossed the Great Hall carrying her bag and sat opposite him. Not beside. Not at another table. Draconia remained with Pansy among the Slytherins, giving Hermione the distance she had asked for. Harry pushed the peppermint toads across the table. Hermione looked at them.

"These are not an apology?"

"No. I bought them before I did anything requiring one."

She considered. Then she opened the packet. Ron arrived with toast and took the place beside Harry. His gaze moved between them.

"Am I entering something?"

"Always," Harry said.

"Should I leave?"

Hermione ate a peppermint toad.

"No."

Ron sat. The answer did not repair everything. It made room for breakfast. Across the hall, Draconia unwrapped one piece of crystallised pineapple and placed it upon her tongue. Her eyes found Harry's. She smiled. Harry smiled back. Hermione saw. Her mouth tightened. She looked down at the packet, chose another peppermint toad and asked Ron to pass the toast. Harry did not mistake the ordinary request for everything being repaired. He was grateful she made it anyway. Above the staff table, a new Ministry notice unrolled.

PENDING INVESTIGATION INTO THE HOGSMEADE DISTURBANCE, ALL STUDENT PRACTICE OF SHIELD CHARMS IS SUSPENDED.

Neville read it from three seats away. His hand moved beneath the table and closed around his wand. The charmed Galleon in Harry's pocket warmed. Harry revealed the reply from Hermione, then passed it on through the master to the other twenty coins.

THURSDAY. SHIELDS.

Harry looked at Hermione. She had already taken out her timetable. This time she angled it so Ron could read the open space on Thursday beside her, then let Harry take the quill when he reached for it. Across the Hall, Draconia touched her coin beneath the table and looked towards the new decree. They would have to speak again. There were things Harry still did not know how to say, and a hand he wanted to hold without making the rest of his life disappear around it. For now, Hermione wanted the toast passed, Ron had begun complaining about the Ministry banning a spell which kept Quaffles out of his face, and Thursday still had room for all of them.
