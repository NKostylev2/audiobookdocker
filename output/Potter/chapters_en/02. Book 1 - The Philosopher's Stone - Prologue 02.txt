Prologue II

Something heavy landed on Harry's stomach.

He jerked upright with one arm raised to protect his face, certain for a confused instant that Dudley had found him. The blankets caught around his knees. Whatever had struck him slid from his chest and hit the floor with a thump, and Harry stared at the gap in the curtains, waiting for the laugh that usually followed.

Nobody laughed. There was no cupboard door, no strip of hall carpet beneath it. Crimson hangings stirred around his bed, and the air smelled faintly of woodsmoke.

Pale morning light filled the circular dormitory beyond the curtains. Neville was asleep in the next bed, one hand hanging over the edge. Dean Thomas had disappeared almost entirely beneath his blankets, while Seamus Finnigan had somehow turned himself around during the night and now slept with his feet on his pillow. The names came back to Harry with the room: introductions made while unpacking, questions about lessons, somebody asking whether all the beds were this comfortable. Hogwarts. He was at Hogwarts.

A small brown owl watched him from the bedpost, its bright eyes suggesting that he had behaved unreasonably. Harry lowered his arm. The owl clicked its beak and looked pointedly at the book on the floor.

"All right," Harry whispered.

Harry leaned over the edge of the mattress and retrieved Hogwarts: A History. Hermione's name was written neatly inside the cover, with no room left for anyone to mistake its owner. A scrap of parchment had been tucked beside it.

Breakfast: half past seven, in the Entrance Hall. We can compare timetables before lessons. Bring this book. I've marked the most useful map of the school.

Please read the map before setting off.

Below that, squeezed into the remaining space, she had added:

The owl belongs to the school. It will expect a piece of breakfast.

Harry looked from the note to the owl. Being woken by a book was an improvement on being woken by Dudley, though he would have preferred Hermione to discover a third method.

"You could've waited downstairs."

The owl ruffled itself without remorse and settled more firmly on the post. Harry took that to mean it intended to stay until paid.

He dressed quietly, checking the note twice to make sure he had understood where to go. His robes hung properly from his shoulders instead of sliding halfway down his arms. There was nobody to tell him to hurry up or get out of the way, and he took a moment to fasten them without listening for footsteps.

The common room looked different without the previous night's crowd. Grey light caught in the tall windows and along the edges of a chessboard abandoned near the fire. One black piece was attempting to persuade a white pawn to move while nobody watched. Harry paused to listen, until the pawn accused him of taking sides. He tucked Hermione's book under his arm and left them to it.

Finding the portrait hole was easy. Finding breakfast proved less straightforward. Harry had remembered the turns from the previous night, but the first staircase carried him one floor higher than expected and deposited him before a tapestry of wizards attempting to teach ballet to trolls. He opened the book at Hermione's marker. The map showed the right staircase and failed to explain why it was presently somewhere else.

By the time he reached the Entrance Hall, he had passed the same one-eyed witch three times and entered a corridor that smelled strongly of vinegar. He had begun to suspect that keeping his own map would require a great deal of rubbing out.

Hermione waited at the foot of the marble staircase, already dressed and carrying enough books for several possible mornings. Her hair had been brushed with determination and was beginning to recover from it. One shoe tapped against the floor. Harry had seen that expression before: Aunt Petunia wore it over missed chores. Hermione, however, was holding a timetable rather than a list of things he had done wrong.

"You're late."

"Good morning."

"I said seven-thirty."

"You sent an owl into my bed."

"I sent a book. The owl chose where to put it."

Harry glanced towards the Great Hall. "Have you eaten?"

"No. I was waiting."

Harry stopped beside her. He had expected her to have eaten, or to be annoyed because he had interrupted whatever she planned to do next. Hermione shifted her books and glanced towards the Great Hall. She seemed to find waiting for him perfectly reasonable until he looked surprised by it.

"The staircases change at set intervals, or possibly according to destination. I'm not sure yet. The map in the book is incomplete, so I've started adding—"

"Potter."

Draconia stood beneath one of the stone arches leading towards the dungeons.

The two boys from the train loitered several steps behind her, one broad and fair-haired, the other dark-haired and heavyset. Both looked as though they would rather be eating. Draconia's braid was neat and her chin raised, but one sleeve had folded awkwardly beneath her bag strap. Harry wondered how long she had been standing there. He was beginning to recognise the difference between her arriving somewhere and arranging to look as though she had.

Harry smiled. "You found us."

"You are standing in the Entrance Hall. It was not a demanding search."

Hermione's gaze sharpened. "We agreed to meet here."

"We did not."

"You said we had Potions together on Friday."

"That is in four days."

"And that the library is available to every House."

"I was making a general observation."

Harry looked between them. "Were you waiting too?"

"No," Draconia said.

The dark-haired boy gave an enormous yawn.

"We've been here for—"

Draconia turned her head. He shut his mouth halfway through the complaint and became intensely interested in the floor. Harry decided that pointing this out would be a poor reward for being waited for.

"Your friends can come to breakfast with us," he offered.

"They are not my friends."

The dark-haired boy looked offended. "Our fathers are friends."

"Yes," Draconia replied. "That is how inheritance works."

She swept past Harry into the Great Hall. After a moment, the boys followed her towards Slytherin while Harry and Hermione turned towards Gryffindor.

"Does she always speak to people like that?" Hermione asked.

"I've known her one day."

"You seem to understand her."

"So do you."

Hermione considered this without appearing entirely pleased by the conclusion. Inside the Hall, breakfast covered all four House tables. Toast stood in high racks beside eggs, sausages and grilled tomatoes; steam rose from jugs of something Harry had not yet dared to try. He helped himself, then took another piece of toast when nobody commented.

The brown owl arrived through a high window and landed on the back of the bench. Harry set aside a morsel from his plate. It swallowed the payment, inspected him for any further debts and flew away. Hermione watched it go with satisfaction.

"Reliable," she said.

"Heavy," Harry replied.

Hermione sat opposite him and immediately opened Hogwarts: A History between the plates.

"You shouldn't read while you're eating," Harry said.

"I'm not reading. I'm showing you the map."

"That's reading with an audience."

She ignored him and turned several pages, explaining which pencilled arrows belonged to her and which corridors the book had left out. Harry was halfway through his eggs when something white struck his shoulder and dropped beside his plate.

Across the Hall, Draconia was buttering toast. No owl had delivered the tightly folded parchment, and no student stood between their tables. Harry glanced beneath the bench in case he had missed some new method of sending things, then unfolded it.

What did the Hat say to you?

Hermione leaned over the book. "What's that?"

Harry showed her.

"How did she send it?"

"I don't know."

Hermione examined the parchment, then searched the Hall with immediate suspicion. A second note struck her forehead. Harry tried not to laugh as Hermione unfolded it.

Stop investigating the delivery method. I borrowed two enchanted paper birds from an older student.

To reply: write underneath, fold along the old creases, tap the tail and say my name.

Hermione turned the note over. "That's quite clever."

Across the Hall, Draconia glanced up, and Hermione sat straighter, as though praise might be overheard from fifty feet away. She passed the first note back and offered her quill. Harry wrote in the space beneath Draconia's question.

It said I could do well in Slytherin. But it chose Gryffindor. I didn't ask it to.

He tried to refold the parchment and produced something which looked as though it had been stepped on. Hermione took it, matched the old creases and tapped the tail with her wand.

"Draconia Malfoy."

The crooked bird twitched in her hand.

"She put the instructions underneath," Hermione explained, looking pleased that following them had worked.

The paper bird flapped across the Hall. It nearly fell into a Ravenclaw's porridge, recovered and landed beside Draconia's hand.

Draconia read the message, then looked up. Harry could not make out her expression from that distance, but her shoulders settled and she stopped turning her knife between her fingers. When he smiled, she glanced down quickly.

Her reply arrived with far greater accuracy.

I thought you hadn't.

Harry added two words beneath it.

How come?

The answer came while Hermione was explaining the quickest route to Charms.

You looked at me before you went to Gryffindor. I didn't think you were saying goodbye.

Harry read that twice. She had wondered, just as Hermione said she would. He wished he had found a way to tell her the night before.

He folded the note and put it in his pocket. Beside him, Hermione had fallen silent, her finger still marking the corridor he was supposed to be learning.

"What?" he asked.

"Nothing."

"You're looking at me strangely."

"I'm trying to determine whether we are still discussing timetables."

"We are."

"We haven't for several minutes."

Harry turned the book towards himself. "Which way is Charms?"

Hermione pointed to the map with slightly more force than necessary.

At the Slytherin table, Draconia ate the rest of her breakfast without looking their way. Harry suspected she was smiling. He bent over the map properly this time; Hermione had waited long enough for him already.

By Friday, Harry had learned four important things about Hogwarts.

The first was that looking as though he knew where he was going encouraged people to give him directions before he admitted he needed them. Following someone with the same lesson usually worked. Following someone with the same House colours had once taken him to a classroom full of fifth-years, so he had become more particular.

The second was that a distracted ghost could pass straight through him, leaving him cold all the way to his teeth. Saying "Excuse me" afterwards did very little good.

The third was that Hermione considered an unanswered question something which ought to be dealt with immediately. The fourth, learned while waiting outside three different classrooms, was that being friends with someone in another House did not cause their timetables to agree.

Gryffindor and Slytherin shared Potions and Charms, but the rest of the week's lessons seldom matched. Their tables stood far apart in the Great Hall and their common rooms occupied opposite ends of the castle. Students tended to move with their own Houses even between lessons, falling into groups that seemed to have formed before Harry arrived. Finding Draconia meant leaving the group, choosing the right corridor and hoping she had done the same.

Hermione treated the difficulty as something that could be solved with parchment. By Wednesday, she had listed their shared lessons, free periods and three meeting places equally inconvenient to both common rooms. Harry thought this admirably fair until he had to find the furthest one.

Draconia objected to the phrase scheduled friendship and appeared in the library at the first scheduled time anyway. Harry arrived seven minutes late after a staircase took him to the fourth floor. Through the shelves, he could already hear the girls arguing in the fierce whispers required by Madam Pince.

"This table is directly beneath a leaking window," Draconia said.

"It isn't raining."

"It will."

"The weather cannot possibly be your objection to everything."

"No. Your table selection is my objection to this particular thing."

Harry dropped into the chair between them. "Hello."

"You're late," they said together.

After that, they met whenever they could. They studied in the library, ate bread and cheese in sheltered courtyards and learned which suits of armour could be trusted for directions. Hermione corrected their essays. Draconia explained customs the books had mentioned without making clear how anyone lived with them. Harry listened to both, adopted whichever advice worked and occasionally discovered a third method that annoyed them equally.

Keeping the arrangement meant remembering to wait when lessons finished late and sending a note when they could not come. Harry began checking the library door without thinking about it. More often than he expected, one of the girls looked up at the same moment.

He noticed how many students watched when Draconia sat beside them. Conversations at nearby tables lowered or stopped. Slytherins sometimes stared until she looked back, and older students called her away with a familiarity she did not return. Harry caught fragments about family dinners and people she ought to know. She always came back, though once she sat down so abruptly that Hermione moved the inkpot without comment.

Hermione drew attention of another kind. She answered questions before most of the class had finished considering them, her hand rising with such speed that Seamus once ducked. Praise made her sit straighter and turn to the next page. Laughter behind her had the same effect, though Harry began to notice that she held the edge of the book more tightly on those occasions. He had thought knowing all the answers would make school easy. Apparently it did not.

"You haven't read the chapter," she said one afternoon in the library.

Harry turned another page. "I'm reading it now."

"You were supposed to read it before today."

"Then today's Harry is solving yesterday's Harry's mistake."

"That is not a sustainable system."

Draconia did not lift her gaze from her own essay. "It is if today's Harry never meets tomorrow's."

Hermione frowned. "That makes no sense."

"It does to me," Harry said.

"That is not reassuring."

Draconia's quill paused. "He understands the practical application."

"He understands an excuse."

"They are often the same thing."

Harry looked between them. "I'm going to read now."

Neither objected.

He completed almost three uninterrupted paragraphs before Draconia reached across him and moved Hermione's inkpot away from the edge. A moment later, Hermione's sleeve brushed the place where it had been. She looked from the dry cuff to Draconia.

"Thank you."

Draconia shrugged without looking up. "It would have ruined the notes."

"They're my notes."

"Yes."

Hermione studied her for another second before returning to the book. Harry smiled into his chapter.

Harry began to remember things like that long after the lessons themselves had blurred: an inkpot moved in time, a book left open at the page he needed, Hermione remembering which pastry Draconia preferred. They argued over nearly everything and still made room for each other's bags. Slowly, Harry stopped feeling that he had to keep finding reasons for all three of them to stay.

Sometimes he arrived to find they had started without him. The first time, he stood unnoticed at the end of a library shelf while Hermione and Draconia bent over the same book.

"That interpretation is outdated," Hermione whispered.

"It is traditional."

"Tradition doesn't make something correct."

"No, but neither does being printed recently."

Hermione turned a page. "The revised edition cites primary sources."

"Written by whom?"

Hermione checked the footnote. Draconia leaned closer, and both bent over the names at the bottom of the page. Harry backed quietly around the shelf and approached again with louder footsteps. This time they looked up and moved a stack of books to make room. He sat down feeling pleased with himself, though he had done nothing except arrive.

Potions took place in a dungeon colder than any room had a right to be in September.

Glass jars lined the walls, filled with pale things that seemed to alter shape whenever Harry looked away. He tried not to examine the one closest to his shoulder. Brass scales stood on the worktables beside stained chopping boards, and a bitter smell caught at the back of his throat beneath sharper traces of herbs and old smoke. Even the flames under the cauldrons seemed to leave the room cold.

Hermione chose a table near the front.

Draconia sat directly behind them with Daphne Greengrass, a thin Slytherin girl who greeted Harry politely before checking the equipment. They had settled on separate tables because Hermione wanted a clear view of the board and Draconia said she refused to spend an entire lesson being leaned across. Harry could still turn to speak to her.

Snape entered without a sound, his black robes brushing the ends of the worktables. Conversation died before he reached his desk. Harry set his quill down carefully.

"There will be no foolish wand-waving in this class," the professor said.

His voice was quiet, forcing everyone else to become quieter.

Harry listened. For the first time since arriving at Hogwarts, a professor spoke about magic without making it sound cheerful. Snape described potions as something exact and dangerous: glory sealed in bottles, the body altered through knowledge, death delayed by the correct combination of ingredients.

Harry wanted to understand how any of that was possible. He leaned forward, forgetting the chill, until Snape's gaze settled on him. The interest in the professor's voice vanished.

"Potter."

Harry's stomach tightened.

"What would I get if I added powdered root of asphodel to an infusion of wormwood?"

The question meant nothing to him.

Beside him, Hermione's hand rose.

Harry had read the assigned chapter twice. Hermione had asked questions after the first attempt and discovered how little of it he remembered. Now he could picture the page without finding the part he needed: plant names, warnings, a drawing of roots that all looked much the same. Beside him, her raised hand offered the answer Snape would not take.

"I... I don't know, sir."

Snape's mouth thinned.

"Let us try again. Where would you look if I asked you to find me a bezoar?"

Hermione's hand strained higher.

Harry searched his memory. A bezoar was a stone. It came from—

"A goat's stomach."

Hermione's hand lowered.

Snape gave no indication that Harry had answered correctly.

"And what is the difference between monkshood and wolfsbane?"

"They're the same plant," Hermione whispered almost soundlessly.

Harry heard her. Before he could repeat the answer, Snape turned towards their table.

"Five points from Gryffindor," he said, "for Miss Granger's inability to remain silent."

Hermione went rigid.

Harry looked up. "But I hadn't answered."

"An observation even you could hardly have missed."

A few Slytherins laughed. Draconia did not.

Anger warmed the back of Harry's neck. He had known teachers who disliked him, usually after Aunt Petunia spoke to them. He could hardly imagine her writing to Hogwarts. Snape hadn't given him a chance to do anything wrong; he had begun with Potter, and everything Harry said seemed to make it worse. Hermione sat rigidly beside him, her hand curled around the quill she had not yet used.

"The answer is that they're the same plant," he said.

Snape's eyes fixed upon him.

"Another five points."

"Why?"

The word escaped before Harry could stop it. Hermione caught his sleeve beneath the table. Snape moved closer.

"Because, Potter, this is my classroom. You would be wise to learn the difference between answering a question and challenging the person who asked it."

Harry met his gaze. Behind Snape, Draconia shook her head once, not disagreement but a warning. Harry looked down.

"Open your books. Page one hundred and thirty-seven," Snape said.

Pages rustled throughout the dungeon.

They were to brew a simple potion for curing boils. Harry welcomed having something to do with his hands. Hermione read the instructions while he laid out the ingredients, checking each label before unwrapping it. For a few minutes, the work went smoothly. Then she began reaching across him to correct things he had not yet done.

"You're cutting them too thickly," she murmured.

"I haven't started."

"Your knife is in the wrong position."

"It's on the board."

"It should be angled."

Harry adjusted it. Hermione tilted it back again, and he took his hand away before they could begin arguing over whose fingers belonged where.

"Not that far."

Across the aisle, Neville tipped porcupine quills into his cauldron while it still stood over the flame. The mixture hissed. He reached for the handles, too late, and pink froth climbed over the rim.

"Move!" Hermione cried.

Harry caught her sleeve and dragged her clear as the potion surged across Neville's table. A bench scraped backwards. Somebody shouted. Drops struck the floor and ate through the toe of a shoe, while angry red boils rose along Neville's forearms. He stared at them with his mouth open, too frightened to move until Seamus pulled him away from the cauldron.

Professor Snape swept forward.

"Idiot boy!"

Neville's face crumpled.

Harry realised he was still holding Hermione's sleeve and let go. Snape ordered Seamus to take Neville to the hospital wing, then cleared the spilled potion with a sharp movement of his wand. Harry waited for him to ask whether anyone else had been hurt.

The professor looked at him instead.

"Why did you not prevent him from adding the quills?"

Harry stared. "He wasn't working with us."

"You were close enough to observe."

"So was everyone."

A silence followed. Snape's expression became almost pleasant.

"Five more points from Gryffindor."

Before Harry could answer, a glass jar fell from the shelf behind Snape and shattered. Every head turned. Pickled beetles scattered across the stone. Draconia stood beside the ingredients shelf with one hand still raised; Harry had seen her leave her table while Seamus was helping Neville.

She looked at the glass with an expression of carefully arranged dismay.

"My sleeve caught it," she said.

Snape looked from the broken jar to her perfectly fitted cuff.

"Careless, Miss Malfoy. Two points from Slytherin."

"Yes, Professor."

"Clean it. Without magic."

He set a dustpan and brush beside the mess. Draconia crouched without arguing, and Snape turned away from Harry.

Hermione waited until he was out of earshot. "Her sleeve didn't touch it."

"I know."

"She did that deliberately."

"I know."

Hermione glanced back. Draconia was gathering beetles into a neat pile with the brush. She did not look towards them, but the corner of her mouth moved. Harry smiled down at his chopping board. Hermione pressed her lips together and resumed reading the instructions.

After class, they waited near the dungeon stairs while Draconia finished cleaning. She emerged rubbing a sore place at the base of her thumb. Her usual careful expression was spoiled by a strand of hair which had escaped her braid and stuck to her cheek.

"You didn't have to do that," Harry said.

"I know."

Hermione folded her arms. "You lost two points."

Draconia looked at her. "You lost five for whispering."

"I was helping."

"So was I."

Hermione glanced at Draconia's hand, then back towards the classroom. She did not mention the points again. Harry leaned against the wall.

"Thanks."

Draconia's gaze moved to him.

"You should still read ahead."

"I did."

"Then remember ahead."

"That seems harder."

"It is."

Hermione shifted her books. "We can revise together before the next class."

Draconia inclined her head. "Apparently, he requires both of us."

Harry pushed away from the wall. "I answered two out of three."

"After I told you one," Hermione said.

"And I prevented you from losing another ten points," Draconia added.

Harry started up the stairs.

"Both of you are exhausting."

They followed. Hermione began explaining which passage he should reread; Draconia said he ought to start with the index. Harry shook his head without looking back. Neither voice sounded angry, and he was getting rather good at telling the difference.

Autumn reached Hogwarts gradually.

Leaves darkened along the edge of the Forbidden Forest, and rain arrived in sudden bursts that sent students crowding into doorways. Damp wool became part of the smell of the corridors. Harry learned where to dry his shoes and how much reading Hermione meant when she said only a little. During those early weeks, Draconia persuaded the older student to teach her how to enchant paper birds herself, and Hermione insisted on learning too. Lessons began to fit into a week Harry could recognise.

Flying refused to fit into anything he had expected.

On the training grounds, a cold wind lifted the ends of their robes and stirred the grass around a line of battered broomsticks. Harry looked at the one by his feet. The handle was scratched, several twigs stuck out sideways, and he could not imagine trusting it with his weight. Then Madam Hooch told them to call their brooms into their hands. His rose at once.

Draconia's did too. She gave him a quick, pleased glance. Hermione's rolled over, and Harry decided that commenting on it would be unwise.

Before any of them had properly mounted, Neville kicked off too hard. His broom carried him above the class while he clung to the handle, calling for help. Harry watched the little figure tilt against the sky. Then Neville slipped. He hit the ground with a sickening thud, and the class surged towards him.

Madam Hooch knelt beside him and examined his wrist. A small glass ball had rolled from his pocket; Draconia picked it up before anybody stepped on it. Harry said as much when a Gryffindor demanded to know what she was doing.

While Madam Hooch led Neville towards the castle, warning the rest of them not to leave the ground, Draconia turned the Remembrall in the light.

"He'll want this back," Harry said.

"I know."

"Then why are you holding it like that?"

"Because Madam Hooch ordered us not to fly. I'm considering whether returning it would justify a minor breach."

Hermione stared at her. "It absolutely would not."

Draconia mounted her broom.

"Fortunately, I wasn't asking."

She rose before Hermione could stop her, scarcely higher than the tops of their heads.

"That isn't returning it," Hermione said.

Draconia climbed a little further. Harry gripped his own broom. He knew Madam Hooch had told them to stay down. He could still hear Neville crying, and the sensible thing was to wait until someone explained how to do this properly. But the handle had jumped into his hand as though it belonged there. He swung one leg over and pushed off.

The ground fell away, and he forgot the argument. Cold air rushed beneath his sleeves. He leaned forward; the broom moved where he wanted it. No waiting for the right words, no page he could almost remember, no hands reaching across to correct his. His fingers loosened on the handle. He climbed after Draconia, laughing when she looked over her shoulder and discovered how close he was.

"You've done this before," she accused.

"Never."

"That's irritating."

She tossed the Remembrall towards him.

"Here, then. See if—"

A gust caught the loose sleeve of Harry's robe as he reached. The glass slipped past his fingers. Draconia's smile vanished, and for an instant both of them stared at the ball falling towards the grass.

Harry went after it.

He did not pause to work out how to make the broom dive. His body leaned, his hands followed, and the ground rushed towards him. The Remembrall spun below, catching a brief flash of sunlight. He stretched one arm out, felt the broom tilt beneath his knees and closed his fingers around the glass.

Grass blurred into individual blades. Harry pulled up. His shoes skimmed their tips before he levelled out, the ball safe in his hand and his heart beating so fast that he could feel it in his throat.

When he landed, Hermione reached him first.

"That was completely reckless."

"I caught it."

"That does not make it less reckless."

Draconia touched down beside them, looking first at Harry and then at the ball.

"It makes it successful."

Her voice was steady. The hand gripping her broom was not.

"You shouldn't have thrown it."

"He shouldn't have followed."

"You knew he would."

Draconia glanced at Harry. "Yes."

Professor McGonagall appeared before either girl could finish explaining why the other was responsible. Harry's delight went cold at the sound of his name.

"Miss Malfoy, you will explain yourself to Madam Hooch when she returns. Mr Potter, come with me."

Harry handed the Remembrall to Hermione and followed. With every step towards the castle, he found a new reason to think he would be sent home. He had been told not to fly. Everyone had heard it. There would be no point pretending he had misunderstood.

By dinner, he had a place on the Gryffindor Quidditch team.

He was still trying to make sense of the afternoon when Draconia came across from Slytherin and sat at the end of the Gryffindor bench. She had been made to copy the school's flying rules and explain why they applied to her, an experience she described as unnecessary.

"I'm going to be the Seeker," Harry told them.

Hermione stared. Draconia looked impressed for approximately half a second.

"First-years are not usually permitted to play."

"Professor McGonagall made an exception."

"I heard."

"You don't sound happy."

"I'm considering how unbearable you'll become."

Harry tore a piece from his bread. "You threw the ball."

"Yes. Clearly, I have made a serious error."

Hermione looked from one to the other, then laughed. Draconia stared at her, caught between being offended and joining in. The effort showed so plainly that Hermione laughed harder, and at last Draconia gave up.

Harry looked down at his plate, smiling. For most of his life, laughter at a table had meant that somebody had done something to him. He broke another piece from his bread and listened to the girls agree, for once, that Professor McGonagall's methods were difficult to understand.

Not every day was easy.

Some Gryffindors asked why Harry spent so much time with a Malfoy. They rarely did so when Draconia was present. Slytherins were less cautious. Harry heard one older boy ask Draconia whether she had adopted "a pair of strays."

Draconia replied that she preferred strays to pedigreed disappointments. A few people laughed, and the older boy looked away. Hermione walked on as though none of it mattered.

That evening, she spoke very little in the library. Harry had grown used to her explaining what she was reading, correcting his page number or asking whether either of them understood the question. Now she sat with her book open and her mouth pressed shut. He caught himself waiting for her to say something, just as he waited for Draconia when she was late.

She was staring at a page without turning it. Harry nudged a Chocolate Frog towards her.

Hermione looked at it. "What's that for?"

"You've been reading the same paragraph for ten minutes."

Draconia glanced up.

Hermione's cheeks coloured. "I'm thinking."

"That's usually louder," Harry said.

The look she gave him made Harry wish he had simply pushed the chocolate closer. He opened his mouth to apologise, but Draconia shut her book first.

"Cassius Flint is an idiot."

Hermione blinked. "What?"

"The boy in the corridor. Father says his family has spent three generations choosing husbands and wives from the same six surnames. I wouldn't let him decide who was worth talking to."

Hermione's mouth tightened. "I don't care what he thinks."

"I know."

"You plainly don't."

Draconia's fingers rested upon the closed cover of her book.

"I know you shouldn't," she corrected. "That is not the same."

Hermione looked at her. Harry stopped turning the page. Draconia pressed one finger against the edge of her book, as though considering whether she could reopen it and pretend she had said nothing.

"He likes making people stop what they're doing and listen to him," she added. "You don't have to help."

Hermione lowered her gaze to the Chocolate Frog.

"Your family cares about those things."

"Yes."

The answer came at once. Hermione looked up, and Draconia's finger stopped moving along the cover. Harry wondered whether she wished she had chosen a different example. She had used her father's opinion to defend Hermione and now had to explain what she thought of his other opinions.

"Do you?"

Harry had wondered the same thing. Draconia's attention moved briefly towards him, then returned to Hermione.

"I don't know," she admitted.

Draconia looked down after saying it. Harry had expected a neat answer, perhaps an explanation of why her family was different from Flint's. The admission left her looking younger. Hermione considered it for a long time.

"At least that's honest."

Draconia released a quiet breath. "Don't sound so astonished."

"I'm not astonished."

"You are."

"I'm revising my assessment."

"Of course you are."

Hermione broke the Chocolate Frog in half and placed one piece beside Draconia's hand. Draconia ate it without comment.

They returned to their books, though Hermione had to read her last paragraph again. Draconia ate her half of the chocolate slowly, careful to keep crumbs off the page. Harry still had questions he did not know how to ask. For now, he was glad neither girl had packed her bag.

By Halloween, the castle had begun to feel almost familiar.

Harry knew which staircases usually moved after lunch and which landings to avoid when he was already late. The stubborn classroom door near the tapestry of Barnabas the Barmy opened if asked politely; it had nothing useful to say about the vinegar smell beyond it. A suit of armour outside the Charms corridor gave directions in exchange for gossip, and Harry had learned to check them before trusting it with anything important.

Even the attention surrounding his scar had lessened.

He was still Harry Potter, but he was also the Gryffindor first-year who occasionally arrived in Transfiguration without the correct textbook, the new Seeker who had not yet played a match and the boy usually found between Hermione Granger and Draconia Malfoy while they debated something beyond his understanding.

He rather liked being known for those things. A forgotten textbook could be borrowed, and an argument in the library usually ended with somebody passing him a biscuit.

Halloween morning began with feathers.

Professor Flitwick stood on a stack of books at the front of the classroom. Autumn sunlight lay in pale squares across the desks, catching on the feathers laid beside their inkpots. He demonstrated the levitation charm with a delicate movement of his wrist. His feather rose, turned a graceful circle and landed on his hat. Harry glanced at his own. It looked no more interested in flying than Hermione's broom had.

"Swish and flick," he squeaked. "Remember: a controlled motion and clear pronunciation. Wingardium Leviosa!"

Gryffindors and Slytherins shared the lesson. Harry sat with Hermione, while Draconia occupied the desk behind them beside Daphne. Hermione levitated her feather on the first attempt.

Draconia managed it on the second.

Harry's feather rolled onto its side. He checked the other end in case he had somehow been pointing at the wrong part, tried again and achieved exactly the same result.

"You're moving from the elbow," Hermione said.

"I'm moving my wand."

"Too much of your wand."

"That doesn't make sense."

"It does if you understand the mechanics."

Draconia leaned forward. "His grip is too tight."

Hermione glanced back. "His movement is too broad."

"They are connected."

"I know."

"Then why are you correcting the result rather than the cause?"

Hermione's eyes narrowed. "Would you like to teach him?"

"No," Harry said quickly.

Both girls looked at him.

"I'd like to keep all my eyebrows."

Neither seemed to know how to answer that. Harry took advantage of the pause to turn back to his feather. It remained stubbornly on the desk.

Hermione reached over and adjusted his fingers around the wand. "Loosen your grip."

Draconia tapped the back of his wrist.

"And stop expecting it not to work. You have to mean it."

"I'm not."

"You are."

"How can you tell?"

"You make that face."

"What face?"

Hermione and Draconia exchanged a look. Harry decided he did not want them to explain the face. He loosened his fingers, drew a breath and raised his wand.

"Wingardium Leviosa."

The feather rose. Not far. It hovered a few inches above the desk, trembling in the air before drifting sideways and landing in Hermione's hair.

Harry stared at the feather caught among Hermione's curls. She reached up and removed it, studying him over the quill end. Behind them, Draconia laughed before remembering they were in a lesson and lowering her voice.

"You did that deliberately," Hermione said.

"I absolutely didn't."

"You're smiling."

"He does that when accused," Draconia observed.

"Thank you for your help."

"You're welcome."

Hermione placed the feather back upon the desk, though she was smiling too.

By the end of the lesson, Harry could lift the feather and keep it above his desk for several seconds. Hermione said that was progress; Draconia said it was considerate of him to stop decorating Granger. He left with both girls beside him, arguing over who had given the more useful advice.

That afternoon, Draconia did not come to the library.

At first, Hermione continued working as though she had not noticed. She arranged their books, uncapped the ink and began the levitation essay. Harry watched the door between sentences. Beside Hermione lay a space large enough for another book, and nobody moved anything into it.

"She's late."

Hermione counted the lines on her parchment. "By six minutes."

"You noticed."

"I notice the time."

Five more minutes passed. Rain began tapping against the window they had once argued about, and footsteps kept approaching along the aisle without bringing Draconia. Harry closed his book. Hermione's quill stopped at once.

"She might be with her House," she said.

"She would've told us."

"Perhaps she couldn't."

"That's why we should find her."

Hermione looked towards the entrance, then down at the unfinished essay. They had planned to work until dinner. Draconia had missed meetings before, but a paper bird always arrived with an explanation and, usually, an opinion about whatever had delayed her. Harry had kept several of them. Nothing had come today.

"We don't know where the Slytherin common room is," she said.

"We know it's in the dungeons."

"The dungeons contain dozens of corridors."

"So we'll ask."

"Slytherins?"

Harry stood and began gathering his things. Hermione sighed.

"You never think through the second half of a plan."

"You do."

"That is not permission to use me as the second half."

She packed her books while she said it. In the Entrance Hall, Harry had just turned towards the dungeon stairs when a paper bird swooped from an upper corridor. One wing had torn, and it spiralled into his chest instead of landing.

He caught it with both hands. The message contained only four words.

Don't come looking. Please.

Hermione read over his shoulder.

"She wrote please," Harry said.

"I can see that."

"She never writes please."

"I know."

Harry looked at Hermione. Her books were already pressed against her chest, her bag strap pulled firmly onto her shoulder. Neither suggested finishing the essay. They ran.

The dungeons were colder beyond the Potions classroom.

Their footsteps echoed from the stone, loud enough that Harry slowed whenever they reached a corner. Two portraits offered contradictory directions. He had just turned towards the passage outside Potions when Daphne Greengrass came up the stairs.

"Have you seen Draconia?" Hermione asked.

Daphne glanced behind her. "A prefect called her over. I thought she was showing her something."

"Where?"

She pointed towards a narrower passage lined with classroom doors. Harry thanked her and went that way, Hermione close behind. Before they reached the end, they heard voices.

Draconia stood near an unused classroom, with three older Slytherins blocking the way ahead.

Harry recognised Cassius Flint. Beside him stood a narrow-faced girl with a prefect's badge and another boy whose name Harry did not know.

None had seen Harry and Hermione.

"You are making yourself ridiculous," the prefect said.

Draconia's shoulders were straight and her chin raised. Harry could see her hands behind her back, one gripping the wrist of the other so tightly that her fingers had gone pale. Her bag rested against the wall, beyond the prefect's feet.

"Then you should find the experience familiar."

Flint stepped closer. "This isn't a joke."

"No. Jokes usually improve with your absence."

The other boy snorted before the prefect silenced him with a look.

"You sit with them in public," she said. "You pass notes across the Great Hall. People have begun asking whether your father knows."

"My father's knowledge is not your responsibility."

"It will become ours when his daughter forgets who she is."

Harry moved forward. Hermione caught his sleeve and drew him behind the corner before the older students could see them.

"Wait," she whispered.

He looked at her.

"She hasn't asked for help."

"She sent us a note."

"She told us not to come."

"You don't believe she meant it."

Hermione's grip loosened.

"No," she admitted.

Ahead, Flint said, "Potter will lose interest. That Mudblood would turn you in to a professor the moment a rule became inconvenient. Then you'll come back here and expect everyone to forget."

Draconia kept her face still, but her shoulders rose a fraction and did not settle. Beside Harry, Hermione's grip tightened on his sleeve at the slur. He turned towards her. She had stopped looking at him and was staring into the corridor.

She stepped out before he could.

"That is a remarkably confident prediction from someone who doesn't know either of us."

All four Slytherins turned. Harry joined her. Draconia's expression flashed from shock to anger.

"I told you not to look for me."

"Yes," Hermione replied. "It was unconvincing."

Flint laughed. "The Mudblood thinks she can speak to us. Touching."

Hermione's cheeks coloured, but her voice remained steady. "Hermione Granger. If you intend to discuss me, learning my name seems a reasonable minimum."

"I know your name."

"Then use it."

Draconia looked at Hermione instead of Flint. For a moment, she forgot to arrange her expression, and Harry saw how completely the interruption had caught her off guard.

The prefect's gaze shifted to Harry. "This doesn't concern Gryffindors."

Harry moved to Draconia's side. "Seems like it does."

"You don't understand what you're interfering with."

"No," Harry said. "But I understand three against three."

That calculation appeared to trouble the other boy.

Flint looked less impressed. "Going to fight us, Potter?"

Harry had not considered what came after standing there. The boy beside Flint was much taller than he was, and three against three suddenly seemed less useful than it had sounded. Hermione shifted her books into the crook of one arm.

"No," she said. "He's going to draw enough attention that a professor arrives. You'll then explain why three older students cornered a first-year in an empty corridor."

The prefect glanced towards the stairs. Somewhere above them, a bell began announcing dinner, and voices gathered in the nearer corridors. Harry had no idea whether a professor would hear him shouting from here. He drew a breath anyway. The prefect's mouth tightened.

"This isn't finished, Malfoy."

Draconia's eyes had recovered their usual chill.

"It was finished before you began."

The older students departed. Flint shouldered past Harry hard enough to make him step back; the other boy avoided looking at any of them. Harry waited until their footsteps had faded into the deeper dungeons, then let out the breath he had been holding.

Then he turned to Draconia. "Are you all right?"

She rounded on him.

"I told you not to come."

"I know."

"You could have made it worse."

"I know."

"You don't even know what they wanted."

"You."

Draconia stopped.

Harry gestured at the empty corridor. "They wanted you to pick them."

"No. They wanted me to behave properly."

"Same thing," Hermione said.

Draconia looked from Harry to Hermione. She seemed to be searching for an answer which would make them leave, but her eyes kept returning to the empty space where Flint had stood. Harry wanted to say they would not let him come back. He knew he could not promise that, so he stayed beside her.

"You shouldn't have come."

Hermione hugged her books closer. "Probably not."

"I mean it."

"So do I."

Draconia turned away, her jaw tight. Harry thought of the note circling into his chest upon a torn wing.

"Why did you send the message?"

"To tell you not to look."

"You could've sent nothing."

Draconia said nothing. The dinner bell stopped, and the sound of students heading upstairs began to fade. Harry could hear water dripping somewhere behind the walls.

Hermione moved beside Draconia, leaving enough room for her to turn away if she wanted. She bent to retrieve the bag from where the prefect had been standing and held it out.

"They were wrong," she said.

"About which part?"

"All of it."

"You don't know that."

"I know Harry won't lose interest."

Harry smiled faintly. Hermione glanced at him, then back at Draconia.

"And I wouldn't report you merely because a rule became inconvenient."

Draconia looked at her.

Hermione's expression became uncomfortable. "I might strongly advise you to tell a professor yourself."

"That sounds more likely."

"But if you refused, I would first determine why."

"By asking several hundred questions?"

"As many as necessary."

Draconia's mouth twitched. She took her bag from Hermione and slipped the strap over her shoulder.

"The feast will have started," Harry said.

Hermione checked the time and made a distressed sound. Draconia glanced towards the stairs, where the last voices had died away.

"I told you. You shouldn't have come."

Harry did not much want to sit beneath the Great Hall's bright candles while people watched them. Draconia looked as though she wanted it even less.

"You'd better help us steal food from the kitchens, then."

"We are not stealing food," Hermione said, hurrying after him.

"Borrowing?"

"You cannot borrow something you intend to eat."

Draconia followed more slowly.

"My family's elves say the kitchens are near the Hufflepuff common room."

Hermione stared at her. "You know where they are?"

"I know where most useful things are."

"You could have mentioned that sooner."

"You were both busy making speeches."

Harry laughed. The sound travelled ahead of them through the dungeon corridor. A moment later, Draconia's joined it.

They never reached the kitchens.

Halfway up the dungeon stairs, they heard a scream. It rose from somewhere above and cut off sharply. All three stopped. For an instant, Harry thought the older Slytherins had returned.

Then the smell reached them.

It rolled down the staircase in a wave so foul that Hermione covered her mouth with one sleeve. It smelled of blocked drains, spoiled meat and something damp that had been left underground for years.

A heavy impact shook dust from the ceiling. Draconia's face lost all colour.

"What was that?"

Another thud answered. Harry climbed to the landing, the girls close behind, and looked around the corner. At the far end of the passage, a student in school robes fled into a side corridor. The footsteps hurried away. Harry began to ask what had happened, then saw the shadow moving over the wall.

The creature emerged slowly into torchlight. Its grey shoulders filled the passage beneath a head much too small for them. One thick hand dragged a wooden club along the flagstones, scraping pale lines into the stone. Harry stared at the weapon, then at the enormous fingers curled around it.

Harry's mind supplied the word before he could properly accept it.

"Troll."

Hermione grabbed the back of his robe and pulled him out of sight.

"We need a professor."

The troll stopped and turned its head. All three held their breath in the recess beside the stairwell. From the top step came a small, familiar croak.

Trevor sat barely a pace from Harry's shoe. He had no idea how the toad had got there. For a moment, his mind returned absurdly to the ribbon Hermione had tied around the box on the lake.

The troll saw Trevor and lurched forward. Harry thought he could scoop him up and duck back before it reached the stairs. He was so close. Hermione's grip slipped from his robe as he darted out.

"Harry!"

He scooped Trevor from the step. A vast shadow fell across his hand. Harry looked up and discovered that the club was already above him, moving much faster than anything that size ought to move.

"Down!"

Draconia's voice cracked across the corridor. Harry threw himself flat. Silver light struck the troll's wrist, and its arm jerked sideways. The club hit the wall beside the stairwell, smashing stone and bringing a slab of masonry down across the upper steps.

The troll roared. Dust filled Harry's mouth. He scrambled towards the girls with Trevor trapped against his chest, then tried to turn back to the stairs. Broken stone covered their entrance; beyond it, part of the top step had fallen away. Hermione stood on the landing with her wand raised, white dust caught in her hair.

"Locomotor Mortis!"

The spell caught the troll as it shifted its weight. Its legs locked together, and it swayed, clutching at the wall with one enormous hand. For a hopeful instant, Harry thought it might stay there. Then the hand slipped.

"Move!" Hermione shouted.

Harry rolled aside as the troll crashed down. The impact shook the floor and sent Trevor leaping from his hands. The toad landed near Draconia and she snatched him up.

The troll planted both hands on the stone and began pushing itself up. Its knees parted as Hermione's jinx gave way.

"We have to get past it," Harry said.

It lay between them and the route to the Entrance Hall. The stairwell behind them was choked with broken stone; the only other passage ran a few yards to a storeroom. Hermione tried the handle. It did not move. She came back with her wand clutched in both hands.

"We can't. Not while it has the club."

The troll reached for the weapon. Draconia raised her wand.

"Then move the club."

Harry understood first. He pointed his wand. Hermione joined him half a second later.

"Wingardium Leviosa!"

The club shuddered. Harry felt his wand tug against his fingers, as though the spell had caught hold of something he could not lift. The weapon rose a foot from the floor, tilted and dropped beyond the troll's reaching hand.

"Again," Hermione said.

They tried again. Harry loosened his grip as Hermione had shown him that morning and watched the club instead of the troll's hand. It rose higher, dragging against both spells. His arm shook. Hermione stood close enough for their sleeves to touch, her lips moving around the words as the weapon reached the troll's shoulder.

Draconia held Trevor beneath one arm and kept her wand pointed towards the creature.

"What exactly is your plan?"

Harry gritted his teeth. "I thought you liked intention."

"I prefer useful intention."

The troll stood. Its head turned towards the floating club. Then towards Harry and Hermione.

Draconia's eyes widened. "Higher."

"We can't," Hermione gasped.

Draconia pushed Trevor into her bag and drew the flap over him. Harry had just enough time to wonder why she was putting her wand down before she raised it again beside theirs.

"On three."

The troll charged.

"One," Hermione said.

The club dragged upward.

"Two."

Its end struck the ceiling. The troll was almost upon them.

"Three!"

All three drove their wands down. The club fell and struck the troll's head with a noise Harry felt in his teeth.

For a moment, nothing happened. The creature stared at them. Harry backed towards the storeroom, drawing the others with him. Then the troll's knees bent.

Draconia seized the back of Harry's robes while Hermione caught his arm. They stumbled sideways together, colliding with the storeroom door as the troll toppled forward. Its shoulder hit the floor hard enough to shake the nearest torch out of its bracket.

The torch guttered out on the stone, leaving half the corridor dark. Harry waited for another roar. All he heard was Hermione breathing beside him and the frantic little scrabble of Trevor in Draconia's bag. He realised his own wand was still raised and could not persuade his hand to lower it.

"Is it dead?" he whispered.

"It's breathing," Hermione said.

The troll gave a vast, snuffling snore.

Draconia slowly lowered her wand.

"Unconscious," she said. "I think it's unconscious."

A breath escaped Harry in a sound too sharp to be a laugh. He let his knees fold and lowered himself against the wall, trying to stop the next one. Hermione sat beside him, stared at his face, then looked at the troll. Its snore had begun to rattle the fallen club.

"We nearly died," she said.

The words ought to have made him stop. Instead, she began laughing too, one hand pressed against her mouth.

Draconia looked between them.

"You're both deranged."

Trevor pushed his head beneath the edge of her bag flap, blinking as though he had been inconvenienced. Harry laughed harder. Draconia looked down at him, attempted to say something and gave up. She slid down the wall beside the others, still holding the bag firmly shut.

That was how the professors found them: sitting against the dungeon wall, filthy with stone dust, laughing beside an unconscious mountain troll while a toad peered from Draconia Malfoy's schoolbag.

Professor McGonagall arrived first, followed by Snape and Quirrell. Her face turned white.

"What," she said, in a voice far more frightening than the troll's roar, "do you think you are doing?"

The laughter stopped. Harry struggled to his feet and steadied himself against the wall. His knees no longer seemed to belong to him. Professor McGonagall looked at the broken stairwell, the club and the dust covering their robes, taking in each part before returning her gaze to their faces.

"We were—"

"Searching for Professor Snape."

Hermione interrupted him. She had risen too, one side of her robes covered in dust and her hair wilder than ever. Before speaking again, she glanced at Draconia. Harry understood what she was leaving out: the empty classroom, Flint, the prefect's questions about Draconia's father. He closed his mouth and let her continue.

"We realised we had taken the wrong corridor after leaving the library. We heard something and thought it might be an injured student. By the time we understood it was a troll, it was already between us and the stairs."

It was not a particularly good lie. Professor McGonagall looked from Hermione to Harry, then Draconia.

"All three of you happened to take the same wrong corridor?"

"We were studying together," Harry said.

"At dinner?"

"We lost track of the time," Draconia added smoothly.

Professor Snape looked from one dusty face to another. Harry could not tell whether he believed them. The professor secured the club with a flick of his wand, then examined the troll without coming within reach of its hands.

Quirrell leaned against the wall, staring at the troll. "How—how did you manage to subdue it?"

Hermione glanced at the club resting beside its head.

"We used the levitation charm."

Professor McGonagall followed her gaze. Something like disbelief passed across her face. Then anger returned to cover it.

"It is sheer luck that the three of you are alive. The school was warned of the troll during the feast. Your prefects were told to take you back to your common rooms, and none of them knew where you were."

Harry looked from Quirrell to the troll. They had heard no warning in the dungeons; he had not known the rest of the school knew it was there.

"Five points from each of your Houses for missing the feast without telling anyone where you were," Professor McGonagall continued.

Harry opened his mouth, then closed it. Draconia had wanted to send them away. Explaining where they had been would mean explaining why.

Professor McGonagall continued, "However, I cannot ignore the presence of mind required to defend yourselves. Five points will also be awarded to Gryffindor and Slytherin."

Harry counted the points and arrived back where they had started. Professor McGonagall's expression suggested that the arithmetic was the least important part of the evening.

"Your hands," she said.

They held them out. She checked their palms and wrists, then tilted Harry's chin towards the remaining torchlight to examine a scrape beside his cheek. The firm touch made him stand still. She was looking for an injury; it took him a moment to understand that.

"Madam Pomfrey will see you before lessons tomorrow. And you will go directly to your common rooms tonight. Professor Snape will escort Miss Malfoy. I will take Mr Potter and Miss Granger. There will be no detours and no further adventures tonight."

Trevor croaked from inside Draconia's bag. Everyone looked down. Draconia closed the flap over his head. Professor McGonagall shut her eyes.

"Return Mr Longbottom's toad first."

They followed the professors past the unconscious troll. Snape cleared enough of the broken stone to inspect the stairwell, then told Quirrell to remain until help arrived. Harry kept turning to make certain the creature had not moved.

In the Entrance Hall, Snape waited near the dungeon stairs while McGonagall stood beneath the marble staircase with Harry and Hermione. The Hall doors were closed. Voices carried down from the upper landings, and Harry wondered what the other students had been told.

Draconia removed Trevor from her bag and handed him to Harry. A torn scrap of parchment clung to one damp foot.

"What has he done?"

Harry looked inside the bag. One of her parchment rolls had uncurled, leaving its end crumpled beneath a muddy print. He eased the scrap from Trevor's toes and held it out.

"I think it was your homework."

Draconia stared at the damaged roll, then at the toad. Trevor blinked.

"I was wrong," she said. "The troll should have had him."

Hermione reached out and took Trevor before Draconia could reconsider.

"You don't mean that."

"No," Draconia admitted. "Which is irritating."

Snape called her name. Draconia adjusted her bag strap but did not move at once. Harry had said goodnight to her every evening since they arrived. Tonight, he wanted to know who would be waiting when she reached her common room.

"Thank you, Harry," she said quietly.

"Anytime, Draco."

One eyebrow rose at the shortened name. Before she could comment on it, she looked at Hermione.

"You too, Granger."

Hermione shifted Trevor against her chest. "You're welcome."

Draconia glanced towards Snape, then back at them.

"Try not to get yourselves killed before tomorrow."

"No promises, Malfoy."

A slow smile appeared upon Draconia's face. Harry recognised it from the train—the smile she tried to prevent and never quite could.

"Goodnight, Granger."

"Goodnight, Draconia."

Harry could not remember hearing Hermione use her first name as a farewell before. Draconia noticed too; her fingers paused on the bag strap.

"Goodnight, Pot—Harry."

She followed Snape into the dungeons. McGonagall took Harry and Hermione upstairs, slowing her pace when she noticed Harry trying to keep up. At Gryffindor Tower, the Fat Lady asked for the password. Hermione supplied it, and they returned Trevor to an ecstatic Neville just inside the portrait hole.

McGonagall spoke quietly to the prefect before leaving. A few minutes later, a plate of toast and two goblets of juice found their way to Harry and Hermione.

Inside, the common room was still crowded with students discussing the troll. News had travelled faster than they had. Nobody knew who had defeated it yet, and the rumours already involved three professors, a suit of armour and, somehow, Peeves wielding a sword.

They took two chairs near the dying fire, away from the loudest accounts. Harry balanced the plate on his knees and tried to break a piece of toast. His fingers would not do what he wanted. He set it down, drew his hands into his sleeves and hoped Hermione had been watching the fire.

She moved from her chair to the rug beside his, settling with her back against the worn cushion. For a while, she said nothing. The warmth reached his face, and he could smell the butter on the toast. He had been hungry on the stairs. Now the idea of eating made his stomach tighten.

"You ran towards that without even considering the danger," she said.

"Trevor was there."

"It was a toad."

"He's Neville's."

"That doesn't make him worth dying for."

Harry looked down at her. She was rubbing at a spot of dust on her cuff long after it had disappeared. Her voice sounded sharp, but when she stopped speaking he heard the uneven breath beneath it.

"I didn't think," he admitted.

"I know."

"You and Draconia helped."

"We nearly didn't have time."

"But you did."

"That isn't the point."

Harry looked into the fire. The final log had split down the centre, exposing a bright seam of orange.

"What is the point?"

Hermione was quiet for so long that he thought she might not answer.

"Why does it always have to be you?"

Harry frowned. "I was closest."

"Today."

Something in the way she said it made him look at her again. Hermione picked loose fibres from the edge of the rug.

"You did it on the platform too. You didn't know how the barrier worked, but you went first. And in flying—you followed Draconia without knowing whether you could control the broom. Tonight, you saw the troll and decided you were the one who had to move."

"No one made me."

"I know."

Her voice softened.

"That's what worries me."

Harry looked at his sleeves. At home, the worst part of doing something dangerous had usually been explaining the broken thing afterwards, or being blamed when Dudley noticed. Hermione was looking at him as though the danger itself mattered, even now that everybody was safe.

"I'll... I'll think next time," he said.

She gave him a severe look. "Properly?"

"I'll try."

"That isn't especially reassuring."

"It's what I've got."

Hermione leaned her head back against the chair. After a moment, Harry lowered one hand from his sleeve. The shaking had almost stopped.

"You came looking for Draconia too," he said.

"Yes."

"You could've gone to find a professor instead."

"Would you have waited?"

Harry considered saying yes. She tilted her head to look at him.

"Probably not."

"Then I wasn't letting you go on your own."

He broke off a small piece of toast and offered it to her. She took it without moving from the rug. Harry ate the rest of the slice slowly, grateful that she had stayed close enough for him to hear her breathing.

Around them, the accounts of the troll grew more ridiculous. Harry heard somebody insist that it had carried two clubs. Tomorrow, people would ask where he and Hermione had been, and somebody would want to know why they had been with Draconia. He could imagine the looks already.

He remembered her wand rising beside his and Hermione's, then their hands catching his robes as the troll fell. Across the castle, she would be trying to rescue an essay from the bottom of her bag. He hoped someone had found her something to eat too.

Hermione rested her head against his chair and closed her eyes.

"We still have the levitation essay due Monday."

Harry stared at her.

"You're thinking about homework?"

"I'm trying not to think about the troll."

"Oh."

The fire settled with a soft crack.

"Did Trevor really ruin Draconia's essay?"

"There's a toad footprint right through the conclusion."

Hermione opened her eyes. For a moment, Harry thought she was going to explain how to remove it. Then her mouth twitched.

His laughter came more easily this time. Hermione joined him, and neither hurried to stop.
