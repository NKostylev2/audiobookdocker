Eleven minutes after the notice went up on Sunday morning, Neville cast a Shield Charm beneath it. Harry had barely finished reading the suspension of all unauthorised shield practice; he would remember the time because Ron had complained that the Ministry had managed to ruin breakfast before nine. Harry was halfway across the entrance hall when the first crash came from the balcony. He had been trying to decide whether Hermione's request for the marmalade at breakfast meant they were all right, or merely that she wanted marmalade. Ron had advised him to assume the second until further notice. Neville, walking several paces ahead with a book under one arm, looked up before either of them. Peeves hung upside down over the balustrade. The brass candlestick in his hands was almost as long as a second-year's forearm. He let it go with a delighted shriek, directly above a Hufflepuff who had stopped to tie her shoe. Neville dropped his book.

"Protego!"

The shield rose crookedly, its upper edge bending towards the staircase. Neville planted his heels as the weight came down, one shoulder lifting as though he could brace the spell with his body. Brass struck light with a clang which echoed through the hall. The candlestick flew sideways into a suit of armour; the knight's helmet spun twice upon its neck and began swearing in what Harry hoped was a language nobody present understood. The Hufflepuff stayed crouched by her shoe. She stared at the place the candlestick had fallen, then at Neville's wand.

"Wand," Montague demanded.

He had been standing beside the doors throughout. Harry had not seen him move when Peeves released the candlestick. He moved quickly enough now, Warrington following at his shoulder, both silver badges bright against their robes. Neville drew his wand against his chest. The confidence of the spell left his face almost at once, replaced by the old expectation of having done something wrong. Harry stepped beside him.

"The candlestick fell."

"I saw."

"Then you know why he cast. Leave him alone."

Montague kept his hand out. "Unauthorised Shield Charm. Hand it over, Longbottom."

The Hufflepuff got to her feet. Her undone lace trailed behind her as she moved closer to Neville.

"He stopped it hitting me."

"The prohibition does not contain an exception for—"

"Falling objects?" Hermione asked.

She had followed them out of breakfast. Draconia joined her from the Slytherin table, still carrying an apple she apparently no longer wanted. There was a cautiousness between the girls which Harry had noticed over the toast: they gave each other room to speak, and neither quite knew what to do when the other left it. He hated recognising that. He was grateful they had come anyway. Draconia looked back at the pink notice suspended above the Great Hall doors.

"It says practice is suspended," she said. "Neville wasn't practising."

"Professor Umbridge will decide that."

"She can ask the girl it nearly hit," Draconia replied. "Unless witnesses have been banned as well."

Warrington glanced upwards. Peeves had begun searching the balcony for something else to drop. Several students moved away from the stairs. Montague seemed prepared to ignore all of them in favour of the wand beneath Neville's hands.

"You can't take it," Hermione said. "The patrol's authority is to report a breach to a professor. That's what Professor McGonagall has already told you."

"I can escort him to—"

"Then go and find her," Harry said. "Neville's staying here. With his wand."

People had stopped around them. Harry recognised Hannah by the doors, Anthony upon the stairs and Pansy beside one of the pillars. They watched without reaching for wands or saying anything which Montague might use against them. The Hufflepuff remained beside Neville, rubbing one dusty knee through her robes. Montague looked from her to Harry, then at the growing number of students who had seen the whole thing. His jaw moved.

"This will be reported."

"Include Peeves," Hermione said.

He walked away without answering. Warrington followed after a moment, looking back once at the candlestick. Above them, the knight had moved on to insulting the poltergeist's ancestry with sufficient enthusiasm that even Peeves seemed uncertain how to respond. Neville let his breath out slowly.

"I didn't think," he said.

"You didn't need to stop and think about it."

Neville turned towards Harry, still looking as though he expected a reprimand.

"You saw it falling and knew what to do," Harry explained. "That's what we practised for. You weren't supposed to stop and check a notice first."

Neville looked at the wand in his hand. Harry remembered the first meeting, the way Neville had apologised when the practice dummy turned towards him. There had been nothing apologetic about the shield beneath the falling brass. The Hufflepuff retrieved the candlestick from beside the armour. Its stem had bent almost into a hook. She carried it carefully, presenting the undamaged end to Neville.

"You should keep it."

"Why?"

"In case he says the spell didn't work. Or that I wasn't there."

Neville accepted it. She gave him a small, embarrassed smile, finished tying her shoe and went to join her friends. Hermione picked up his book. Draconia finally took a bite of the apple. Around them the hall resumed its Sunday noise: footsteps, the doors swinging shut, somebody calling for a friend who had gone on ahead. Harry could still hear the knight muttering when they reached the first staircase. Neville carried the bent brass all the way upstairs.

Harry and Draconia did not kiss again immediately. By Tuesday he had discovered that knowing she wanted him to kiss her was very little help in choosing when to do it. He had thought the first kiss would help with that. It had answered at least one question; surely answering a question ought to make things simpler. Instead he became aware of her hand on his sleeve during Potions, of her knee brushing his beneath the library table, of the brief hesitation before she said goodnight. Each seemed to require him to do something, and he generally worked out what that might have been several minutes too late.

Hermione was there for most of those moments. She still helped him find references and still corrected Draconia when her arguments became particularly unreasonable. Sometimes she laughed with them and then looked down at her work before the laughter had quite ended. Harry found himself checking her face whenever Draconia touched him. He knew Hermione noticed. He did not know how to stop without making it seem he had stopped caring. On Tuesday they took their usual table by the tall library windows. Rain had turned the view of the grounds grey, and somebody had lit the lamps early. Hermione sat opposite Harry. Draconia chose the end, leaving an empty chair between the places where they would normally spread their books and complain about being crowded. For forty minutes, their conversation concerned essays.

Harry wrote the same line twice. He crossed out the second version, stared at the first and put down his quill.

"Are we going to spend the whole afternoon pretending we're only thinking about homework?"

Hermione did not look up. "You should finish your sentence."

"I did. Twice."

"The one after it, then."

"Hermione."

She kept one finger upon the page. "What do you want me to say?"

He had expected irritation. The tiredness in the question was harder to answer.

"I don't know. That we can talk about something which isn't homework."

"We can. I haven't forbidden conversation."

"No, but we've been behaving as though Madam Pince's going to deduct points if somebody mentions lunch."

Draconia turned a page. "She may. I wouldn't test it."

Hermione's mouth moved at one corner. Harry nearly pointed it out, then thought better of making her responsible for reassuring him with a smile.

"I'm trying," he said instead. "I keep making it worse."

Hermione closed her book around her finger.

"You keep looking at me whenever Draco moves."

Draconia's quill stopped.

"I don't mean to," Harry said.

"I know. But it makes me feel as though you're waiting for me to fall apart. I'm not going to do that every time she touches your arm."

"We're worried about you," Draconia said quietly.

"Then ask how I am. Don't decide from across the table."

Harry rubbed at the ink on his thumb. Outside, rain ticked against the glass; a branch moved across one narrow pane, leaving a trail of water behind it. He could remember another afternoon here when Hermione had fallen asleep over a book and Draconia had pretended not to notice before moving the lamp away from her face. He wanted them to be able to have afternoons like that again. Wanting it did not give Hermione a reason to stop hurting.

"How are you?" he asked.

She gave him a look. "Don't sound so frightened of the answer."

"I'm frightened of most answers at the moment."

That won him the beginning of a smile.

"Still hurt," she said. "Better than Saturday night. Worse when you both become unbearably careful with me."

Draconia glanced at the book under Hermione's hand, then leaned across to turn it around. Hermione stared at her.

"I knew."

"You were reading the index upside down."

"I was thinking."

"I hope it was something interesting. You looked very determined."

Hermione laughed before she could help it. Harry let himself laugh too, and Madam Pince hissed from behind the nearest shelf. All three lowered their heads with the automatic obedience of people who knew she could hear a sweet wrapper through two walls. When the librarian had passed, Hermione looked at Harry again.

"Do you want to kiss her?"

He caught his breath badly enough to choke.

Hermione glanced towards the nearest shelf. "You could try answering without alerting Madam Pince."

"You can't ask me that out of nowhere and expect me not to choke."

Draconia had gone pink around the ears. She kept her eyes upon Hermione, but Harry could see her listening for his answer.

"Yes," he said.

Hermione looked down. He watched her take the answer in, and wanted to soften it until he remembered where softening the truth had left them last year.

"Then you probably ought to," she said. "At some point. You look as though you're both waiting for someone to issue permission."

"Here?"

"Harry."

"I thought I'd better check. I seem to keep getting this wrong."

"Somewhere I don't have to watch would be appreciated."

Draconia put down her quill. "Are you sure you want to talk about this?"

"I don't want to spend all afternoon wondering whether you'll stop speaking when I leave the table. I'm jealous, Draco. I'm not stupid."

The sharpness made Draconia lower her eyes. Hermione took a breath, then reached for the book again without opening it.

"Sorry. That wasn't fair. I know you're trying as well."

"You don't have to apologise for everything," Harry said.

She looked at him for a moment. "Neither do you."

He nodded, although he suspected all three of them would need reminding. Draconia resumed copying her notes. Hermione opened her book the correct way up and turned it so Harry could see the paragraph he had been attempting to find. He moved into the empty chair between their work. His elbow struck both sets of notes within thirty seconds. Hermione rescued her ink bottle; Draconia gave him the look which usually meant she was about to suggest he had survived on luck alone. Neither asked him to move back.

On Thursday, the second of November, all twenty-one members came to the D.A. meeting. The hidden room decorated itself with copies of Educational Decree Twenty-Seven, each parchment at the centre of a practice target. Beneath them, the members took turns casting the Shield Charms they had been forbidden to practise. Hermione claimed she had not requested this. Nobody believed her. They practised shields in motion. Cedric organised rotating lines while Ron and Angelina sent controlled jinxes from the raised platform. Neville's barrier no longer appeared by accident. It formed whenever he chose, uneven at its edges but strong enough to deflect three spells before breaking.

The bent candlestick stood upon a shelf near his bag. Neville had told Harry that Professor McGonagall heard the patrol's complaint on Sunday afternoon, asked where Peeves appeared in its account and dismissed it. No apology had arrived from Montague. The shield had stayed on the record as a response to danger rather than unauthorised practice. Halfway through the lesson, the room changed. The targets withdrew. Cushions gathered in a circle. The practice floor dimmed. Harry had asked for somewhere they could talk without making the conversation a performance. Cedric understood. He stood at the centre while students sat, then explained that he wanted to discuss the graveyard. Nobody had to stay for that part; the quiet room was open, and he would send word before practice resumed. Harry was grateful for the warning even though he knew what Cedric intended to say. He looked around the circle and waited until everyone had chosen a place.

"After Hogsmeade," he said, "the Ministry claimed Harry organised a dangerous confrontation and used other students to conceal his activities. Some of you have received letters saying the same."

Several people nodded. Susan Bones held a folded Ministry notice between her fingers. Zacharias looked at the floor.

Cedric continued. "The same argument appeared after the Tournament. Harry was called unstable, attention-seeking and too traumatised to understand what he saw. I was called grateful enough to repeat his story."

Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia. He knew what came next. Cedric had asked that morning. Not for forgiveness after speaking. Permission before. Harry had nearly refused. Then he remembered Neville keeping the candlestick because someone called it proof.

"There are parts of the graveyard I have not described publicly," Cedric said. "Not because they contradict our account. Because they concern what Harry did to keep me alive. He has agreed that I may tell you."

Hermione turned towards Harry.

"You agreed?"

"Yes."

"Why didn't you tell us first?"

"We talked this morning. I said he could. I didn't think I could get through explaining it twice."

Hermione looked down at their joined knees. Harry wished he had told her what they were planning before sitting here; he had been so occupied with getting through one account that he had not thought about how it would feel to hear it without warning.

"I should have said," he added quietly. "I'm sorry."

She nodded once. On his other side, Draconia had become motionless. Cedric began. He did not make it heroic. He described the Cup lying between two graves, the first Killing Curse and Harry throwing them both out of its path. He spoke slowly, stopping once to take water from the glass beside his chair. Harry watched the glass rather than his face. Cedric explained the binding which dragged him behind the broad base of a gravestone, and the shield Harry forced through its cords. Grass thickened into roots around him. The wood, rather than the Shield Charm itself, took the second Killing Curse, blackening under the impact. The roots then closed so tightly that nobody outside could see him breathe. The men standing over them believed he had been killed.

"Nott examined the roots and said, 'Dead,'" Cedric told them. "They believed him. They left me there because they thought there was nobody left to rescue. Harry was keeping the whole thing up. I could feel when it weakened."

"When the others arrived, nobody checked again," Cedric said. "I could hear them talking around me. As long as those roots held, they had no reason to look for somebody alive."

The room was silent. Harry looked at the floor. Cedric described Voldemort's return without giving the ritual details. He spoke of Harry refusing to beg, using sarcasm until speech became difficult, attempting to keep Voldemort's attention away from the grave behind him.

"Every curse weakened the binding," Cedric said. "I could feel it through the tether. Harry reinforced it each time. I can't tell you what choices he saw. I only know he kept it up while they were hurting him. I was waiting for it to break. It didn't."

Hermione's hand found Harry's. Her fingers were cold.

"How long?" Ginny whispered.

Cedric looked at Harry before answering.

"I couldn't tell you. There wasn't a clock. It felt much longer than it can have been."

Harry swallowed. He remembered trying to count at first, losing the numbers and starting again because counting gave him something to do which did not belong to Voldemort.

"There were gaps," he said. "He asked questions. Sometimes he waited."

It was the first thing he had contributed to the account. Cedric nodded without asking him for more.

"Long enough for me to wonder whether he could keep going," he continued. "Then one of the ropes would tighten again, and I'd know he was still trying."

Ginny pulled her sleeves down over her hands. Harry could hear somebody breathing through their mouth near the back of the circle. The room held the small sound without producing a comforting answer of its own. He kept his eyes upon the grain of the floorboards. He knew which marks belonged to the room and which his mind supplied: the pale line beside his shoe became the edge of a gravestone; a knot in the wood became the polished tip of a black shoe. His fingers remembered dirt beneath the nails. His back remembered stone. Then Hermione's thumb crossed the scar upon his hand again. The floor became wood.

Someone near the back began crying without sound. Harry could not tell who and did not look. Luna sat with her knees drawn up and her eyes fixed upon Cedric. Susan had crushed the Ministry notice inside one fist. Even Zacharias, who questioned almost everything on principle and several things merely for exercise, had no interruption prepared. Neville's gaze had dropped to the bent candlestick upon the shelf. Harry understood the connection before Neville spoke it. One act could become evidence in either direction. The Ministry had spent months making Harry's survival proof that he was unstable. Cedric was telling them about choices Harry had barely understood while making them. Harry wished that being alive could have been enough without anybody having to decide what it proved. He pressed his free hand against the cushion and reminded himself that he had agreed to this conversation. He could still ask Cedric to stop.

Draconia stood. For one second Harry thought she was leaving. She crossed the small distance in front of him, knelt and put both arms around his shoulders. The embrace was not careful. Harry's face pressed against her neck. Her body shook once beneath his hands.

"Draco," he murmured.

"Don't tell me it wasn't that bad," she said against his hair.

"I wasn't going to."

"You looked as though you were." Her grip loosened a little. "Just let me hold you for a minute."

Harry let himself lean into her. Hermione remained beside them, still holding his hand. Her thumb moved across the place where the Blood Quill had written. Cedric completed the part he remembered: the roots breaking, his first full breath, the masked man close enough to strike with a piece of stone. He had taken the man's wand before a Stunner brought him down.

"That is where my memory stops," he said. "I woke back at Hogwarts with Madam Pomfrey beside me. What happened between those moments came from Harry and Mr Malfoy, and from the cord still holding us together when we landed."

He gave the rest more briefly. Lucius had driven the Cup within Harry's reach and shielded both boys. Harry could have taken a handle then, but Cedric lay too far away. Instead he cast a new tether, dragged Cedric through the grass and pressed their palms to the handles together. A curse struck as they left. Harry had arrived bleeding and barely conscious, and Sirius caught him when he collapsed.

"He could have left me," Cedric said. "The Cup was there. He was hurt, and nobody here would have blamed him. He stayed until he could bring me with him. So when somebody tells you Harry hides behind other people, remember who was behind the gravestone, and who was keeping him alive."

No one applauded. Harry was grateful. Neville raised his hand as though they were in class.

"What are we supposed to do with that?"

Cedric considered.

"Learn what he's teaching you. Help each other. And if Harry tells you to leave while he stays behind, don't assume he's thought it through just because he sounds certain."

Ron looked at Harry.

"We're doing well so far."

Laughter moved quietly through the circle. It did not break the seriousness. It made the truth survivable. Harry lifted his head from Draconia's shoulder. She did not let go.

"Everyone's looking," he muttered.

"They can look somewhere else," she said, although she loosened her arms enough for him to sit up. Her hand remained at the back of his neck. "Tell me if you want me to move."

"Not yet," Harry said.

Hermione tightened her grip upon his hand. For the rest of the meeting, Cedric taught. He changed the exercise without announcing why. Students worked in groups of three: one casting, one shielding and one standing behind the shield with their wand lowered. The third position was the most difficult. It required trusting somebody else's spell when every instinct urged them to raise their own. Harry took the unarmed place first. Neville stood in front of him. Ginny faced them from ten paces away with her wand drawn.

"You needn't prove anything," Neville said quietly.

"I'm not. You need somebody to practise on."

"That sounds like proving something with extra words."

Ron laughed from the next group. "He's learning. Soon he'll use four sentences to deny it."

Harry folded his arms. "Are we practising or holding a meeting about my vocabulary?"

Ginny announced a low-powered Stinging Jinx before casting. It struck Neville's shield and scattered into sparks. Neville did not flinch. The second spell drove him back half a step. The third bent the barrier almost to Harry's chest, but Neville braced both feet and held it until the light steadied. When the exercise ended, Harry tapped the bent candlestick with one finger.

"You should put that somewhere visible."

Neville considered the shelf. "Here?"

"Here."

By the time everyone left, the room had built a narrow wooden case around it. A brass plaque appeared beneath the bent base.

ELEVEN MINUTES.

Neville crouched to look at the words.

"I didn't ask for that," he said.

"Keep it," Ron replied. "Best thing the Ministry's timed all year."

Harry stayed in the circle after the others began collecting bags. Cedric sat beside him without attempting to finish the conversation, and Draconia remained close enough for Harry to feel her knee against his. Hermione brought the glass of water from the centre. He drank because it was there, and because nobody asked whether he felt better before allowing him to return to his dormitory.

On the twenty-fifth of November, Harry woke to rain hammering the dormitory window. By breakfast it had turned to sleet, and Gryffindor still had to play Slytherin. The Great Hall windows rattled beneath the wind. Students arrived in scarves wrapped to their eyes, stamping mud from boots and shouting predictions across the tables. Red and green banners hung from the rafters. Two had already attempted to strangle one another above the doors. Ron did not eat. He sat with both hands around a cup of tea, watching steam rise as though it contained tactical advice. Harry placed toast upon his plate.

"No."

"It isn't poisoned."

"I can't."

"You ate before the train journey."

Ron looked at him.

"The one which ended in an attack?"

"I was going to mention your excellent performance afterwards."

"Try leading with that."

"You kept eleven Quaffles out yesterday."

"Practice isn't the same."

"No. But today you can imagine how annoyed Warrington will be every time you save one."

"How?"

"Might give you something to think about besides being nervous."

Ron considered. He picked up the toast. Across the hall, Draconia wore a green scarf over her uniform. A narrow red ribbon had been tied around one wrist. Pansy noticed Harry looking and raised both eyebrows. Draconia followed her gaze. Harry touched two fingers to his mouth. The gesture was subtle by his standards. Draconia's face coloured immediately. Pansy nearly fell from the bench. Hermione kicked Harry beneath the table.

"Ow."

"You said subtle."

"That was subtle."

"The Slytherin fifth-years are applauding."

Harry looked. Blaise raised his cup. Harry returned to his breakfast.

"I regret everything."

"No, you don't," Hermione said.

The statement hurt both of them less than it would have a few weeks earlier. Hermione returned to her tea, and Harry resisted the urge to ask whether she meant it. She had told him what she needed; this morning he could at least stop examining every smile. The teams entered the pitch beneath freezing rain. Wind pressed Harry's robes against his body. The stands rose around them in blurred towers of colour, every face obscured by scarves and weather. Madam Hooch stood at the centre with the Quaffle beneath one arm and sleet collecting upon her short grey hair. The Inquisitorial Patrol occupied the front row beside Umbridge. Montague captained Slytherin. He shook Angelina's hand hard enough to make the intention visible.

"Try not to lose control," he told Harry as the teams mounted.

Harry adjusted his gloves. "Try not to lose to turnips."

Montague's smile vanished. The whistle blew. Harry kicked off into the storm. The sudden drop of the pitch beneath his boots loosened something which had been tight since breakfast. Here, at least, he knew where to put his hands and what to watch. Angelina called to Katie through the rain. Somewhere behind the hoops Lee Jordan began a commentary Harry could barely hear, then McGonagall interrupted him with a correction that carried perfectly. Visibility disappeared above fifty feet. The Quaffle became a dark shape passing between robes. Bludgers emerged from grey air with almost no warning, their iron surfaces wet enough to slip away from every bat strike. Harry rose above the formation.

The Firebolt shuddered beneath the crosswind. Harry lowered his chest towards the handle and tucked his elbows in, reducing the amount of himself the weather could catch. Rain ran down his glasses until every light upon the pitch acquired a second, uncertain edge. He wiped the lenses with his sleeve, remembered the charm Hermione had taught him and cast it before his next circuit. Drops broke apart at the glass. Beyond them the pitch remained a blur of wet colour, but he could see it now. Below, Slytherin scored first. Green erupted from one side of the stands. A chant began.

Harry could not distinguish every word through the wind. He heard Ron's name, poor and useless repeated in a rhythm which carried across the wind. Harry looked towards the green stands. Draconia had risen from her seat. He could not hear what she said to the boys behind her, but one stopped singing long enough to look away.

Ron's next save missed by three feet. Slytherin scored again. Angelina called time. The Gryffindors gathered beside the hoops, brooms shuddering in the gale.

"Ignore them," Katie told Ron.

"Helpful," he replied.

"Watch the Chaser's shoulder," Harry said.

Ron looked at him. "I'm a Keeper, not one of Malfoy's ambush victims."

"Same principle. Warrington looks left before throwing right. Pucey drops his elbow before a low shot. You know the song. You don't know them."

The chant continued. Ron breathed through his nose.

"Shoulder's easier."

"Exactly."

They returned to play. Warrington approached from the right, looked left and threw towards the centre hoop. Ron was already there. He caught the Quaffle against his chest. The Gryffindor stands roared. The chant faltered. Ron saved the next shot with one glove. The third struck his broom and rebounded into Alicia's hands. Gryffindor scored twice before Slytherin recovered. Confidence altered him by degrees. Ron stopped waiting at the centre hoop and began moving before the Chasers released. Once he went too early, leaving the right hoop open, and Harry heard the familiar groan from the red stands. This time Ron did not look towards it. He flew back into position, rubbed rain from his eyes and saved Warrington's next shot with both hands.

The match became something less orderly than Angelina had drawn upon the board. Katie lost the Quaffle in a gust and recovered it below the usual line of play. Fred drove a Bludger away from Alicia, then had to duck when George returned it rather sooner than expected. Harry spent several minutes above the Slytherin Seeker, watching the other boy's eyes while pretending to search the far end of the pitch. Neither found anything except rain. By the time Lee announced sixty to forty for Gryffindor, Ron had started shouting directions. He swore when Katie ignored one, then laughed as a Slytherin shot struck his shoulder and spun him halfway around. The Quaffle remained in his hands. Even Angelina laughed at that, briefly, before demanding he pass it. Harry saw the Snitch.

It hovered beside the commentator's platform, almost indistinguishable from a gold clasp upon Umbridge's coat. He noticed the movement rather than the colour: a quick flicker which went against the rain instead of with it. He dived. The Slytherin Seeker followed, close enough for Harry to hear the hiss of wet robes before the wind swallowed it. Ninety feet. Eighty. The Snitch dropped beneath the platform. Harry could see the support beams now, thick dark wood crossing the space behind the front row of seats. There was a gap.

He rolled towards it before properly considering its width. His shoulder nearly struck the upper beam. The Firebolt answered the movement, turning sharply enough to force Harry against the shaft, and the sole of his boot scraped wood. Behind him the other Seeker pulled up. Somebody in the stands shouted his name. Harry reached for the Snitch. Its wings brushed his fingertips, then it shot upwards towards the lower edge of the platform. He was going too quickly to follow on the broom. His left hand left the broom and caught the beam.

For a suspended instant he could feel everything at once: the jar through his shoulder, the broom passing beneath him, the rain striking his exposed cheek. His grip slid upon wet wood. The Firebolt continued several yards beyond his boots. Harry swung under the beam and saw the Snitch within reach. He let go. Gold closed inside his right fist. The empty space beneath his feet arrived immediately afterwards. Harry fell. There was no clever part to the next second. The platform receded above him; faces became pale ovals leaning over its edge. His stomach seemed to remain there while the rest of him dropped. His right hand remained clenched around the Snitch. He dragged his wand from the holster strapped to his right forearm with his left hand and pointed at the broom flying clear of the stand. The unfamiliar grip made the first syllable catch in his throat; he forced it out before he could think about the ground.

"Accio Firebolt!"

It came towards him handle-first. Harry twisted, caught it across his chest and clung while the shaft knocked the breath from him. For another twenty feet he could do nothing except hold on. Then his knees found the broom, the point rose and the fall became a low, shuddering sweep across the mud. Madam Hooch's whistle cut through the pitch. Harry pulled up beyond the centre circle. His shoulder hurt. His right fist still held something alive and struggling, and he opened it far enough for the wings to beat against his glove. Sixty to forty became two hundred and ten to forty. Gryffindor had won by one hundred and seventy points. The stands exploded.

Ron left the hoops at speed and collided with Harry in mid-air. Both nearly fell. Angelina reached them and began shouting while hugging them hard enough to make breathing unnecessary. On the ground, students flooded the pitch. Harry dismounted into mud and noise. Fred and George lifted Ron onto their shoulders. Ginny climbed onto a bench to lead a new chant containing Ron's name and several claims concerning Slytherin's inability to score through an open door. Hermione reached Harry first.

"You let go of your broom."

"Temporarily."

"Under the stand. In this weather."

"I Summoned it."

"I noticed. After you started falling."

She hugged him anyway. Harry held her, wet robes and all. When she stepped back, Draconia stood behind her. The green scarf remained around her neck. The red ribbon had come loose from her wrist.

"Your House lost," Harry said.

"I noticed."

"You look pleased."

"I wanted us to win. I also wanted you to land in one piece. Apparently that was asking too much."

"Were you cheering?"

"Certainly not."

Pansy appeared behind her. "She shouted when you fell."

Draconia turned. "I will end you."

Pansy disappeared into the crowd. Harry smiled.

"You shouted?"

"You released your broom during a sleet storm."

"It worked."

"That is not a defence."

"It was Quidditch."

Draconia seized the front of his wet robes and kissed him. The kiss was brief, cold and entirely visible. The crowd became distant. When she released him, she looked startled by herself. Harry touched his mouth.

"Was that for winning?"

"It was to stop you arguing."

"I may argue more often."

"I could go back to shouting. I was quite good at that."

Harry looked towards Hermione. She had seen. Pain remained. So did a small, reluctant smile.

"At least nobody can say you didn't congratulate him," she told Draconia.

Draconia's face went scarlet. Ron was carried past them, singing his own name badly. Madam Hooch caught Harry before he could follow, took the Snitch from his glove and moved his left arm carefully through the range which had pulled at the beam.

"You will have that checked before you celebrate," she said.

"It's only sore."

"Then Madam Pomfrey will have a very easy examination."

Harry looked towards Draconia. She had folded her arms. Hermione was doing the same thing on his other side. He recognised the absence of a useful argument.

"All right."

The check cost him ten minutes and confirmed a bruised shoulder rather than a torn one. Pomfrey cooled it, gave him a salve for the evening and made him demonstrate that he could lift his arm without wincing. By the time he reached the changing room, Ron had survived two verses of the twins' new song and looked ready to beg for the weather instead. The changing room became too small for the celebration.

Rain hammered the roof while the team crowded between benches, dripping upon discarded towels and speaking over one another. Angelina attempted a tactical review for nearly a minute before Fred poured pumpkin juice into the silver cup she was using as a pointer. Katie had lost one glove. Alicia had somehow acquired a Slytherin flag and was using it to dry her boots. Ron sat in front of his locker with his hair plastered to his forehead and an expression of stunned suspicion, as though the victory might be withdrawn if he appeared pleased too soon. Harry dropped onto the bench beside him. His shoulder ached when he reached for a towel. He could still feel his hand slipping on the beam; catching the Snitch had done nothing to make that part clever. Next time he would have to let it go, or find another way through. He hoped he would remember that before he was already falling.

"You stopped fourteen."

"Thirteen. One hit the post."

"You frightened the post into helping. It counts."

Ron looked around the room. Every few seconds someone shouted his name again.

"They'll remember the catch," he said.

Harry understood. Ron had spent years measuring himself against brothers whose achievements already occupied trophy cabinets. Even now, after winning the match from the hoops, he expected the final story to belong to the Seeker.

"They'll remember you stopped Warrington four times after he started bowing to the crowd between shots."

"He did look like a prat."

"A damp prat."

Ron smiled.

Harry nudged his shoulder. "Shoulders were easier?"

"Much. Hate it when you're right."

"That is becoming a crowded club."

"Hermione founded it. Malfoy funds it."

Harry threw a towel at his face.

Ron caught it and, after a moment, said, "She kissed you in front of the entire school."

"Not the entire school. Some people were under umbrellas."

"Is that how privacy works in your head?"

Harry glanced towards the door. Hermione and Draconia waited outside with the rest of the students; he could hear Hermione objecting to somebody's claim that falling counted as a valid Seeker strategy.

"It doesn't work very well in my head," he admitted.

Ron dried his hair with the towel. "No. But she looked terrified when you fell."

"Draco?"

"Both of them. Different sorts."

The answer quieted Harry. Ron did not explain what he had seen. He merely stood and held out one hand.

"Come on. Before the twins compose a fourth verse."

Harry accepted the hand and let Ron pull him upright. Outside, the corridor was packed. Hermione had preserved a space beside her by glaring at anyone who entered it. Draconia leaned against the opposite wall, green scarf still bright among all the red, pretending she had not been watching the changing-room door. Harry stepped between them.

Hermione looked at his wet hair. "You changed nothing."

"I removed one sock."

"Why only one?"

"The other has developed principles."

Draconia's mouth twitched. "Perhaps it objects to being endangered without consultation."

"I knew you would understand."

Hermione took out her wand and dried all three of them with a charm sharp enough to make Harry's hair stand in six directions. Draconia tried to flatten it, stopped when her fingers reached his scar and touched the skin there with unexpected gentleness. The corridor continued around them—singing, stamping feet, Fred announcing that Ronald Weasley had personally defeated weather—but Harry felt the pause. Draconia lowered her hand first. Hermione saw that too. She linked her arm through Harry's and pulled him towards the stairs. After half a step, she reached back and caught Draconia's sleeve.

They went to the tower together. Hermione, still wearing her prefect badge, confirmed that Draconia was their guest for the celebration. The Fat Lady let her through with them; she asked for no further explanation, though the look she gave the red ribbon was unbearable. The common room was already full of drying cloaks and people demanding accounts of the catch. Draconia stayed close to Harry's chair until Pansy sent word from outside the portrait that she was waiting to collect her for dinner. Nobody seemed interested in asking why a Slytherin was there. For one afternoon, Umbridge's notices received no attention, Harry's scar remained quiet and nobody asked him to defend an account of what had happened. They wanted to know about the catch. Ron wanted to know who had put another verse in the song. Hermione wanted someone to move the wet cloak which was dripping onto her chair. He was fifteen, finally dry, still sore beneath his left shoulder and happy enough to discover that the ache did not have to claim the whole afternoon.

December arrived quietly. Frost whitened the grounds. The lake darkened to iron beneath the windows, and the mountains disappeared behind low cloud for days at a time. Evergreen garlands appeared inside the castle, though Umbridge required every wreath to be inspected for concealed political symbols. Professor Flitwick submitted a holly branch for approval with six hundred and twelve berries charmed to sing the Ministry anthem in different keys. The inspection office returned it after three hours.

Students studied for end-of-term examinations, practised shields in secret and learnt to hide charmed Galleons inside increasingly inventive places. Hannah Abbott kept hers beneath a false label on a potion vial. Pansy wore hers inside a shoe. Fred swallowed his during a patrol search. The first reminder warmed somewhere beneath his ribs; George stopped laughing long enough to ask Harry to stop sending messages, and Madam Pomfrey retrieved the coin while informing both twins that she would never approve their approach to secure storage. Occlumency continued every Friday. The cracked detector had been withdrawn after September's lesson; Flitwick rebuilt its damaged base and tested it with Snape before it returned to the classroom. The separate charm Remus could use to interrupt a lesson was checked as well. Neither repair made the instrument a reliable measure of Harry's scar connection. Snape made him repeat that distinction before they resumed.

The lessons still tired Harry, but he no longer arrived wondering what Snape intended to do. They agreed on the exercise first, and Harry knew that ending it would not cost him the next lesson.

Harry learnt to identify the moment an ordinary memory became a path. He learnt that anger did not make him responsible for what came through the scar. Naming it helped him notice when his own frustration acquired a hunger or purpose which did not seem to belong to him. Some evenings he caught the difference easily. On others he needed Remus to ask a question and wait while he found an answer. Snape tested the boundaries only with permission.

"External pressure," Harry would say.

"Anchor," Remus would reply.

Train window. Fields moving backwards. Wheels beneath the floor. People choosing to return. Harry closed the compartment door. At first, the pressure remained outside for seconds. Then a minute. By the end of November, Snape could press against the boundary from three directions while Harry maintained it and recited potion ingredients aloud.

"You have omitted powdered moonstone," Snape said during one lesson.

"I'm defending my mind."

"Multitasking remains a useful skill."

"Would you like me to brew something as well?"

"Not if I am expected to survive the evening."

Remus sat beside the brass detector, recording the colour shifts. It still showed white when Snape applied the agreed pressure; it had registered no unexplained red during the recent lessons. Harry tried to take comfort in that without treating it as a promise. Voldemort might know the connection had been noticed. Silence could mean distance, or that the instrument had missed something. Harry did not like how much room it left for waiting. After each session, Remus remained. Sometimes they discussed Harry's progress. Sometimes Remus taught Patronus work, making Harry produce the stag without Dementors so that safety rather than terror became part of the casting. Sometimes they spoke about James and Lily.

"Did my dad always think he could win?" Harry asked one evening.

His Patronus moved around the empty classroom, hooves leaving silver light across the floor. Remus sat upon a desk with his walking stick resting between his knees.

"No."

"Sirius makes it sound like he did."

"James liked people to think he was certain. Sirius needed that from him sometimes. He tends to remember the part which helped, rather than the evening James admitted he was terrified."

The stag paused near the window.

"When was he frightened?"

Remus considered.

"Before every Order mission. When Lily was pregnant. The first time he held you. Whenever Sirius disappeared for longer than promised."

"Did he tell anyone?"

"Eventually. Your mother had methods."

"What methods?"

"She put his broom in a wardrobe after his third night flying patrol instead of sleeping. Then she sat outside it and waited for him to explain. He tried being indignant. It didn't last."

Harry looked at the Patronus.

"That sounds like Hermione."

"Lily would consider that a compliment."

"And Draco."

"Lily might require more evidence."

Harry smiled. The stag crossed towards Remus and lowered its head. Silver antlers passed through the professor's hand without resistance. Remus's face changed.

"He looks like James's Animagus form," he said.

"Everyone says."

"Not exactly."

Harry waited.

"James's antlers curved more sharply. I still remember the way he lowered his head before charging at something he ought to have left alone. Your Patronus stands differently too, at least when you cast it here."

"How?"

"It watches the doors."

Harry looked at the stag. It stood between him and the classroom entrance.

"Is that bad?"

"No. It is yours."

Remus's hand moved through silver fur again.

"You aren't copying him," he said. "I can't tell you exactly why your Patronus took that shape. I can tell you I never saw James's stag stand quite like this. You don't have to be him to keep something that reminds you of him."

Harry held the Patronus until the edges became transparent.

"What did yours do?" Harry asked.

Remus looked away from the fading stag. "My Patronus?"

"Did it watch doors?"

"People, mostly."

"That sounds nicer."

Remus smiled faintly. "Sometimes. It also kept everyone looking away from me. I was rather good at arranging that."

Harry sat upon the desk opposite him.

"Is that why you know when I'm lying?"

"You are not especially gifted at it."

"I fooled Umbridge."

"Dolores Umbridge rarely checks an answer she wants to believe. You had rather an advantage there."

Harry smiled. Remus rarely insulted anyone directly. When he did, the precision made it worth waiting for.

"Sirius says you used to talk James out of things."

"Sirius remembers selectively. I talked James through things. Occasionally the direction happened to be out."

"What was the worst one?"

Remus's expression became distant with the effort of choosing among too many candidates.

"There was an evening involving six enchanted suits of armour, a staircase and Professor Slughorn's private collection of crystallised pineapple."

"What did you do?"

"Improved the timing."

Harry laughed. For several minutes the classroom held something easy. Remus described James attempting to ride a suit of armour down the stairs, Sirius insisting he had calculated the landing and Lily arriving just in time to discover that his calculations had not included the wall. The story contained no war, no betrayal and no final photograph. They were children inside it, arrogant and ridiculous and alive. Harry stored the sound of Remus laughing with them. He did not know he was doing it until the laughter ended. When it faded, Remus looked more tired than before.

"Are you going away for Christmas?" Harry asked.

The question surprised him. So did Remus's hesitation.

"The Order may require me elsewhere."

"Where?"

"I don't know yet."

"You mean you cannot tell me."

"That as well."

Harry packed his wand into the forearm holster.

"You could say no."

Remus looked at him.

"To the mission?"

"To going alone. I know you have reasons. I just don't want everyone assuming you'll do it because you never complain."

The words landed harder than Harry intended. Remus's fingers tightened around his stick.

"That is not why I accept assignments."

"I didn't mean it was the only reason. I mean you can ask for someone to go with you."

"Harry—"

"You told me not to spend myself."

"And now you are returning the lesson."

"It was irritating. I thought you should experience it."

Remus laughed softly. Then the humour left.

"I will be at Hawthorn for Christmas morning," he said. "If I am asked to leave before then, I will tell you rather than disappearing."

"Promise?"

"Yes."

Harry accepted it. He took longer over putting his books away than he needed. Remus waited beside the desk instead of looking at the door. They walked back towards the stairs together, and Harry asked whether Sirius had really tried to blame the wall for the accident with the armour. Remus said the wall had lacked the necessary imagination to defend itself.

The vision came in the early hours of Sunday, the seventeenth of December. Harry went to sleep at half past midnight after finishing a Transfiguration essay he had intended to complete before supper. Earlier, in the library, he had asked Draconia whether kissing before an examination improved memory retention. She claimed no evidence existed. Harry offered to participate in a controlled trial. Hermione pushed his unfinished essay across the table and suggested he try studying first. Draconia had laughed, then kissed him goodnight at the staircase when Hermione had gone ahead. Remembering it made the last inch of parchment rather difficult to finish.

The dormitory settled around him. Ron snored once and stopped. Neville muttered the Latin name of a plant. Wind moved against the tower windows, carrying the soft scrape of snow. Harry had made his last check-in before Sirius went to bed: both guardians were at Hawthorn, and they had agreed fresh verification questions for the night. He checked that the closed mirror remained beneath his pillow, within reach.

Harry named the room. Gryffindor Tower. Saturday becoming Sunday. Four other boys breathing. Door unlocked. Wand beneath his pillow. Mirror within reach. He closed his eyes. Cold stone replaced the bed. Harry moved without feet. The world lay close to the floor, blurred at its edges and sharpened by scent. Dust. Metal. Human sweat carried from far along a corridor. The air tasted of old magic and mice behind walls. He knew the place. Department of Mysteries. Not the corridor outside the Hall of Prophecy. Another passage: circular, windowless and guarded by a single wizard seated beside a door. Arthur Weasley. He wore Ministry robes beneath a heavy cloak. A thermos stood near his chair. His head had fallen forward, though one hand remained around his wand. Harry watched him from the darkness. No.

Not Harry. The body carrying his awareness slid nearer without sound. Long muscles moved against stone. The ground passed beneath a belly. A tongue tasted warm blood before it existed. Snake. Harry tried to pull away. The vision tightened. Arthur woke. His eyes found the darkness. The snake struck. Fangs entered flesh above his knee. Harry felt resistance, cloth tearing, the pulse of blood against his mouth. Satisfaction flooded him—hot, simple and monstrous. Arthur shouted and raised his wand. The snake struck again. This time at his side. Harry screamed. He woke on the floor beside his bed. His mouth tasted of blood. Ron was above him.

"Harry?"

Harry seized his wrist.

"Your dad."

Ron went white.

"What?"

"Department of Mysteries. Corridor. Snake. He was bitten."

The vision remained around Harry: Arthur's blood, the snake's hunger, the terrible satisfaction which still felt lodged behind his teeth. Check. Do not run. Mirror first. "Stay with me," he told Ron. "We have to tell Sirius. Don't go anywhere yet." Harry reached beneath the pillow with shaking hands. Sirius answered after one flash. He had a dressing gown over his nightshirt. Harry could hear someone moving behind him; Remus appeared in the background a moment later, pulling a coat over his own nightclothes.

"Verification phrase," Sirius said immediately.

They had changed the questions at that evening's check-in, as they did each day. Sirius asked his now, his eyes fixed upon Harry rather than the room behind him.

"Who was blamed for the burned toast tonight?"

Harry forced his mind through panic. The question had been chosen at their last check-in.

"Remus. You said he distracted you."

"Confirmed. Your response?"

Harry gave his return challenge, forcing himself to listen for the answer rather than rush past it. Sirius supplied the response they had agreed at the same check-in.

"The blue cup. Winky wouldn't let me use the chipped one."

Sirius's face sharpened.

"That is me. Tell me what happened."

Harry did. Remus moved before the account finished. He drew the Order communication mirror from inside his coat.

"Arthur was assigned Ministry watch tonight," he said. "Dumbledore changed the rota at eight. Harry could not have known."

Ron made a broken sound.

"Is he there now?"

"We verify with the second contact," Sirius said. "Remus?"

Remus sent a Patronus to alert Dumbledore, then opened the Order contact mirror. Voices overlapped. Kingsley answering from somewhere dark. Tonks swearing. Dumbledore asking for the exact corridor. Harry described the door, chair and brass number fixed near the ceiling.

"Forty-seven," he said. "The number was forty-seven."

Remus repeated it.

"Kingsley confirms the post matches your description," he said. "That doesn't confirm the attack yet. He is taking a team to check it now."

Ron grabbed the bedpost.

"How long?"

"Under a minute."

"He was bleeding."

"They are moving."

Harry closed his eyes. The snake waited behind them. He opened them again.

"Tell them the snake might still be there," he said. "It may be waiting. Don't send anyone in alone."

Remus passed the warning. "They know. Kingsley is approaching behind a shield, with Tonks covering him." Dumbledore's voice came through another mirror.

"Harry, can you see Arthur now?"

Harry looked inward and recoiled.

"No."

"Do not attempt to reopen the connection."

"I wasn't."

"Good. Remain where you are. Professor McGonagall is coming to you."

"What about Dad?" Ron demanded.

"The response team has reached the outer Department."

Seconds became physical. Harry counted Ron's breaths. Neville woke and sat rigid inside his curtains. Dean and Seamus emerged from their beds, confused and silent after seeing Ron's face. Footsteps struck the spiral stair. McGonagall entered in a tartan dressing gown with her wand raised.

"Potter?"

"I saw Arthur Weasley attacked. We checked Sirius first. Dumbledore has people checking the corridor."

She crossed directly to Ron.

"Dress. Both of you."

The mirror flared white. Remus returned to view.

"They found him."

Ron stopped breathing.

"Alive," Remus said quickly. "Unconscious. Kingsley has him. The snake is gone."

Ron sat heavily upon Harry's bed. Harry's knees failed. McGonagall caught his shoulder before he reached the floor.

"St Mungo's," Sirius said through the mirror. "We will meet you there."

"How?" McGonagall asked.

"Dumbledore is opening the school Floo."

The procedure had worked. Harry had reported what he saw. Adults had checked the location and found Arthur there. Arthur was alive. Relief should have filled the space. Instead Harry tasted blood and remembered enjoying it.

It was not yet one in the morning when they reached St Mungo's. The hospital possessed none of the brightness of daytime healing. The reception lamps had been lowered. Portraits slept above rows of hard chairs. Healers crossed the tiled floor in lime-green robes, speaking in voices trained not to become panic even when their shoes carried blood. Kingsley was waiting near the double doors when they arrived. He told McGonagall that Arthur had already been taken through, then looked at Ron and repeated the word alive before anything else. Molly stood outside them with Ginny against one side and the twins close upon the other. She wore a coat over her nightdress. One slipper was blue and the other yellow. Ron reached her first.

Molly caught Ron against her, keeping Ginny tucked beneath her other arm. Fred and George moved closer until the embrace contained too many limbs to identify. No one made a joke about it. Harry stopped several feet away. He had saved Arthur. He had bitten him. Both thoughts felt true inside his body. Sirius found Harry near the wall. He had dressed properly before coming through the Floo, though his shirt was fastened incorrectly and his hair remained wild from sleep. Remus stood beside him with Harry's coat over one arm and a glass of water in his hand.

"Minerva sent this through after you," Remus said.

Harry allowed him to put it around his shoulders.

"I need to tell you something."

Sirius's hand settled at the back of his neck. "All right."

Harry looked at the Weasleys.

"Not here."

They found an empty examination room along the corridor. Sirius closed the door but did not lock it. Remus lit one lamp. Its warm circle left the corners dim. Harry stood rather than sitting.

"I wasn't watching the snake."

Neither man interrupted.

"I was inside it. I moved when it moved. I felt the fangs go in. I could taste—"

His stomach turned. Harry swallowed.

"It liked it."

"The snake?" Remus asked.

"Me."

The word emerged too loudly.

"I felt satisfied. Hungry. When Arthur bled, I wanted more."

Sirius reached towards him. Harry stepped back. The movement hurt Sirius. Harry saw it and could not undo it.

"What if he was seeing through me?" Harry asked. "What if I didn't have a vision? What if I was there somehow?"

"You were in bed," Sirius said.

"My body was."

"Your body matters."

"Not if he can use it."

Remus placed the glass of water upon the examination table, near enough for Harry to take it when he wanted.

"Whose impulse made you call Sirius?"

Harry stared at him.

"Mine."

"Whose impulse warned the Order that the snake might be waiting?"

"Mine."

"Whose impulse is frightened by having enjoyed the attack?"

"Mine."

"You woke wanting to help Arthur. That was you. Whatever came through the connection, it didn't make that choice for you."

"What if next time I don't recognise it?"

"Then you tell us what feels different. We help you work it out. You don't have to recognise everything alone."

"You weren't inside it."

"No."

Remus did not claim understanding he lacked. Harry pressed his palms against his eyes.

"I can still taste it."

Sirius stood several feet away, respecting the retreat Harry already regretted.

"May I come closer?"

Harry lowered his hands.

"You don't have to ask."

"Tonight I do."

Harry nodded. Sirius crossed the room slowly and put both arms around him. Harry held on. The embrace did not remove the taste. It gave his body another sensation to believe: wool beneath his fingers, Sirius's chin against his hair, a heartbeat which belonged to someone human and alive.

"Feeling it doesn't make you the snake," Sirius said.

Harry closed his eyes.

"How do you know?"

"Because I spent twelve years in Azkaban thinking things the Dementors wanted me to think. Horrible things. Some felt like mine while they were happening." Sirius held him a little closer. "I know how hard it is to believe yourself afterwards. I believe you."

The answer reached somewhere Remus's reasoning could not. Harry breathed. A knock came at the door. Dumbledore entered with Snape behind him. The headmaster looked at the distance between Harry and the examination bed, the unlocked door and Sirius's arms around him.

"Arthur is in surgery," he said. "The healers believe he will survive. The venom complicates clotting, but your warning allowed treatment to begin before the damage became irreversible."

Harry sagged against Sirius.

"Can we see him?"

"When the healers permit it."

Snape placed the brass detector upon the table. Its repaired base carried a dark seam.

"We can check for the forms of pressure this instrument recognises," he said. "It may give us another part of the account."

Harry looked at the instrument.

"Now?"

"No spell is required. It observes without entering your mind. We compare the reading with what you report; we do not take it as a verdict."

"And if I say no?"

"Then we do not use it."

The answer came without impatience. Harry checked himself. He wanted to know.

"Use it."

Snape opened the brass eye. Blue light appeared. It flickered once, gold at the centre from Harry's remembered fear, but no red thread formed.

"No recognised external pressure is registering," Snape said.

"Because he left?"

"Possibly. The instrument cannot prove the link is inactive. We cannot establish whether Voldemort initiated the vision, experienced it with you or remained unaware until the snake communicated separately."

"He knows Arthur was found."

"Soon, if not already."

Dumbledore removed his spectacles.

"Which means he may infer that you saw the attack and that the Order responded through you."

Sirius's arms tightened.

"Then he knows we'll check," Sirius said. "We have to assume he may try to interfere with that."

"We should assume he may learn anything Harry remembers," Remus said. "The phrases can help us begin a check. They cannot settle it by themselves."

"He will plan around it."

Harry looked at Dumbledore. "How?"

"He may try to make you afraid to wait. Or interfere with the mirrors first. We should prepare for both possibilities without assuming we know which one he will choose."

Remus had said almost the same during the prophecy discussion. The shape of the future appeared without detail: a person Harry loved in pain, every mirror dark, seconds becoming a decision.

"Then we change the procedure," Harry said.

Hermione would have said it. Draconia would already be identifying who benefited from each failure. Dumbledore nodded.

"Later today, after you have slept. With you, Hermione and Draconia, Sirius, Remus and Professor Snape. For now, you remain with your family."

Harry glanced towards the Weasleys beyond the door.

"They aren't—"

Sirius's look stopped him. Harry corrected himself.

"Right."

Arthur survived surgery. The healer said the words at four seventeen. Molly sat down upon the floor. Not because she fainted. Her knees simply stopped carrying her. Ginny descended beside her. Fred covered his face with both hands. George leaned against the wall and laughed once, the sound breaking into something else halfway through. Ron crossed the corridor towards Harry. Harry braced for gratitude. Ron struck him in the shoulder. Not hard.

"Ow."

"You saw Dad being attacked and still remembered the bloody procedure."

"That seems like something you shouldn't hit me for."

Ron hugged him. The movement was abrupt enough to knock Harry back one step.

"Thank you," Ron said into his shoulder.

Harry's prepared refusal disappeared. He hugged Ron back.

"He isn't safe yet."

"He's alive enough for Mum to terrorise the healers. That's close."

Across the corridor, Molly had already stood and begun asking when she could see Arthur, whether he was in pain and which healer would stay with him until he woke. The questions came so quickly that the healer lifted one hand and promised to answer them in order. Ron released him.

"Did you know it was real?"

"No."

"But you told us."

Harry heard the question beneath it. Why believe a mind already used against him?

"Not knowing is why we checked."

Ron nodded slowly. The answer mattered to both of them. A little later, McGonagall returned with Neville, whose grandmother had agreed that he could stay with his friends until morning. He sat beside Ron and held a cup of tea which neither of them drank. Hermione and Draconia arrived through the hospital Floo at five. Hermione told Harry that McGonagall had sent word to their Heads of House soon after the boys left. They had waited for permission and an escort, unable to settle back to sleep. Once Arthur was out of surgery, the professor had come for them and waited while they dressed. Draconia added that Umbridge had attempted to stop them at the Floo until a message from Narcissa arrived asking whose name should appear upon the refusal.

"She found the necessary permission very quickly after that," Draconia said.

Hermione reached Harry first. She stopped before touching him.

"May I?"

Harry opened his arms. Hermione held him tightly. Draconia came around them and placed one hand at the centre of his back. Harry caught her wrist and pulled until the embrace contained all three. Harry held both girls without trying to work out what the embrace meant for any conversation they had left unfinished. He had wanted them there since waking. Now they were warm and solid against him, and he could stop listening for the sound of somebody arriving to explain why he had been wrong.

"You followed the procedure," Hermione said when they separated.

"Mostly. I forgot the communications partner."

"That was during Hogsmeade."

"I am combining failures for efficiency."

"Don't. You did the important part this time."

Draconia studied his face.

"What aren't you saying?"

Harry looked towards the Weasleys.

"Quiet room."

They found a visitors' alcove behind the stairwell. Harry told them about the snake. Hermione went pale. Draconia's hand moved instinctively to her side, where the train attacker's binding curse had cut her. She let go of her sleeve and looked back at Harry; he remembered how still she had become whenever the diary was mentioned.

"Did it feel like the diary?" Harry asked.

"No," she said. "The diary made me trust it first. It took time. By the end I couldn't tell which thoughts had started with me." She rubbed her thumb against her sleeve. "You woke knowing something was wrong. That matters."

"Not while it happened."

"But now?"

"Now."

Draconia nodded. "Then hold on to that. We can work out the rest when you've slept."

Hermione took out no parchment. Instead she held Harry's left hand and placed it against his own chest.

"Your heartbeat," she said. "Not the snake's."

Harry felt it beneath his palm. Fast. Uneven. His. Draconia covered his hand with hers.

"You told them where to find Arthur," she said. "Whatever you felt before you woke, you did that afterwards."

"I know," Harry said. "It just doesn't feel separate yet."

"Try to remember that too. I know it won't make the other part disappear."

Harry looked at her. The words came from someone who had raised a wand against him while another mind used her hand. He believed her.

Arthur woke shortly after seven, while the sky beyond the ward windows was still grey. Only family were allowed inside at first. Molly, the children and Bill entered in groups because the healer refused to permit six Weasleys near one bed simultaneously. Sirius and Remus waited with Harry near the ward doors. Harry did not expect to be included. Molly came out during the second group.

"Arthur wants you. Sirius can come in as well, if you'd like."

Harry looked behind him.

"Me?"

"Unless there is another Harry who saw a snake bite him."

"Possibly in Spell Damage."

Molly's mouth trembled. She pulled him into a hug fierce enough to erase the joke.

"You brought them to him," she whispered. "Whatever anyone says afterwards, you brought help to him."

Harry held her.

"I only saw."

"And spoke."

She released him and wiped her face with the heel of one hand.

"Go in before he attempts to walk out and prove surgery was unnecessary."

Arthur lay inside a private room near the end of the ward. Bandages covered his side and left leg. His skin appeared almost grey against the pillows, but his eyes were open behind crooked glasses.

"Harry," he said.

His voice was rough. Harry stopped near the foot of the bed. Arthur lifted one hand.

"Closer. I haven't the strength to thank you at this distance."

Harry moved to the chair. Sirius sat a little way behind him, leaving Arthur room to take his hand.

"You don't have to thank me."

"I have been informed you find gratitude uncomfortable. I intend to proceed carefully."

Harry almost smiled. Arthur's hand settled over his.

"I remember waking," he said. "I remember the snake. The next thing I remember clearly is Kingsley shouting from behind a shield. He told me why he came. You got him there, Harry."

"I was inside it."

Arthur's grip weakened but did not withdraw.

"The snake?"

Harry nodded.

"I felt it bite you. I felt—"

He could not say satisfaction again. Arthur understood enough.

"When I was under the Imperius Curse during a raid in the last war," he said, "I wanted to unlock a house and invite three people inside. The desire felt entirely reasonable. For several seconds, it felt kind."

Harry stared. Arthur rarely spoke about the first war.

"Did you?"

"I unlocked the first ward. Molly broke my nose before I reached the second."

"Mrs Weasley?"

"A magnificent woman."

Arthur adjusted his hand around Harry's.

"I remember wanting to open that door. I also remember being furious with Molly for stopping me. Neither was something I would have wanted before the curse, or afterwards. It took me a while to trust that."

Harry looked at the bandages.

"Are you frightened of me?"

Arthur's eyebrows rose.

"No, Harry. You called for help. You got people to the right door." Arthur's hand stayed over his. "I'm worried about what this is doing to you. That doesn't mean I'm afraid you'll hurt me."

Something inside Harry loosened. Arthur squeezed his fingers once.

"Also, if you intended to become a snake, I imagine you would choose one with glasses."

Harry laughed. It came out wet and unsteady. Arthur smiled.

"There you are."

The Ministry arrived at eight. Not the Aurors who had rescued Arthur. Not Amelia Bones or Kingsley. Undersecretary Fawley, from the Ministry's security office, came with two officials and Dolores Umbridge, who entered the hospital waiting room wearing pink wool and the expression of someone attending an administrative inconvenience. Sirius stood before she finished removing her gloves. Remus moved to the chair beside Harry, close enough to hear any question which slipped past his guardian.

"No."

Umbridge smiled. "I have not asked a question."

"You travelled from Hogwarts to question an injured child before breakfast. I am saving time."

"Mr Potter witnessed an attack inside a restricted Ministry department. National security requires an account of how he obtained that knowledge."

"He has given an account to Dumbledore and the Auror Office. Arrange any further questions through Amelia Bones. He has been awake most of the night."

"An Auror account does not settle a breach of Department security."

"Neither carved words into his hand either, so they began with an advantage."

The smile disappeared. Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia near the windows. Ron occupied the chair opposite, knees moving restlessly. Fred and George had gone to find breakfast. Ginny slept with her head in Molly's lap. Fawley looked towards Harry.

"Did Arthur Weasley communicate his assignment to you?"

"No," Harry said.

"Did Sirius Black?"

"No."

"Albus Dumbledore?"

"No."

"Then you admit accessing restricted knowledge by an unknown magical means."

Harry stared at him.

"I admit seeing somebody nearly die and telling people where he was."

"You described details unavailable outside the Department."

"That's why they believed me."

Umbridge spoke over Sirius's shoulder. He stayed between her and the chairs.

"You have previously reported visions involving You-Know-Who. Some have contained demonstrably impossible events."

Draconia's fingers closed around the arm of her chair. Umbridge knew about the false images Voldemort had forced into Harry during the graveyard. Harry had described those forced images during the August hearing. Umbridge was using his attempt to explain them as though it were evidence that none of his perceptions could be trusted. She offered them now as proof that saving Arthur was suspicious.

"That is enough," Remus said. "His medical history is not an invitation for speculation."

"Trauma can produce compelling fantasies," Umbridge continued, ignoring him. "It can also encourage a young person to seek significance by placing himself at the centre of adult events."

Harry felt anger rise. His scar remained quiet. Table beneath his fingertips. Hermione at his left. Draconia at his right. Ron breathing opposite. Sirius standing between him and the officials. Sunday morning. St Mungo's.

"Arthur had puncture wounds exactly where I saw the snake bite him," Harry said. "Kingsley found him at the corridor number I gave. You can call it a fantasy after explaining how it reached the right floor."

"We are investigating whether operational information was supplied to you beforehand."

"By Arthur? Before arranging to be attacked?"

Fawley frowned. "Nobody has alleged arrangement."

"You brought two security officials to a hospital because a boy called for help," Hermione said. "You are alleging something. You simply haven't decided which version can survive evidence."

One official reached for a recording quill. Sirius caught the man's wrist before he could release it. "No recording without agreement." Lucius Malfoy spoke from behind the officials. Harry had not seen him enter. He stood behind the officials in black robes, silver cane held beneath one arm. Narcissa occupied the doorway beside him.

"Unauthorized recording of a protected minor's medical account violates the panel order issued in August," Lucius said. "I would expect Undersecretary Fawley to remember. His signature occupies the fourth page."

Fawley's face tightened.

"This is a security inquiry."

"Then obtain a warrant."

"Mr Malfoy, your familiarity with Ministry procedure does not grant—"

"My familiarity is why you dislike my presence."

Narcissa crossed to Draconia. She touched her daughter's shoulder before looking at Harry.

"Have you eaten?"

Harry blinked.

"Arthur was attacked."

"That was not my question."

"No."

Narcissa took a wrapped roll from the breakfast tray the twins had brought back, rather than opening another parcel from home. Harry had seen the tray arrive and failed to take anything from it. She put the roll into his hand and sat close enough that he could not pretend to have forgotten it. Fawley attempted to continue while Harry was being made to eat. Sirius refused to let him turn the breakfast tray into an interview and repeated his request for Bones. She arrived before Fawley had finished disputing his right to end the conversation. She carried a file beneath one arm and enough authority to make both security officials straighten.

"The Auror Office has opened an attempted-murder investigation," she said. "Harry Potter is a material witness and protected minor. Further questioning requires my office, his guardian and counsel."

"The Department of Mysteries—"

"Can submit its questions in writing."

"Minister Fudge expects—"

"Minister Fudge may expect quietly. This is a hospital."

Umbridge looked at Harry.

"You will return to Hogwarts when the holiday concludes."

It sounded like a threat rather than a timetable. Harry swallowed the food Narcissa had given him.

"I was planning to."

"Your extracurricular activities will receive particular review."

"I look forward to your investigation of falling candlesticks."

Sirius coughed to conceal a laugh. Umbridge left with Fawley and the officials. Lucius watched the corridor after them.

"They appear more concerned about who received the information than about the attack," he said.

"Of the snake?" Ron asked.

"Potter saw an attack inside their most secret department," Lucius said. "People they did not summon responded before their own security staff. Look at the questions they asked. I suspect they would find this less embarrassing if they could prove somebody had told him what to say."

Harry looked at the doors behind which Arthur slept.

"They're going to call it something else."

"An escaped experimental creature," Lucius said. "A security exercise. An injury sustained during authorised work. The explanation matters less than ensuring no one connects the animal to Voldemort."

"Can we prove it?"

Amelia opened her file.

"The venom does not match any registered Ministry creature. Residual magic in the corridor indicates remote control or possession. That is evidence. It is not yet a name."

Harry understood. Another truth strong enough to survive privately and weak enough to be disputed in public.

The verification meeting took place that afternoon at Hawthorn. Dumbledore moved it from Hogwarts after Sirius pointed out that discussing compromised safeguards inside a school monitored by Umbridge would demonstrate the sort of strategic thinking usually associated with decorative fruit. Harry sat at the kitchen table. The room smelled of bread and woodsmoke. Narcissa had left a copy of the healer's written instructions upon the dresser, beside the list of things Molly wanted brought back to the ward. Snow pressed against the windows. Dobby moved between cupboards preparing food for the Weasley children, who had been brought to Hawthorn while Molly remained at St Mungo's. Winky objected to the number of emergency blankets in the sitting room and then added four more.

Dumbledore had arranged early holiday leave with their families before opening the school Floo; none of the children needed to return to Hogwarts that evening. Harry sat between Ron and Draconia, with Hermione opposite and Dumbledore, Snape, Sirius and Remus taking the remaining chairs. Ron had asked to stay when the others began discussing what had happened. Harry wanted him there. Hermione had already rewritten the protocol.

"The first weakness was that Harry had to begin the check while he was still frightened," she said. "Ron was there, but we hadn't agreed what he should do. We shouldn't leave it for them to work out in the middle of the night."

"Communications partner," Ron said.

"Assigned in advance where possible. During sleep, the nearest informed person becomes responsible."

"Me," Ron said.

Harry looked at him.

"You don't have to."

"I'm in the bed beside yours. Unless Neville wants night duty—"

Neville was asleep upstairs. McGonagall had allowed him to accompany the twins after speaking to his grandmother; he had spent the waiting hours beside Ron and, when it was finally over, fallen asleep in the first chair offered to him.

"You," Harry agreed.

Draconia placed a second parchment beside Hermione's.

"The phrase helped us check who answered," Draconia said. "It didn't prove he was safe, or where he was. Sirius could give it while somebody held a wand on him. We need to ask about what's happening around him as well."

"A view through the mirror," Harry said. "Turn it around. Show the room."

"Which Voldemort could reproduce inside a false vision," Snape replied.

"Not if a second person confirms through another object," Hermione said.

"Independence matters more than the number of objects," Remus added. "If I am standing beside Sirius and repeating his answer, you have still heard only one account. The second person must check for themselves, or reach someone who can."

They built layers. A vision triggered the mirror. The first contact began the identity and location check with a question Harry had not prepared in advance. A communications partner contacted a second person separately. No one travelled until two sources confirmed the danger or a trusted adult accepted responsibility for acting upon incomplete information. The adults agreed to update their departure questions daily and add a changing token they could show through a mirror. A stolen answer or token remained possible; neither replaced a separate check. The new arrangements would be given to the Order before the next watch.

"And if every channel fails?" Harry asked.

Nobody answered immediately. Harry could imagine the silence too easily: one dark mirror, then another, and someone still hurting while he tried the next name.

Remus looked at him. "You tell the nearest trusted person. You do not leave alone."

"What if waiting means someone dies?"

"Then tell us that too," Dumbledore said. "We must decide how to respond. You shouldn't be left choosing between waiting alone and going alone."

Harry's hand tightened beneath the table.

"Easy to say when it isn't your vision."

"Yes," Dumbledore replied.

The lack of argument unsettled him.

"I will not pretend these procedures make the choice painless," the headmaster continued. "They distribute responsibility. If the evidence remains uncertain, an adult must decide with you and bear the consequence beside you."

Not for him.

Beside. Harry looked at Sirius. His guardian nodded.

"No solo rescue," Sirius said. "For any of us."

"Including Remus," Harry added.

Remus raised an eyebrow.

"Especially Remus," Sirius said.

"I regret attending."

"You promised Christmas morning," Harry reminded him.

"I remember."

Snape tapped the brass eye.

"The mental boundary requires alteration as well. Until now, Potter has practised refusing entry. Last night the link transmitted perception without first applying recognisable pressure."

"Because I was asleep," Harry said.

"Partly. Dreams already loosen sensory ownership. We will begin practising observation without identification."

"What would I actually have to do?"

"You can learn to recognise when a sensation belongs to the view you are receiving. We start with differences you can name: height, movement, heartbeat. You do not need to treat the whole impression as your own."

Draconia's fingers stopped upon her parchment. Harry looked from her hand to Snape, remembering how the diary had made her raise a wand she did not want to use. He was beginning to understand why she had gone still.

"No," Sirius said.

Snape looked at him. "Compelling objection."

"You are not putting him inside another mind to practise being used."

"Nor did I propose it. We begin by viewing a recorded memory from outside the basin. If Potter chooses to continue, a supervised charm can restrict the view to the donor's senses. It does not put him inside a living mind. Professor Lupin remains present with the separately tested interruption charm, and the detector records whatever external pressure it can recognise. Neither would be treated as proof that the scar link was absent."

"And if he says stop?"

"We stop."

Sirius looked at Harry.

"Your choice."

Harry thought of Arthur's blood filling a mouth which had felt like his. He wanted a way to notice the difference sooner. He also wanted sleep, and for a moment he could not imagine looking into any kind of memory without tasting blood again.

"I'll look at the first exercise," he said. "After I've slept. That doesn't mean I've agreed to the other part yet."

Draconia reached beneath the table and took his hand. Harry rested their joined hands beside the parchment, where he could see them rather than the imagined blood on his palms. Hermione looked at them. The hurt crossed her face, but she leaned forward and placed her hand over both of theirs.

"Try the first part," she said. "Then see how you feel. You can still change your mind about the rest, even if that part goes well."

"Agreed," Harry replied.

Dumbledore glanced at their joined hands, then looked away long enough to put his spectacles back on. Harry could not tell what he was thinking. For once he did not try; Hermione's thumb rested against his knuckles, and Draconia had stopped gripping him quite so tightly.

The Weasleys stayed at Hawthorn until Christmas. Arthur remained in hospital. Molly travelled between his ward and the cottage by Floo, arriving with healer instructions, leaving with clean clothes and growing more dangerous each time someone suggested she rest. Bill and Charlie came when work allowed. Percy sent a formal note through the Ministry post. Molly later mentioned that he had also visited Arthur after midnight without his badge. She told Harry very little about it, but kept the note folded in her coat instead of putting it with the other post.

Sirius had cleared the storage attic and enlarged it to make a temporary sleeping room for Ron and the twins, with Dobby fetching spare mattresses and Winky checking every blanket before anybody was allowed near one. A charm upon the smallest window gave the room a cheerful summer view until Ginny complained that it made choosing clothes impossible. The window returned to snow. Downstairs, Dobby arranged a waiting bench clear of the Floo after Arthur's visitors nearly collided twice in the hearth. Sirius slept upon the sofa because he insisted the guest rooms belonged to people who had not already fallen asleep in every room of the house. In the last few minutes of Christmas Eve, Harry found him beneath two blankets with one foot hanging over the arm.

"You own the house," Harry said.

Sirius opened one eye. "Rumour."

"Take my bed."

"No."

"You're too tall for the sofa."

"The sofa is too short for me. Blame the furniture."

Harry sat upon the rug beside him. The house had quietened as midnight approached. Pipes clicked behind the walls. Snow brushed the windows. Somewhere upstairs, Fred and George argued in whispers about whether Shield Hats could be trained to recognise Ministry badges. Sirius moved one blanket aside. Harry leant against the sofa. Sirius's hand settled in his hair, untangling it without any realistic hope of success.

"Nightmare?" he asked.

"Not exactly."

"Vision?"

"No."

"Then what?"

Harry watched firelight move across the ceiling.

"I was thinking about the next one."

Sirius's fingers paused.

"You don't know there will be one."

"Neither do you."

"No."

The honesty made room for fear rather than arguing it away.

"What if I see Remus?" Harry asked.

The hand in his hair became still.

"Why Remus?"

"Voldemort used pictures of you and the girls in the graveyard. He knew who I'd be afraid for. If he can take that from my memories, he might know about Remus too. And Remus goes on missions. There could be times when nobody can reach him."

Sirius looked towards the stairs as though Remus might be standing there.

"Then we verify him."

"What if he doesn't answer?"

"We contact the person coordinating his mission. Dumbledore. Someone who can check where he was sent. The compass can help you find a place you've marked for help, but it can't find Remus. That part is our responsibility."

"And if none work?"

Sirius sat up. The blankets fell around his waist. He looked tired, older than the man Harry imagined from photographs and younger than the years Azkaban had taken.

"Then you come to me," he said. "We decide together. If the decision is to go, you do not go without me."

"You might get hurt."

"Yes."

"That isn't reassuring."

"I know," Sirius said. "But I'm not going to send you into danger while I stay somewhere safe. If someone needs help, we work out how to get it to them together."

Harry looked at the fire. Sirius rested a hand on his shoulder and waited until Harry looked back at him.

"You called when Umbridge locked the door. You checked when Arthur was attacked. Those choices saved you and him. Voldemort may learn the shape of our protections. He does not get to make you ashamed of having them."

Harry swallowed.

"All right."

"You don't sound convinced."

"I'm still beginning to."

Sirius smiled.

"I can work with that. You don't have to get there tonight."

Footsteps crossed the landing. Remus entered carrying three mugs of tea.

"I thought I heard voices."

Harry checked the clock. Midnight had passed.

"It's Christmas," he said.

"Technically."

"You promised Christmas morning."

Remus handed him a mug. "I said I'd be here."

Harry accepted the tea. Sirius made room upon the sofa. Remus sat at the opposite end, his knee against Harry's shoulder. For a while, none of them spoke. Beyond the glass, light from the sitting room lay across the snow. Harry could see the edge of the front path and the dark shapes of the trees beyond it. Harry did not know what the next night would bring. For once, he let the question wait beyond the windows. Sirius was warm beside him, Remus had kept his promise, and neither seemed in a hurry to leave. He drank the tea while they argued quietly about whether Christmas began at midnight or when somebody put the kettle on.

By the time Remus conceded that Sirius had confused a festival with breakfast, Harry's eyes had closed.
