6

Harry leaned into the window and listened to the wheels settle into their familiar rhythm. His hand had stopped shaking by the time the trolley arrived.

The witch opened the door with one hand and steadied the cart with the other. A tower of Chocolate Frog boxes leaned towards her sleeve without quite falling. She caught the top one, put it back and smiled at him as though nothing more complicated than a misplaced sweet was likely to happen today.

"Anything from the trolley, dear?"

Harry looked at the shelves. Chocolate Frogs, Cauldron Cakes, liquorice wands and brightly coloured sweets had survived every version of his life. The sight of them returned him to eleven more completely than Madam Malkin's stools had done. He had once bought enough to cover a seat because nobody had taught him the difference between abundance and safety.

"Two pasties," he said. "A packet of Drooble's and one of those."

He pointed towards a box which promised the NEW AND IMPROVED range of Bertie Bott's Every Flavour Beans.

"What's new and improved about them?"

The trolley witch inspected it. "The unfortunate flavours are more accurate."

"That sounds like a warning rather than an improvement."

Harry paid. The witch glanced at the empty seats, his black jacket and the silver ward line around the window. Her eyes lingered upon the concealed place at his forearm where the holster changed the fabric's shape by less than an inch.

"Keep the door closed through open country," she said.

Her voice had altered. Harry looked at her shoes. Auror boots beneath a white apron.

"I thought you were selling sweets at the platform."

"Same job. Different trolley." She tipped her head towards the next carriage. "Tonks has gone to check the forward wards with Kingsley. I'm covering this section until she gets back."

"Is the flower seller coming too?"

"She's with the London escort. You've got us now." Her gaze returned to his face. "If somebody asks you to leave this compartment, make sure you recognise them. You know the check."

She pushed the trolley onwards. Harry closed the door. The train rocked gently beneath him. London receded beyond the glass, brick becoming allotments, warehouses and wider pieces of sky. Hedwig slept with her head beneath one wing. Hermione's bag stood beneath the opposite seat. Draconia had left a pair of gloves beside it and would deny this counted as forgetting something. Harry ate the first pasty while it was hot.

He had reached the second when the door opened again. Cedric entered carrying his trunk at one end while a Hufflepuff boy Harry recognised as Owen Cauldwell struggled with the other.

"You said you had it," Owen complained.

"I did until the corridor turned."

"The train turned. You still had your end."

"And then my end had the wall." Cedric shifted his grip. "Left a bit. No, your left. Thank you."

They lifted the trunk onto the rack. Cedric thanked Owen, promised to find the Hufflepuff compartment later and shut the door behind him. Harry held out the second pasty.

"Here. I bought an extra, unless you've already eaten."

Cedric accepted it. "Not since breakfast. You might have saved Owen another argument."

He sat facing Harry. For a moment, the compartment contained only the rhythm of wheels and the crackle of pastry paper. Cedric brushed crumbs from his jumper and missed most of them. Harry watched one settle in the fold of his sleeve, absurdly pleased by the evidence that even Cedric Diggory could not eat neatly on a moving train.

Neither had to explain why he had chosen this compartment. That helped. Harry had spent so much of the summer being asked how he felt that an offered pasty and a seat opposite sometimes did more for him than a carefully arranged conversation. Cedric's gaze moved once around the room. Door. Window. Luggage rack. Under the seats. The small safety check had become familiar enough that Harry pretended not to notice.

"How was the platform?" Harry asked.

"Mum cried. Dad told everyone I was returning to finish what I started. Cho threatened to hex him if he said it to a reporter."

"Did he stop?"

"He said it more quietly."

Harry smiled.

Cedric looked at the empty seats. "Prefects?"

"Draconia says thirty minutes. Hermione says twenty."

"And Ron?"

"Didn't read the second page."

"Twenty-five, then."

"My conclusion exactly."

Cedric ate half the pasty and lowered it.

"You all right?"

Harry looked towards the glass. The station had vanished. The key rested against his chest, warmed by skin rather than magic.

"I wanted to get off."

"So did I."

Harry turned back.

Cedric rubbed one thumb across the pale rope scar around his wrist. "I nearly told Mum I was ill."

"Were you?"

"My stomach was doing a fairly good impression." Cedric picked at the paper around his pasty. "I couldn't decide whether telling her would make it better or just make her worry more."

"You could've stayed."

"So could you."

"We'd have had to share Hawthorn."

"That decided it."

Harry threw a bean at him. Cedric caught it, examined the colour and ate. His expression collapsed.

"What?" Harry asked.

"Burnt hair."

"That'll be the improvement."

Cedric swallowed with difficulty. "I'm going to write a complaint."

They began testing the box. Harry got lemon, grass and something he identified as old envelope. Cedric got cinnamon, soap and a flavour which made him stare into the distance before declaring that no living creature should produce it. The compartment door opened during an argument over whether fear counted as a flavour. Luna stood outside with Neville and Ginny.

"It does," Luna said. "Mostly copper and forgotten birthdays."

Harry moved his bag. "Come in."

Ginny took the place beside Cedric. Neville sat near the door with a potted plant upon his knees. Luna settled beside Harry and opened a copy of The Quibbler upside down.

"What is that?" Harry asked Neville.

The plant had a squat, grey-green body covered in fleshy swellings. One pale purple flower trembled near the top, and a larger nodule swelled whenever the train whistle sounded.

"Mimbulus mimbletonia. Gran gave it to me. It has a defensive response."

"Violent?"

"Not if you leave it alone. Gran thought it would be useful for my Herbology project."

The bulb inflated.

Neville placed both hands around the pot. "Nobody touch it."

Harry immediately wanted to touch it.

"What does it do?"

"Stinksap."

"That sounds harmless."

"It got into Gran's hat. We had to bury the feathers."

Harry drew his hand back.

"Good judgement," Cedric said.

"I am developing it."

Ginny took the Bertie Bott's box. "Fred says these are rigged."

"Fred sells sweets which turn people into birds," Harry replied.

"Yes, but he labels them."

She chose a green bean and ate it.

"Sprout," she said.

Neville looked interested. "Brussels or magical?"

"Brussels."

"Unlucky."

Conversation filled the compartment without effort. Ginny talked about Chaser trials. Neville explained that the plant's Stinksap possessed several medicinal properties no one appreciated after smelling it. Luna read aloud a Quibbler article proposing that the Ministry's new educational programme was funded by a secret coalition of textbook manufacturers and creatures living beneath the Wizengamot chamber.

"The textbook part is plausible," Hermione said from the doorway.

Everyone turned. The three prefects had returned. Hermione's hair had begun escaping its tie. Ron carried a stack of patrol schedules and looked personally wronged by each one. Draconia had changed into her uniform during the meeting; her travelling cloak was folded over one arm, and her badge remained straight despite the crowded corridor behind her. Harry checked the clock.

"Twenty-eight minutes."

Draconia stepped inside. "The Head Boy had to introduce himself twice. Apparently the first time lacked authority."

"Generous," Harry said.

"We have assigned patrols," Hermione said. "Two emergency brooms in carriage two, two in six and two in the luggage van. That's number ten on the plan. Prefects have the release phrase."

"Which is?"

"We were told to give it to anyone helping with an evacuation," Hermione said, lowering her voice. "I'll tell you in a minute. I don't want half the train trying the cupboards before lunch."

"You think someone would?"

She looked at him.

"Fair point," Harry said.

He smiled.

Ron dropped the schedules onto an empty corner of the seat. "We're paired by patrol section, not House. Hermione and I have carriages four through six after lunch. Malfoy has seven through nine with Anthony Goldstein."

"Draconia," Harry said.

Ron glanced at her. "Draconia, then. Sorry."

Draconia looked briefly surprised, then nodded. "Anthony's already checking the rear section. We'll take turns after lunch." Hermione sat beside Harry, displacing Luna by several inches. Luna moved without complaint and continued reading upside down. Draconia remained standing. Every other seat was occupied.

Harry looked around. Then at Draconia.

"Problem?" she asked.

"Apparently."

He shifted towards Hermione until a narrow space appeared on his other side. It was not wide enough. Draconia examined it.

"There isn't room, Harry. Your elbow's already in Granger's ribs."

"I'll move it. She hasn't complained yet."

"I was about to," Hermione said, shifting her books onto her knees.

Ginny covered a laugh. Draconia sat. The three of them fit only through determined disregard for personal space. Harry's shoulder pressed against Hermione's. Draconia's thigh remained against his from knee to hip. The train took a bend, forcing everyone closer. Harry became aware that Cedric was watching him over Ginny's head with considerable amusement.

"Not a word," Harry said.

"I didn't say anything."

"Your face did."

"Apparently that's going around."

Hermione opened the patrol booklet. "There were additional instructions. If the train sounds three long whistles, students remain in compartments and prefects report to assigned sections. If it sounds one long and two short, we prepare to move students forward."

"And if it sounds continuously?" Cedric asked.

Ron read from his sheet. "Emergency stop or evacuation."

"At speed?"

"We wait for the escort to choose a safe exit," Ron said. "It's underlined. Twice."

"Useful," Harry said, and meant it this time.

Draconia took Ron's booklet and turned to the second page.

Ron looked offended. "I read that one."

"This morning?"

"On the platform."

"A triumph."

She read silently. Harry watched the landscape beyond the window. Fields broadened beneath a pale sky. The train had left the thick suburbs and begun its long line north. Telegraph poles passed at regular intervals, each one appearing, crossing the glass and vanishing behind them.

"No roof access," Draconia read. "No chasing anyone who gets away. And the Portkeys stay blocked until an escort opens a designated release point."

Harry looked at her. "So even if we need to leave quickly, we have to find an escort first?"

"Yes. They checked the release points before departure, but the escort still has to open one for us. We can't just set off a Portkey in the corridor."

"Comforting phrase."

"You have two Portkeys," Hermione said quietly.

"I know."

"If anything happens—"

"I verify. I don't act alone. I do not duel an army from the roof."

Ginny looked up. "That was specific."

"Moody."

"Fair."

Harry took another bean from the box. Draconia caught his wrist.

"That one is speckled."

"And?"

"The speckled ones are usually organic."

"Most food is."

"I mean things that have been left somewhere to rot. Don't say I didn't warn you."

Harry ate it. He regretted the decision immediately.

"What?" Hermione asked.

Harry chewed with heroic difficulty.

"Compost."

Neville brightened. "What sort?"

"Why is that something you need to know?" Harry reached for his drink. "Actually, don't tell me. I'm trying not to think about it."

Laughter moved through the compartment.

Draconia released his wrist. "I did warn you."

Harry swallowed. "You made it sound like a challenge."

"By telling you it was revolting?"

"You made it a challenge."

"I was trying to save you from eating compost."

"Next time, try telling me it's delicious. I'll be suspicious."

Hermione accepted the box from Ginny and selected a plain brown bean. "You could occasionally listen when someone protects you."

"I listen. I heard every word of that warning."

"And swallowed it anyway."

"I didn't say I always learn."

She ate the bean. Her expression froze.

Draconia looked interested. "What?"

Hermione forced herself to swallow. "Ink."

Harry grinned. "Improved?"

She struck him with the patrol booklet. For nearly an hour, the Hogwarts Express was only a school train.

The trolley passed again. Students moved through the corridor searching for friends. A group of second-years argued about whether prefects could deduct points on the journey. Ron told them no, then spent five minutes checking the booklet after they left. Cedric went to greet the Hufflepuffs and returned with Cho. There was no longer room on either bench, so she spread her folded cloak on the floor and sat beside the door with her knees tucked up. Cedric offered to swap; she pulled his ankle until he joined her. Neville's plant sneezed once without releasing anything. Luna folded The Quibbler into a hat and placed it upon Harry's head.

Hermione objected to the ink touching his hair. Draconia said the hat improved it. Harry kept it on to annoy both of them and discovered this was impossible; Hermione removed it while Draconia looked disappointed.

They played Exploding Snap upon the floor between their shoes. Cedric cheated badly and called it tactical card management. Harry said Sirius would approve, which Cedric did not seem to regard as an accusation. Ron returned from showing a first-year where the toilets were and joined for one round before remembering he had promised to patrol the corridor.

"You can finish," Harry said.

Ron looked at the cards, then at his badge.

"Hermione will know."

"She is in the room."

"Exactly."

He left.

Hermione watched him go. "He is taking it seriously."

"Terrifying," Harry said.

"You encouraged him."

"My influence is dangerous."

Draconia rested her head briefly against Harry's shoulder while rearranging her cards. The movement was casual enough that no one commented. It ceased being casual when she did not lift it again. Harry looked down at the pale crown of her head. Hermione saw. She shifted closer upon his other side until her knee fitted against his.

Cedric drew a card and hid a smile. Harry studied his cards with absolute concentration. It was difficult to concentrate on the game when Draconia's hair brushed his jaw and Hermione kept leaning across him to reach the pile. He liked having them close. He also wished Ginny would stop watching his face as though he were the more interesting game.

"Your ears are red," Ginny said.

"Carriage is warm."

"It's September."

"There are nine of us in here."

"And yet nobody else's ears are red."

Harry looked at his cards. "Some of us work harder at Snap."

"You're losing," Hermione said.

"Exactly. Very stressful."

The cards exploded. Smoke filled the compartment. Everyone coughed, laughed and accused Cedric. Through the haze, Harry saw a dark shape pass the window. It was close enough to blot out the sky, then gone before he could make sense of it. His laughter stopped. He lowered the cards and looked again, waiting for the ordinary explanation that did not come.

"What?" Draconia asked.

Harry stood. Outside, open fields rushed beneath a sky gone suddenly colourless. The sun remained above the clouds, but a shadow crossed the land parallel to the railway. There. A rider upon a broom. Black robes snapped behind them. A smooth mask hid the face. The rider turned towards Harry's window. The first whistle screamed. Not one long note.

Three long blasts. The silver ward line ignited around the glass, and the compartment door slammed shut. Harry heard others closing down the corridor, one after another. The train accelerated hard enough to slide the sweets across the seat.

The speed threw Harry against the window. Draconia caught the back of his jacket before his face struck glass. Hermione seized the seat. The cards upon the floor shot beneath the door, which had sealed itself inside a frame of white light. Hedwig opened her wings and shrieked from the luggage rack. Three whistles. Remain in compartments. Prefects report to assigned sections.

"Nobody opens that door," Hermione said.

She was already standing.

Ginny pointed at her badge. "You're leaving."

"We have to report to our sections."

"You just told us not to open it."

Hermione hesitated, then pulled out the booklet. "I meant don't try the handle. We wait for an escort to clear the corridor, then use the badge. It should only open from this side."

Another dark rider swept past the window. Then another. Harry counted four before the train entered a shallow cutting and the embankment hid the sky. Spells struck from above, red and blue light splashing across the silver wards. Each impact travelled around the window in branching veins. The glass held.

"Death Eaters?" Neville asked.

His voice was tight. Both hands still held the plant.

"I can't see their faces," Cedric said. He had drawn his wand. "But they're firing at us. Get away from the window."

Luna put down her newspaper and moved Neville's pot out of the way so he could crouch beside her. The train burst from the cutting. A green skull burned above the fields. The Dark Mark was enormous. Its serpent tongue extended across the clouds, vivid against the pale early-afternoon sky. Beneath it, black figures appeared in the air ahead of the engine with consecutive cracks. Already astride brooms, they dropped several feet while finding their balance and divided along both sides of the track. Students screamed in the next compartment. The sound travelled through the wall.

Hermione held up both hands. "Listen. The wards are holding. Doors are sealed. Stay below the windows and do not use any Portkey until someone confirms the transport wards have released."

"What about you?" Neville asked.

"I have to reach carriages four through six."

"We have to reach seven through nine," Draconia said.

She looked at Harry.

"No," he said.

"I know you don't want me to go. I don't particularly want to go either."

"You're not walking into the corridor while people are firing at the train."

"There are younger students in those carriages who won't know what the whistles mean. Someone has to tell them to stay down."

"So send an adult."

"The escort told us to keep them in their compartments. We're not meant to fight, Harry. We're meant to stop people running into the corridor while the Aurors hold it."

The answer hit him with the next curse. Blue fire spread across the window ward. It burned without heat, searching the edges, then vanished when the runes repelled it.

Cedric stepped between them. "We go together until the corridor divides."

"You aren't a prefect," Hermione said.

"I can help keep them together."

Hermione glanced towards the faces appearing at the next compartment window. "Stay beside me. No going off on your own."

"Same to you," Cedric said.

Harry pulled the mirror from his jacket. The glass reflected his own face and the green mark above the fields. He pressed two fingers to the lower corner.

"Sirius," he said. "Open the road. We're on the Express. Masked riders, spells hitting the windows. I'm with Hermione and Draconia."

The frame heated. Gold light travelled beneath the glass once: the extraction alert had reached Hawthorn. It did not tell Harry that Sirius had heard every word, or that help was already on its way. There was no face in the mirror yet, and he could not keep staring at it while curses struck the train.

He put it back inside his jacket. This was not a dream giving him a place to chase. He could see the riders and feel each impact through the seat beneath him. He had reported what was happening where he actually was.

"Ginny," he said, "you know the release phrase for the brooms?"

"Only prefects—"

Hermione gave it to her. Ginny's eyes widened.

"If the escort orders everyone forward, take Neville and Luna to six. Take the owl cages too, if you can. Don't open the broom cupboard unless an escort tells you to evacuate."

"I can help now."

"You help by keeping this compartment intact," Hermione said. "Shield the door. Neville, put the plant beneath the seat. Luna, watch both windows and tell them where spells are coming from."

Luna nodded as though this were an ordinary request. Neville pushed the pot beneath the bench. Hermione slung her own bag across her body, pulled Harry's from beneath the seat and pushed it into his hands. He slung it over his shoulder, feeling the reassuring bump of the second Portkey against his hip. Draconia tightened the strap so it would not catch on a door handle. Her fingers fumbled once with the buckle. Neither of them mentioned it.

Harry looked at the sealed compartment door. "How do we open it?"

Draconia pressed her prefect badge to the white line.

"Carriage seven. Prefect override."

The light turned blue. The door slid aside. Noise struck them. Students shouted from sealed compartments. The train wheels hammered the track faster than Harry had ever heard, every joint passing beneath them in a rising metallic rhythm. Spells scraped across the roof. Somewhere ahead, glass broke despite the wards.

The trolley witch stood in the corridor without her apron. Beyond her, Tonks was helping a younger prefect move two children out of the connecting doorway; Harry recognised her voice before the brown-haired face she had worn at breakfast. The trolley witch wore dark red Auror robes from throat to boots, her wand in her right hand and a shorter one tucked into her belt. The sweet trolley lay overturned behind her, Chocolate Frogs escaping between the wheels.

"Prefects to sections," she shouted. "Everyone else inside!"

"I'm with them," Harry said.

Her eyes found his scar.

"You're the principal target."

"Then take me somewhere they can't see me."

"Forward. The medical compartment in three is reinforced. I'll get you—"

The ceiling exploded. Plaster, metal and splintered wood filled the passage. Cedric threw a shield above them. Debris struck blue light and fell. Through the jagged opening, daylight flashed between black robes and broom tails. The Auror fired upward. Tonks threw a shield across the next doorway, caught the younger prefect by the collar and pushed him behind it. One attacker rolled away. The other took a Stunning Spell in the shoulder and spun from view.

"Forward, below the windows!" she shouted. "I'll cover this opening. Prefects, keep them down. Do not engage unless they're inside!"

A broom handle struck the roof beside the hole. Tonks caught it and swung herself through the opening before the rider could aim again. Harry heard a shout above them and saw her take the broom, but the next spell drove him back against the wall. When he looked again, she had vanished beyond the roof edge.

Harry moved. He, Hermione, Draconia and Cedric ran towards the front of the train. The corridor swayed beneath their feet. Doors remained locked upon either side. Faces appeared behind compartment glass: frightened first-years, older students with wands drawn, a frightened cat thrusting its head through a basket lid before its owner pulled it back inside. At the coupling between carriages seven and six, Hermione stopped.

"My section."

Harry caught her hand.

"You owe me a game without cheating," he said.

"That was Cedric."

"You advised him."

Her expression changed.

"This isn't funny."

"I know. Come back anyway."

Hermione squeezed his fingers once.

"You too."

She and Cedric crossed into carriage six. Harry started after them, intending to keep going towards the reinforced compartment. Before he reached the threshold, another impact twisted the connecting door in its frame. He pulled at the handle; metal scraped, but the opening was too narrow even for his shoulder.

The trolley Auror shouted from behind them. "Back into seven! Stay below the windows. We're clearing the front route!"

Harry caught a last glimpse of Hermione through the bent frame, moving the nearest students away from the glass. Then smoke obscured her. He turned back to Draconia, trying to make himself believe that being out of sight was not the same as being lost.

"Your section is the other way," he said.

"I am aware."

"We're going together."

"For now. Until they clear that door." She looked at his hand still gripping the bent handle. "Come away from it. You'll be cut if it moves."

They turned towards the rear. The next impact struck beneath the train. The floor jumped. Harry hit one wall. Draconia collided with him. For one suspended instant, the wheels left the rails and the whole carriage seemed to float. Then iron screamed. The train landed hard enough to drive Harry to one knee. Green light raced along the floorboards. Every silver ward reversed.

The window lines went black. The sealed compartment doors unlocked together. Emergency lamps burst overhead. Harry's brass-button Portkey heated against his waist, then became so cold it burned. He tore open the pouch. The button had split down the centre.

"Those marks in the floor," Draconia said, staring at the green lines. "They're coming up from the rails. It looks like the grounding curse from the briefing."

Something burned briefly through the lining of Harry's bag. He snatched it away from his hip and opened the seam pocket with numb fingers. The bent Knut had gone dull; the tiny link engraved around its edge was broken. Neither Portkey could take him out. This had done more than block their use: the same pulse that blackened the train's wards had broken both little objects. Harry could not repair them, and he could not afford to discover what activating a damaged Portkey might do. The mirror remained warm against his chest, but a crack had appeared across one corner. Spells hit the windows.

Without silver light to spread the force, glass shattered inward. Harry dragged Draconia down. Shards crossed above them and embedded in the opposite wall. Wind filled the passage, carrying smoke and the hard mineral smell of curses. Compartment doors opened. Students poured into the corridor.

"Inside!" Draconia shouted.

Nobody listened. A second-year Slytherin had blood across her forehead. Two Ravenclaws pulled a crying first-year between them. Someone attempted to run forward and was knocked sideways when the train took a bend at impossible speed. Draconia backed through an open compartment doorway and climbed onto its nearest seat, putting herself above the heads filling the corridor.

"Stop! Look at me—yes, all of you. I'm your prefect. I need you to listen."

The Malfoy voice cut through panic. People stopped.

"Windows are breached. Compartments are no longer secure. Older students shield the outer side. Everyone else forms pairs and moves forward when I say. No running. If you fall, the person beside you stops."

"Where are the adults?" a boy demanded, his voice breaking on the last word.

"The escort is holding the next carriage. We'll get to them. Find someone's hand. Now."

They did. Harry looked at her. Wind tore pale hair across her face. Her badge shone above her heart. She was frightened. He could see it in the hand gripping the luggage rack and nowhere else.

She looked down at Harry. "The windows. Can you hold this side while I get them moving?"

He raised his wand. "Yes. Tell me when you're ready."

A curse entered through the broken window. He blocked it. The shield burst against his arm with enough force to numb his fingers. Harry clenched his teeth until feeling returned to his fingers. Draconia waited for his nod before helping the nearest first-year out of the compartment.

Draconia fired through the opening. Her spell snapped the binding around a masked rider's broom bristles. The loose twigs streamed behind him; he dropped sharply, struggling with the handle, until another rider caught his robes and hauled him away.

"He's falling back," she said. "Move them now, before the others come round."

Harry took the outer position. Draconia organised the line. They advanced through carriage seven beneath fire from both sides. Older students cast shields over broken windows. Harry filled gaps when someone ducked or lost their grip on a wand. He missed one spell completely; it burned a hole through the seat behind him, and for several strides he could not stop thinking about where a child had been sitting. Red light struck his shoulder and spun him into a compartment frame. Draconia hauled him upright without interrupting her count.

"Twelve. Thirteen. Fourteen. Potter, focus. Fifteen."

"I'm trying."

"Your sleeve's burning. Put it out."

Harry extinguished it with a sharp movement of his wand. The skin beneath it stung, but his fingers still moved. He looked back towards the line.

"Sixteen," Draconia continued, her voice shaking once before she steadied it. "Keep going. Both hands on your partner."

At the junction with carriage eight, behind the line they were sending forward, Anthony Goldstein appeared with his glasses cracked and two younger Ravenclaws clinging to his robes.

"The last passenger carriage is cut off!"

Draconia stopped counting.

"How?"

"They hit the coupling. The doors sealed when the wards reversed. There are at least thirty students behind it."

"Emergency brake?"

"It won't respond. One of the escort tried the manual control too."

A continuous whistle began. It did not rise and fall. It screamed without pause from the engine, a single mechanical note carrying the length of the train. Emergency stop or evacuation. The train did not slow.

"Move these students forward," Draconia ordered Anthony. "Tell every prefect you see that the rear section is isolated."

"What are you doing?"

"Getting them out. Hold these students here until the escort clears a route forward."

Harry followed her.

Anthony caught his sleeve. "Potter, they want you at the rear."

"I'm not going out to them. There are children behind that door."

He eased his sleeve from Anthony's hand. The Auror who had been covering the roof was helping an injured colleague farther forward; Harry could not see a safe way of getting her attention above the noise. He and Draconia turned into carriage eight, telling themselves they needed only to reach the locked door and pass the children through. Neither said what they would do if it refused to open. Smoke crawled along the ceiling. A luggage rack had collapsed across the corridor. Harry and Draconia levitated it together while three fourth-years dragged an injured boy from beneath.

"Can you walk?" Harry asked him.

The boy nodded too quickly.

"That means no," Draconia said.

She stretched the fallen seat canvas between two lengths of broken railing. Harry used Vincula to secure the boy to the improvised stretcher, checking that the cords lay across his hips rather than his injured leg. Two of his friends took the ends. He kept asking for his shoes until one of them promised they were under the blanket. They reached the final coupling.

The door to carriage nine had fused inside its frame. Beyond the narrow glass, Pansy Parkinson struck the other side with both fists. Millicent Bulstrode and several older students cast spells at the lock from within. None crossed the black ward line. The metal floor between carriages buckled with every movement of the train. Draconia pressed her badge to the frame.

"Carriage eight. Prefect override."

The badge flared and threw her backwards. Harry caught her.

"The override's feeding into the wrong ward," she said, rubbing the wrist that had taken the shock. "It isn't opening anything."

"Can Hermione reverse it?"

"Not from this end. The master controls are forward, near her section."

Outside, three masked riders descended alongside the coupling. One pointed a wand directly at Harry. The amplified voice reached through wind and iron.

"Harry Potter. Rear platform. Alone."

Harry looked at the sealed door. At least thirty students behind it. Attackers holding formation outside. Beyond them, the red luggage van rocked on the same damaged section of track. Draconia moved closer to Harry, keeping her body between his hand and the outer door. The voice continued.

"Come willingly, or the final carriage leaves the rails."

Inside carriage nine, Pansy heard. Her face changed. Behind Pansy, heads turned towards the window. Harry could not see everyone, but he saw a little boy mouth his surname and look for him. He could not make himself look away. He reached for the coupling handle. Draconia seized his wrist.

"No."

"They'll derail it."

"They may derail it after you surrender."

"Thirty people—"

"I know. I can see them too." Her grip tightened. "But you don't know that they'll keep their word. Please don't give them a chance to take you before we've tried anything else."

The carriage lurched. Metal screamed beneath them. Harry saw Cedric bound in the grass at the foot of a grave. Thirty students became one body, then two, then everyone he had ever watched suffer while an enemy explained that surrender would make it stop. Half a second. Gravel. No. Floorboards. Moving train. Draconia's fingers over his pulse.

"What do we do?" he asked.

Draconia looked through the glass at the plate above the coupling, then down at the scorched override beside the door. She tried the override once more. Nothing happened.

"There's a local control under that roof plate. The override's connected to it." She looked back through the glass. "If we can break the wrong connection, the main ward might take over again."

She pointed to the roof beside the coupling, directly below a raised control housing. Harry could follow the scorched line from the door into the ceiling, but he could not see what lay inside the plate. He looked back for an escort. There was only smoke, the shouting from behind the locked door, and another masked rider coming round for a clear shot.

"Back. Cover your face."

Harry crouched behind a seat. Draconia's first spell peeled the inner lining away; her second broke a hole through the metal. She flinched as the last fragments tore upwards. Wind tore into the carriage. Through the opening, open sky and black robes appeared. Draconia climbed onto the seat.

Harry stared. "No roof access."

"We are not pursuing anyone."

"We're going onto the roof."

"The control's directly above us."

"It isn't meant for students."

"I know." Her hand shook as she gripped the broken edge of the opening. "I'm not pretending this is sensible. I just can't get to it from here."

She looked back at the trapped children. Harry followed her gaze, then pressed his bag flat against his back and tightened the strap again. There was nothing sensible left about the opening above them. There was only a door that would not move. He cast a tether around the nearest intact seat support and fastened its other end to his belt. Draconia did the same, testing the cord with a hard pull. It could not make the roof safe, but it might stop a slip becoming a fall. Then Harry followed her up.

The roof was a country made entirely of wind. It struck Harry flat the instant his shoulders cleared the opening. The train thundered beneath him, scarlet metal vibrating against his chest. Fields blurred on either side. Smoke from the engine streamed overhead in a dark river. Draconia crawled towards a silver plate above the coupling. A masked rider dropped from the sky.

Harry rolled. The curse carved through the roof where his spine had been. He fired from his back. The attacker shielded, broom skidding sideways in the air. Draconia reached the plate and tore open its cover. Black-green light filled the mechanism.

"The ward is knotted into the coupling!"

"Can you break it?"

"If no one kills me for thirty seconds!"

"I'm trying! Keep your head down."

Three riders approached. Harry pushed onto one knee. His shoes slid against the curved roof. There was no rail, no stable horizon, only the engine dragging them north at reckless speed. The tether tugged at his waist when he moved too far from the opening, so he crawled back until it slackened and braced his knee against a roof seam. He cast a Shield Charm wide enough to cover Draconia. The first volley struck.

Red. Purple. White. His shield bent. Harry poured strength into it. The magic pressed down through his wand, shoulder and knees. Behind him, Draconia spoke a counter-curse in rapid Latin. The black light inside the control plate pulsed. One attacker climbed higher. Harry saw the angle.

"Down!"

He released the shield and threw himself across Draconia. The curse passed where her head had been. It struck the coupling. Iron split. The final carriage dropped back by half a foot.

Inside, students screamed. Harry caught the edge of the control plate as the roof shifted. Draconia clung to his jacket with one hand and drove her wand into the ward mechanism with the other.

"Finite!"

The light in the plate faltered. For a moment Harry thought she had done it; then the curse surged back through the connection and struck the damaged housing. Silver and green light collided. The blast lifted both of them. Harry hit the roof upon his side. His wand remained in his hand. Draconia slid towards the edge. He caught her wrist.

Her body struck the side of the train. The tether snapped taut and kept her above the wheels, but the cord was scraping against the jagged metal of the opening. Harry could see individual strands giving way. He held her wrist with both hands and pulled her towards the roof seam before it could break.

"Draco!"

She looked up. Wind tore the fear from her face and left it naked. Harry flattened himself, braced both feet against a raised vent and pulled. Pain opened through his shoulder. Draconia found the window ledge with one shoe, pushed and reached the roof with her other hand. A rider turned towards them.

Harry could not lift his wand without releasing her. The attacker's mask reflected the Dark Mark. A silver otter passed directly through his mask. The rider jerked his head aside at the unexpected light and sound. His broom followed the movement, taking his aim wide. Hermione's voice rang from the otter.

"Main release in six is responding. Your local control is clear. Door nine should open. The coupling's breaking—get everyone forward!"

The Patronus vanished. Draconia reached the roof. Harry dragged her against him. Neither let go.

"The main control," Draconia gasped. "She got that end working. Mine must have broken the local link."

Below them, the black ward line flickered silver. The sealed door opened. Students began spilling forward from carriage nine. The amplified voice outside became a shout.

"Take Potter!"

Eight riders turned towards the roof. The damaged tethers had snagged around the broken ward housing. They cut themselves free, but the nearest volley struck between them and the opening. Harry could not get Draconia across that exposed strip while the riders had a clear aim. The nearest rider came low enough for the end of his robes to strike the train. His wand moved before his broom had steadied. Harry shoved Draconia behind the raised ward housing and cast.

"Protego!"

The first curse broke upon the shield in a sheet of orange sparks. The second went through it. Harry twisted. Purple light passed beneath his arm and opened the roof from one side to the other, peeling scarlet metal back like torn paper. Heat struck his face. The train gave a deep, ugly shudder beneath them.

"They're cutting the roof apart," Draconia said. Her voice had lost its usual steadiness. "Harry, we need to get down."

"I know. Wait for them to turn—I'll cover you."

Another volley came. They flattened themselves behind the ward housing. It was no higher than Harry's forearm and had not been built with human survival in mind. Spells crossed above them, close enough to raise the hair along Harry's neck. One struck a ventilator on the rear carriage and burst it apart. Black fragments vanished into the countryside. Draconia reached past him and fired without looking.

Her spell hit the nose of a broom. The wood swelled into a knot of roots. Its rider swore as the handle writhed beneath him and dropped sharply away.

"Seven," she said.

"He's still on the broom."

"He's falling behind. That gives us a few seconds."

The other riders divided. Four climbed above the train. Three dropped level with the windows. Harry understood the shape of it a moment too late. The riders coming level with the windows were turning towards the students crossing between the carriages. Those above were keeping Harry and Draconia pinned down. Harry had been watching the curses aimed at him and nearly missed the attack beneath them.

"Inside," he said.

Draconia saw it too. She turned towards the hole they had made. The curse came from the left. Harry shouted her name. She moved, but not far enough.

The cutting curse caught her across the side. It did not throw her. It passed through cloth with a thin flash of white and left a line which seemed almost harmless for one impossible second. Then blood spread beneath her ribs. Draconia inhaled sharply.

Harry's vision narrowed. The graveyard returned in pieces: rope biting his wrists, Cedric's weight against his back, Voldemort's voice describing pain before giving it to him. The masked rider blurred into the pale face Harry remembered beneath a hood. The train roof became stone. No. Harry forced his hand against the metal. Cold. Vibrating. Moving. Train. Draconia was on one knee, alive and furious. Now. Harry turned his wand upon the rider.

"Expelliarmus!"

The spell left him like a physical thing. It broke the rider's shield, tore the wand from his hand and struck him squarely in the chest. He flew backwards from his broom. Another attacker caught the back of his robes before he reached the track, but the rescue dragged them both away from the train. Harry crawled to Draconia and pressed her own hand more firmly against the cut.

"Keep your hand there. Press hard."

"I am." Draconia tried to take another breath and winced. "Just get me off the roof."

Her voice came out too thin. The blood had already reached his knuckles. He searched for the gauze in his bag, then abandoned it when a spell struck the metal inches from his wrist. They had to get below the roof.

"Look at me," he said. "We're going down together."

She nodded once. A black-gloved hand appeared through the hole in the roof. Harry nearly cursed it.

Pansy's voice rose from below. "It's me! Don't hex me—just give me your hand!"

"Draco first," Harry said. "She's hurt."

"Get down!"

Draconia reached the opening first. Pansy and Millicent caught her by the arms and lowered her into the carriage. Harry covered them. Green light struck the ward housing and split it down the centre. Silver fire surged out. For one bright second, the whole rear half of the train became visible as lines of magic. Wards ran around each window, beneath every wheel and across the couplings like the bones of a vast animal. Black knots clung to them wherever the attackers had struck. Harry saw one moving beneath him.

Not magic. A man. He hung from the side ladder between carriages, one boot planted against the coupling while he raised his wand towards the stream of students crossing through it. Harry fired.

His spell struck the ladder, bending a rung across the attacker's wand arm. The man flinched, his aim forced away from the children. He looked up. Harry saw his own reflection in the mask. Then the train entered a cutting and stone walls rose on both sides.

The rider tore his sleeve free of the bent rung an instant before the rock took him. His broom snapped upwards. Harry ducked as robes skimmed his hair. Pansy caught the back of his jacket.

"Potter!"

She dragged. Harry dropped through the hole as a stone bridge crossed overhead. Darkness swallowed the roof. The bridge passed less than a foot above it with a roar which shook the breath from him. He landed across two seats.

"Ow."

"You are welcome," Pansy said.

They were back inside carriage eight. Harry landed on the bag strapped beneath him and felt something crack inside it, but there was no time to check. Pansy hauled him upright and pointed through the newly opened connecting doors.

"They're still coming. The little ones are at the back."

Draconia had already crossed the coupling into nine. Harry followed, keeping one hand on the rail until both feet reached its floor. The final passenger carriage looked as though a storm had been trapped inside it. Every window was broken. Curtains streamed into the corridor. Glass covered the seats and floor in bright fragments, sliding backwards whenever the train climbed. Luggage had burst from the racks. A white owl beat frantically against the wire of a crushed cage while its owner tried to hold the door straight.

The students nearest the forward exit were moving. Not quickly enough. The coupling groaned beneath their feet with each turn of the wheels. The gap between carriages widened, closed and widened again. Through it, Harry saw track passing in a grey-black blur. Draconia stood beside the opening with blood running over her hand.

"You should sit down," Harry said.

"Not until the little ones are through."

"You're bleeding, Draco."

"I know. I can feel it." She looked at the children waiting by the gap. "Help me keep them moving, then you can make me sit down."

Harry tore the field gauze from his bag. She let him press a folded pad against her side, biting her lip when his fingers found the cut. He could not tell how deep it was. He knew only that the dressing darkened as soon as it touched her.

"Keep pressure on it," he said. "Let me lift them."

She nodded towards the children. "After them."

She pointed. Two masked figures had entered through a broken window near the rear. One landed inside a compartment. The other struck the corridor, rolled and came up casting. Millicent raised a shield in front of three second-years. The curse hit hard enough to drive her through the compartment door.

Pansy screamed her name. Draconia moved before Harry could stop her. Her first spell caught the attacker at the ankle. Ice climbed his boot and pinned it to the floor. Her second shattered his mask. He was younger than Harry expected. Twenty-five, perhaps. Ordinary brown hair. A narrow face twisted by concentration. He stared at Draconia's robes and the blood beneath her hand.

"Malfoy," he said.

Recognition altered his voice. Draconia heard it. So did Harry.

"Stay away from them," she said, her wand aimed at his chest.

The man smiled.

"Your father chose badly."

He slashed his wand. Harry's Shield Charm met the curse between them. The impact burst every remaining lamp. Sparks rained across the corridor. Darkness lasted half a second.

"Stupefy!" Pansy shouted.

Red light struck the man in the shoulder. He spun, tore his foot free of the ice and sent a spell blindly towards her. Draconia changed its course. It hit the ceiling. Harry drove a silent Disarming Charm into the attacker's ribs. The man's wand flew. Millicent, climbing out of the broken doorway with blood under her nose, punched him in the face. He fell. Pansy stared at her.

"What?" Millicent demanded.

"Nothing. I just—" Pansy swallowed and looked towards the children. "Help me move him. He's blocking the passage."

The second intruder emerged from the compartment behind them. Harry turned. Draconia was faster.

"Incarcerous."

Ropes struck the man's chest, wound around his arms and tightened. He cut through the first coil. Harry added another. Pansy stunned him. Millicent caught him before he fell into a group of younger students and let him strike the floor with only a little more force than necessary.

"Both alive," Harry said.

Pansy swallowed. "Fine. Where do we put them?"

The trolley Auror appeared at the forward doorway with another member of the escort behind her. Blood had soaked the second Auror's sleeve, but both had their wands raised. Harry pointed to the prisoners. Millicent dragged the nearest wand away with her shoe.

"Wands and Portkeys off them," the Auror ordered her colleague. "Take them through. I'll hold the windows."

The second Auror bound the men's arms against their bodies and levitated them across the coupling one at a time, keeping them low enough to pass beneath the door frame. Harry waited until the second pair of boots cleared the threshold. Nobody was going to be left in here, however much he hated them. The coupling screamed. Everyone looked forward. A bolt the width of Harry's wrist tore loose and vanished beneath the train. The floor dropped. Screams filled the carriage.

"Go!" Harry shouted.

The orderly line collapsed into movement. Older students shoved trunks aside and lifted younger ones across the bucking threshold. The forward carriage pulled away, returned and slammed close enough to crush fingers. Draconia braced one hand against the wall and used the other to cast a Cushioning Charm across the gap. Her spell flickered. Blood had reached the waist of her skirt.

Harry caught a first-year beneath the arms and passed him to Anthony Goldstein on the other side. A girl followed. Then an owl cage. Then another child, this one crying so hard he could not release Harry's neck.

"I need you to let go," Harry said.

The boy tightened his grip.

"What's your name?"

The boy shook his head against Harry's collar. Harry could feel his teeth chattering through the fabric.

"You don't have to tell me. See the boy with the glasses? Anthony. He's going to hold you next. Look at him."

Anthony crouched at the far edge and put his wand away so that both hands were visible.

"I've got you," Harry said. "One hand off my neck. Just one."

The boy's fingers dug harder for a moment, then loosened. Harry kept his arm beneath him while Anthony took his other hand. They moved him across between them; he began crying properly only when his feet reached the next carriage. Harry waited to hear Anthony say he had him before letting go. Outside, the riders regrouped. Spells entered through the windows. Pansy shielded the crossing beside the trolley Auror. Millicent worked her way down the compartments, checking under seats and inside the lavatory while Draconia continued counting.

"Twenty-two. Twenty-three."

"You are done," Harry told her.

"Twenty-four."

"That's not an answer."

"Twenty-five."

The train lurched left. Draconia's knees folded. Harry caught her against his chest. For once she did not immediately push him away. Her forehead rested beneath his chin. He could feel the rapid, shallow pattern of her breathing.

"I am all right," she said.

"You can stop saying that."

Harry kept his palm over the dressing. Her breathing warmed the hollow beneath his chin. She did not feel like the girl who had arranged herself so neatly against his shoulder during Snap; she felt cold through her clothes, and he wanted everyone else out of the carriage so that he could concentrate on her without feeling guilty about it.

"Don't let go yet," she whispered.

"Wasn't planning to."

Pansy looked at them from behind her shield. "Potter, when you move her, support the other side. You're pulling the cut."

Harry adjusted his arm immediately. Draconia made a small sound into his jacket, then lifted her head. He waited for her eyes to focus before helping her straighten. Then the next curse struck Pansy's shield. The moment vanished.

"Twenty-six," Draconia said, and forced herself upright.

Harry counted seven students still waiting, besides the four of them. The trolley Auror moved forward to receive them on the steadier side of the coupling. Harry saw Cedric on the other side of the coupling. He had lost his school cloak. One sleeve of his jumper was torn, and soot darkened half his face. He carried an emergency broom beneath one arm.

"The corridor ahead is blocked," Cedric shouted. "The roof came down at the back of six, right across the connecting doors. Hermione's keeping the students clear, but they can't get through."

"Then why have you got a broom?" Harry asked.

Cedric looked through the broken window. The countryside ran beside them at murderous speed.

"Getting them out through the windows. The escort's covering the field side."

Harry stared.

"That is my sort of bad idea."

"You were busy."

Cedric kicked open the rear door of carriage eight. Wind flooded the connecting platform. Cho Chang appeared behind him with the second emergency broom, her braid almost entirely undone. Ginny came after her carrying two battered, privately owned brooms.

"Those two came from six," Ginny said. "The van's cargo ward released when the main control came back. I Summoned these before the rear doors stuck again. Anthony's and mine."

She looked towards Harry. "Luna took the owls forward. Neville's with Hermione. They're all right."

Harry had forgotten to ask. The shame of it lasted until another scream came through the smoke.

"Four will do for another run," Cedric said. "The escort's keeping the riders off that side while we cross."

"For what?" Pansy demanded.

"There are eighteen still trapped in six. The windows on the field side are still usable. We fly them forward to four; the Auror there has cleared a landing window."

"While Death Eaters are attempting to remove us from the train?"

Cedric glanced at the masked riders.

"An Auror's already gone out to cover us. We wait for her signal, keep below the windows and cross one carriage at a time." He looked at Cho. "We've done the first run. We can do another."

Harry smiled despite everything.

"You've already brought some out?"

"Three. They're with one of the medical attendants in four now." Cedric looked back towards the smoke. "We're going back for the rest."

Cho mounted her broom on the open platform.

"I need a shield on both sides."

Pansy looked at the failing coupling, the last students and Draconia's wound.

"I can hold this side."

Millicent stepped beside her.

"So can I."

Draconia turned towards Ginny.

"Give me one."

Harry tightened his arm around her.

"No."

"Do not begin."

"You're going to faint standing still."

"I'm still standing."

"Because I'm holding you."

Draconia looked down at his arm around her. For once she had no quick answer. "Then keep holding me."

Ginny held the broom beyond Draconia's reach.

"I'm with Harry."

Draconia looked at the broom, then at the blood soaking her dressing. Ginny lowered her voice.

"Let us do it. You've got them this far."

The final first-year crossed. Only Pansy, Millicent, Harry and Draconia remained in carriage nine. The coupling shifted again. This time it did not return. The gap opened to three feet. The rear carriage began to fall away.

"Jump!" Cedric shouted.

Millicent went first. Anthony and Ginny caught her. Pansy followed, striking the opposite wall shoulder-first. Harry moved Draconia towards the edge. Her knees shook when she tried to brace herself for the jump. She caught his sleeve.

"I can't make that on my own."

The carriage dropped farther back. Four feet. Five. Harry could no longer reach the other side without a running start, and there was no room to take one. The line beneath the carriage sparked. Metal tore. Cedric threw the broom. Harry caught it with one hand.

"Get on," he said.

Draconia swung one leg over. Harry climbed behind her, wrapped his free arm above her wound and kicked away from the dying carriage. The coupling broke. Carriage nine fell backwards. Its wheels jumped from the rail. The luggage van followed, dragged sideways by the rear coupling. Scarlet wood tilted, struck the gravel and broke open in a storm of timber and sparks.

Harry looked back at the windows. Millicent had checked every compartment. The two prisoners had gone forward with the escort. He repeated those facts to himself as the carriage rolled, because some part of him still expected to see a hand reaching out of it. The broom dropped beneath the train's slipstream. Draconia seized the handle. Harry fought it upwards. Wheels thundered beside them, close enough for the spokes to become solid circles. Gravel struck his face. The ground seemed near enough to touch and moved too quickly to exist.

"Pull!" Draconia shouted.

"I am pulling!"

"Left! The wheel—"

"I see it!"

The broom answered both of them. It climbed. They rose beside carriage eight. Ginny leaned from the open platform with one hand extended. Cedric held the back of her robes. Harry pushed Draconia ahead.

Ginny caught her wrist. Cedric pulled them both through. Harry cast a Braking Charm and let go of the broom before following; it hovered against the handrail instead of continuing into the wheels. He struck the platform with his knees and nearly went out the other side. Cho's Shield Charm caught him in midair and dropped him onto the floor. For half a breath, nobody moved. Then Harry rolled towards Draconia.

"Still here?"

She opened her eyes.

"Still here," she said. "Don't ask me to do that again."

"Wasn't planning to."

He meant it too plainly. Her expression softened.

"Harry—"

An explosion tore through the carriage ahead. The train lurched. Cho grabbed her broom. Cedric released the hovering emergency broom from Harry's Braking Charm. Ginny passed Anthony his own and mounted the fourth.

"We're out of time," Cedric said.

He looked at Harry. Not because Harry's face had been in the papers. Not because an audience waited or a camera had turned towards them. Because they had stood in a graveyard together and both knew what happened when one person tried to carry everyone.

"Get her into seven, away from this end," Cedric said. "Susan's trying to get a healer through from three. We'll get Hermione forward."

Harry looked down at Draconia's blood. Then forward, where smoke filled the next carriage and Hermione was somewhere inside it. Draconia read the choice before he made it.

"No," she said.

"You're hurt."

"And Granger's still behind that roof. Take me forward. I don't want to be here on my own."

"Draco—"

"Take me to her."

Harry heard the fear beneath it. He looked for the trolley Auror, but she was moving the last children through seven, crouched below the windows with her wand raised. The Auror saw the dressing and pointed them forward. "Into seven. Tell Bones she's losing blood. Keep pressure on that cut—I've got these children."

Harry nodded. They could wait for help in seven, away from the failing rear coupling. If the route through six opened, they could keep going towards the reinforced medical compartment. He checked the pad, tightened the strip of gauze holding it and helped her stand. First, he had to get her out of here.

"You lean strategically," he said. "I walk."

Her arm went around his shoulders.

"Don't go too fast," she said.

"Tell me if I am."

Behind them, Cedric, Cho, Ginny and Anthony climbed into the open air. Ahead, the train burned. They crossed carriage eight through a current of terrified students. The corridor had become a narrow river flowing towards the engine. First-years clutched sleeves, pets and one another. Older students moved along the outside edge with their wands raised. Prefects stood at each connecting door and counted people through, their badges flashing whenever the damaged wards recognised them.

Harry and Draconia edged along the wall, moving more slowly than the students around them. The smoke thickened. It came from carriage six, rolling along the ceiling in dark layers. Beneath it, emergency lamps painted every face red. The smell was wrong for ordinary fire. Bitter. Metallic. It caught at the back of Harry's throat and made his scar throb.

"Don't breathe deeply," he said.

"I'm trying." She coughed into her sleeve and tightened her grip on him. "It's getting worse."

"Can you keep walking?"

"For now."

He shortened his steps to match hers. The train tilted into another turn. Harry caught the wall with his shoulder. Draconia's weight pulled against him. Her shoes slipped once on broken glass, then found the floor again. At the forward door, Ernie Macmillan stood with Susan Bones. Together, they were directing younger students into the safer compartments in seven while an older Ravenclaw sealed broken windows with sheets torn from the seat coverings. The way through six was blocked. Ernie saw the blood.

"Hospital compartment is in three."

"Hermione," Harry said. "Where is she?"

Susan pointed through the smoke. "Other side of the collapse."

"Is she hurt?"

"Not when we last saw her."

Not when. The words lodged beneath Harry's ribs. Draconia felt him quicken.

"Do not run," she said.

"I wasn't."

"You were about to. I can't keep up if you do."

Ernie blocked the doorway.

"You can't get through. Part of the roof came down. Hermione and Padma were opening the windows. Another strike bent the frames. They're running out of ways through."

"Let me see," Harry said.

Ernie looked at Draconia. "You need to keep going to three."

"I know. Let me see first."

Susan moved aside just far enough for him to look. The middle of carriage seven had been cleared. Students crouched in its compartments, leaving the corridor free for anyone who could reopen the forward route. Smoke reduced it to a tunnel of red light and dark outlines. Flames climbed the wall at the far end without consuming the wood, green at their base and colourless at their tips.

"Cursed fire," Draconia said.

"Can we put it out?"

"Not with water. It's feeding on the damaged ward."

Harry tried a Suppression Charm. The flame shrank from the end of his wand, then returned as soon as he stopped.

"The escort has to isolate that section," Draconia said. "Stay away from it."

Twenty feet ahead, the rear roof of six had folded through the connecting doorway. Its torn edge projected into seven. It rested diagonally across the carriage, a mass of metal, timber and crushed luggage extending from floor to ceiling. Through narrow gaps, Harry could see movement. Hands. Robes. Someone lifting a small child towards an open window. Hermione's voice carried through the wreckage.

"One at a time! Keep hold of the frame until the rider has you."

Harry stopped breathing. Draconia sagged against him. Relief had weakened her more than fear.

"Granger!" she shouted.

Silence answered.

Then, "Draco?"

Hermione appeared at one of the gaps. Soot covered her face. Her hair had escaped entirely and seemed to occupy most of the opening. Blood ran from a cut at her temple, but she was standing. Harry's body unlocked one part at a time. Hermione saw him. Her eyes moved over his face, both arms, his chest and the hand holding Draconia upright. Then she saw the blood.

"What happened?"

"A cutting curse caught her on the roof," Harry said.

Hermione stared at him. "The roof?"

"Later," Draconia whispered. "Please, Granger. Later."

"How deep?"

"I couldn't see properly," Harry admitted. "I've kept a pad on it, but it won't stop bleeding."

Hermione's gaze dropped to his stained hand. "All right. Don't take the dressing away yet."

Hermione pushed one arm through a slit beside the bent door frame. The gap brought her face close enough to see, but a jagged strip of metal kept them apart. Draconia lifted her free hand. Their fingertips could not touch.

"I'm here," Draconia said.

Hermione shut her eyes for half a second.

"So am I."

Harry watched the two of them find steadiness in the sound of each other's voices. Then Hermione opened her eyes.

"Cedric's taken seven. There are fourteen still here, plus me. The window frames shifted after the last strike. Cho can't get anyone else out until we clear them."

"And you?" Harry asked.

"I can climb through after them."

Draconia examined the wreckage. "No, you cannot."

"I beg your pardon?"

"The support above your left shoulder is carrying the roof. If you move beneath it, the weight shifts into the corridor."

Hermione glanced up.

"I know that."

"Then don't go under it." Draconia's voice rose, and she had to stop for breath. "Please, Granger. Not while it's moving."

"It's the only opening left." Hermione looked back at the students. "They can't stay in here."

Harry lowered Draconia carefully onto a seat.

"Not anymore."

Hermione recognised his expression.

"Harry, no."

"You don't know what I'm doing."

"Neither do you. That is the problem."

He pressed his palm against the wreckage. Metal vibrated beneath it. Not only from the train. The collapsed roof had its own strained movement, every piece holding another piece in place. Breaking it apart would bring the rest down. Lifting all of it was impossible. Changing it might not be.

McGonagall had made them practise on buttons, beetles and little squares of wood, correcting every uneven edge before she allowed them to try something larger. Harry could hear her insisting that wanting a thing changed was not the same as knowing what to change it into.

He took his hand off the wreckage and looked properly. One thin sheet of metal hung between two heavier braces. It was bent, but it was not carrying the roof. If he could change that sheet without touching either brace, there might be room to crawl through.

"I'm going to make a door," he said.

Hermione stared through the gap. "Out of what?"

"That thin sheet between the braces. If I soften it, we can roll it back."

Hermione looked along his wand. "Wait. Show me exactly which piece you mean."

Draconia looked at the diagonal beam. "The centre panel only. Leave the supports unchanged."

Hermione followed her gaze. "That sheet isn't supporting anything," she said. "But the brace above it is. Draco, hold the near end. I'll hold this one."

Draconia raised her wand from the seat. Her first attempt shook; Harry waited while she steadied it against her knee. Hermione spoke the incantation again, slowly, and Draconia matched her movement until both braces stopped shifting.

"Just the sheet, Potter. Not the frame."

"I am still here."

"I noticed. What can you make it into?"

Harry thought of the little leather wallet he had produced from a wooden box during revision. It had taken him four attempts. He remembered the failed seams as clearly as the finished one.

"Leather," he said. "Plain leather."

Hermione nodded. "We hold the weight. You make an opening."

Harry looked from one to the other.

"May I save you now?"

Hermione's mouth almost smiled. "Centre panel. Only enough to crawl through. Leave the upper brace alone."

Harry nodded. "Tell me if it starts to move."

"I will. Don't rush it."

Harry placed his wand against the scorched metal. He pictured the thin sheet separately from the wood and steel holding it in place. Only that sheet. Hermione steadied the far brace with the spell she had shown Draconia. Harry used the painted edge of the thin sheet to guide his wand, keeping its tip well clear of both supports. Harry imagined the smooth leather of the wallet, the way it folded beneath his thumb. He tried to leave everything outside the marked square alone.

"Permuto corium."

The spell entered the wreckage. Nothing happened.

"Is it changing?" Draconia asked, struggling to see past his arm.

"Not yet. Hold it there. I'll try again."

Harry tried again. This time, the metal softened. Scarlet paint darkened to brown. Jagged edges drew together. The thin metal sheet folded into leather while the outer beams remained fixed. It sagged towards Harry. Above it, the brace creaked against Hermione's charm, and she tightened her grip on her wand.

"It's through!" Hermione called. "Don't change any more—roll it towards you."

Harry caught the softened edge and pulled, grateful for an instruction he could follow without thinking about the weight above them. Together, she and Harry rolled the leather panel upwards. A passage opened. Smoke poured through. Draconia stood. Harry pointed at the seat.

"Do not."

"You require help holding it."

"Keep your wand on that brace. Please."

Roger Davies moved to Hermione's side of the opening and took over her bracing charm before she crawled through. Harry waited until his light held steady, then lifted the leather high enough for her to pass. The moment she reached them, she caught Draconia by both shoulders.

"Let me see."

"There are still students—"

"Harry is holding the opening. Let me see."

Something in Hermione's voice ended the argument. Draconia lifted her torn shirt enough to expose the wound. The curse had opened a clean line beneath her ribs. It was not as deep as the amount of blood suggested, but its edges glimmered faintly black. Hermione went still.

"It is cursed."

"Can you stop it?" Draconia asked.

Hermione looked at the dark edge again. "Not properly. I can change the dressing and keep pressure on it until we reach a healer."

"Then do that." Draconia lowered her voice. "I'm not asking you to fix everything."

Hermione pulled a roll of bandage from the emergency pouch at her belt. Harry glanced over.

"You've got more gauze? Mine's nearly gone."

"In the pouch Sirius gave us." Hermione pulled it open with her teeth, then looked up. "Hold the panel. I've got this."

She replaced Harry's blood-soaked pad and wrapped Draconia's side with hands which remained steady until they passed behind her back. There, where Draconia could not see, Harry watched her fingers tremble. He looked away, then raised the leather a little higher so she could see the other side.

With the bandage in place, Hermione took up her wand and aimed through the opening. "Roger, I've got your brace. Help Padma through."

She waited until their spells overlapped before he lowered his wand. The remaining students crawled through: four first-years, then three second-years. Roger brought Padma, supporting her injured ankle while she slid beneath the leather. A Hufflepuff girl with burns along one sleeve followed them, then two Slytherins helping a small Ravenclaw boy who had refused to release his kneazle.

Harry held the transfigured panel while each passed. The weight increased. His arm began to shake.

"Last one!" Hermione called.

The pale purple flower appeared beneath the leather, followed by the plant's lumpy grey-green body. Neville's plant floated through first, guided by a Levitation Charm. Neville crawled after it. Harry stared.

"You saved the plant?"

"I had it with me when the roof came down. I'm not leaving it here."

The Mimbulus mimbletonia inflated furiously.

"It looks furious."

"That is how it expresses fear."

Harry understood that better than he wanted to. Neville dragged the pot clear. The wreckage shifted.

"Drop it!" Hermione shouted.

Harry released the panel as Hermione and Draconia let their bracing charms go. The leather slammed down. The entire collapsed section settled another foot, crushing the passage closed. They had everyone. Harry counted the last two heads again before letting himself believe it.

Then the rear connecting door opened. Three masked figures entered from carriage eight. Harry could see shattered compartment doors behind them and no sign of the escort who had held that passage earlier. He moved between the men and the students gathered at the blocked forward end of seven. Ernie and Susan pushed the children into the nearest intact compartments, then raised shields across the doorways. Harry caught Susan's eye; she nodded once, and he knew the students had someone beside them if he could no longer hold the corridor. One of the attackers carried a short silver object in his left hand. A ward spike. He drove it into the wall.

The silver lines around carriage seven turned black. Both doors slammed shut.

"Behind us," Hermione ordered.

The students moved. She did not look to see whether they obeyed. She stepped beside Harry and raised her wand. Draconia took the other side, one hand pressed to the new bandage. The three of them faced the corridor while Ernie and Susan held the children back. Ahead, the attackers separated, each choosing a different angle. For a strange second, the carriage became quiet enough for Harry to hear glass moving across the floor. The centre attacker tilted his head.

"Potter."

"I've been called worse."

"Come with us, and the children walk away."

Harry looked at the black door behind them.

"Open the door," Harry said. "Let them go first."

The man's wand lifted.

"Last opportunity."

"Then you're not offering them anything."

Hermione's shoulder touched Harry's. Draconia's sleeve brushed his other arm. Neither told him what to choose. They had already chosen with him.

"No," Harry said.

The carriage erupted. Hermione cast first. Her spell struck an overturned seat between the attackers. It slammed sideways across their path. The men divided, two going left and one right. Draconia caught the right-hand attacker with a Full Body-Bind before he landed.

Harry shielded the students. The remaining two fired together. Hermione deflected one curse into an empty compartment. It struck a cushion, filling the doorway with blue feathers. Harry blocked the second. It struck his shield, split and travelled along both walls, cutting every lamp in half. Darkness fell.

Wands illuminated it in fragments. Red light. White. Green reflected against smoke. Harry ducked a curse he could not identify and fired beneath it. His spell struck a mask, cracking it over one eye. Draconia bound the attacker's wand arm to his side. Hermione swept his legs from beneath him. The third man broke Draconia's Body-Bind. He rose behind a seat. Harry saw the wand turn towards the children.

"Protego maxima!"

His shield filled the width of the carriage. The curse hit. The air compressed. Every window blew outwards.

Harry slid six feet across the floor. His heels struck a seat bracket. Pain climbed both legs. The shield held. Keeping the shield between the attackers and the children, Harry left a narrow opening beside the compartment wall. Hermione fired around its edge. Three ropes sprang from her wand, caught the attacker around the chest and pinned him to the ceiling. Draconia turned the loose edge of his mask into a metal clasp that fastened to the luggage rail beside his head, trapping him in place without covering his mouth.

"Two," Hermione said.

"One and a half," Harry corrected, looking at the man fighting the binding around his arm.

"I was rounding."

The ward spike pulsed. Black light raced beneath their feet. The bound attacker smiled beneath the cracked edge of his mask. Harry felt the trap close.

"Down!"

The floor discharged. Dark magic climbed through the carriage in branching lines. It entered shoes, hands and every piece of metal. Harry's muscles locked. Hermione cried out beside him. Draconia struck the wall so hard her wand left her hand. The pulse passed, leaving his legs trembling. The shield vanished before he could steady his wand.

The first attacker tore free. He did not aim at Harry. He aimed at Hermione. Draconia saw it from the floor. She threw herself across the space between them. The Stunner struck her in the back. Her body went rigid. She fell against Hermione, taking them both to the floor. Harry heard the impact.

Everything inside him became still. He heard the girls strike the floor, then the small scrape of the attacker's boot as he stepped closer. For a moment he could not feel his own hands. He knew he ought to check Draconia's breathing, but the man was reaching past her towards Hermione. The attacker stepped over Draconia.

"One of them will make you cooperate."

He reached for Hermione. Harry moved. His legs still shook from the grounding pulse. He nearly fell on the first step, caught the back of a seat and used it to haul himself between the man and Hermione. The man's wand touched Harry's throat.

"There you are."

Harry smiled.

"Get away from her."

He drove his forehead into the cracked mask. Bone broke. The man staggered. Harry caught his wand wrist with both hands, turned and slammed it into the luggage rack. Once. Twice. The wand fell. Someone shouted behind him. Harry did not hear the words. He raised his own wand.

"Expelliarmus!"

The man was already unarmed. The spell lifted him from the floor anyway. He struck the roof. Metal bent around his body. When he fell, Harry hit him again.

"Harry!" Hermione shouted.

The second spell threw the attacker through a compartment wall. Wood burst. He disappeared beneath it. The man bound to the ceiling cut through the ropes and tore the straps of his mask away from the clasp holding him to the rail. Harry turned. The attacker hesitated. For the first time since the siege began, one of them looked afraid. Good.

Harry's magic filled the carriage. He did not cast. He did not need to. Loose glass rose from the floor. Curtains lifted against the direction of the wind. The broken lamps swung towards him as if the whole train had become a compass and Harry was north. His scar burned. Beneath the noise of the carriage, Harry thought he heard laughter. He could not tell whether the old connection had opened or he was remembering the graveyard too vividly; either way, the urge that came with it was familiar and frightening.

Show them.

Harry pointed his wand. The attacker raised both hands.

"Potter—"

"You wanted me."

"Harry."

Hermione's voice reached him from the floor. He had moved farther from her than he meant to, and he could not remember deciding to take those steps.

He saw Draconia motionless upon the floor and blood at Hermione's temple. Then the real faces blurred with images he knew from the graveyard: Cedric bound in the grass, people he loved lying still because Voldemort wanted him to see them that way. For a moment he imagined Sirius beside a wrecked motorbike, although he had not seen Sirius since the platform. He could not make the picture leave.

Show them what happens.

Harry's wand trembled.

"Move away from them," he said.

The man did. One step. Harry advanced. The train rocked beneath them, but he no longer felt it.

"Harry, he's surrendering."

"He came onto a school train."

"I know."

"He used her."

"I know."

Hermione's voice broke on the second word. Harry looked at her. She had pushed Draconia onto her side and was holding two fingers beneath her jaw. Fear filled her face, but her wand remained trained upon the attacker.

"She's breathing," Hermione said. "Harry, Draco is breathing."

The carriage door behind the attacker exploded inward. Harry spun. Red light came through the smoke. It struck Hermione. Her head snapped backwards. She collapsed beside Draconia. Something in Harry broke. Not loudly.

Not cleanly. It simply ceased to hold. He cast before the new attackers entered. The broken door burst down the rear corridor, struck the first masked figure and carried him into the wall. The glass suspended around Harry shivered and shot towards the open doorway. Several shards struck masks; the rest scattered across the passage in a bright, uncontrolled spray. Harry heard a scream, but could not tell who had made it.

Shouts answered. Harry followed the broken door. For those few seconds, he stopped looking towards the students behind him. He stopped checking where his spells would go beyond the men in front of him. Susan shouted from a compartment doorway, but he did not turn. Behind him, a child screamed, and he still did not look back.

He remembered only two girls on the floor and the fact that the train still contained people responsible. He followed them through the rear doorway into carriage eight. The corridor held four attackers. Harry gave them no time to form a line. His first curse turned the floor beneath them into ice. His second tore away a splintered window frame beside the nearest attacker. The remaining glass burst into the field, leaving Harry a clear view of the man's wand hand. Wind punched through the carriage. Two attackers fell. A third caught a brass rail and raised his wand. Harry struck his wand hand with a Disarming Charm. The man released the rail with a cry.

"Confringo!"

The wall beside Harry exploded. He walked through the fragments. Pain struck his cheek. Warm blood crossed his mouth. He tasted iron and remembered somebody else's laughter. The attacker at the far end fired green. Harry dropped. The Killing Curse passed over him and struck the carriage behind. A first-year screamed. Harry's rage changed shape. Cold became heat.

"Reducto!"

The attacker's shield shattered.

"Expelliarmus!"

His wand disappeared through the open window.

"Incarcerous!"

Ropes wrapped around his upper arms, chest and knees. Harry jerked his wand sideways. The man hit the wall and remained there, suspended above the floor. Another attacker rose behind him. Harry did not see him.

"Left!"

Harry ducked because the voice sounded certain. A red curse passed over his head.

"Stupefy!"

The answering spell came from behind Harry and struck the attacker squarely in the mask. He fell. Ron Weasley stepped through the smoke from the forward doorway, coming out of carriage seven. Harry had last seen him returning to his patrol, before the whistle; he had no idea how Ron had got here, only that somebody was finally looking at the same stretch of corridor he was.

His prefect robes had been torn from shoulder to hem. Blood ran from one nostril. He held his wand in his right hand and a broken broomstick in his left. Harry stared at him.

"You took your time."

Ron glanced at the wreckage.

"Got our section to four. An Auror brought me back round the outside to seven—then someone hit the broom." He glanced down at the broken handle. "He got me through the window. Bones said you'd gone after them. I checked the girls, then came to find you."

His attempt at a grin was crooked. Harry looked at the split handle and, for the first time since leaving seven, noticed how badly his own legs were shaking. Two masked figures entered through the windows. Ron threw the broken broomstick. One attacker blocked it.

Harry used the opening. His spell struck the man's knee. Ron's caught his chest. The attacker folded between them and crashed into a seat. The second landed behind Ron. Harry shouted.

Ron dropped flat without looking. Harry's curse passed over him and caught the rider before both boots reached the floor. Ron rolled, rose and placed his back against Harry's.

"How many?" he asked.

Harry counted movement in the smoke.

"Too many."

"Useful."

"Three standing. More coming through the windows."

"Better."

Spells came from both ends of the carriage. Harry shielded left. Ron shielded right. The impacts drove them together. Shoulder to shoulder. Back to back. Neither moved.

"Three on your side," Ron said. "One's favouring his right leg."

"You can see that?"

"He keeps putting his weight on the other foot." Ron swallowed. "You take the one by the door. I'll watch him."

Ron broke his shield, fired low and restored it before the next curse arrived. Harry heard someone fall behind him.

"Two," Ron said.

Harry smiled. It felt sharp.

"Show-off."

"Draconia makes it look easier."

The attackers advanced. Ron counted under his breath, loud enough for Harry to hear the rhythm. Harry glanced over his shoulder and nodded; neither could hold a full shield and aim accurately for much longer.

"Three. Two. Now."

Both boys dropped their shields. Harry struck high. Ron struck low. The spells crossed, one tearing a wand away and the other taking the attacker's feet. Harry turned before the man hit the ground and caught the next curse against a shield no wider than his hand.

"Behind," Ron said.

Harry pivoted.

"High," Harry replied.

Ron ducked. Harry disarmed the man above him. Ron rose beneath the falling wand, caught it and used it to stun its owner. Ron flinched as the unfamiliar wand kicked in his hand, then tucked it into his belt and raised his own again.

"Taking that off him," he said breathlessly. "Before he gets any ideas."

Harry checked the fallen man's empty hands and turned back to the door. The carriage shook. Outside, a broom struck the roof and scraped along it. Somewhere ahead, the whistle changed from its continuous scream to three short blasts. Harry did not know what they meant, but Ron went pale.

"Bridge!"

"What?"

"We reach a bridge in less than a minute. They hit the rails ahead."

"How do you know?"

"Driver sent a Patronus to the escort. The brakes aren't responding, and the engine wards are pulling us faster. They've laid the same curse across the bridge approach."

The last standing attacker began to retreat. Harry followed. Ron caught the back of his jacket.

"No."

"He'll get away."

"The girls are behind us."

Harry stopped. The words reached him when nothing else had. Hermione and Draconia lay in carriage seven behind them. Younger students remained under attack. The man at the end of the corridor was not the whole war, however much Harry wanted him to be. Ron looked at him. Not frightened of Harry. Frightened for him.

"They're alive," Ron said. "I checked before I followed you. Bones saw Granger take a Stunner. Draco's breathing."

Harry swallowed. The attacker reached the door.

"Let him go," Ron said.

Harry hated him for being right. For approximately one second.

"I could still hex you," Harry said.

"Queue forms on the left."

Harry lowered his wand. The masked man vanished through the rear exit. Harry heard a broom scrape the platform beyond, then saw a black shape drop away from the window. Then a motorcycle flew past the windows. Harry blinked.

It moved alongside the train thirty feet above the ground, black wheels spinning uselessly through the air. A tall figure leaned over the handlebars. Dark hair streamed behind him. His coat snapped like a flag. Sirius looked through the broken window. He saw Harry. Relief crossed his face so violently that it resembled pain.

Then he saw the masked rider descending behind the carriage. Sirius turned the motorbike into him. The rider saw him at the last instant. His broom climbed. Sirius climbed with him.

The motorbike struck the broom from beneath and broke it cleanly through the centre. Its sidecar—the one Sirius had spent the last week fitting for an emergency passenger—swung level with the falling rider. The man rolled across it, lost his mask and clung to the metal rim with both hands. Sirius looked down at the white face beside his knee.

"Hands where I can see them!"

The man reached inside his robes. Sirius caught his wrist and struck him once; his grip on the rim broke. A second rider dived and caught him by the robes before he reached the ground. Both vanished beneath the train's smoke. Sirius brought the motorbike level with Harry's window.

"Kingsley confirmed your alert! Status?"

"Rear carriage gone. Students clear. Two down behind us. Hermione and Draconia are hurt."

Sirius's face changed at their names, but he did not look away from the sky.

"Cedric?"

"Outside. Evacuating six."

"Of course he is."

Ron leaned towards the window. "Bridge in less than a minute!"

Sirius swore. He twisted one handle. The motorbike surged forward, but a wall of green light appeared across the tracks half a mile ahead. It ran from field to field, cutting through hedges and trees without touching them. Where it crossed the railway, six black shapes stood fixed between the sleepers. Ward spikes.

The same silver instruments the attackers had used inside the train, driven into the earth and joined by coils of sick green magic. Beyond them, the rails climbed onto a stone bridge over a river. The train whistle gave three more blasts. No brakes. No room to stop. Harry looked ahead through the broken carriages. The engine was hidden by smoke, but he could feel it pulling harder, gathering speed instead of losing it.

"Why are we accelerating?" he shouted.

Sirius looked towards the rails.

"He's trying not to," Sirius shouted. "That thing's pulling on the engine wards. The brake linkage is still cursed."

Harry looked at the uninterrupted sparks beneath the wheels and understood why the whistle had not stopped. Four broom riders came over the trees. For half a second, Harry thought the attackers had reached the bridge first. Then the leading figure sent a silver lynx across the sky. Kingsley's voice boomed over the train.

"Inside escort, brace all students. External team, break the anchors."

Tonks flew behind him with her hair a fierce electric blue. Two Aurors followed, one carrying a long pole with a hooked end. They dived towards the first spike. Masked riders intercepted them. The sky above the bridge filled with curses. Sirius accelerated towards it.

"Get everyone down!"

"You're going forward?" Harry asked.

"Kingsley needs help at the anchors. Stay with Ron. Get the others down."

"Sirius—"

The motorbike pulled ahead. Sirius looked back. For an instant, the siege vanished from his face and Harry saw the man who had stood beside a bedroom door at two in the morning because Harry's lamp was still on.

"I'm coming back," Sirius said.

Then he was gone. Harry stared after him. The words reached him through the engine, the wind and every promise which had ever failed. He chose to believe them.

"We need the students on the floor," Ron said.

Harry turned. Ron had already begun. He moved along the corridor kicking compartment doors open and ordering everyone beneath the level of the windows. Harry ran back towards carriage seven. Hermione and Draconia remained where he had left them.

Draconia lay on her side, hair across her face. Hermione was sprawled beside her with one arm trapped beneath her body. Neither moved when the train took another bend. The stillness hit Harry harder than the attack had. He dropped between them.

"Hermione."

He touched her throat. Pulse. Fast, but present. He placed his other hand against Draconia's neck. Pulse. Two lives beneath his fingers. Harry bowed his head once. Then the floor began to glow. The ward spike remained embedded in the wall. Black light crawled outward from it, thickening as the train approached the barrier. Harry pointed his wand.

"Reducto!"

The spell struck the spike and rebounded. It hit the roof, leaving a smoking crater.

"Resistant," Ron said behind him.

Harry looked around. Ron stood in the doorway. His expression altered when he saw the girls properly.

"You take Draco," he said. "Keep her on her good side. I'll lift Hermione."

He crossed the carriage, crouched beside Hermione and slid one arm beneath her shoulders. Harry stared.

"Can you lift her?"

"Yeah. Help me get her arm free first." Ron knelt beside Hermione and shifted his grip. "I've carried Fred home before. He was less cooperative."

The attempt at a joke failed halfway out. Ron crouched lower, supporting Hermione's head before he moved her. "You take Draco. I'll get her."

He lifted Hermione with an awkwardness which did not make him less careful. Her head rested against his shoulder. Harry gathered Draconia. She weighed less than he expected. He hated that. One arm went beneath her knees, the other around her back. Blood warmed his sleeve immediately. Her head fell against his chest.

"This does not count as strategic leaning," he told her.

Her eyes did not open. They moved into an intact compartment a few steps from the collapsed roof, where they could brace against the seat rather than stand exposed in the corridor. There was no clear route beyond the wreckage into six. The bridge filled the forward windows. Stone arches. Black water. A wall of green light across the approach. The train entered it.

Every remaining window became green. The carriage charms collapsed. The small light at the tip of Harry's wand flickered and went out. His holster loosened. The Featherlight Charm upon the luggage failed, and trunks crashed from racks throughout the train. Ahead, from the medical compartment where Luna had taken the cages, Hedwig screamed. Outside, a broom lurched as its rider skimmed the green field. The curse was strongest along the rails. The train's charms failed first, then the smaller enchantments carried inside it began to falter. The flying team climbed away from that narrow band, their brooms steadying as they gained height.

The engine roared. Harry's scar split open. He nearly dropped Draconia. Cold laughter moved behind his eyes. For a moment Harry could not hear Ron over it. He saw nothing that told him where Voldemort was or what he knew; there was only the burning scar and that awful sense of somebody enjoying his fear. Harry forced his gaze back to Draconia's face. She was here. He had to keep holding her. The Dark Mark above the train flared. Harry's knees struck the floor.

"Harry!" Ron shouted.

"Keep moving."

"You're bleeding."

"Not new."

Blood ran beneath Harry's glasses from his scar. He adjusted his hold on Draconia and rose. Outside, the first ward spike exploded. Silver light opened a hole in the green wall. Kingsley flew through it. His cloak burned along one edge. Below him, an Auror hooked the second anchor and tore it sideways out of the sleepers. Tonks followed upside down, pursuing a masked rider beneath the level of the tracks. Cho and Ginny carried a second-year between them towards carriage four while Cedric wheeled above, shielding all three. Ginny's damaged broom buckled under the added weight of the child. She dropped. Harry made a sound he did not recognise. Cedric dived.

He clamped his knees around the broom, caught the back of Ginny's robes with one hand and hooked his other arm beneath the child. His broom dipped under the sudden weight; he could not hold them there for long. Cho seized Ginny's broom arm. For a few terrible seconds, all four hung from two brooms beside the bridge. Then Anthony Goldstein flew beneath them. Ginny landed across his broom. Cedric shoved the younger student towards Cho and climbed. They cleared the first stone arch as the train reached it. Harry could not breathe.

"They're clear," Ron said.

"I saw."

"Then move."

Harry moved. The locomotive struck the second section of green light. Metal shrieked. The front wheels lifted from the rails. Every person in the carriage rose with them. Harry turned in the air, folding his body around Draconia. He hit the ceiling shoulder-first. Ron and Hermione struck a compartment wall. Students slid along the floor as the front of the train tilted towards the river. Outside, Sirius flew low along the bridge approach. He emerged beside the third spike and drove the motorbike's front wheel directly into it. The engine coughed as it entered the cursed strip; Sirius's boots struck the verge before he wrestled it back into the air.

The spike tore from the ground. Green light snapped. The front wheels came down. The impact travelled through every carriage. Harry struck the floor. Draconia remained against his chest. Something cracked beneath him which might have been a seat bracket or one of his ribs. The train crossed onto the bridge. The river appeared through the open windows on both sides, dark water turning far below. Tonks tore another spike clear as Sirius brought his front wheel down onto the maintenance ledge beside the tracks. Two anchors remained.

An Auror removed one with the hooked pole. The final spike pulsed. No one was close enough. A masked rider hovered beside it, wand raised. Kingsley was occupied above. Tonks had disappeared below the bridge. Sirius turned the motorbike, but the train was already between him and the target.

The rider smiled behind his mask. A jet of red light came from the train. It hit his broom. The broom spun. The rider's curse missed Sirius and struck the stone parapet.

The red spell had come from Cedric. He was flying level with carriage four, one hand on his emergency broom and the other extended towards the bridge. Sirius dropped below the outer edge of the bridge and crossed beneath its next arch, clear of the train above him. He appeared on the far side and levelled the bike with the narrow maintenance ledge beyond the parapet. His boots found the stone as the motorbike swept past him, held aloft by the charm built into its frame.

Harry watched him land beside the spike. For one impossible instant, Sirius Black stood on the outside edge of a railway bridge with a train bearing down upon him.

"He promised," Harry whispered.

Sirius raised his wand.

"Bombarda!"

The spike shattered. Silver light returned. It raced beneath the engine, along the rails and through every remaining carriage. Magic slammed back into Harry's wand. The black spike inside the wall cracked down the middle. The train passed Sirius with inches to spare.

He leapt from the bridge. Harry surged towards the window. The motorbike swept beneath Sirius, answering the wand movement he had made before jumping. He caught the sidecar rim and dropped into it, one boot still hanging over the edge. He disappeared behind them.

The Hogwarts Express reached the far bank. For the first time since the whistle began, the brakes answered. The engine screamed. Sparks rose higher than the carriages. Wheels locked, released and locked again. The train left the bridge, tore through half a mile of open country and entered a wide field bordered by ash trees.

It slowed. Telegraph poles stopped flashing past and began to separate into individual shapes. Harry counted them, then the longer pauses between them, because counting was easier than believing. The engine stopped. Silence did not follow.

Metal ticked as it cooled. Injured people cried. Owls shrieked from cages. Somewhere, an alarm bell continued ringing because nobody had told it the emergency was over. Above all of it, Harry heard Draconia breathe. He remained upon the floor for several seconds. His body did not understand the difference between stopped and safe. Every muscle waited for the next impact. His wand stayed raised. The blood from his scar reached the bridge of his nose and gathered beneath his glasses. Ron lowered Hermione carefully.

"We're down," he said.

"I noticed."

"You look like you haven't."

Harry looked through the nearest window. The field did not move. Smoke travelled slowly over flattened grass. Broken glass and scraps of roof metal lay beside the tracks. The wrecked rear carriage and luggage van were farther back, beyond the cutting, out of sight. Far above, the Dark Mark remained fixed against the afternoon sky. Harry placed Draconia upon the seat. His arms resisted when he tried to let go.

"Harry," Ron said.

"I know."

He did not. His hands had locked beneath her knees and shoulders. For as long as he held her, he could feel her breathing. Putting her down meant trusting breath to continue when he was no longer checking it. Ron seemed to understand. He did not tell Harry that Draconia was fine. He did not say the danger had passed or that the cut looked worse than it was.

"I'll keep a hand here," he said, and placed two fingers against the pulse in her wrist.

Harry looked at him.

"Promise?"

Ron's face became solemn. "Yeah."

Harry released Draconia. The carriage doors reopened when the broken ward spike finally died. Fresh air entered through them, carrying the scent of grass, smoke and hot iron. The trolley witch appeared in the rear connecting doorway without her apron. She wore the same dark Auror robes Harry had seen during the attack and had a burn across one cheek. Two healers followed with folding stretchers and bags which opened into shelves of potion bottles.

"Walking wounded outside," she ordered. "Anyone unconscious remains where they are until assessed. Do not activate unauthorised Portkeys. The field is unstable."

Her gaze found Harry.

"How many in here?"

"Two unconscious. Draconia has a cursed cut beneath her ribs. Hermione took a Stunner and hit her head earlier. Several attackers down in seven and eight. At least four bound or Stunned."

The report came from somewhere calm inside him.

The Auror nodded once. "You?"

"Fine."

"Incorrect," Ron said.

"Nobody asked you."

"He took a curse to the shoulder, hit the ceiling, hit the floor and his scar started bleeding when we crossed the field. Possibly other things. It was busy."

The Auror looked at Harry's face.

"Sit."

"I can help."

"You can sit where the healer can see you."

"I need to stay with them."

She pointed to the seat beside the stretchers. "Then stay there. I'm not sending you away."

She pointed at the seat. Harry sat. The healers separated Hermione and Draconia only far enough to examine them. A wand passed above Draconia's wound, producing a web of black lines in the air. The healer's mouth tightened.

"Blood-binding curse," she said. "Designed to resist closure. Not deep. We can contain it for transport; either St Mungo's or Hogwarts, if their dark-curse kit is ready."

Harry's lungs opened.

"And Hermione?"

The second healer drew a circle of blue light around Hermione's head. "Concussion and magical shock. Her breathing is clear. Pupils are responding."

"When will she wake?"

"Mr Potter—"

"When?"

The healer met his eyes. "Soon, I expect. I will tell you if that changes."

Harry nodded. The honesty helped more than reassurance would have. Footsteps pounded through the carriage behind them. Harry reached for his wand. Sirius came through the broken door. His hair was wild, his coat had lost one sleeve and blood covered the side of his neck. He carried a masked man's wand between his teeth because both hands were occupied holding another attacker by the back of his robes. Sirius dropped the man in front of the Auror.

"This one has a Portkey in his boot," he said around the wand.

The Auror took the prisoner and turned him onto his side. "Alive?"

Sirius removed the wand from his mouth. "Stunned. Keep him that way until you've searched him."

"You were instructed not to engage beyond the bridge."

"He was reaching for his boot. I wasn't leaving him loose beside the children."

Then he saw Harry. Every performance left his face. He crossed the carriage in three strides. Harry stood because he could not remain seated while Sirius looked at him like that.

Sirius reached for him, stopped at the burned sleeve, and looked helplessly towards the healer. Harry had never seen him quite so unsure where he could safely put his hands. Then Sirius cupped the uninjured side of his face, his thumb trembling against Harry's cheek.

"I'm all right," Harry said.

"You're covered in blood."

"They're checking me. I'm still here."

Sirius nodded too quickly, as though that was the only part he could bear to understand yet. Sirius looked past him. He saw Draconia and Hermione on the stretchers the healers had unfolded beside the seats. Ron kneeling between them. The black residue around the wound. The blood on Harry's clothes which did not all belong to Harry. His face went grey.

"They're alive," Harry said quickly. "Both of them. The healers said—"

Sirius pulled him into his arms. The embrace hurt Harry's shoulder. He did not care. Sirius held the back of his head with one hand and Harry's jacket with the other. His breathing was rough beside Harry's ear. Harry waited for Sirius to say something and was grateful when he didn't. He did not have a joke ready either. Harry closed both hands in the remains of Sirius's coat.

"You came back," he said.

Sirius's grip tightened.

"I said I would."

"I know."

The words broke halfway through. Harry pressed his face against Sirius's shoulder and allowed the carriage to disappear for three breaths. Four. Five. Sirius did not release him first. When Harry finally drew back, Sirius kept both hands upon his shoulders.

"Tell me you stayed off the roof," he said, looking at the torn lining overhead.

Harry looked at the torn roof lining, although the opening he had climbed through was in carriage eight, behind them.

"Mostly."

Sirius followed his gaze.

"Harry."

"There were ward controls."

"Of course there were."

"I came back inside."

"After climbing onto it."

"That still counts as returning."

Sirius closed his eyes. The laugh which escaped him sounded perilously close to something else.

"We'll revisit the wording."

Harry looked at Ron.

"I did go after them," Harry said. His voice dropped. "I left the girls in seven. Ron stopped me following the last one out."

Ron appeared horrified by the attention.

"I grabbed his coat."

Sirius looked between them, and for a moment his smile faltered. Harry knew that look from old photographs and stories interrupted halfway through. He could not tell which memory Sirius had found this time. Then Sirius blinked, crossed the small distance between them and offered Ron his hand.

"Thank you for bringing him back," he said.

Ron took his hand awkwardly. "He listened. Eventually."

Sirius nodded, still holding Ron's hand. "That mattered. Thank you."

"I'm used to grabbing Fred before he does something stupid," Ron said, glancing away from the adults. "This was rather worse."

Sirius considered this. "I like him."

"That isn't the compliment you think it is," Harry said.

Draconia moved. Harry was beside her before the healer could object. Her eyelids tightened. She drew a shallow breath and looked first at the ceiling, then at the unfamiliar hands holding a bandage against her side.

"Do not move," the healer told her.

"Where—"

Her gaze found Harry. Panic vanished so quickly he almost missed it.

"Still here?" he asked.

Draconia swallowed. "You have asked me that already."

"You were unconscious. It seemed worth checking."

"I heard you the first time," she whispered. "I just couldn't answer."

Harry laughed. It came out broken. Draconia raised one hand. Her fingers found his wrist and closed around it, exactly where she had held him upon the train roof.

"Granger?"

"Beside you. Concussion. She'll wake soon."

Draconia turned her head. Hermione's hand lay between the stretchers. Draconia reached towards it. The movement pulled at her wound. The healer tried to stop her, but Harry understood. He moved Hermione's hand the final few inches and placed it inside Draconia's. Her fingers closed.

"The students?" she asked.

Ron answered. "They're still checking names. Nobody's missing from the rear carriage."

"And six?"

"Cedric's finishing the last run. Let them count, Draconia. We can't do it from in here."

Draconia looked at him.

"You went back for them?"

"I carried Granger. Harry had you."

Draconia studied him, then gave a small nod. "Thank you."

Ron looked so uncomfortable with being thanked twice that Harry almost smiled. Then Hermione's fingers twitched. Everyone stopped. Her eyes opened slowly.

"Why," she whispered, "are you all looking at me like that?"

Ron removed his hand so quickly he nearly struck the healer.

"You were Stunned," Ron said. "The healer said not to move until she'd checked you."

Hermione frowned and tried to lift her head anyway. The healer put a gentle hand on her shoulder, and she stopped. Harry lowered his forehead against the edge of her stretcher. The wood was cool beneath his skin. Hermione turned towards him.

"Harry?"

"Here."

"Draco?"

"Here," Draconia said. "Don't sit up. It hurts more than you expect."

Hermione saw their joined hands. Her grip tightened. Harry placed his own hand over both. For a moment, the three of them remained connected upon the floor of a broken train while healers worked around them and Sirius stood close enough to catch whatever fell. No one attempted to make the moment smaller with humour. Then Hermione looked at the blood covering Harry.

"Is that yours?"

"Some of it."

"Harry."

"Most of me is mine."

Draconia shut her eyes. "He is clearly concussed."

"I agree."

The healer pointed towards the door. "Visitors outside. We're moving the stretchers now. Anyone who is still arguing can do it after I've checked their breathing."

Outside, Aurors marked separate areas for the wounded and the prisoners. Harry watched a healer turn an abandoned trunk into a treatment bench while another stretched canvas between two trees. People were making the field usable in small, urgent pieces; nobody waited for it to become orderly.

Long tables were conjured beneath white canvas awnings. Healers marked injuries with ribbons: green for safe to travel, amber for observation, red for immediate transport. At first Harry could not bear the sight of red beside anyone's name. A healer had to explain that it meant the person needed moving first, not that there was nothing left to do for them. Captured attackers lay bound beneath the supervision of six Aurors. Three had escaped after the bridge. Others had vanished beyond the rails while the grounding field still held the train. Kingsley made an Auror mark each departure point. From where Harry sat, every one appeared to be outside the narrow strip of ground beneath the broken carriages.

"A corridor through their own curse," Harry heard Kingsley say. "They prepared the exits before they attacked."

Harry sat upon an overturned trunk beneath an ash tree while a healer closed the cut across his cheek. His left arm rested in a sling. She had checked his ribs twice and told him they were bruised, not broken; he tried to remember that whenever breathing pulled at his side. Sirius stood close enough to interfere with every instruction.

"He needs Blood-Replenishing Potion," Sirius said.

"He has had it."

"He's still pale."

"He needs time for it to work. Another dose would make him ill."

Sirius looked at the empty bottle in her hand, then at Harry's face. "What can I do?"

"Mr Black."

"Tell me what he needs."

"Sit beside him. Let me finish this cut without having to work around your elbow."

Sirius moved six inches. Nearby, Draconia lay upon a stretcher beneath an amber blanket. The curse had been contained but not removed. Hermione sat beside her with a bandage around her head and the expression of someone deeply offended by being instructed not to read. Ron had acquired a green ribbon and refused to leave until every Gryffindor first-year had been counted twice. Cedric landed at the edge of the field carrying the last emergency broom.

The bristles were gone. Half the handle had burned black. He dismounted, took two steps and sat down abruptly in the grass. Harry rose. The healer pushed him back.

"I know him."

"He has a healer going to him. Keep still until I've finished."

Cedric lifted one thumb from the grass.

"All clear," he called.

Cho sat beside him. Ginny dropped onto his other side. Anthony lay flat upon his back without consulting anyone. The count moved through the field. Eight hundred and twelve students aboard. Eight hundred and twelve found, including those taken directly to St Mungo's. The escort officer read the final confirmations into a charmed brass horn so that everyone in the field could hear.

No student dead. Two members of the escort were still under treatment; the officer said they were alive too. The relief did not sound like cheering. It travelled as a collective exhale, the whole field releasing air it had held since the first whistle. Harry looked at Cedric. Cedric looked back.

Neither smiled. They did not need to. A line of gold appeared near the tracks after the escort marked out a cleared patch of ground. Ministry arrival wards.

Figures emerged from Portkeys in orderly groups: Hit Wizards, clerks, two photographers and officials wearing robes too clean for the field. Professor McGonagall arrived with Madam Pomfrey and Professor Flitwick. Dumbledore followed, blue robes snapping in the wind, his face stripped of its usual mildness. Remus came through the final Portkey before it closed. He still carried his cane. His injured ribs made running impossible. He attempted it anyway.

Sirius met him halfway. They caught each other's forearms. No question passed between them. Sirius pointed towards Harry, Hermione and Draconia. Remus's eyes closed once. Then he reached Harry and held the back of his neck, forehead almost touching Harry's.

"Half a second," Harry said.

Remus looked at the ruined train.

"You took it?"

"Sometimes."

"We'll practise the others."

Harry nodded. He could accept correction when it sounded like a future. Dolores Umbridge arrived last. She stepped from a Ministry Portkey wearing pink wool and a matching hat. Mud touched the toe of one shoe. Her displeasure at this was visible before she looked at the train.

Behind her, a clerk opened a green folder. Umbridge surveyed the field. The wounded. The captured men. The shattered windows. The blackened rails and the place at the rear where an entire carriage no longer existed. Then she smiled. Her clerk touched his wand to the brass horn still standing beside the arrival point, and her voice carried over the noise of the field.

"What a dreadful transport incident."

The words crossed the field more effectively than a shout. Students turned. Sirius made a low sound beside Harry.

Umbridge continued towards Dumbledore. "The Minister has authorised immediate assistance following an attack by unidentified masked criminals. Until identities and motives are confirmed, students must be discouraged from repeating inflammatory speculation."

Harry looked upwards. The Dark Mark remained above them. It had begun to thin at the edges, but the skull was still unmistakable against the pale sky.

"Unidentified," Cedric repeated from the grass.

Umbridge's eyes moved towards him.

"Mr Diggory. I understand this experience may have been distressing."

Cedric stood. Cho tried to stop him. He rose anyway.

"I saw their masks," he said. "I heard them demand Harry. I watched them cast the Killing Curse inside a carriage full of children."

"And your courage will be recognised," Umbridge replied. "But it is important that personal fear does not become political certainty."

"I was afraid," Cedric said. His voice shook once, and he steadied it. "That doesn't mean I imagined them. They were close enough for me to hear what they said."

Silence moved outwards from him. Harry saw younger students look at Cedric differently. Not as the Champion who had survived. Not as the boy newspapers placed beside Harry whenever one story required two faces. As the person who had flown back for them. Umbridge's smile tightened.

"Nevertheless, until the Department concludes—"

"There are hundreds of people here who can tell you what happened," Hermione said.

She stood beside Draconia's stretcher, one hand braced against it when the field shifted beneath her.

Umbridge looked at the bandage around her head. "Miss Granger, you should rest."

"Eight hundred and twelve students were on that train," Hermione said. "Not all of us saw the same thing, but plenty saw the masks and heard what they wanted. You've got prisoners, the broken spikes—and that's still above us." She pointed towards the Mark, then lowered her arm as the field seemed to tilt.

"None of which independently identifies You-Know-Who."

"You could try looking at all of it," Draconia said from the stretcher.

Heads turned again. Pansy stood several yards away with Millicent. Both moved closer to Draconia without seeming to decide to. Umbridge regarded the Slytherin blanket, the Malfoy face above it and the blood staining the edge.

"Miss Malfoy. Your family has already endured harmful accusations because of irresponsible associations. I would advise caution before creating another."

Harry felt Sirius move. Draconia only raised her chin.

"They cut me," Draconia said. She had to stop and wet her lips. "I saw one of their faces after his mask broke. He knew my name. He said Father chose badly."

Her hand tightened around the blanket.

"I'd like you to ask him what he meant."

Umbridge looked at Harry. Of course she did.

"Mr Potter, I expect you understand the importance of allowing responsible authorities to establish the truth before frightening your classmates."

Harry looked behind her at the train. One side of it had been opened from roof to wheels. Smoke continued rising from the rear carriages. Blood darkened the grass beneath three awnings. The Dark Mark watched all of them from above.

"You're right," he said.

Umbridge's smile returned.

"I'm pleased—"

"It may have tripped."

Somebody laughed. Harry thought it was Ginny. Then Ron joined her. The sound moved through the older students and found the younger ones. It was not amusement, exactly. It was disbelief made sharp enough to use. Umbridge's smile vanished.

"This is not a joking matter."

"I know," Harry said. "The train has had a very difficult afternoon."

Even Professor McGonagall's mouth moved. Umbridge faced the field.

"No student is to provide an account to the press until a Ministry liaison has recorded a formal statement. Unauthorised photographs, written descriptions and speculative correspondence may compromise an active investigation. Commissioner authority extends to communications made on school grounds once you arrive at Hogwarts."

McGonagall stepped between her and the girls' stretcher, catching Hermione's elbow long enough to help her sit down. "These children will give statements when the healers say they are fit. Until then, you will address requests to the staff."

"Professor, the Commissioner's authority—"

"Does not make you their healer."

Dumbledore joined her, his voice quieter. "We will preserve the evidence and arrange separate accounts. Their families will be contacted. No child will be asked to answer questions alone."

Umbridge turned towards him, still holding her smile in place. While she argued about procedure, students began looking at one another. Hermione reached into her bag. The bag had survived the siege. Harry did not know how. It might have survived the end of the world if properly labelled. She removed a notebook.

"We should keep our own copies," she said quietly.

Draconia heard. So did Ron. So did Pansy, Cedric, Cho, Ginny, Neville and every prefect standing close enough to understand. The idea moved without needing to be announced. Students began talking. Not to the reporters. To one another.

They wrote names and carriage numbers, spells they had seen, and times they could remember. When somebody recognised a voice or a face, Hermione asked them to say how, rather than putting a name to a mask because it seemed likely. Older students knelt beside younger ones and wrote while the details remained fresh. Prefects exchanged lists. A Ravenclaw produced parchment. A Hufflepuff supplied ink. Pansy gathered those from carriage nine who were ready to talk and checked their names against Draconia's count. When a first-year shook his head, she marked his place on the list and let him stay beside his brother. Ron sat beside three Gryffindor first-years.

"Start with the whistle," he said.

Cedric asked Cho to record the bridge. Harry watched an older Hufflepuff ask a crying child whether she wanted to write or only have somebody sit with her. The girl shook her head at the parchment. He put it away and stayed. At the next table, a Ravenclaw wrote down the whistle sequence while her friend argued that the second blast had been shorter. Nobody made them agree. Harry began to understand what Hermione wanted: separate accounts, including the gaps, before anyone decided which parts were inconvenient. Dumbledore watched too.

His eyes met Harry's across the field. There was pride in them. There was also regret. Harry had seen enough regret from adults to last several lifetimes.

He looked away first. Beyond Dumbledore, a first-year sat with an empty owl cage pressed against his chest while a healer washed soot from his face. The owl itself was perched in the ash tree overhead, refusing every attempt to coax it down. The boy kept looking between it and the healer, unable to believe that losing it could still be the worst thing he had to tell his parents.

Harry wanted to go over. His knees would not hold him when he tried, and Sirius quietly put a hand beneath his elbow. Harry stayed where he was. Neville reached the boy instead, set his plant down and began talking to the owl as though it had only picked an inconvenient branch. Once the healer had finished, Sirius helped Harry settle on the grass with his back against the trunk. He sat beside him, and Remus lowered himself carefully on Harry's other side, setting his cane within reach. Their shoulders enclosed him without trapping him.

"You all right?" Sirius asked.

Harry considered the question properly. Hermione was writing despite the healer's repeated objections. Draconia watched her with one hand pressed over the bandage beneath her ribs. Ron listened to a child describe a masked man at the window and did not interrupt once. Cedric sat in the grass surrounded by students who now knew exactly why he had returned. The train was broken, and Harry knew none of them would simply board another one and forget this afternoon. Hermione's hand shook when she turned a page. Draconia kept checking the field for familiar faces. Ron stopped writing whenever one of the children needed a moment. They were alive, but that was not the same as being all right.

"No," Harry said.

Sirius nodded. Remus's hand settled between Harry's shoulders.

"Me neither," Sirius replied.
