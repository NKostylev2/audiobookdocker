3

Harry woke in a cold sweat. For several seconds, he lay still beneath the unfamiliar, patchy blankets and listened for the sound that had dragged him from sleep. There was no fist against the door, no barked order from the landing and no Aunt Petunia opening curtains with enough violence to imply that daylight was Harry's fault.

Morning light filled room eleven. It entered through a broad window opposite the bed, pale gold at the edges and softened by the old glass. Dust turned lazily above the floorboards. The wardrobe stood partly open, revealing spare blankets and three empty hangers rather than a hidden tunnel, a basilisk or Vernon Dursley.

Harry suddenly remembered where he was. The Leaky Cauldron. Relief loosened his body one piece at a time. He had slept for nearly six hours.

That might not have been remarkable to anyone else. Hermione considered eight the minimum required for responsible thought, while Draconia claimed she could function on four and then became personally offended whenever anyone witnessed the results. To Harry, six uninterrupted hours felt suspiciously extravagant.

Harry reached beneath the pillow. His wand met his fingers immediately. The wood was warm, familiar.

He held it for several breaths, reassured by the ordinary solidity of the handle. At Privet Drive, every unexpected sound required calculation: distance to the door, location of Vernon, whether the cupboard was locked and how long reaching the wand would take.

Harry put on his glasses.

Hedwig had returned during the night. She slept upon the wardrobe with her head beneath one wing. Two letters rested on the desk below her, one in Hermione's neat writing and the other addressed only to Potter.

Harry wanted a bath first. He wanted clean clothes, food and several uninterrupted minutes in which no one knew he had inflated a relative and travelled alone through London after midnight.

The corridor outside his room was empty. A faded carpet ran between crooked walls lined with framed notices, photographs and advertisements for products which had not existed for at least forty years. Somewhere below, crockery knocked together, and a man laughed.

Harry found the bathroom opposite the stairs. Hot water arrived immediately.

He stood beneath it until steam filled the little room and the skin across his shoulders loosened. Dirt from Privet Drive and the Knight Bus spiralled towards the drain. The soap smelled of cedar rather than Petunia's sharp lemon, and nobody struck the door to inform him he had occupied the room too long.

Harry washed his hair twice. When he stepped out, the mirror had disappeared behind fog. He wiped a circle clear with his palm.

The same thin body he had become comfortable seeing stared back. Harry had seen his face every morning without properly looking at it. His cheekbones showed more than they had at the beginning of summer. Dark half-moons remained beneath his eyes despite the night's sleep. A yellowing bruise marked one shoulder where he struck the wardrobe after a nightmare, and Marge's grip had left faint shadows around his wrist.

Nothing looked serious. Taken separately, each mark possessed an ordinary explanation. Together, they made Harry understand what Hermione would see.

He dressed quickly.

The bruise disappeared beneath his shirt. His fringe covered the scar. He tugged the sleeves down over his wrists and returned to the bedroom.

He sat back on top of the bedcovers and pulled out the two envelopes he had received. Hermione's letter was six pages.

The first four had been written before Hedwig arrived. Harry could identify the exact place the news reached her because the handwriting changed from controlled lines into darker strokes which slanted towards the right.

Harry,

You cannot write "don't panic" directly after announcing that you have left your relatives' house in the middle of the night.

That is not how instructions work.

I'm glad you have your wand and are at the Leaky Cauldron. Mum has already contacted Tom through the Ministry guest office to confirm you arrived. She says this was unnecessary because your note included evidence, but she contacted him twice.

What happened?

You said there was an argument. What sort of argument? Was anyone hurt? Did your uncle try to prevent you from leaving? Did the dog appear again?

We are coming to London on Saturday. Dad wanted to come this morning, but the Ministry says you are safe, and Mum says arriving before you wake will only make you believe you've caused a crisis.

You have not caused a crisis; don't put yourself down.

The sentence had been underlined twice.

Please do not leave Diagon Alley before we arrive. Please eat breakfast. Please ask Tom for another room if the door does not lock properly.

Love from,

Hermione

Harry smiled slightly as he neatly folded and placed the letter on his bedside table. Draconia's letter began more abruptly.

Potter,

If you ever write "Don't worry, I'm figuring it out" after telling me you've run away in the middle of the night, I'll come and find you myself.

Mother has confirmed your room, the inn's defensive wards and the location of two Ministry officials assigned to the building. Father is attempting to discover why the Minister for Magic considered greeting you personally more important than recovering the dangerous fugitive who has apparently crossed half of Britain without detection.

We will arrive on Saturday. I requested today, but Father has business first.

Father said something about provoking the Ministry. Mother said something quieter, which caused him to reconsider, but we are still arriving on Saturday.

Did your uncle hurt you?

Harry stared at the question. He pictured Vernon blocking the stairs. Marge's fingers around his wrist. Petunia asking what they would tell the neighbours.

No one had struck him. That made the correct answer obvious. He read further.

Do not answer quickly merely because you dislike the question.

Harry looked towards Hedwig.

"She's becoming unreasonably good with these questions."

The owl remained asleep. At the bottom, Draconia had written:

I am glad you left them, but don't do it as you did again. I'm still angry about the way you did it.

Harry sighed as he placed Draco's letter next to Hermione's.

Saturday was two days away. The delay felt strange. Part of him had expected Lucius to step from the fireplace before sunrise, followed by Narcissa, both Grangers, Hermione and Draconia carrying a list of everything Harry had done incorrectly.

Two days belonged to him. The prospect created a small, bright feeling which was not quite happiness but had the same direction.

Harry picked up his money pouch. Breakfast first. For once, he intended to follow instructions.

Breakfast in the Leaky Cauldron was a revelation.

Tom set down two fried eggs, three sausages, toast, mushrooms, and tomatoes before Harry without needing to negotiate. A pot of tea arrived beside the plate. Butter occupied its own dish rather than being scraped across bread in quantities thin enough to permit transparency.

Harry looked at the food.

"Is someone else coming?"

Tom glanced around the empty corner table. "Not that I know of."

"This is all mine?"

"If you want it."

Harry touched the edge of the plate. The eggs were still hot. Butter melted into the toast. One sausage rolled slightly when the table shifted beneath his arm.

He began eating. Tom did not stand nearby watching. He returned behind the bar, where he polished glasses and spoke with a witch wearing a hat covered in artificial cherries.

Harry ate one sausage, half the eggs and a piece of toast before his stomach tightened. Hunger remained, but the quantity in front of him had become uncomfortable in a different way. He found himself looking towards the bar, half expecting Tom to remove the plate once he slowed.

At the next table, a wizard unfolded the Daily Prophet. Sirius Black's face appeared above the front-page crease.

Harry looked away and finished both eggs. Harry wrapped two slices of toast in a napkin and slipped them into his pocket. Tom appeared as he did so.

Harry froze. The barman's eyes moved from the napkin to Harry's face.

"I can bring more later," he said.

"I'm full."

"All right then, lad, but so you know, we do serve lunch from eleven and dinner from 5."

Tom then cleared his throat and picked up the plate.

"Kitchen serves until ten at night, but we do have a night cook. If the door's closed, just knock."

Harry nodded. Tom turned and carried the dishes away.

Harry looked down sheepishly, trying to avoid the other guests' gaze. His eyes suddenly caught a folded note waiting beneath the teapot. It had his name in formal green ink.

Mr Harry Potter is permitted to reside at the Leaky Cauldron until the commencement of the Hogwarts term. During this period, he is expected to remain within Diagon Alley and its immediately connected premises. He is not to enter Knockturn Alley, travel unaccompanied into Muggle London or leave the district after dusk.

These restrictions are for his personal security.

No disciplinary action will be taken regarding the accidental magical incident of the previous evening.

Cornelius Fudge's signature sprawled beneath the final line.

Harry read the note twice, and tried not to look irritated.

It still did not explain why Sirius Black's escape changed the Ministry's attitude towards underage magic. Nor did it explain how staying inside a shopping street would protect Harry from a man who escaped Azkaban.

He folded it and placed it in his front pocket. Tom looked up when Harry approached the bar.

"I need to go to Gringotts."

"An Auror at the entrance will follow."

Harry stopped. "What Auror?"

Tom nodded towards a man seated near the front window. He wore brown robes, held the newspaper upside down and had spent the past five minutes pretending not to look at Harry.

"That's subtle."

"He works nights."

The Auror turned one page backwards.

"Does he have to follow me?"

"Minister's instruction."

"I'm not likely to become safer if everyone knows someone's guarding me."

Tom considered the man.

"Fair point."

Harry crossed the room. The Auror lowered his newspaper. Harry stopped beside the table.

"I assume you are the one following me?"

"No."

"Right, well the paper's upside down."

The wizard looked at it.

"So it is."

"I'm going to Gringotts."

"Enjoy."

Harry continued towards the door. The Auror folded the newspaper, stood and followed at a distance of six paces.

Harry sighed.

The rear courtyard of the Leaky Cauldron was cool beneath the morning shadow. Bins stood beside the brick wall. A rusted drainpipe climbed towards the upper windows, and damp gathered between the stones despite the summer heat.

Tom tapped the correct bricks with his wand.

The wall opened. Diagon Alley waited beyond it.

Morning had only begun, but the street was already awake. Shopkeepers dragged signs onto the pavement—owls called from the dark interior of Eeylops. Cauldrons rang as an assistant stacked them outside a shop, and the air carried a complicated mixture of bread, potion ingredients, parchment and smoke.

Sunlight reached the crooked upper storeys before touching the street. Windows shone overhead. Signs swung between buildings at angles no Muggle council would have permitted.

Harry stepped through. The first time he entered Diagon Alley, Hagrid walked beside him and explained the world as it appeared.

The second time, Hermione and Draconia surrounded him with competing opinions before he had crossed half the street. And now, for the first time, nobody told him where to go. It was, in Harry's mind, weirdly freeing but lonely.

Harry stood in the moving crowd with money in his pocket, a room behind him and an entire day that belonged to no one else.

The freedom felt larger than it should have. He turned and headed towards Gringotts.

The goblin behind the counter examined Harry's key, then examined Harry. Neither inspection appeared favourable.

"Vault six hundred and eighty-seven."

"Yes."

"Identification."

Harry showed the key again.

"That proves you've got a key."

"It has my name attached."

"It has a label."

Harry reached for his glasses. "I can show you the scar, but that seems like an unreliable banking procedure."

The goblin's mouth tightened. Perhaps it was amusement. Goblin expressions did not invite certainty. A second goblin was summoned. He inspected Harry, whispered to the first and produced a form requiring three signatures. Harry signed where indicated.

The goblin compared each signature to a record.

"You have altered the angle of the final letter."

"I'm thirteen."

"Is that relevant?"

"My handwriting changes."

"Unfortunate."

Harry was eventually allowed into the cart. The Auror attempted to accompany him. The goblin at the gate held out one hand.

"Account holder only."

"I'm assigned to his protection."

"You have not been assigned access to his vault."

"He may be a target."

"Then protect him without violating Gringotts policy."

The gate closed in the Auror's face.

Harry smiled all the way underground.

The cart plunged through narrow tunnels, wheels shrieking against iron tracks. Cold air pulled at his clothes. Torchlight flashed across damp stone and vanished behind them.

Harry's enjoyment survived the first corner.

The cart descended into darkness. Rock pressed close on both sides. Somewhere ahead, metal ground against stone.

Harry felt his stomach drop as flashes of the chamber resurfaced. Green light, carved snakes, a voice moving through pipes. He gripped the cart's edge. The goblin beside him glanced over.

"Is there a problem?"

"No."

Harry fixed his attention upon the tracks. Iron rails, wooden supports, torches every twenty feet. No water, no serpent. The cart turned sharply.

By the time they reached his vault, his hands hurt from holding the railing.

The goblin slid his hand down the middle of the door and inserted the brass key, unlocking the door.

Large piles of gold shone inside. Harry stared at the piles of coins and felt the familiar disbelief. The Dursleys spoke about him as an expense while more money than Harry could count waited beneath London under his name.

He filled the pouch cautiously.

"May I ask a question?" he said.

"You have."

Harry looked at the goblin. The goblin looked back.

"Another one."

"Fine."

"If someone dangerous wanted to know when I accessed this vault, could they?"

"Gringotts does not disclose client activity."

"To the Ministry?"

"No."

"To Sirius Black?"

The goblin's face became unreadable.

"No."

Harry nodded. The answer didn't tell him whether Black had tried to ask. He returned to the surface with more money, a bruised hip and the growing suspicion that every adult institution communicated primarily through omissions.

The Auror waited beside the marble doors. He fell into step when Harry emerged. For the next ten minutes, Harry walked without destination purely to discover whether the Auror would continue.

He did.

Harry passed the Apothecary twice, entered a shop selling brass instruments and inspected a pocket sundial he did not need. The Auror waited outside with increasing impatience.

When Harry crossed the street for the third time, the man spoke.

"Do you know where you're going?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Here."

Harry entered Flourish and Blotts.

The bookshop was cool, crowded and full of whispers. Strangely, most of them were not human. Several books amongst the shelves had opinions.

A display of dream dictionaries murmured interpretations at passing customers. Books on household charms complained whenever dust touched their covers, and a stack of advanced Divination texts predicted its own collapse so loudly that no one approached.

Harry wandered between the shelves. His school list remained inside his trunk, but he had read it enough times to remember the titles. Standard Book of Spells, Grade Three. Intermediate Transfiguration. Unfogging the Future. The Monster Book of Monsters.

The final title had been crossed from his list because Hagrid's copy already waited upstairs, restrained by belts and nursing a grievance.

A shop assistant wearing dragon-hide gloves approached.

"Hogwarts third year?"

"Yes."

"Move aside, I hope you brought extra bags."

The man, whose right sleeve was torn and whose neck bore strange scratches, approached a ladder beside a massive metal cage. Even weirder was the fact that the cage didn't house any animal, but hundreds of the monster books that Hagrid had sent him.

"Oh, I uh, I own the book."

The assistant, whose face had seemingly regained some colour at the relief, looked at Harry's uninjured hands.

"Do you?"

"It lives beneath my bed at the moment."

"Stroke the spine."

Harry stared.

"What?"

"Stroke it along the spine. Calms them."

Harry thought of the belts, pillow and damaged shoelace.

"Hagrid didn't mention that."

"Well, they often forget."

"They?"

"People who like the book."

The assistant moved back to a counter labelled refunds where a large stack of 'Magical Me' copies, most half destroyed, sat.

Harry shook his head and moved about the large bookshelves, selecting his textbooks. He wandered for nearly twenty minutes before he found the defensive section.

It occupied two narrow aisles near the rear, protected by a sign warning customers not to practise curses indoors. Harry ran one finger along the spines.

Defensive Wards for Domestic Spaces.

Recognising Concealment and Compulsion.

Counter-Curses: An Intermediate Guide.

The Mind Under Magic.

His hand stopped upon the final title. The cover was plain blue cloth. No face stared from it, no teeth appeared, and no author's portrait smiled with Lockhart's painful confidence.

Harry removed it from the shelf.

The contents divided mental magic into categories: enchantment, influence, possession and magical coercion—one chapter concerned recovery after prolonged control.

Draconia would already own it. She might have read every book on the shelf since returning from Hogwarts. She would call it preparation. Hermione would call it research. Neither would use the word fear.

Harry added the book to his pile. The next shelf contained practical defence. He chose two volumes above his year and a third concerning nonverbal recognition of curses. The stack grew heavy in his arms.

"You do not need all those."

Harry turned. The Auror stood at the end of the aisle.

"I thought you weren't following me."

"I'm not."

"You followed me into a bookshop."

"I like books."

The newspaper remained tucked beneath his arm.

Harry glanced at it.

"What's your name?"

The Auror hesitated.

"Snotfoot."

"Is that your real name?"

"Yes."

"Unfortunate."

Snotfoot's mouth twitched before he corrected it.

"Those books are too advanced for a third-year student."

"The dangerous criminal looking for me probably won't restrict himself to third-year spells."

The twitch disappeared. Harry held his gaze.

"Is he looking for me?"

Snotfoot returned to the entrance. Harry bought every book.

By noon, Diagon Alley had become crowded.

Families moved between shops carrying school lists and arguing children. Owls called overhead. A crate of self-stirring cauldrons clattered outside Potage's while two assistants chased one which had rolled into the street.

Harry carried his purchases to Florean Fortescue's.

The tables outside the shop were nearly full. He claimed the smallest one beside the fountain and ordered a sandwich, pumpkin juice and a dish of chocolate-and-raspberry ice cream.

Snotfoot sat three tables away. Harry removed the wrapped toast from his pocket. He felt a twang of disappointment as he unravelled it from the paper. The toast- or rather what was left- was mushed-up crumbs.

He stared at it.

The action felt absurd now. The Leaky Cauldron kitchen stood minutes away. Harry possessed enough money to purchase lunch for everyone at the café. Still, throwing food away remained impossible.

He fed the pieces to a group of sparrows beneath the table. Florean delivered the ice cream himself.

"Mr Potter."

Harry prepared for questions. Florean merely placed the dish down.

"Studying history this year?"

"I don't know."

"You should. History is less useless when taught by someone with a pulse."

Harry smiled.

"Don't tell Professor Binns."

"He stopped listening in 1892."

Florean returned to the counter.

Sunlight moved slowly down the buildings, warming the cobbles. The fountain splashed nearby. A witch argued with a parchment seller over ink prices while her son attempted to befriend a basket of self-folding aeroplanes.

No one ordered Harry to hurry.

After lunch, he continued down the alley. He bought new quills, parchment and ink. He chose school robes at Madam Malkin's, where the assistant measured his wrists twice because she did not believe the first number. Harry also bought two jumpers, three properly fitted shirts, and trousers that had never belonged to Dudley.

The clothes felt unfamiliar when he tried them on. The shirt seams sat upon his shoulders rather than halfway down his arms. The sleeves ended at his wrists. His reflection looked narrower without the excess fabric, but more deliberate. It felt strange but right.

At Quality Quidditch Supplies, a crowd blocked the front window. He approached out of curiosity. Then he saw the broom.

It hovered above dark blue velvet, rotating slowly. Every line seemed built for movement: a polished ash handle, perfect twigs, and silver footrests folded cleanly against the sides.

A small card stood beneath it.

THE FIREBOLT

THE MOST ADVANCED RACING BROOM IN THE WORLD

There was no price.

Harry leaned closer to the glass. Flying remained the only form of movement which never felt like escape. On a broom, the ground released him without demanding an explanation. There were no doors, corridors or adults deciding where he belonged. Wind left no space for memory.

The Firebolt turned, causing sunlight to travel along its handle. Harry imagined mounting it above the Hogwarts pitch. The fantasy lasted until he noticed Snotfoot reflected in the window behind him.

The Auror was scanning the crowd. Harry felt his perceived freedom shrink slightly.

That evening, Harry returned to Room Eleven carrying so many parcels that Tom helped him upstairs.

"You know school doesn't begin tomorrow?" the barman asked.

"I wanted to be prepared."

Tom looked at the defence books.

"Prepared for what? War."

"Hogwarts."

"Fair enough."

Harry unpacked everything across the bed.

The new clothes went into the wardrobe. Schoolbooks formed a stack beside Hermione's broom kit. He freed the Monster Book, stroked its spine and watched in amazement as it shivered, relaxed and opened peacefully in his hands.

"You could have explained that."

The book's pages rustled. Harry read the first chapter while eating dinner downstairs.

Afterwards, he attempted one of the defensive exercises from the blue book. It involved identifying lingering magical influence by holding one's wand above an object and observing the colour of the response.

Harry tested the potions case, which turned green. Then Hermione's broom kit, which glowed a soft blue. The Monster Book snapped at the wand and produced orange sparks.

Harry tested the torn Hogsmeade form last. Nothing happened. The two halves lay across the desk. Vernon had torn directly through the signature line, though the upper section remained readable.

Harry could ask Professor McGonagall whether another adult could sign. Lucius was a school governor. The Grangers would agree immediately. Hagrid would sign without reading and then accidentally provide his thumbprint instead.

The form specified parent or guardian. Harry folded both pieces together and placed them inside Intermediate Transfiguration. A tap came from the window.

Draconia's owl waited outside. It entered the moment Harry opened the latch and landed upon the desk. The new letter was thick.

Potter,

Father has confirmed that Minister Fudge omitted several important facts from your conversation.

First, Sirius Black has been sighted twice since his escape. The first report placed him south of London. The second is being withheld from the Prophet, which means it is either false or considerably more alarming.

Second, the Ministry believes Black may attempt to reach Hogwarts. Lastly, no one will explain why this concerns you; I wouldn't be surprised if someone lets something slip soon, though.

Father distrusts the official account. That doesn't mean Black is safe. But a government capable of overlooking an enchanted diary inside a school should not be treated as an infallible source of truth.

Do not leave Diagon Alley. We will arrive tomorrow morning.

Below it, in handwriting which belonged to Narcissa:

Dear Harry,

Draconia has informed me that you have eaten and are safely settled. As she received this information from you, I am treating only the second half as confirmed.

Tom will provide anything you require. Please allow him. We shall see you tomorrow.

Let us know if you need anything,

Narcissa Malfoy

Lucius had added a final line.

Be cautious.

Harry laughed; the sudden sound startled the owl.

"Sorry."

He read the three messages again. Concern arrived from the Malfoys in layers: Draconia disguised it as intelligence, Narcissa as instruction and Lucius as two words which left no room for argument.

Harry placed the letter beside Hermione's. Then he noticed the envelope contained something else. A narrow newspaper clipping had been folded inside.

The headline read:

BLACK REPORTED NEAR MANCHESTER

A moving photograph showed a blurred figure crossing behind a row of abandoned buildings. The man's face was not visible, but his posture matched the Azkaban photograph: tall, thin and moving quickly.

The following morning had barely reached nine when the door to room eleven flew open. Harry turned from the wardrobe and was greeted by Hermione as she stood in the doorway. Her hair sat bushy as ever, disturbed by travel, with a canvas bag over one shoulder and an expression made almost entirely from fury and relief.

Behind her, Mr and Mrs Granger were still climbing the stairs. Harry had time to say, "Morning," before Hermione crossed the room and wrapped both arms around him.

The force drove him back one step. Hermione held on. Her hair pressed against his face. One arm circled his shoulders; the other closed around the middle of his back with enough strength to establish that dentistry developed unexpected muscle.

Harry's hands remained awkwardly at his sides as he slowly registered what had happened before he lifted them and hugged her in return. Hermione exhaled, her breath slightly shaking.

"I'm all right," he said.

She pulled away.

"No."

Harry blinked.

"No?"

"You can't say you're fine before I've even seen you."

"What are you going to do, examine me?"

Hermione seized his wrist. Her fingers closed over the place Marge had gripped. Harry flinched, moving his arm back up, trying to play it off as a shiver. Hermione released him instantly; her face changed immediately.

"Did I hurt you?"

"What, no."

"You moved."

"You...Surprised me."

She looked at the faint marks around his wrist.

"Who did that?"

Harry pulled the sleeve down.

"Just Marge."

Hermione's eyes sharpened. "Your aunt?"

"Vernon's sister."

"She grabbed you."

"It was days ago; I probably won't see her again. Don't worry about it."

"That doesn't change who did it."

"I'm aware."

Mr and Mrs Granger finally stepped through the doorway. Mrs Granger stopped when she saw Harry. Her gaze moved over him with a speed which pretended not to be an examination. She saw the new clothes, dark circles and Hermione standing close enough to prevent escape. Then she crossed the room and embraced him too.

Mrs Granger smelled faintly of peppermint and the hand cream she kept at the dental surgery. She touched the back of Harry's head once before releasing him.

"Hello, love."

The word struck somewhere unprotected. Harry smiled because he could not think of another response.

"Hi."

Mr Granger entered more slowly. He did not hug Harry. Instead, he held out one hand. Mr Granger's grip was warm and firm.

"I'm pleased to see you in one piece," he said.

Harry looked at the man's face. Anger lived beneath his calm. Though not directed at Harry. The distinction still took effort to believe.

"Sorry you had to come all this way."

Mr Granger's expression broke. Hermione closed her eyes. Mrs Granger looked towards the window.

"What?" Harry asked.

"Nothing," Mr Granger said.

"That word is becoming overworked."

Hermione looked at him sharply. Harry realised he had borrowed Mrs Figg's sentence. Before anyone could ask, footsteps sounded in the corridor.

Harry knew who was coming before Lucius Malfoy appeared. He entered the room wearing black travelling robes despite the warm morning. His silver-headed cane touched the floor once. Narcissa followed, pale hair gathered behind her head beneath a narrow-brimmed hat.

Draconia came last, stopping in the doorway. Hermione was still beside Harry. Mrs Granger stood near his other shoulder. Mr Granger occupied the space between Harry and the hall.

Draconia's eyes moved across all of them.

"You began without me."

Hermione folded her arms. "You were late."

"We arrived at the appointed time."

"We arrived early."

"That does not make us late."

"It makes you late."

Harry looked between them.

"Hey, Draconia."

Draconia turned towards him. Her composure held for half a second. Then she crossed the room and struck him on the shoulder.

"Ow."

"That," Draconia said, "was for leaving your relatives' house after midnight without a destination."

"I had a destination..."

"One you selected it after leaving."

"That still counts."

"It does not."

She hit him again, more lightly.

"And that was for telling me 'don't panic.'"

"I'm beginning to regret telling you anything."

"You could try telling us before everything goes wrong."

Hermione nodded. "She's right."

"You two agreeing is very unsettling."

Draconia's eyes travelled over his face. The irritation remained, but the edge had changed. Her gaze dropped to his wrist, then to the new clothes and the half-eaten breakfast plate upon the desk.

Harry saw her count the food. Her expression eased by one degree. Narcissa approached. Unlike Hermione and her mother, she did not embrace Harry immediately. She stopped close enough to study him and lifted one hand.

"May I?"

Harry did not know what she intended but nodded anyway. Narcissa adjusted the collar of his new shirt. Her fingers lingered briefly against the fabric near his shoulder before falling.

"You bought this yourself."

"Yes."

"It fits you well."

"That was the idea."

A small smile touched her mouth.

"I am glad."

Lucius remained near the door. His attention had settled upon the marks around Harry's wrist. He looked up. "Who," he asked, "put their hands on you?"

Harry looked down.

"Marge."

"The inflated woman."

"She wasn't inflated when she did it."

Lucius's grip tightened around the head of his cane. Mr Granger watched him.

"Perhaps," Mrs Granger said carefully, "we should all sit down."

Room eleven did not contain enough chairs. Tom solved the problem by opening a private parlour downstairs. The parlour held a long table, eight chairs and a fire which had been allowed to burn down beneath the summer heat. Tea waited at one end. Someone had placed biscuits upon a plate.

Harry sat between Hermione and Draconia.

Lucius removed his gloves.

"Begin at the argument."

Harry looked around the table.

"I've already told everyone what happened."

"You wrote three sentences," Hermione said.

"I got to the point."

"They omitted your aunt grabbing you."

"That happened earlier."

Draconia leaned back. "They also omitted your wand being locked away in violation of the agreement."

"I didn't know it was part of the agreement."

"You should not need a written agreement to retain access to your own property."

"It was in Vernon's house."

The adults went still. Harry frowned.

"What?"

Mr Granger rested both forearms upon the table.

"Nothing stops belonging to you because it enters someone else's house."

"I know."

"Do you?"

The question was gentle. Harry looked at him.

"I took it when I left."

"That is not what Richard means," Mrs Granger said.

Harry reached for a biscuit and changed his mind. Narcissa noticed and subtly pushed the plate closer.

"Tell us about the week."

Harry did. He began with Vernon's bargain concerning the Hogsmeade form. He described Marge's arrival, the dogs and the questions about St Brutus's. The room grew quieter as he continued. Harry kept the account factual.

Facts were safer than reactions. Marge grabbed his wrist. Colonel slept outside his door. Vernon made him miss dinner after the argument about potions. Petunia said Marge did not understand. Mrs Figg admitted she had been reporting to Dumbledore since Harry was little.

He left out the nightmares. He did not mention eating the last biscuit in a park because dinner had ended before he finished. Hermione interrupted anyway.

"You told us they were feeding you."

"They were."

"How often?"

Harry looked at her.

"Every day."

"That is not an answer."

"It is literally an answer."

"How many meals?"

"It varied."

"Harry."

He glanced towards the adults. The question felt too personal for a table of seven.

"Usually two."

Mrs Granger's fingers tightened around her cup.

"Usually?"

"Sometimes three."

"And sometimes one?" Mr Granger asked.

Harry shrugged. Lucius went very still. It was a different stillness from Draconia's. Hers guarded what lay beneath it. Lucius's seemed built to prevent immediate action.

"There was food in the house," Harry added.

No one answered.

"I could have taken something."

"Could you?" Narcissa asked.

Harry remembered the sausage entering the bin.

"If no one saw."

Hermione made a small sound. Harry turned; her eyes shone with furious tears. The sight alarmed him.

"Please don't."

"Don't what?"

"Cry over me."

"I'm not."

"You nearly are."

"Then stop making excuses for them."

"I'm explaining."

"You always explain."

Harry looked away. Draconia's hand rested upon the table beside his. Her fingers had curled inward, nails pressed against her palm.

"Why did you not use the emergency vial?" she asked.

"I wasn't injured."

"Starvation is an injury."

"I wasn't starving."

"No," Hermione said. "You were merely hiding bread from a kitchen that serves food all day."

Harry turned towards her.

"How do you know that?"

"Tom told Mum."

Betrayal flickered. Tom had promised nothing. Harry knew that. The feeling arrived regardless.

"I said it was a habit."

Mr Granger looked down. Narcissa closed her eyes briefly. Harry felt the room's atmosphere become unbearable.

"Can everyone stop?"

No one spoke.

"This is why I didn't want to say anything."

Lucius's gaze lifted.

"Why?"

"Because now you're all here, out of your way because of me."

The answer emerged more sharply than Harry intended.

"Mr Granger's angry. Hermione's crying. Draco looks like she's planning a murder. Mrs Granger and Narcissa keep looking at me like—"

"Like what?" Narcissa asked.

Harry stopped like he was something discovered wounded. Like any honest answer might break beneath handling.

"I don't know."

Draconia looked at him.

"You think we're upset with you."

"I- No."

"You apologised when we all arrived."

"Because you've all travelled."

"We chose to."

"Because of me."

"For you," Hermione corrected. "Not because of you."

"That's the same thing."

"No, it isn't."

The distinction seemed obvious to everyone except Harry. He pushed his chair backwards.

"I'm going upstairs."

Draconia caught his sleeve, her fingers gently touching his skin. Harry felt his heart jump as the motion suddenly registered, his hand cramping. Draconia instinctively pulled back. Her hand released the fabric immediately. Draconia placed both hands in her lap and looked up, her silvery eyes meeting his gaze.

"Please stay."

It wasn't an order, and no insult hid the concern. Harry looked at her. The room remained full of adults and questions. He let himself slide back to the seat again. Narcissa poured tea into an empty cup and placed it before Harry.

"Your relatives' choices have upset us," she said. "You have not."

"I didn't say—"

"You did."

Harry looked into the tea. Steam moved across the surface.

Mr Granger leaned back. "I am angry with the adults who were responsible for you. I am not angry that you told us."

"I didn't tell you much."

"You told enough."

Lucius spoke last.

"Your uncle's failures do not become an inconvenience created by anyone who notices them."

Harry's eyes lifted. Lucius held his gaze. No sympathy softened the words. He delivered them with the same cold certainty he might use in the Wizengamot. Harry found it easier to accept.

"What happens now?" he asked.

"Your return to Privet Drive has been ruled out for the remainder of the summer," Lucius said.

"I gathered that."

"You will remain here under Ministry protection."

"I gathered that too."

"We have requested permission for you to stay elsewhere."

"Professor Dumbledore refused," Hermione said.

Draconia's mouth tightened. "Again."

Harry looked at Lucius.

"Why?"

"The wards established through your mother's sacrifice require a prolonged period of residence with her surviving blood relative each year."

"How prolonged?"

"Dumbledore has supplied several descriptions and no useful measurement."

"Father is taking that well," Draconia said.

Lucius's eyes moved towards her.

"I am pursuing clarification."

"You sent him fourteen pages."

"Eleven."

"Mother removed three."

Narcissa lifted her cup. "They became repetitive."

Harry almost smiled.

"What happens if I don't go back next summer?"

Lucius's expression changed.

"According to Dumbledore, the protection may weaken."

"Against Voldemort."

The name settled heavily over the table. Mr and Mrs Granger did not flinch. Narcissa's fingers tightened around the cup. Draconia looked directly at Harry.

Lucius answered.

"Against anyone acting with his power or intention."

"But he's gone, just a wraith, right?" Harry said.

No one responded quickly. A chill moved beneath his skin.

"That's what Dumbledore told me."

"We do not know what he is," Lucius said.

Harry thought of Tom Riddle stepping from the diary. Sixteen years old. Smiling and becoming solid while Draconia disappeared.

"Not dead enough," he murmured.

Narcissa heard. Her face lost colour. The room remained silent until Hermione reached for Harry's hand beneath the table. The Hogsmeade form was discussed next.

Harry placed the torn pieces upon the table. Hermione reacted as if Vernon had destroyed an original manuscript.

"He tore it?"

"In fairness, I had inflated his sister."

"After he tore it."

"Yes."

"Then it is unrelated."

"Vernon would disagree."

"Your uncle appears to disagree with observable reality whenever convenient."

Draconia drew the two halves towards herself.

"This can be repaired."

"Hogwarts will know," Hermione said.

"Only if repaired badly."

"You cannot forge a guardian's signature."

"I can forge several."

"That does not improve it."

"It improves our options."

Lucius held out one hand. Draconia surrendered the parchment. He examined it.

"As a school governor, I may be able to authorise an exception."

Hermione brightened.

Lucius continued, "It is unlikely."

Draconia's expression flattened. "Then why say it?"

"Because I will not promise what I cannot deliver."

Harry looked towards the form.

"What about the Grangers?"

Mrs Granger shook her head. "We are not your legal guardians."

"Narcissa?"

"The same problem applies," she said.

"Sirius Black can escape Azkaban, but I can't visit a sweetshop because Vernon tore a form."

No one found that funny. Lucius folded the pieces along the tear.

"Black's escape should not be treated as an achievement."

"He did something no one else has done."

"So did you," Draconia said.

Harry looked at her, puzzled. She glanced towards his scar. Lucius placed the form in front of Harry.

"I shall speak to Dumbledore."

"Will he listen?"

"Listening is one of his more developed methods of avoiding agreement."

Hermione looked surprised. Mr Granger concealed a smile behind his cup. For the first time that morning, the tension eased.

Shopping began after lunch. The adults attempted to accompany them. Hermione objected first.

"We know Diagon Alley."

"There is an escaped murderer," Mrs Granger said.

"Two Aurors are following Harry."

Snotfoot stood beside the parlour door, pretending not to listen. Draconia looked towards him.

"One of them reads newspapers upside down."

Snotfoot gave a delayed frown as he adjusted the parchment once again.

"The danger remains," Narcissa said.

"So does the embarrassment," Draconia replied.

Lucius lifted one eyebrow. "You will survive it."

Harry intervened before the discussion became permanent.

"What if they follow farther back?"

Hermione looked dissatisfied. Draconia appeared to regard any visible adult supervision as a diplomatic failure, but both eventually agreed. The trio stepped into Diagon Alley together.

For the first fifty yards, no one spoke about Privet Drive. Harry knew this was deliberate. Hermione walked on his left, her canvas bag striking her hip. Draconia occupied his right, wearing pale summer robes and gloves despite the heat. Neither touched him.

The consideration felt almost louder than conversation. Harry looked towards the cauldron shop.

"So what are we arguing about first?"

Hermione glanced at him.

"What?"

"We've been walking for nearly a minute."

Draconia examined the street. "His concern is justified."

"I do not begin every outing with an argument."

"You told the Auror his posture was increasing our visibility."

"It was."

"You began before we left the inn."

"That was an observation."

"Delivered argumentatively."

Harry nodded. "There we are."

Hermione looked at him properly. A reluctant smile appeared.

"What did you buy yesterday?"

"Books."

"I know that. Tom said you returned with half of Flourish and Blotts."

"Not half."

Draconia tilted her head. "A quarter?"

"Three defensive books plus our school list."

"You already have defensive books," Hermione said.

"Not these."

"Which ones?"

Harry told her. Hermione stopped in the middle of the pavement. A family walked around her.

"You bought Counter-Curses: An Intermediate Guide?"

"Yes."

"It covers fourth- and fifth-year magic."

"That's why it said intermediate."

"You won't understand the theoretical sections."

Draconia resumed walking. "Then someone will need to explain them."

Hermione followed. "That isn't the point."

"It is precisely the point."

"They overlap with two books he already owns."

"Repetition may eventually force the material through."

"I can hear you," Harry said.

Neither acknowledged him.

Hermione adjusted her bag. "Spending money on three versions of the same information is wasteful."

"Spending money on survival is rarely wasted," Draconia replied. "Hogwarts has demonstrated no intention of providing it without supplementary reading."

"You cannot prepare for every possible danger."

"Not with that attitude."

Harry walked between them while the argument continued. The sound settled around him; it was familiar, somehow settling.

Something in his chest eased. They reached Madam Malkin's. A bell rang above the door. Robes in every colour hung from brass rails, some turning gently to display their embroidery. Measuring tapes drifted between fitting platforms like pale snakes.

Madam Malkin emerged from behind a curtain.

"Mr Potter."

Her eyes moved to Draconia.

"And Miss Malfoy."

Recognition brightened her face.

"Still acquainted, then?"

Harry looked at Draconia.

"She follows me."

Draconia removed one glove finger by finger. "He becomes lost without supervision."

Hermione stepped inside. "They met here before either of them understood basic social interaction."

"I understood it," Draconia said.

"You insulted his clothes."

"They were dreadful."

Harry looked at his new shirt. "She wasn't wrong."

Madam Malkin laughed and directed them towards the fitting platforms.

Hermione had outgrown her sleeves. Draconia required adjustments to the waist of her winter robes but refused to admit why until Madam Malkin informed her that twelve-year-olds were supposed to grow.

Harry's measurements caused a different reaction. The tape circled his chest twice, then tightened. Madam Malkin frowned.

"You've lost weight since June."

Harry looked towards Hermione. She had heard.

"So has Draconia."

Draconia stood upon the neighbouring platform.

"No, I have not."

Madam Malkin checked her notes. "Half an inch at the waist."

"That is measurement error."

"You were measured three times."

Draconia's expression became distant. Harry recognised the retreat. Not embarrassment about size. Something else. He looked at the curtain behind her platform. It had been drawn nearly closed while Madam Malkin pinned the hem. Draconia kept glancing towards the narrow opening. Harry stepped down from his own platform and crossed the shop.

He pulled the curtain fully back. Draconia looked at him.

"Better light," he said.

Sunlight from the window reached the fitting area. Her shoulders lowered, and her stiff posture seemed to ease.

Madam Malkin resumed pinning.

Flourish and Blotts took nearly two hours.

Hermione's school list was covered in annotations. Beside the required books, she had written prices, edition preferences and the order in which each shop section should be visited.

Draconia examined the page.

"You have scheduled browsing."

"Otherwise we become distracted."

Harry looked towards a display entitled ONE HUNDRED SPELLS TO IMPROVE YOUR SOCIAL STANDING.

"What if something deserves distraction?"

"Then we alter the schedule."

Draconia's gaze moved down the list.

"Ancient Runes, Arithmancy, Care of Magical Creatures, Divination and Muggle Studies."

"Yes."

"You selected every elective."

Hermione took the list back. "I'm interested in all of them."

"Three occur at the same time."

"The timetable may be adjusted."

"By whom?"

"Professor McGonagall."

Draconia looked at Harry.

Harry shrugged. "Hermione frightens clocks."

"I do not."

A book fell from the shelf behind her.

Hermione turned.

Harry lowered his voice. "See?"

Draconia's mouth twitched. Hermione purchased enough books to strain the bottom of her bag. Several belonged to subjects not listed for third years. When Harry asked about them, she explained that introductory reading would prevent inefficient adjustment later.

A sealed envelope protruded from one volume. The Hogwarts crest marked the front.

Draconia saw it.

"What is that?"

Hermione pushed the envelope deeper into the book.

"Nothing."

Harry looked at her.

"That word is becoming overworked."

Hermione's cheeks coloured. "Professor McGonagall wrote regarding my timetable."

"What did she say?" Harry asked.

"That arrangements are being considered."

"Which arrangements?" Draconia pressed.

Hermione reached for another book. "Private academic arrangements."

Draconia's eyes narrowed.

Harry stepped between them, holding A Beginner's Guide to Hippogriff Etiquette.

"Do you think they really become offended by eye contact?"

Neither answered.

"Hermione?"

"You're supposed to look it in the eye."

"Draco?"

"They are large birds with the emotional regulation of Pansy Parkinson."

Hermione took the book from him. "You cannot compare a magical creature to a classmate."

"I have met both."

"You haven't met a hippogriff."

"I might change my mind when I see one."

Harry left them arguing and selected his remaining textbooks. At the till, Hermione discovered his advanced defensive purchases.

The debate began again, following them into the street. The Magical Menagerie seemed to announce itself through noise. Birds shrieked within the front window. A purple toad blew bubbles from one nostril. Several white mice performed a skipping routine upon a miniature stage while a large tortoise with a jewel-encrusted shell attempted to eat the sign describing it.

Hermione stopped.

"We're only looking."

Draconia regarded the window. "People say that before purchasing animals."

"I'm not purchasing anything."

"You brought a carrier."

Hermione looked down. A folded wicker basket hung from her bag.

"That is unrelated."

Harry smiled. "Of course."

Inside, the shop smelled of straw, food and every species present. Cages occupied the walls from floor to ceiling. Cats slept inside baskets, ravens watched customers with unpleasant intelligence, and a pair of lizards changed colour whenever anyone spoke.

Hermione moved directly towards the cats. Draconia remained near the entrance; Harry noticed her scanning the shop.

The habit matched his own too closely to ignore.

"Expecting something?" he asked.

Draconia looked at him.

"Crowded shops are inefficient for shopping."

"That wasn't what I asked."

"A rare moment of comprehension."

Harry waited. Her hand remained near the pocket containing her wand. Before he could press, an orange blur launched from beneath a cabinet.

It struck Hermione's legs. She cried out and caught the counter. The animal landed, turned and sat. It was either a very large cat or a small lion assembled incorrectly. Ginger fur covered a broad body. Its legs bent outward. Its face appeared to have collided with a wall and accepted the result as permanent, while its tail expanded behind it like a feather duster under enchantment.

Hermione stared, her eyes narrowing as if she was inspecting whether the animal had done wrong or not. The cat stared back. Draconia moved beside Harry.

"That creature is defective."

The cat's yellow eyes shifted towards her.

"Don't insult it," Harry said.

"It cannot understand me."

The cat hissed. Draconia drew one foot backwards.

"Coincidence."

Hermione crouched. The cat approached her immediately. It rubbed its flattened face against her hand, then climbed halfway into her lap before she had agreed.

The shopkeeper appeared from behind the rear curtain.

"Oh, that's rare; he likes you."

Hermione scratched behind one orange ear. "What's his name?"

"Crookshanks."

Draconia looked at Harry. "That's not weird at all."

"He's been here for years," the shopkeeper continued. "Very particular."

Crookshanks began purring. The sound resembled a small engine. Hermione's expression softened.

Harry knew the decision had already been made.

"You brought the carrier," he said.

"I bring it whenever we visit animal shops."

"How often does that happen?"

"Twice."

Draconia folded her arms. "A longstanding policy."

Crookshanks turned suddenly. Every hair along his back rose. A customer had entered carrying a wire cage. Inside it, three ordinary grey rats shifted beneath the straw.

Crookshanks sprang from Hermione's lap. The cage owner shouted. Rats squealed. Crookshanks struck the wire with both front paws, flattening his ears and making a sound far too deep for his body.

The shopkeeper seized him around the middle.

"No!"

Crookshanks twisted, eyes fixed upon the rats. The largest rat pressed itself into the far corner. Hermione helped pull the cat away.

"He doesn't like rats," Harry observed.

"He's a cat," Draconia said.

"That seemed personal."

Crookshanks continued staring until the customer left. Then he returned to Hermione and sat upon her foot. Draconia looked down at him.

"You are not bringing that animal onto the train."

Hermione stroked his head. "He'll be in the carrier."

"He attempted murder within thirty seconds."

"You called him defective."

"And he proved me correct."

Crookshanks swiped at the hem of Draconia's robes.

Outside the shop, the wicker basket shook violently while Crookshanks objected to confinement. Draconia moved several feet away.

"You know," Harry said, "he might like you eventually."

"I do not need approval from malformed livestock."

A ginger paw emerged between the wicker slats. Draconia stepped farther away. Hermione smiled with unbearable satisfaction.

Lunch became a negotiation between Hermione's principles, Draconia's standards and Harry's determination to eat somewhere before either found a new shop.

They returned to a cafe that sat next to Florean Fortescue's.

The adults occupied a larger table several yards away. Mr Granger and Lucius were discussing something with such rigid politeness that Harry suspected each sentence concealed an argument. Narcissa and Mrs Granger seemed to speak more easily. Proudfoot sat near the fountain pretending to admire the architecture.

The trio took a smaller table. Crookshanks's basket occupied the fourth chair. Hermione ordered tomato soup and bread. Draconia chose a salad after interrogating the waiter about six ingredients.

Harry ordered roast chicken, potatoes and chocolate-and-raspberry ice cream.

Harry, who felt rather pleased with his order, looked back to the table only to be greeted by Hermione's piercing gaze.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You're doing it again."

"Doing what?"

"Judging what I order."

"I was not."

Draconia unfolded her napkin. "You were."

Hermione turned. "You counted his breakfast."

"I observed it."

"You moved the plate closer when he stopped eating."

"The table was uneven."

"It was not."

Harry looked between them.

"You realise discussing whether you're watching me still counts as watching me?"

Both fell silent.

"Excellent," he said. "Growth."

When the food arrived, Harry ate most of it before the day started going wrong. Halfway through lunch, a tray crashed inside the shop. Draconia was on her feet before the plates finished striking the floor, her wand appearing in her hand immediately. Conversation at nearby tables stopped.

The waiter responsible stared at the broken dishes. Draconia stood frozen; the colour had left her face. Her wand pointed towards the shop entrance, but her eyes were not focused upon anything in the café.

Harry stood, reaching out a hand for her wand arm.

"Draco."

She did not respond. Hermione rose slowly.

"Draconia."

The second time seemed to reach her. Draconia looked at the wand, then at the watching customers.

She lowered it.

"I—he deserved it," she said.

Her voice was too controlled. She sat stiffly, reminding Harry too much of last year. The thought made him uneasy. The waiter began collecting broken plates. Noise gradually returned around them.

Harry glanced towards the adult table. Narcissa's gaze remained on their table. She remained seated, but her hand gripped the edge of the tablecloth. Lucius was watching his daughter with an expression of carefully contained alarm.

Draconia picked up her fork.

"You don't have to pretend that didn't happen," Harry said quietly.

She looked at him.

"I was startled."

"You nearly cursed a tray."

"It was moving aggressively."

Hermione's mouth opened. Harry caught her eye. She closed it. Draconia stared at the salad. Harry lowered his voice.

"Was it the sound?"

"No."

"Someone behind you?"

Her fingers tightened around the fork. Harry knew he was right.

The diary had approached Draconia through secrecy. Tom Riddle had lived inside her private thoughts, speaking when no one else could hear. By the time she understood what he was, he no longer needed permission to enter.

"Does it happen often?" he asked.

Draconia's eyes rose.

"Does your scar hurt often?"

Harry sat back.

"That isn't an answer."

"Neither was yours."

Hermione looked between them. "Both of you could answer."

"No," they said together.

Hermione folded her arms. Harry picked up a potato and readjusted his posture. Draconia resumed eating.

After lunch, Narcissa took Hermione and Mrs Granger to examine school supplies in a new stationery shop. Draconia went with them after extracting a promise from Harry not to leave the street, approach unfamiliar animals or purchase anything capable of poisoning him.

"That removes most of Diagon Alley," Harry said.

"Do your best."

Lucius remained outside Quality Quidditch Supplies.

Harry noticed him standing beside the window, eyes fixed not upon the Firebolt but the reflections moving across the glass.

Mr Granger had gone to exchange Muggle money at Gringotts. Harry approached.

"Waiting for someone?"

Lucius looked at him.

"No."

"That seems suspicious."

"It is possible to stand without plotting."

"Draco says otherwise."

"My daughter has inherited an unnecessarily theatrical understanding of politics."

"She said she got it from you."

Lucius ignored that. His gaze moved towards Harry's parcels.

"More books?"

"Hermione made me return one."

"Which?"

"Household Hexes for Hostile Visitors."

"A poor choice."

"She said it was irresponsible."

"It is poorly written."

Harry smiled. Lucius turned away from the window.

"Walk with me."

It sounded like an instruction. Harry fell into step anyway.

They moved north along the Alley. Crowds parted subtly before Lucius, responding to his expensive robes, cane and expression without consciously deciding to. Proudfoot followed at a greater distance.

For several shops, Lucius said nothing.

Draconia's silences were sharp and expectant. Lucius's silences made it feel as if someone were preparing a room before anyone entered.

At last, he spoke.

"Sirius Black may not be what the Ministry claims."

Harry looked at him.

"You wrote that."

"I am repeating it because you have a documented habit of disregarding written warnings."

"I kept the letter."

"Well done. Now read it."

"What do you think he is then?"

Lucius's cane touched the stones.

"I do not know."

"That isn't very helpful."

"I would rather tell you I don't know than pretend otherwise."

Harry thought of Fudge's insistence.

"You knew him?"

"The Black family and the Malfoys share numerous social connections. Sirius's mother was my mother's third cousin."

"So you're related."

"Distantly."

"Draco's related to him?"

"Most old magical families are related if one possesses sufficient patience and poor judgement."

Harry considered that.

"What was he like?"

"Loud."

Harry waited. Lucius's mouth tightened.

"Reckless. Arrogant. Deliberately offensive."

"He sounds familiar."

Lucius gave him a cool look. Harry smiled innocently.

"Sirius rejected his family, their politics and most advantages attached to his name," Lucius continued. "He left home while still at Hogwarts."

"Why?"

"Because his parents supported Voldemort."

The name passed through the crowd unnoticed. Harry looked ahead.

"And he didn't?"

"Not publicly."

"That sounds like you don't believe him."

"I believe Sirius Black detested the Dark Lord's ideology. He made that opinion impossible to misunderstand."

"But the Ministry says he served him."

"Yes."

Lucius's tone flattened. The contradiction opened between them. Harry remembered the newspaper photograph. Rage beneath madness—a man who supposedly betrayed his closest friend after abandoning his own family over Voldemort.

"Why didn't anyone say this when he was arrested?"

"There was no trial."

Harry stopped. Lucius walked two more paces before turning.

"No trial?"

"Black was found laughing in a street containing thirteen bodies. Numerous witnesses identified him. He did not deny responsibility in any coherent manner."

"So they sent him to Azkaban."

"Yes."

"Without asking why?"

"The Ministry believed the answer obvious."

Harry looked towards Snotfoot. The Auror pretended to examine a shop sign.

"How can someone go to prison without a trial?"

"That's a long political story, Harry, but fear permits governments to classify speed as justice."

The answer did not sound like something Lucius would have said two years earlier. Harry resumed walking.

"Do you think he's innocent?"

"No."

The certainty surprised him. Lucius continued before Harry could speak.

"I think the accepted account contains gaps. That does not make Black harmless. He may be guilty of a different betrayal, or dangerous for reasons the Ministry has never understood."

"Is he looking for me?"

Lucius studied him.

"I believe so."

A cold knot formed in Harry's stomach.

"Why?"

"That is among the gaps."

They passed beneath a hanging sign. Shadows moved briefly across Lucius's face. Harry touched his wand through his pocket.

"Bloody dog."

"What dog?"

Harry explained.

Lucius listened without interruption. His expression grew harder with every appearance Harry described.

"Fudge said it was probably a stray."

"Fudge once attempted to classify three escaped manticores as unusually aggressive livestock."

"Did it work?"

"One ate the report."

Harry glanced behind them. No dog moved through the crowd.

"Draco said some magical creatures can look like dogs."

"She is correct."

"What creatures?"

"Boggarts. Certain forms of transformed Dark magic. Grim hounds, though none have been documented in Britain for a century."

"Anything else?"

Lucius hesitated.

"Could Black control any of those creatures?"

A reluctant flicker crossed Lucius's expression. It might have been approval.

"Unlikely, given the dog has not attacked you—"

"No, but I mean it did keep approaching me."

"Has it attempted to lead you anywhere?"

"No."

"Has it touched any object belonging to you?"

"No."

"Then it probably was just a stray, or an escaped house dog. But I would suggest continuing to avoid any abnormalities you find."

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"For once, everyone is correct."

They reached the Leaky Cauldron entrance. Harry stopped before the archway.

"Why are you helping me?"

Lucius's face became unreadable.

"With Black?"

"With everything."

Crowds moved around them. A child laughed near the owl shop. Somewhere above, a bell announced the hour. Harry continued.

"You hated my father; you don't exactly seem to agree with most of the politics Dumbledore stands for."

"I disliked your father."

"Snape says that distinction is important."

Lucius's mouth thinned at the name.

"You didn't like me when we met."

"You were wearing atrocious robes and had no understanding of your position."

"That hasn't changed much."

"The robes have."

Harry waited. Lucius looked towards the Alley rather than at him.

"My daughter trusted an object I once failed to recognise as dangerous," he said. "You entered the Chamber when every adult responsible for her remained above it."

Harry's right arm ached.

"I didn't do it so you'd owe me."

"I know."

"Then—"

"Debts are not created only by demand."

Harry frowned. Lucius returned his attention to him.

"You brought my daughter back, Harry."

The words were quiet. No grand declaration followed them.

Lucius had spent the previous year measuring Harry against his name, blood and usefulness. The Chamber reduced every judgement to a simpler fact.

Harry had gone after his daughter. Lucius had not been able to.

"She would have done it for me," Harry said.

"Most likely."

Lucius seemed more troubled by that than reassured. He opened the door to the inn.

"Take care of yourself more."

"I am."

Harry entered before Lucius could inspect whether he obeyed.

Later that afternoon, Harry returned to room eleven and found Hermione and Draconia standing over the Monster Book.

Crookshanks's basket lay open upon the floor, the door open. The cat had escaped.

He crouched beneath the bed, his tail lashing, one orange paw extended toward the book's ridged spine. The Monster Book growled. Crookshanks growled back.

Hermione knelt beside the bed.

"Crookshanks."

The cat ignored her. Draconia stood near the wardrobe holding Harry's pillow defensively. Harry stopped inside the door.

"What happened?"

"Granger released the creature."

"He needed water."

"He immediately attempted to kill your textbook."

"It growled first."

"It is a book."

Harry put down his parcels.

"The book likes being stroked."

Hermione looked at him. "What?"

Harry reached beneath the bed. Both animals turned towards his hand. He stroked the book's spine.

It shivered and relaxed.

Crookshanks sat back, offended by the sudden peace. Hermione stared. Draconia lowered the pillow.

"How did you discover that?"

"The shopkeeper told me."

Draconia looked towards the window. "I may have spent several weeks threatening mine unnecessarily."

Harry laughed.

"You knew how to calm it," Hermione said.

"I knew there was probably a method."

"You told me to stroke it before I opened the package."

"I was off what Hagrid's letter told me."

"It bit through my sleeve."

"Then my guess lacked refinement."

Crookshanks climbed onto Harry's bed. He sniffed the blankets, walked across the pillows and sat upon Draconia's discarded glove.

"Move," she told him.

Crookshanks began washing one paw. Draconia reached for the glove. The cat placed one paw upon it. Harry sat at the desk to watch.

"I think this is going well."

"It has learnt theft," Draconia said.

Hermione picked up the water bowl. "He likes the texture."

"It is dragon hide."

"That explains it."

"No, it does not."

Harry leaned back in the chair.

Sunlight filled the room. Hermione's books occupied half the bed. An orange cat had claimed Draconia's gloves. Their voices moved around him, familiar enough that he did not need to follow every word.

His eyes closed.

"Harry?"

He opened them. Hermione stood beside him.

"What?"

"You fell asleep."

"I blinked."

"For nearly a minute?"

"It was a thorough blink."

Draconia rescued her glove and examined it for damage.

"What time did you sleep?"

Harry glanced at the clock.

"Last night."

"What time?"

"I didn't check."

Hermione looked towards the bed. "You should lie down."

"I'm not tired."

"You fell asleep sitting upright."

"Bad chair."

Draconia touched the chair back. "Solid mahogany."

"Treacherous material."

Neither smiled.

Harry sighed.

"This is what I meant downstairs."

"What?" Hermione asked.

"You're both watching."

Her expression softened. "We're worried."

"I know."

"Then why are you saying it like an accusation?"

Harry did not have an answer. Draconia placed the glove inside her pocket.

"Because being observed feels like losing control."

Harry looked at her. The words carried too much accuracy to concern only him. Draconia turned towards the window before either of them could respond. Crookshanks settled upon Harry's pillow.

"I'm not sleeping while that's there."

"He's clean," Hermione said.

"He attacked a book."

"The book attacked him."

"Those are not mutually exclusive."

Harry lifted Crookshanks. The cat became unexpectedly long in his arms. He purred against Harry's chest, then caught one claw in the collar Narcissa had adjusted.

Harry attempted to free it. Crookshanks climbed onto his shoulder. Draconia laughed. It was brief and unguarded. Harry looked at her with the sharpest gaze he could manage through the discomfort. Hermione covered a smile.

"What?"

"Nothing," Draconia said.

"That word is becoming—"

"Don't you dare."

Harry grinned. For the rest of the afternoon, they unpacked books, attempted homework and argued over whether Crookshanks should be allowed near the Monster Book unsupervised.

The Grangers returned to Oxford that evening.

Hermione remained at the Leaky Cauldron with permission to stay in the room adjoining Harry's. The Ministry approved after Mrs Granger presented Fudge's office with six written questions concerning security and refused to leave until each received an answer.

The Malfoys took rooms at another wizarding hotel two streets away. Lucius described the Leaky Cauldron's mattresses as a historical artefact that should be in a museum.

Draconia remained until dinner. Afterwards, Harry went upstairs to arrange his purchases. Hermione and Draconia stayed in the private parlour, claiming to need space for their books.

Neither opened one. The room had emptied. Firelight moved weakly across the grate, and the last voices from the bar reached them through the closed door.

Hermione sat with Crookshanks in her lap. Draconia occupied the opposite chair. Her back remained straight despite the hour. One glove lay across the armrest, and she had been adjusting the same fastening for nearly a minute.

"He looks worse," Hermione said.

Draconia did not ask who.

"He's taller."

"He's thinner."

"Both can be true."

Hermione stroked Crookshanks's fur.

"He apologised when my parents arrived."

"I heard."

"He apologised because they came to see whether he was safe."

"I heard that too."

"And downstairs, when Dad became angry—Harry looked embarrassed."

Draconia's fingers stopped.

"He thinks we'll be angry if he tells us."

Hermione looked at her.

"What does that mean?"

"He believes that if someone notices he is hurt, the resulting trouble belongs to him."

"That's ridiculous."

"Yes."

"Then why does he believe it?"

Draconia's gaze moved towards the ceiling. Footsteps crossed the room above them. Harry's, probably. Light and uneven, followed by the sound of a trunk lid closing.

"Because every adult in that house taught him to."

Hermione's hand stilled upon Crookshanks.

"He told us there was food."

"There was."

"That doesn't count."

"To him, it does."

Draconia picked up her glove.

"When we first met, his robes were several sizes too large. He said his relatives preferred durability. He did not understand why that was strange until I reacted."

"You insulted them."

"They were offensive."

"They were his clothes."

"I was eleven."

"You're thirteen now."

"My technique has improved."

Hermione nearly smiled. The silence which followed was less hostile. Crookshanks stretched across her knees.

"Do you think he knows?" she asked.

"Knows what?"

"That people care about him."

Draconia looked towards the door.

"He knows the words."

"That wasn't what I asked."

"No."

The answer came quietly. Hermione lowered her gaze to the cat.

"I try to tell him."

"So do I."

"You usually hide it inside an insult."

"He usually listens to those."

"That is because you trained him to."

"Granger."

Hermione's mouth twitched.

Draconia continued, "The problem is not a lack of information. His understanding of it may be damaged."

"You make him sound like a broken instrument."

"He walks into danger, forgets meals and apologises when other people are cruel to him. Something is clearly wrong with the mechanism."

"That might be deliberate stupidity."

"It may be damaged."

Hermione laughed, causing Crookshanks to open one eye and make a dissatisfied noise before shutting his eye again. Draconia looked satisfied. Then Hermione's expression became serious again.

"He's afraid of us watching."

"Yes."

"But if we don't, he hides everything."

"Yes."

"What are we supposed to do?"

Draconia examined her glove's fastening.

"I don't know."

Hermione stared. Draconia rarely said those words. She would offer an incomplete theory, a strategic delay or an insult directed towards the question. Admitting ignorance seemed to cost her.

"We can't force him," Hermione said.

"No."

"We did today."

Draconia's jaw tightened.

In the café, the tray had struck the floor. Her wand had appeared before she thought. Everyone looked at her. Harry asked what happened, and when she refused to answer, he stopped.

He had offered her the boundary he didn't know how to ask for himself. Draconia looked at Hermione.

"We need to become better at waiting."

Hermione frowned. She hated waiting. Draconia could see it in the immediate tightening of her shoulders. Waiting meant allowing unknowns to exist without attacking them with research.

"What if something gets worse while we wait?"

"Then we notice."

"We already notice."

"We can notice. We don't have to make him feel worse about it."

Hermione absorbed that. Above them, the floorboards creaked. Harry moved once more across Room Eleven.

"He bought a book on magical possession," Hermione said.

Draconia went still.

"I saw it in his parcel."

"He did not mention that."

"Perhaps he bought it for himself."

The possibility was absurd. Both knew it. Draconia looked towards the fire.

"I don't need it."

"I didn't say you did."

"You were about to."

"No. I was going to say he's worried about you."

Draconia's fingers closed around the glove. Hermione continued gently.

"You aren't sleeping either."

"You don't know that."

"You left breakfast before eating yesterday. You nearly cursed a waiter today. You won't stand with your back to a door."

"That last habit is sensible."

"You keep checking your hands."

Draconia looked down. She had not realised she was doing it. Her fingers rested upon the chair arm, pale and entirely her own.

The diary had used those hands.

It had written through them, opened doors with them and raised her wand against Harry. Some mornings, Draconia still woke with ink beneath her fingernails and spent several breathless seconds searching the room for words she did not remember writing.

The ink always came from ordinary homework. Knowing this never shortened the fear.

"Hermione."

Draconia rarely used her first name. Hermione understood the warning.

She lowered her voice. "I'm worried about you too."

Draconia's throat tightened. She looked away before Hermione could see.

"That seems inefficient."

"Probably."

"We cannot both monitor Harry if you divide your attention."

Hermione stroked Crookshanks again.

"I think we'll manage."

Neither girl said anything further. The fire sank lower.

On the table between them, Hermione's sealed letter from Professor McGonagall protruded from her bag. Draconia's gaze touched it once.

Waiting, she discovered, was unpleasant, but she intended to practise. Lucius and Narcissa returned to their hotel shortly before eleven.

Their suite occupied the highest floor and overlooked Diagon Alley through tall windows charmed against outside observation. The rooms were tastefully decorated, aggressively expensive and still inferior to Malfoy Manor, according to Lucius.

Narcissa removed her earrings before the mirror. Lucius stood near the window with a letter in his hand.

She watched him through the reflection.

"You have read that three times."

"Twice."

"Four."

Lucius folded the parchment. The wax seal had been black. No crest marked it. None was necessary. Narcissa placed the earrings inside their case.

"Nott?"

"Yes."

"What does he want?"

"To meet."

"Don't."

"He claims others will attend."

"That does not improve it."

Lucius moved towards the fire. The letter remained between his fingers.

"Several old families have received similar invitations."

"Old families," Narcissa repeated.

The phrase had once carried pride. Tonight, it sounded like a diagnosis.

"He mentions Black."

Narcissa turned.

"What about him?"

"That the Ministry's attention is conveniently occupied."

"Convenient for whom?"

"He does not say."

"Then he wants you to ask."

"Yes."

Lucius held the letter above the flame but did not release it. Rain began tapping against the enchanted window, though the Alley below remained dry. The suite's weather charms responded to mood unless corrected.

Narcissa crossed the room and touched her wand to the glass; a soft hum filled the room before a sudden flash of light. The rain suddenly stopped hitting the window.

"Sirius would never have served Voldemort," she said.

Lucius looked at her.

"You sound certain."

"I knew him."

"You knew him as a child."

"I knew what our family made him hate."

Lucius turned towards the fire.

"He was found among the bodies."

"You realise Sirius was only ever quiet after something happened. Even when Bella would throw him down the stairs, nothing would make him react, not like that."

Lucius thought of the Azkaban photograph.

"Sirius Black was devoted to James Potter," he said.

"Yes."

"Devotion can sour."

"Not into that."

"You are allowing childhood affection to shape judgement."

"And you are allowing the Ministry to save you the inconvenience of forming your own."

His eyes sharpened. Narcissa met the look.

Years earlier, she might have withdrawn the accusation or softened it into something more acceptable. The Chamber had altered her too.

Their daughter had nearly died because respectable men preferred convenient explanations. Lucius looked at the black seal.

"Nott possessed an object belonging to the Dark Lord."

"A diary he described as worthless."

"Which he planted in Draconia's belongings after I refused him."

Narcissa's face hardened.

They had reconstructed the act from household wards, guest records and the confession Nott made under pressure after the Chamber. He claimed ignorance of the diary's true nature. Lucius believed that much.

Voldemort had distributed dangerous objects without explaining their purpose. His followers had obeyed without understanding the scale of what they carried.

Now Nott wanted to meet while Sirius Black occupied the Ministry.

"No," Narcissa said again.

Lucius placed the letter into the fire. Black wax melted. Parchment curled around orange flame.

"He will not accept silence."

"He accepted using our daughter as revenge."

"He will regret that."

"He already regrets being discovered. That is not the same thing."

The final corner blackened. Lucius watched until the letter became ash.

"What did Harry ask you?" Narcissa said.

He glanced over.

"Why I was helping."

"What did you tell him?"

"The truth."

Narcissa waited. Lucius moved away from the grate.

"I told him he brought Draconia back."

"That is part of the truth."

"I thought it was enough."

"For you?"

He did not answer. Narcissa sat upon the end of the bed.

Lucius's motives had rarely required moral examination. He protected the Malfoy name, expanded its influence and ensured his family emerged advantageously from every arrangement. These principles had survived scandal, war and political reversal.

Then Harry Potter entered the Chamber.

A half-starved boy raised by Muggles had done what Lucius's money, name and connections could not. He saved Draconia without asking what the rescue might earn him.

Lucius disliked debts he could not define. He disliked even more the realisation that obligation was no longer the only reason he cared whether Harry survived.

"Voldemort may be gone," Narcissa said, "but his servants are beginning to look at the empty place he left."

Lucius looked towards the dead fire.

"Yes."

"And Sirius?"

"I do not know."

"Will you tell Dumbledore about Nott?"

"I'm not sure."

Narcissa's expression cooled.

"I want proof."

"You have your certainty."

"Yes."

"You may not receive it before another child is endangered."

The words landed precisely; Lucius met her eyes. Narcissa stood.

"I will not lose Draconia because we waited for proof that satisfied men who were not at risk."

She entered the bedroom and closed the door. Lucius remained beside the fire. Ash shifted across the grate. After several minutes, he crossed to the writing desk and removed a fresh sheet of parchment.

He wrote no greeting.

I require a discreet conversation concerning activity among former adherents of the Dark Lord.

The sentence looked dangerously close to confession.

Then he folded the parchment, sealed it without the Malfoy crest and addressed it to a man who had spent most of the war attempting to place him in Azkaban.

He did not send it. The letter remained inside the locked drawer. For the first time, that felt like cowardice rather than caution.

The final weeks of August settled into a rhythm.

Harry ate breakfast with Hermione most mornings. She arrived carrying books, Crookshanks and more information than the hour required. Draconia joined them before ten, usually accompanied by Narcissa or one of the Manor's security wizards, and objected to the quality of the tea before drinking two cups.

They completed their school shopping slowly.

There was no need to hurry. Harry had already purchased most of what he required. Still, Hermione discovered omissions in his potion supplies, and Draconia rejected his choice of ink because it dried grey and made everything look as though it had been left in the rain.

They visited the Apothecary, where Hermione sneezed into a barrel of powdered beetle eyes and denied doing so. They spent an hour in a shop selling enchanted stationery because Draconia wanted parchment that resisted water, fire, and "Potter's handling." Harry found a quill that insulted poor spelling and followed Hermione around with it until she threatened to snap it.

At Florean's, they worked through the summer menu.

Harry discovered that Draconia hated mint but ate it rather than admit she ordered incorrectly. Hermione became unexpectedly competitive about guessing flavours while blindfolded. Crookshanks stole whipped cream from Harry's bowl and then sat upon his school list.

Harry kept expecting the ordinary happiness to reveal a cost. Whenever Hermione laughed, some part of him imagined the sound ending. Whenever Draconia walked ahead into a shop, his gaze followed until she emerged again.

He felt his sleep improve slowly inside the Leaky Cauldron, though improvement was not the same as peace. He woke two or three times most nights. Sometimes his scar hurt. Sometimes the pipes became the basilisk. Once, he woke kneeling by the door, his wand pointed into the corridor.

He returned to bed before anyone saw. The next morning, Hermione observed that his hand shook while pouring tea. Harry blamed the pot.

Draconia examined it. "The pot appears stable."

"I'm very convincing."

Hermione's schedule became a mystery in its own right.

She spent several afternoons meeting privately with Professor McGonagall at the Leaky Cauldron. When asked, she said only that the school was resolving conflicts in her elective schedule.

Draconia obtained a preliminary timetable from Lucius. Three of Hermione's classes occurred simultaneously. Harry spread the page across the table.

"You can't attend Ancient Runes and Divination at the same time."

"I know."

"And Arithmancy."

"I know."

"That's three."

"I can count."

"Can you be in three rooms?"

Hermione closed her book. "Professor McGonagall is making arrangements."

"What arrangements?" Draconia asked.

"Approved ones."

"What does that mean?"

"That they have been approved."

Draconia looked at Harry.

Harry lowered his voice. "She's joining a travelling circus."

Hermione threw a napkin at him. The sealed envelope disappeared from her bag soon afterwards. Neither Harry nor Draconia discovered where it went.

Draconia's difficulties hid differently. Narcissa had removed the bed curtains and replaced every blank notebook in her room. During the day, she appeared composed. Her robes remained immaculate, her answers sharpened, and her insults arrived without delay.

Still, Harry saw the cracks. Draconia never entered a room without identifying every exit. She avoided reflective shop windows after dark. If a conversation paused unexpectedly, she checked the clock, confirming how much time had passed.

One afternoon, Harry found her standing inside Flourish and Blotts with an open journal in her hand.

Every page was blank, and her face had gone white. He approached without speaking. Draconia closed the book.

"Poor quality."

Harry looked at the cover.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

"Right."

She returned the journal to the shelf. Harry did not ask again. He picked up The Mind Under Magic from a nearby display and handed it to her.

Draconia looked at the title.

"I own this."

"I thought you might."

"The author makes several unfounded assumptions about coercive enchantments."

"You read it."

"Obviously."

"Was any of it useful?"

Her fingers tightened around the book.

"Yes."

Harry nodded.

He replaced it upon the shelf.

Draconia glanced at him.

"What did the Healer say about your arm?"

"I haven't seen him."

"You said you would consider it."

"I considered it."

"And?"

"I didn't go."

Her eyes narrowed.

"You are impossible."

"So I keep hearing."

They stood beside the shelf, both of them aware of the bargain hidden inside the conversation.

Draconia spoke first.

"I will see the Mind-Healer Mother selected if you see the curse specialist."

Harry looked at her.

"That feels manipulative."

"It is mutually beneficial coercion."

"Hermione would hate that phrase."

"She need not know."

"Hermione knows everything."

"Not everything."

They both glanced towards the front of the shop, where Hermione was arguing with the cashier about an incorrectly priced Arithmancy table.

Harry held out one hand.

"Fine."

Draconia looked at it.

Then she clasped his hand.

"Fine."

Neither released immediately.

Hermione turned from the counter.

Their hands separated.

She looked between them.

"What did I miss?"

"Nothing," Harry and Draconia said together.

Hermione's eyes narrowed. The word was, indeed, becoming overworked.

On the thirty-first of August, the Leaky Cauldron filled with Hogwarts students.

Trunks crowded the corridors—owls called from every floor. Parents occupied tables beneath the smoke-darkened beams while children compared school supplies, summer stories and rumours concerning Sirius Black.

His name had moved closer to Hogwarts.

The Daily Prophet reported another sighting in northern England. The photograph showed only a thin figure turning into a rain-soaked alley. The article claimed Dementors would guard the school.

No one Harry asked could explain what Dementors were without lowering their voice. Draconia described them as Azkaban's guards. Hermione found three books which called them creatures that consumed hope.

Lucius said only, "Do not allow one to approach you."

"That advice applies to everything this year," Harry replied.

Lucius did not smile.

That evening, Hermione and Draconia helped Harry pack room eleven. Help quickly became criticism.

Hermione arranged books by subject. Draconia rearranged them by likelihood of emergency use. Harry put the two defensive volumes on top, which satisfied neither system.

Crookshanks crawled inside the trunk. Harry had lifted him out 5 times before giving up. The large cat only moved when the Monster Book occasionally growled.

Eventually, Draconia sat upon the trunk lid before either could enter.

"This has become undignified."

Harry looked at the orange tail protruding beneath her robes.

"Very."

She stood abruptly. Crookshanks emerged and claimed Harry's pillow.

Hermione gathered the remaining parchment. "We need to leave by half past nine."

"King's Cross is fifteen minutes away," Harry said.

"With ordinary traffic."

"We aren't driving."

"Magical delays occur."

Draconia folded one of Harry's jumpers. "Granger intends to arrive early enough to watch the platform being constructed."

"I intend not to miss the train."

"Has that happened often?"

"Once."

"It's not like anyone could get locked out of the platform, Hermione," Harry said.

"Never say never, Potter."

Hermione placed the parchment into his bag. "We're getting there early."

Draconia looked towards Harry. "Precisely on time."

Harry sat upon the edge of the bed.

"What if I arrive separately and meet you both in Scotland?"

"No," they said.

The answer came with perfect unity. Harry smiled. Neither girl appeared aware of what they had done. Crookshanks turned towards the window.

After several hours of determined procrastination, they finished. Night had filled the courtyard. Lamps glowed in the windows opposite, and roof tiles shone faintly beneath the moonlight. Hermione carried Crookshanks to the adjoining room, and Draconia left for her hotel. Harry finally collapsed onto his bed, staring at the cracked plaster peeling from the ceiling. With a sigh, he let the day's tension drain from him and stretched his legs beneath the patchwork sheets.

Tomorrow he would return to Hogwarts, where he could throw himself into school and put the summer behind him.
