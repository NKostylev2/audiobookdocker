By late afternoon, rain had begun to streak the compartment window. Harry wiped a clear patch with his sleeve and watched the fields darken, their stone walls appearing and vanishing through the mist.

For a while, the motion carried everything with it. The conversation, the quieter spaces between it, the shifting light that slipped across the compartment windows and faded again. It became difficult to tell where one moment ended and the next began. Beyond the glass, the countryside stretched wider with every mile, the last traces of the city dissolving into open fields edged with hedgerows, small clusters of trees, and distant hills that rolled gently beneath a pale, cloud-drifted sky.

Harry found himself watching it for longer than he meant to, his gaze following the lines of fences and the occasional winding road that disappeared into the distance. There was something about the movement of it that made it easier not to think too closely about anything else, as though the world outside carried his thoughts away before they could settle.

Hermione, meanwhile, had her book open again, though she was not turning the pages nearly as often as she usually did. Every now and then, her attention would drift, her eyes lifting toward the window or flicking briefly toward Harry, as though she were only half-reading and entirely aware of everything else.

Draconia sat beside Harry and placed her books upon the desk. The slim green notebook remained concealed beneath them.

Lockhart entered three minutes late.

His robes were lilac. His smile was immediate.

"Welcome," he announced, "to what I have no doubt will become your most memorable subject."

Draconia glanced at Harry. "That does not promise usefulness."

Lockhart distributed a lengthy examination.

Harry turned over the parchment.

1. What is Gilderoy Lockhart's favourite colour? 2. What is Gilderoy Lockhart's secret ambition? 3. What, in your opinion, is Gilderoy Lockhart's greatest achievement?

The questions continued for three pages.

Harry wrote himself beside the third question and left the others blank.

Draconia wrote more quickly.

"What are you putting?" he whispered.

"The same answer for all forty-six."

"What answer?"

"Gilderoy Lockhart."

Harry looked towards her parchment.

She might receive full marks.

Hermione completed every answer. She had read the books and possessed the unfortunate ability to remember information even when she considered it useless.

Lockhart collected their papers.

"Only one student remembered that my ideal birthday gift is harmony between all magical and non-magical peoples—though I wouldn't refuse a large bottle of Ogden's Old Firewhisky."

Draconia looked towards Hermione.

Hermione stared rigidly ahead.

"And now," Lockhart continued, "it is time for practical experience."

He lifted a cage from beneath his desk. Something rattled furiously inside it.

"Freshly caught Cornish pixies."

The creatures were electric blue, sharp-faced and approximately eight inches tall. They crowded against the bars, grinning at the class with alarming enthusiasm.

Lockhart placed one hand upon the latch.

Hermione raised hers. "Professor, shouldn't we know the containment spell before—"

Lockhart opened the cage. The room exploded.

Pixies poured into the air. One seized Neville by the ears and lifted him from his chair. Two more overturned an ink bottle across Pansy's essay. Another tore the tail from one of Lockhart's portraits, causing his painted likeness to flee indignantly into the neighbouring frame.

Students shouted and ducked beneath desks.

Harry dragged Hermione sideways as a stack of books flew past her head.

Draconia drew her wand.

"Immobulus!"

Two pixies froze above the central aisle.

Hermione recovered immediately. She cast the same charm at another cluster while Harry climbed onto a desk to reach Neville, who was now dangling from the chandelier.

Lockhart raised his wand.

"Peskipiksi Pesternomi!"

Nothing happened.

A pixie stole the wand from his hand.

Lockhart watched it disappear towards the ceiling.

"I think," he said, backing towards his office, "this presents an excellent opportunity for independent problem-solving."

His door closed.

Draconia stared after him. "Did our professor just abandon us?"

"He delegated," Harry said.

A pixie struck him in the back of the head with a rolled parchment. The three of them worked together.

Hermione froze the creatures nearest the windows. Draconia drove the remainder towards the cage while Harry freed Neville and wrestled the stolen wand from a pixie attempting to force it through a crack in the ceiling.

It took nearly twenty minutes.

When the final creature had been locked away, the classroom looked as though a storm had passed through it. Papers covered the floor. Curtains hung from one bracket. One portrait had been turned upside down and was refusing assistance on the grounds that no qualified rescuer appeared to be present.

Harry handed Lockhart's wand to Hermione.

"What should we do with it?"

"Sell it," Draconia suggested. "We could recover the cost of his books."

Lockhart emerged only after the room became quiet.

"Excellent work," he said, surveying the damage. "Nothing develops practical confidence quite like an unexpected challenge."

Hermione placed his wand upon the desk.

"You didn't teach us how to contain them."

"You contained them nevertheless."

"You left."

"I was observing your response without the pressure of direct supervision."

"You locked the door."

"To ensure complete independence."

Draconia smiled without warmth. "Remarkable."

Lockhart appeared to mistake this for admiration.

"Yes," he agreed. "It often is."

They left before Hermione said something which earned detention.

In the corridor, she stopped and looked back at the closed classroom.

"He is a fraud."

Draconia placed one hand against her chest. "Granger, I am shocked."

"I mean it. He didn't know what to do."

"He knew how to shut a door."

"That is not Defence Against the Dark Arts."

"It defended him."

Harry began laughing.

Hermione attempted to remain indignant and failed. Draconia opened her satchel. The sudden movement caused a small, thin green notebook to slide out from the other parchment paper and textbooks.

Harry reached out, stopping the items from falling from the table. The small silver letters on the books spine glinting in the light

"New book?"

Draconia's hand stopped. Hermione looked over. Draconia swiped the items and closed the satchel.

"Just parchment."

"It had a cover," Harry said.

"Most books do."

"You said it was parchment."

"I said it was nothing important."

The answer was sharper than the question deserved.

Harry's smile faded. Draconia seemed to hear herself. Her fingers tightened around the buckle.

"My mother packed it," she added. "I haven't decided what to use it for."

"Oh."

Hermione adjusted the stack in her arms. "You could use it for Defence notes. You have plenty of empty pages."

"We have nothing to take notes about."

"That isn't the book's fault."

Draconia looked towards Harry. For an instant, apology appeared close to the surface. Then the corridor filled with students leaving another class, and the moment passed.

September went by faster each day.

The castle returned to its rhythms: bells, meals, essays and staircases which changed direction at precisely the least convenient moment. Rain gave way to several weeks of clear autumn weather. The leaves surrounding the lake turned copper and gold.

For a while, the trio's friendship changed only as the new term required.

Harry and Hermione ate breakfast at the Gryffindor table. Draconia met them beyond the Great Hall or in the library. They shared Potions and Defence, studied together in the evenings, and spent Saturdays near the Quidditch pitch.

Draconia earned the Slytherin Seeker position during the second week. She arrived at the library afterward, attempting to appear unaffected.

Harry saw through it immediately.

"You made the team."

Draconia placed her books upon the table. "Yes."

"You're smiling."

"I am not."

"You were when you came in."

"That was relief."

"What's the difference?"

"One is dignified."

Hermione looked up from her essay. "Congratulations."

"Thank you."

"Did your father purchase the new brooms before or after the trials?"

Draconia's satisfaction dimmed.

"After."

"I was only asking."

"I earned the position."

"I didn't say you hadn't."

"You implied it."

"I asked when the brooms were purchased."

Harry looked between them. "She was better than the other Seeker."

Both girls turned towards him.

"You weren't there," Hermione said.

"I've flown with her."

Draconia's face softened. "Thank you."

"You'll still lose to Gryffindor."

The softness vanished.

"Your loyalty was briefly admirable."

By the end of the evening, they had arranged a bet concerning which of them would catch the Snitch first. Hermione refused to participate and was therefore appointed judge.

It should have remained that simple.

At first, Draconia's absences were easily explained.

Quidditch practice took two evenings each week. Slytherin prefects had begun assigning younger students duties within their common room. Professor Snape occasionally kept her after Potions to discuss advanced preparation, and the distance between the dungeons and Gryffindor Tower made spontaneous meetings difficult.

Then she missed a Saturday in the library.

Harry and Hermione waited for forty minutes before a small parchment bird flew through the doors and unfolded itself beside Hermione's elbow.

Something came up. Tomorrow.

No signature was required.

"She could have said what," Harry remarked.

Hermione turned the note over. "Perhaps it concerns her family."

"She would usually complain about it."

"Not everything requires investigation."

Harry looked at her.

Hermione sighed. "Yes, I understand the hypocrisy."

Draconia appeared the next morning and behaved normally enough that neither pressed her. She insulted Harry's unfinished essay, corrected Hermione's translation of a rune neither of them was studying yet and stole the last piece of toast from Harry's plate.

Three days later, she forgot the meeting entirely.

Harry found her outside an unused classroom on the fourth floor. She stood with one hand upon the door, staring at the wood as though she could not remember whether she intended to enter or leave.

"Draconia?"

She turned sharply. For a second, her expression held no recognition. Then it returned.

"Potter."

"We were waiting for you."

Her gaze shifted towards the window. Evening had already darkened the grounds.

"What time is it?"

"Nearly seven."

Something like alarm crossed her face.

"I was studying."

"In there?"

Draconia looked at the door beneath her hand.

"Yes."

Harry tried the handle.

It was locked. Neither spoke. Draconia removed her hand.

"I must have come out and shut it."

"You didn't know which side you were on."

"That is ridiculous."

"You asked what time it was."

"People occasionally lose track of time."

"Not three hours of it."

Her expression hardened. "You have become remarkably interested in my schedule."

"You missed us."

The words stopped her.

Harry had not meant them to sound accusing. He was not angry, exactly. The afternoon had merely reminded him of unanswered letters, and he disliked how quickly that old uncertainty returned when someone failed to arrive.

Draconia looked away.

"I'm sorry."

It was quiet enough that he almost missed it.

Harry's frustration loosened. "Are you all right?"

"Yes."

He waited.

Draconia's mouth tightened.

"I haven't been sleeping properly," she admitted. "And Quidditch is taking more time than expected."

"Why didn't you say?"

"Because I assumed I could manage being tired without forming a committee."

"It would only be Hermione and me."

"That is a committee."

Harry smiled despite himself.

Draconia did not.

"I'll come tomorrow," she said.

"All right."

"And I will send notice if I can't."

"Two hours beforehand."

"Thirty minutes."

"One."

"Agreed."

She walked with him towards the staircase.

Her shoulder brushed his once as the corridor narrowed. The contact was ordinary, familiar and enough to persuade Harry that whatever had happened outside the classroom could wait until morning.

Behind them, the locked door remained closed.

Potions class was still as miserable as ever.

Professor Snape had assigned a Swelling Solution, writing the order of ingredients upon the board before moving among the cauldrons. Harry was slicing dried nettles while Hermione measured puffer-fish eyes beside him.

Across the aisle, Draconia reached for the bat spleens. Harry looked towards the board. The spleens were added after the solution had been removed from the flame.

Draconia's cauldron was still boiling.

"Draco," he whispered.

She did not react. Her hand moved over the cauldron. Harry kicked the leg of her table. The glass jar rattled against the stone. Draconia looked up. Harry nodded towards the board.

She followed his gaze and saw the instruction. Her face lost colour. Draconia withdrew her hand moments before the first piece fell.

Professor Snape stopped beside them.

His gaze moved from the jar to the cauldron and then towards Draconia.

"Bat spleens are added after the solution has cooled, Miss Malfoy."

"I know, Professor."

"Your actions suggested otherwise."

The classroom had become unusually quiet. Snape did not raise his voice. Somehow that made the correction worse.

Draconia placed the jar upon the table. "It will not happen again."

"No," Snape said. "It will not."

He continued down the aisle. Pansy stared at Draconia as though she had begun speaking Gobbledegook.

Harry waited until the other students returned to their work.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

"You nearly melted your cauldron."

"I was distracted."

"By what?"

Draconia cut the remaining ingredients with unnecessary force. "I can cut a root without you watching me, Potter."

Harry became still. Hermione looked up from the scales. Draconia heard the cruelty a moment too late. Regret crossed her face, but Snape turned at the front of the room before she could speak.

"If the three of you have completed your discussion," he said, "perhaps you might attempt the potion."

Harry returned to his nettles. Draconia said nothing for the rest of the lesson. Outside the dungeon, she caught his sleeve.

"Harry."

He stopped. Hermione waited beside him. Draconia released the fabric.

"I shouldn't have said that."

"No."

"I know you were trying to help. Sorry"

"It's... fine," Harry said quietly. "Just remember next time." She seemed to be waiting for him to make the apology easier.

Harry did not know how.

"What is happening?" Hermione asked.

"Nothing is happening."

"You almost ruined a potion you could brew in your sleep."

"I cannot brew while asleep."

"At present, you don't appear to be doing much else while awake."

Draconia's expression sharpened.

Hermione did not retreat.

Harry stepped between them. "We're worried."

Draconia looked at him.

For an instant, the defensive answer disappeared. She looked exhausted and younger than she ever allowed herself to appear.

Then her hand moved towards the satchel at her side.

"I know," she said.

"Then tell us."

"I can't."

The words emerged before she seemed to decide upon them. Hermione went very still. Draconia's fingers closed around the buckle of her bag.

"I mean there is nothing to tell. I'm tired. I made a mistake, and now both of you are behaving as though I've been cursed."

"Have you?" Harry asked.

Her gaze snapped towards him.

"No."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes."

Too quickly.

Draconia stepped backwards.

"I have another lesson."

"So do we," Hermione said.

"In the opposite direction."

She turned and walked away.

Harry watched until the corridor swallowed her.

Hermione folded her arms. "She is hiding something."

"That was obvious."

"She lied."

"That was also obvious."

"We made a rule."

Harry looked towards the corner around which Draconia had disappeared.

"Perhaps she doesn't think it counts yet."

"Then what was the point of making the rule?"

Harry knew that.

Knowing did not tell him what to do about it.

By Halloween, the castle had begun to smell of woodsmoke and cold stone.

Enormous pumpkins appeared around the Great Hall. Live bats slept in the rafters during lessons and filled the corridors at dusk. Suits of armour had been bewitched to sing whenever students passed, though most knew only the opening verse of the same funeral march.

Harry enjoyed it.

Quidditch practice had begun in earnest, his schoolwork remained manageable and the Gryffindor common room grew warmer every evening as the weather outside worsened. For the first time since the summer, several consecutive days passed without Harry thinking about Privet Drive.

Only Draconia prevented the term from feeling entirely right.

She still met them. She still laughed occasionally, argued with Hermione and promised to defeat Harry during their first match. Yet every interaction seemed to require an arrangement now, and half those arrangements changed before they occurred.

Sometimes she stared past them in the middle of a conversation.

Sometimes she looked startled when Harry said her name.

She always had an explanation.

None were quite convincing.

By ten past eight, the third chair at their usual library table remained empty.

Harry had left his Potions essay open beside it, complete with several corrections he knew Draconia would object to. Across from him, Hermione turned a page, paused, and quietly turned it back again. Neither mentioned the unopened books or the chair between them.

Their usual table stood in a narrow recess between the windows and the shelves devoted to medieval enchantments. Rain tapped steadily against the glass, blurring the grounds beyond into washes of grey and green. The fire near Madam Pince's desk had not yet warmed the entire room, leaving the stone floor cold beneath their shoes and the air faintly scented with dust, parchment and damp wool.

"She may have forgotten."

"Draconia doesn't forget things."

"She forgot to bring her Transfiguration notes last week."

"She left them in her dormitory. She knew where they were."

"That is generally how forgetting something works."

Harry looked towards the library entrance again.

A pair of Ravenclaws entered carrying an umbrella, which continued raining upon them despite being closed. Madam Pince hurried from behind her desk and demanded they remove it before it damaged the books.

Draconia did not appear behind them.

After twenty minutes, Hermione closed her book.

"She might have Quidditch practice."

"On Halloween morning?"

"The Slytherin captain doesn't seem particularly concerned with seasonal observances."

"She would have sent something."

Hermione did not answer.

Harry looked down at his essay. One drop of ink had gathered at the point of his quill and fallen onto the parchment, spreading across a paragraph Draconia would undoubtedly have described as poorly structured.

By the time they left for breakfast, the opposite chair had never been occupied.

At lunch, Draconia's place at the Slytherin table remained empty.

Harry saw it the moment he entered the Great Hall. Pansy Parkinson sat on one side of the space and Millicent Bulstrode on the other, leaving a narrow stretch of bench between them. An untouched plate had appeared before the vacant seat.

"She may be ill," Hermione said.

Harry remained standing at the end of the Gryffindor table. "Madam Pomfrey would know."

"We cannot interrogate the hospital wing."

"We could ask."

"That is a less alarming verb."

They sat.

The enchanted ceiling overhead reflected the weather outside. Heavy clouds hung low above the floating candles, and occasional flashes of pale light moved somewhere within them. The sound of the rain was lost beneath conversation, plates and the scrape of benches across the floor.

Harry served himself without looking down.

His attention remained across the hall.

Pansy said something which made Millicent laugh. Neither appeared worried by Draconia's absence. That should have been reassuring. Instead, Harry found himself wondering whether they had noticed she was missing at all.

"She was meant to help me with the Potions essay," he said.

Hermione glanced towards the parchment beside his plate. "You finished that last night."

"She doesn't know that."

"You deliberately left mistakes for her to find?"

"I didn't leave mistakes. I left opportunities for criticism."

"That sounds like the same thing."

"She enjoys it."

Hermione examined his face, and something in her expression softened.

"You wanted to see whether she would come."

Harry reached for his goblet. "She said she would."

Hermione followed his gaze towards the Slytherin table. The empty place had begun to feel too distinct, a gap around which the rest of the table had unconsciously arranged itself.

"We'll find her after the feast," Hermione said.

"Why wait?"

"We have lessons all afternoon, and she may appear before then. If she doesn't, we'll check the hospital wing."

"And then?"

"We ask Professor Snape whether she is in the Slytherin common room."

Harry imagined approaching Snape to ask after his favourite student's whereabouts.

"He'll think we've done something to her."

"We haven't."

"That has never stopped him before."

Hermione had no answer to that.

Harry ate because he knew what would happen if he did not. Hermione would notice, then speak to Narcissa, and Narcissa would somehow arrange for food to pursue him through the corridors.

It had happened once at the Manor.

The sandwich had been relentless.

Even so, he finished lunch without tasting very much of it.

Draconia's place remained empty.

By evening, the rain had stopped.

Clouds still crowded the enchanted ceiling of the Great Hall, moving in dark layers across a sharp white moon. The rest of the room had been transformed.

Hundreds of carved pumpkins lined the walls and floated between the candles, their faces glowing gold from within. Some grinned. Others scowled or snapped their carved teeth whenever a bat flew too close. Garlands of red and orange leaves twisted around the pillars, rustling in a wind no one could feel, while clouds of live bats circled the rafters and occasionally swooped low enough to scatter the first-years.

A group of ghosts drifted through the tables. Nearly Headless Nick emerged from the centre of a silver platter carrying his own head beneath one arm, startling a Hufflepuff badly enough that she dropped her fork into her pumpkin juice.

The scent of roasted meat, cinnamon and woodsmoke filled the hall.

Nearly everyone appeared determined to enjoy themselves.

Harry tried.

He laughed when one of the enchanted pumpkins attempted to bite Peeves and lost several carved teeth in the effort. He listened while Hermione explained that the floating lanterns had probably been hollowed using magic because cutting them all by hand would require an unreasonable number of hours. He ate roast potatoes and accepted a sugared apple which briefly attempted to roll away from his knife.

The evening should have felt uncomplicated.

But his gaze kept returning to the Slytherin table.

Draconia's seat remained empty.

Harry had been watching the doors closely enough that he did not believe she had entered. Still, students moved constantly through the hall, and every glimpse of pale hair made him sit straighter before revealing itself to belong to someone else.

Hermione caught him looking for the sixth time.

"She might have gone to sleep."

"She doesn't sleep."

"She does occasionally."

"You know what I mean."

Hermione placed her fork beside her plate.

"She said she hadn't been sleeping properly. Perhaps Madam Pomfrey gave her something."

"Then why didn't she tell us?"

"Because she did not know this morning that she would miss the feast."

"She missed the library too."

"I know."

Harry looked at her.

Hermione's confidence had thinned. She was worried now, though she continued arranging facts between herself and that worry as if enough reasonable possibilities might keep it contained.

"We'll check the hospital wing first," she said.

"And then?"

"The Slytherin common room."

"We don't know where it is."

"We know it's in the dungeons."

"That describes most of the dungeons."

"We can ask a Slytherin."

"Most of them don't like us."

"Then we'll ask Professor Snape."

Harry looked towards the staff table.

Snape sat with one hand around his goblet, watching Gilderoy Lockhart attempt to charm a decorative pumpkin into resembling his own head. The pumpkin had acquired Lockhart's hair but retained its original crooked teeth.

Lockhart frowned and tried again.

Snape's expression suggested that he considered the pumpkin an improvement.

"He seems approachable," Harry said.

Hermione followed his gaze. "Professor McGonagall, then."

"She'll ask why we're searching for a Slytherin after dinner."

"We'll tell her."

"That we think Draconia has been acting strangely and might be missing?"

"Yes."

"She'll ask what we mean by strangely."

"Then we explain."

Harry thought of Draconia standing outside the locked classroom, uncertain which side of the door she had occupied. He thought of her nearly dropping the wrong ingredient into a boiling potion and the exhausted defiance upon her face when they asked what was wrong.

He thought of the green book glimpsed inside her satchel.

"Do you think it's the notebook?" he asked.

Hermione looked at him sharply. "Why would it be?"

"I don't know."

"You saw it once."

"And she lied about what it was."

"She said it was parchment and then admitted it was a notebook."

"That's called lying."

"It might have been private."

"Draconia doesn't usually become private until after explaining why something is none of our business."

Hermione considered that.

"We'll ask her," she decided. "When we find her."

Dessert appeared before them.

Platters of treacle tart, bowls of fruit and elaborate cakes pushed the dinner dishes aside. A great pudding shaped like a gravestone rose from the centre of the Gryffindor table, only for a sugar skeleton to climb out and begin dancing along the edge.

Harry reached for the treacle tart.

Come.

His hand stopped above the platter.

The word did not belong to any nearby conversation.

It was faint and rough, dragged out until the sound seemed to scrape against the inside of his skull. For one disorienting instant, Harry thought someone had leaned close behind him and spoken directly into his ear.

He turned.

Neville was watching the sugar skeleton. Two older Gryffindors were arguing across the table. Further along, someone had begun singing along with the suits of armour in the entrance hall.

No one was looking at Harry.

Hermione noticed his stillness. "What?"

"Did someone say something?"

She glanced around. "Several hundred people are saying something."

"No. It was different."

"Different how?"

Harry listened.

The ordinary sounds of the feast pressed around him: laughter, cutlery striking plates, bats rustling overhead and Lockhart applauding his pumpkin after it finally acquired his teeth.

Beneath all of it, something moved.

Come to me.

Harry stood so quickly that the bench shifted against the floor.

Hermione's expression sharpened. "Harry?"

The voice was no louder, yet it had changed position. It no longer seemed to hover beside him. It travelled beyond the wall behind the staff table, moving through stone with a slow, gliding purpose.

So long.

Harry stepped away from the bench.

"Where are you going?" Hermione asked.

"I heard someone."

She gathered her robes and followed him. "Who?"

"I don't know."

"That is not a reassuring answer."

"I'm aware."

They slipped from the table and crossed the edge of the hall. No professor stopped them. Lockhart had finally succeeded in charming the pumpkin and was attempting to draw Professor Flitwick's attention to the resemblance, while Professor McGonagall spoke quietly with Dumbledore.

Harry pushed through a side door.

The noise of the feast diminished the moment it closed behind them.

The corridor beyond was empty.

Torches burned within iron brackets, their flames bending in a draught which travelled along the floor. Halloween decorations had been placed here too, though without the Great Hall's candlelight they looked less cheerful. Paper bats twitched upon the walls. A carved pumpkin sat in the corner, its glow too weak to reach beyond its own face.

Harry stood motionless and listened.

Nothing.

Hermione's footsteps stopped beside him.

"What exactly did it say?"

"Something about coming to it."

"That could have been a portrait."

The nearest portraits were empty. Their occupants had left to attend the feast, abandoning several painted rooms with fires still burning and chairs pulled close to tables.

"It wasn't."

"How do you know?"

Harry could not explain.

The voice lacked the shape of ordinary speech. He could understand its words, yet their meaning seemed to arrive before their sound, cold and fully formed within his mind. It had not travelled through the air. It had travelled through something else.

Then it returned.

Hungry.

Harry turned towards the staircase.

"This way."

He began walking.

Hermione caught up within several steps. "You can still hear it?"

"Yes."

"I can't hear anything."

"I know."

"That doesn't mean I don't believe you."

Harry glanced at her.

She had drawn her wand.

They reached the stairs and climbed. Their footsteps echoed through the stairwell, far louder than they should have been after the crowded warmth of the Great Hall. The voice moved ahead of them, threading through the ceiling before sliding towards the first floor.

Harry followed.

They crossed a gallery lined with tall windows. Moonlight fell across the stone in pale rectangles, interrupted whenever clouds moved across the sky. Outside, tree branches tossed in the wind.

Let me tear.

Harry quickened his pace.

"Harry, slow down."

"It's getting away."

"Voices do not get away."

"This one does."

Hermione had to gather her robes to keep from tripping as they hurried around the corner. The gallery narrowed into a passage lined with locked classroom doors. Every portrait here was empty too. Their painted occupants had taken their lamps, leaving only dark rooms behind the frames.

The voice moved above them.

Harry tilted his head, trying to follow it through the stone.

"Is it on the next floor?" Hermione whispered.

"I don't know."

"You said you could hear it."

"That doesn't mean it's using the stairs."

Hermione looked upwards. The thought clearly did not reassure her. They reached the second-floor landing. For a moment, Harry heard only their breathing.

Then the words slid along the ceiling directly above him.

Let me kill.

Harry stopped so abruptly that Hermione walked into his shoulder.

"What?"

He stared towards the corridor ahead.

"It said kill."

The color drained from Hermione's face. Neither moved. Below, the Great Hall doors opened. The first sounds of departing students carried faintly through the castle, still distant enough to feel separated from them by more than floors.

Hermione tightened her grip upon her wand.

"We should get a professor."

Harry knew she was right. Then something moved near the floor.

A cluster of spiders hurried along the skirting board. There were dozens of them, small black bodies streaming from cracks in the walls and joining a single line which fled towards the staircase behind Harry and Hermione.

Hermione watched them disappear.

"They're all going the same way."

"Away from here."

"That isn't normal."

"No."

Ahead of them, a thin trail of water crossed the passage. Torchlight trembled across its surface, turning the reflection of the flame's red and gold. The water emerged from beneath a door further along and spread towards the staircase in shallow, uneven currents.

Harry stepped into it.

The cold passed immediately through his shoe.

"Harry."

"The voice came this way."

"And it said it wanted to kill something."

"Which is why we should find out where it went."

"That is exactly the sort of reasoning rule eight was meant to prevent."

"We're together."

"That does not make this sensible."

"No. It makes it compliant."

Hermione stared at him. Harry continued along the corridor before she could improve the rules retroactively. She followed.

The water deepened slightly near the bend, gathering between the uneven stones. One of the torches had gone out, leaving a long stretch of wall in shadow. Their reflections moved beside them in the dark windows, distorted by old glass.

The voice had vanished.

Harry slowed.

"Hermione?"

"I still can't hear it."

"I know."

"That does not mean it wasn't real."

Harry turned towards her.

She looked frightened. Not doubtful—frightened and trying very hard to arrange the fear into something they could examine.

Harry nodded.

Together, they continued.

Something dark hung near the end of the passage.

At first, Harry thought it was a shadow cast by the final torch bracket. It was too still, too sharply defined against the pale stone, but his mind refused to identify it until they had taken several more steps.

Then the outline resolved.

A tail.

Four rigid legs.

Dark fur lifted permanently along a curved spine.

Harry stopped.

Mrs Norris hung upside down from the torch bracket by her tail.

Water dripped from the tips of her ears. Her body was stiff, every limb fixed in place, and her yellow eyes remained wide open. They reflected the flame burning beside her without blinking.

Hermione's hand closed around Harry's sleeve.

"Is she dead?"

The question emerged almost soundlessly.

Harry moved closer.

Mrs Norris did not react. Her ribs appeared motionless beneath her wet fur, but there was no wound, blood or obvious sign of what had happened to her. She looked less dead than interrupted, as if something had caught her halfway through a movement and removed her ability to finish it.

"I don't know."

"Don't touch her."

"I wasn't going to."

"You were leaning."

"That isn't touching."

"It generally happens immediately before touching."

Harry stayed where he was.

The corridor smelled of wet stone and something sharper beneath it, faintly metallic and unpleasantly sweet. Water continued dripping from the cat, each drop loud within the silence.

Hermione stepped closer without releasing his sleeve.

"This isn't a Full Body-Bind."

"You can tell?"

"A Body-Bind wouldn't hold her eyes like that. And she would have fallen unless someone hung her there afterwards."

Harry looked at the tail looped around the bracket.

The care required to place her there made the sight worse.

Footsteps sounded below them.

The Halloween feast had ended.

The first voices rose from the staircase, cheerful and loud, then changed when the students at the front saw Harry and Hermione standing in the flooded corridor.

Movement slowed.

"What happened?"

"Is that Filch's cat?"

"Why is she hanging there?"

More students arrived behind the first, pushing forward until the passage filled from wall to wall. Their voices faltered into whispers. Those nearest Mrs Norris stopped several feet away, unwilling to move closer.

Harry became abruptly aware of where he stood.

He and Hermione were directly beneath the torch bracket. Water surrounded their shoes. Hermione held her wand, and Harry's hand rested close to his own.

To anyone arriving now, they did not look like people who had simply discovered the scene.

Then Harry noticed the wall.

At first, he saw only a dark streak above Hermione's shoulder.

His gaze travelled upward.

Words stretched across the stone in enormous, uneven letters. They had been written with a dark red liquid which gleamed wetly in the firelight. Heavy drops gathered at the bottom of each stroke before slipping down the wall, leaving long trails behind them.

Harry read the first line.

THE CHAMBER OF SECRETS HAS BEEN OPENED.

A chill moved across his skin which had nothing to do with his wet shoes.

Beneath it, a second sentence had been written in narrower, more violent strokes.

ENEMIES OF THE HEIR, BEWARE.

The corridor fell silent.

Not quiet. Silent.

Even the portraits appeared to understand that something had changed. Those returning from the feast stopped at the edges of their frames, staring out without comment.

Harry did not know what the Chamber of Secrets was.

Some of the older students did.

He saw recognition travel through the crowd. A Hufflepuff girl seized her friend's hand. Two Ravenclaws exchanged a frightened look. Several Slytherins stepped backwards, their expressions changing from confusion into something far more guarded.

Hermione read the words twice.

"What does that mean?" Harry whispered.

"I don't know."

It was rare for Hermione to say those words without immediately following them with a theory.

That frightened him more than the message.

Someone moved through the crowd.

Students protested as they were pushed aside, but the figure continued until Draconia emerged between two older Slytherins.

Harry's relief arrived first.

It lasted less than a second.

Draconia wore her school robes, but they were not arranged with their usual precision. Her tie sat loose at her collar. Pale hair had fallen from whatever fastening once held it and lay tangled around her face. She looked dazed, as if she had been pulled abruptly from sleep and had not yet fully understood where she was.

Her gaze found Harry.

Then Hermione.

Then Mrs Norris.

Every trace of colour vanished from her face.

"Draco?"

Harry's voice seemed to reach her from a distance.

She looked at the writing.

One hand rose towards the satchel hanging at her side before stopping halfway.

"Where were you?" Hermione asked.

Draconia's lips parted.

No answer came.

A strangled cry tore through the corridor.

Filch shoved his way between the students. His shoulders struck two of them aside, but he appeared not to notice. His eyes remained fixed upon the rigid shape beneath the torch.

"Mrs Norris?"

His voice broke upon her name.

The crowd drew back.

Filch reached towards the cat and stopped before touching her. For one terrible moment, his face emptied of anger, suspicion and every other expression Harry had ever seen upon it. He simply stared.

Then he turned.

His eyes found Harry standing nearest the wall.

"You."

Harry stepped backward.

Filch's grief twisted into fury so quickly that the change seemed to seize his entire body.

"You've murdered my cat."

"I didn't—"

"Caught in the act!" Filch shouted. "Standing over her with your wand out!"

"My wand isn't out."

"You did this!"

"He didn't touch her," Draconia said.

Her voice was unsteady, but it carried.

She moved before anyone could prevent her, crossing the water until she stood beside Harry and Hermione. Her shoulder pressed against Harry's. One of her hands closed around his wrist, stopping him from retreating further towards the stained wall.

Filch stared at her.

Draconia lifted her chin.

"He was with Hermione. They found her like this."

"You weren't here!"

"No," Draconia said.

The word cost her something Harry did not understand.

"But I know him."

Hermione moved closer on Harry's other side.

Filch looked between all three of them.

Around them, whispers began to return. The message had already acquired weight beyond the rigid cat hanging beneath it. Students repeated fragments to one another—the Chamber, the Heir, enemies—without knowing what any of them meant.

Harry lifted his eyes to the message.

The dark liquid crawled down the stone, gathering at the ends of the letters before falling into the water around their feet.

THE CHAMBER OF SECRETS HAS BEEN OPENED.

The voice within the walls was silent now. It had led Harry here, spoken to him alone and left him standing beneath a warning he could not understand.

Around him, the corridor slowly found its breath again. Whispers moved through the crowd. Filch stood beneath Mrs Norris, grief and fury twisting across his face. No one stepped closer.

Beside Harry, Draconia's fingers remained locked around his wrist.

At first, he thought she was trying to keep him from moving. Then he felt the tremor passing through her hand and into his own.

Harry glanced at her.

Draconia was no longer looking at Filch. She stared at the writing with a colourless face, her breathing shallow and uneven, as though the words upon the wall had answered a question she had never meant to ask.

"Draco?" he whispered.

Her fingers tightened. She did not look at him.

Above them, another dark drop slipped from the final word and struck the water between their shoes.

The Chamber of Secrets had been opened.
