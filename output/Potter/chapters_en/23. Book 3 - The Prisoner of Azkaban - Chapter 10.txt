Harry stood near the windows and considered leaving. The classroom had not been used in years. She is with him in Gryffindor. You are not.

The sentence remains until Draconia scratches the quill through it. Riddle apologises. That makes her trust him more.

The memory darkened. Draconia sits inside an unused classroom holding the diary shut.

She has decided to tell Harry. A note lies beside her hand.

Meet me after dinner. I need—

Her fingers stop. They lift the quill without permission. The final sentence changes.

I need your help with Potions.

Draconia fights. Her hand folds the note. Her body stands. The diary opens.

You are tired, Riddle writes. Let me help.

Darkness comes. She wakes in the hospital wing. Harry lies beneath white sheets. His arm has been regrown. His hair is untidy against the pillow. He looks tired and annoyed and alive. Draconia tries to speak.

Her mouth smiles. Not hers. Harry sees the difference.

"Draco?"

Her hand draws the wand. Draconia screams inside herself. No sound escapes. The wand points at Harry. Move, she begs.

Her fingers cast. Red light strikes him. His face holds no anger before he falls. Only hurt. Only concern for her.

Riddle uses that concern to move closer. Draconia's body runs. Corridors pass. Students turn. No one stops her.

She is trapped behind her own eyes while another person wears her face through Hogwarts. The Chamber opens. Riddle lays her body upon the stone. He pulls memory from her like thread. Madam Malkin's.

Harry smiling. Hermione arguing. Narcissa adjusting Draconia's collar. Lucius promising the family would never kneel again. Riddle touches each memory and leaves it unclean.

When Harry arrives, Draconia hears everything. She cannot open her eyes. Riddle tells him she is lonely.

True.

He says she fears Harry will choose Hermione. Sometimes true. He says she opened every door willingly.

False.

Almost false. That is worse. Harry fights the basilisk. Draconia feels each strike through the floor. She tries to wake.

Her body does not belong to her. Riddle is dying. The diary breaks. Control releases her slowly. Sound returns first.

Harry crawling through water. His breath. Her name.

"Draco."

He pulls her into his arms. Draconia wants to warn him not to touch her. What if Riddle remains? What if her hand lifts again? Harry holds her more tightly.

"If you die, I'm not going back up there."

Draconia's heart struggles once beneath his hand. She breathes because he has asked.

The memory shifted. Morning beneath the lake. Draconia wakes with her wand pointed at Pansy's bed.

Name.

Age.

Place.

Diary destroyed. Harry and Hermione alive. She says the facts until her own voice sounds convincing. She sees ink move when it does not. She checks potions seven times.

She covers mirrors because sometimes her reflection smiles a second too late. She never tells Harry. He nearly died saving her. Gratitude becomes another demand for silence.

The shared field tightened around the three of them.

Harry felt Draconia's fear when he looked at her after the possession. Every pause became suspicion. Every concern might conceal the question she dreaded.

Is it you?

Harry had never asked. Draconia heard it anyway. Hermione experienced the sharpness Draconia directed towards her and found the insecurity beneath it. Hermione belonged beside Harry in a way House walls denied Draconia. Hermione could reach his dormitory, attend his classes and receive the truths he offered without being asked. Draconia possessed everything Hermione believed made her secure. Neither had understood the other felt replaceable.

The spell carried the knowledge between them without mercy.

Then the memories stopped belonging to one person at a time. The cupboard door opened into the hospital wing. The hospital curtains hung between the pillars of the Chamber. Rain fell through the ceiling of Godric's Hollow while the Time-Turner spun above the cot.

Harry stood in the centre. He was four, eleven and thirteen. His hands changed whenever he looked at them. Hermione lay Petrified beneath a blanket. Draconia stood beside her with red eyes and a wand raised. Lily screamed upstairs. Sirius laughed below. Harry moved towards Hermione.

The floor stretched. He ran. The bed became farther away. Draconia's wand turned towards him.

"Harry," she said in Riddle's voice.

He drew his own. He could not point it at her.

"Stop."

The word filled every room. Nothing obeyed. Hermione appeared beside the cot wearing three sets of robes at once. One version held a book. Another clutched the Time-Turner. A third remained rigid, eyes open and unseeing.

"I solved it," all three said. "I was too late."

Draconia stood behind the cupboard door. Ink covered her hands.

"I let him in."

"No," Harry said.

Vernon's voice answered from the dark.

"Things do not happen for no reason."

The cupboard began closing. Hermione reached one hand through the narrowing gap. Draconia stood beyond her. Harry seized both. The memory fought to separate them.

Their fingers held.

"Five things," Hermione said.

Her voice came from the stairwell, the hospital bed and the classroom at once. Harry could not see five. He saw every room in which he had failed to keep someone safe. Draconia's grip tightened.

"My name is Draconia Narcissa Malfoy."

The Chamber trembled.

"I am thirteen years old."

Riddle's face cracked.

"I am at Hogwarts."

Green light faded.

"The diary is destroyed."

Ink withdrew from her skin.

"Harry and Hermione are alive."

Hermione breathed beneath the hospital curtain. The world steadied. Harry looked at her. She was no longer stone. She held his hand.

"Harry James Potter," she said.

The cupboard became a classroom.

"You are thirteen."

The cot became a chair.

"You are at Hogwarts."

Rain struck real windows.

"Your mother is dead," Hermione whispered. "But she saved you."

The green light vanished. Draconia looked at Harry.

"The Chamber is closed."

The stone pillars broke apart.

"Hermione is awake."

Hermione's fingers tightened.

"I am not possessed."

Draconia's eyes returned to grey.

"You are not alone."

The final sentence did not belong to the ritual. It belonged to both girls. Harry held on. The memories pulled in opposite directions. He saw Hermione trapped outside her own time.

Draconia trapped behind her own eyes. His mother trapped between Voldemort and the cot. Every person he loved became a door he could not keep open.

"No," Hermione said.

Harry did not know which thought she answered.

"You are not responsible for every door."

The cupboard shook. Vernon turned the lock. Harry was four again. He waited in darkness. Draconia knelt beside him.

She did not fit inside the memory. Her knees pressed against the shelves, pale hair brushing Uncle Vernon's coats.

"He did this," she said.

Harry looked at the door.

"I made the plane move."

"You were four."

"It frightened them."

"So they frightened you more."

The child did not understand. The thirteen-year-old did. Hermione appeared on his other side. Tears covered her face.

"They knew," she said.

"No."

"They knew you were hungry."

"I got food."

"Not enough."

"I had soup."

Draconia made a sound Harry had never heard from her. Grief without restraint. The cupboard walls began pressing inward. Harry tried to pull his hands away.

"You weren't meant to see this."

Neither released him. The memory spell tightened. They were no longer merely witnessing. Hermione's horror changed the cupboard. Draconia's rage changed it too.

Harry felt what they felt while looking at the child he had been. Not disgust. Not pity. Recognition of harm so complete that it dismantled every explanation Harry had used to survive it. Petunia had known.

Vernon had known. The cupboard had not been discipline. The hunger had not been an accident. The word Harry avoided rose through all three minds.

Abuse.

"No."

He fought the link. The cupboard door burst open. Light flooded in. The real classroom appeared beyond it. The basin.

The chairs. Their bodies waiting.

"Finite!" Hermione shouted.

Draconia repeated it. Harry could not find his voice. The magic pulled back. Every memory stretched into silver threads connecting their hands, mouths, eyes and scars. The threads resisted breaking. They had learned too much about one another to become separate easily. Hermione reached for the incantation again.

Draconia's hand slipped. The Chamber returned around her. Harry caught her wrist.

"Draco."

Her eyes flashed red. Then grey.

"Hermione," Harry said.

She stood between three clocks, unable to identify the true hour. He pulled. The three of them reached the same moment.

Rain.

Classroom.

Hands joined over black water.

"Now," Hermione said.

Together, they cast.

"Finite!"

The basin shattered.

Harry struck the floor. Water spread beneath him. It was clear now, moonstone glittering among broken pieces of the bowl. His ears rang.

The chairs had overturned. Hermione lay several feet away with one hand pressed to her temple. Blood ran from her nose. Draconia had fallen against a desk, pale hair hiding her face. The charmed hourglass stood upright. Forty-two seconds had passed. Harry stared at it. He had been four years old for longer than forty-two seconds.

He had been his mother.

Hermione.

Draconia.

He could still feel their memories inside his body. Paralysis held his limbs. Ink moved beneath his fingers. The Time-Turner chain cut into his throat. Harry pushed himself upright.

The room swayed. Hermione lifted her head.

"Harry."

He flinched. Her face crumpled. Draconia moved near the desk. She looked at her hands.

Name.

Age.

Place.

Harry heard the ritual because part of it remained inside him.

"Draco," he said.

She looked up. Recognition arrived slowly.

"Harry?"

"It's you."

The words escaped before he considered them. Pain crossed her face.

"Yes."

Hermione crawled towards the shattered basin.

"The spell went further than it was meant to."

Harry laughed. The sound came out wrong.

"You think?"

"I tried to end it."

"It didn't stop."

"I know."

"You said it would stop."

"It should have."

"You said one memory."

"I know."

"It showed everything."

"Not everything."

Harry stared at her. Hermione's face went white.

"That was the wrong thing to say."

"What didn't it show?"

"I don't know. I meant—we couldn't have seen every memory. It only felt—"

"You saw enough."

Harry stood. His knees nearly failed. He caught the back of the chair and forced them straight. Draconia stepped towards him. He moved away.

She stopped. The reaction hurt her. Harry saw it and could not repair it. His skin felt absent. The spell had turned him inside out and left the parts other people were never supposed to touch exposed beneath classroom light. Hermione wiped the blood from her face.

"I'm sorry."

"Don't."

"We didn't know reciprocal resonance would—"

"You copied the warning."

"Yes."

"You read it to me."

"Yes."

"Then you knew."

"We knew it was possible."

"And did it anyway."

Hermione had no answer. Draconia found one.

"You agreed."

The sentence silenced the room. Hermione turned towards her.

"Draco."

"No. He did." Draconia's voice shook. "We told him there was a risk."

"You said one memory," Harry repeated.

"The spell was supposed to show one."

"It didn't."

"I know."

"Do you?"

Draconia's composure broke.

"I was there!"

Harry felt the memory of her trapped behind Riddle's eyes. The anger left him for one second. Shame replaced it. He looked away. Hermione stood more carefully.

"We should go to Madam Pomfrey."

"No."

"We could have magical after-effects."

"No."

"Harry, the spell became unstable."

"I noticed."

"Your nose is bleeding."

He touched his face. Blood marked his fingers. Black ink. Basilisk blood. His mother's red hair beneath green light.

Harry wiped his hand against his robes.

"I'm fine."

Neither girl accepted it. Their disbelief no longer rested upon observation. They knew exactly what the words concealed. That was unbearable. Hermione approached.

Slowly.

"We need to tell someone about the Dursleys."

Harry recoiled.

"No."

"They locked you in a cupboard."

"You knew that."

"I knew where you slept. I didn't know—"

"It wasn't always like that."

The defence came automatically. Hermione began crying. Harry's chest tightened.

"Don't."

"They withheld food."

"I got food."

"It was one day."

The memory contradicted him inside all three minds. Nobody spoke. Harry heard the other days in their silence.

"I was difficult," he said.

Hermione covered her mouth. Draconia's face changed into fury.

"Do not say that."

"It's true."

"You were four."

"I made things happen."

"I don't believe that."

"You do."

"I'm sorry."

"Nothing."

Only briefly. He saw.

"Good to know."

Hermione moved closer.

"We thought if we watched enough, we could prevent another Chamber."

"I know."

"No, you don't. That was the whole point of the Time-Turner."

The words hurt Hermione. Harry felt the hurt and hated that part of him still recognised it from inside her. He turned towards Draconia.

"And you can't check yourself enough to guarantee nothing will ever control you again."

Draconia's face emptied. Harry regretted the sentence instantly. He had chosen the exact wound because the spell had shown him where it was. That knowledge made the cruelty worse.

"I'm sorry."

Draconia laughed faintly.

"Well, you were right. That makes everything all right, doesn't it?"

"No."

"That was Granger's argument."

Hermione flinched. The room began closing around them again. Harry could feel every emotion too clearly. His own shame. Hermione's grief. Draconia's anger. The spell had left the boundaries thin. He needed distance before he used another memory against them.

"I have to go."

"We should not separate yet," Hermione said. "The book said emotional transference may last—"

"I don't care what the book says."

"What if you become disoriented?"

"I'll find Madam Pomfrey."

"You won't," Draconia said.

Harry looked at her.

"No," she continued. "You won't. You'll find another cupboard and sit inside it until you can pretend nothing happened."

The truth landed without mercy. Harry's hand closed around the door handle. Draconia saw his face.

"I didn't mean—"

"You did."

"I am trying to stop you from leaving while you are hurt."

"You don't get to."

"We're frightened."

"So am I."

The admission silenced them. Harry's grip tightened.

"I can still feel it," he said. "Being unable to move. Losing time. Holding the wand while someone else uses it. I can feel all of it."

Hermione pressed one hand to her chest. Draconia's eyes closed.

"I don't know which feelings are mine."

"That will pass," Hermione whispered.

"When?"

"The book said—"

"Stop telling me what the book said."

Hermione fell silent. Harry opened the door. The corridor beyond was empty. Afternoon light lay across the stone, ordinary and pale. No cupboard. No Chamber. No memory. He stepped through.

"Harry," Hermione called.

He stopped without turning.

"We never thought any of it was your fault."

Harry stared at the opposite wall. A crack crossed the stone. Dust had gathered along its edge. He focused upon it until the corridor remained real.

"I know," he said.

The answer should have helped. It did not. Behind him, Draconia's breathing trembled. Hermione shifted near the broken basin. They knew the width of his childhood bed now. They knew how soup tasted after a day without food. They knew that Harry had once believed a spider was the only creature inside Privet Drive permitted to go where it pleased. They knew he had imagined no future after losing them. The secrets had survived because they belonged only to him.

Now they lived inside the two people whose opinions mattered most.

Harry looked back. Hermione's face was wet with tears. Draconia stood rigid beside the overturned chairs, one hand gripping the wrist which had once raised a wand against him. Harry loved them. The spell had made that knowledge impossible to disguise.

It had also made remaining in the room impossible.

"You weren't supposed to know that," he said.

Then he left them with the broken bowl and all the memories none of them could return.
