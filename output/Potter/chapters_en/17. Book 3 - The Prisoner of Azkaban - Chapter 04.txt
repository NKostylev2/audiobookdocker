The compartment darkened. Hermione lit the lamp overhead.

Professor Lupin returned to sleep, though not as deeply as before. His face tightened whenever the train turned. Once, his fingers closed around his coat sleeve with such force that his knuckles showed white.

"It is fraud wearing curtains."

"I have read the foundational texts."

"Reading about theory isn't the same as practical instruction."

"Professor Trelawney has predicted her own retirement every year since 1986."

"That may demonstrate consistency."

"It demonstrates optimism."

Harry had chosen Care of Magical Creatures and Divination.

"I've made a mistake."

"You could change."

"No. Draco hates it now."

Draconia looked up. "Your academic decisions should not be based upon irritating me."

"That's not what I'm hearing."

"How?"

"We discussed this."

"No, you said arrangements."

"Yes."

"What arrangements?"

Hermione folded the schedule.

"Professor McGonagall has resolved it."

Draconia's eyes narrowed. "You still can't be in two rooms at once."

"Then perhaps the timetable is wrong."

"I verify information."

"You invaded the school administration."

"I asked Father."

"That explains both the access and the invasion."

"Could Hermione be creating copies of herself?"

Both girls looked at him.

"It happened with Lockhart's photographs," he said.

"I am not creating copies," Hermione replied.

"Shame. One could sleep."

The line landed too close. Hermione's expression changed. She looked towards her bag. The sealed envelope was gone, but something small and hard pressed against the inner fabric near her hip.

Draconia followed the glance. Neither asked. The train travelled deeper into the rain.

Harry's folded halves of his Hogsmeade form slid out of his bag as he reached for a book. He hadn't meant to bring it out, but he watched the two halves fall and land on the seat.

Hermione saw the tear.

"Did Lucius speak to Professor Dumbledore?"

Draconia answered. "He sent a formal request."

"And?"

"No response."

"There's still time."

"McGonagall won't allow it," Harry said.

"You don't know that."

"The rule says, parent or guardian."

"Rules can allow exceptions."

Draconia leaned back. "A surprisingly mature development."

Hermione ignored her.

"We could explain."

"To McGonagall?"

"Yes."

Harry pictured Professor McGonagall's face when presented with the pieces.

She would understand. She might even be angry for him.

Then she would say the form required a signature.

"I'll ask," Harry said.

Hermione studied him. "You will?"

"Yes."

"Before the first visit?"

"Yes."

"And tell us what she says?"

Harry looked at Draconia. "Has she infected you?"

"This is ordinary suspicion."

"You make that face when you intend to handle something alone," Hermione said.

"What face?"

"The one you made before following the voice into the walls."

"That was a completely different face."

Draconia examined him. "The eyebrows were less defensive."

Harry looked towards Professor Lupin.

"Do I make a face?"

Lupin's eyes remained closed.

"Yes," he said.

Harry sat back.

"Brilliant."

"Your friends appear to have catalogued it accurately."

"They keep files."

"Only metaphorical ones," Hermione said.

Draconia looked out at the rain. Harry stared at her; she offered no clarification.

Shortly after four, the train began slowing.

At first, Harry barely noticed. The wheels' steady rhythm lengthened, each strike against the tracks arriving farther apart. Fields beyond the windows moved more slowly.

Hermione looked up from her book.

"Are we near the station?"

"No," Draconia said.

The rain had hidden most of the landscape, but no houses or platforms appeared through it. Only open ground, low hills and distant trees pressed darkly against the horizon.

The train slowed further. Students entered the corridor. Doors opened. Voices passed between compartments, uncertain and curious. Harry looked towards Professor Lupin.

The man was awake now, though no longer appeared tired. Every trace of sleep had vanished from his face. One hand rested inside his coat near his wand while he listened to the train.

"What is it?" Harry asked.

Lupin did not answer immediately. The wheels screeched to a stop. The sudden absence of motion unsettled the compartment. Luggage shifted above them. Steam hissed somewhere beyond the carriage, then faded.

Rain drummed heavier on the now-still roof.

"What happened?" Hermione asked.

Lupin stood.

"Remain here."

He stepped into the corridor. Draconia rose at once.

"He said to stay here."

"You never listen when people tell you that."

"That is not the point."

Harry opened the compartment door. The corridor was crowded. Students leaned from every doorway, asking questions no one could answer. A prefect pushed through them.

"Back inside. There's an inspection."

"By who?" someone called.

The prefect kept moving.

Lupin stood near the end of the carriage, speaking quietly to an older student. His face had become pale.

Harry felt the first thread of cold pass across his ankles. He looked down; no sign of any crack or hole to the outside. The cold climbed. This wasn't the weather. Weather touched exposed skin in gentle tingles. This moved through shoes, socks and bone. Harry's breath clouded. Behind him, Crookshanks growled.

Every lamp in the corridor went out. Darkness slowly struck the train.

Students began to scream. Harry stepped backwards into the compartment. Someone shut the door. Hermione's hand found his sleeve. Draconia's wand ignited.

"Lumos."

The light at its tip was weak. It should have filled the small room. Instead, the darkness appeared to consume each inch of illumination almost as soon as it formed.

Frost spread across the window. Delicate veins formed around the edges, then thickened, turning the glass white. The rain beyond became inaudible.

Harry heard Hermione's shallow breathing beside him. Crookshanks pressed low beneath the seat. Then even those sounds changed. Each breath became distant, pulled away from the room. Harry's chest tightened. The compartment door moved as a shape appeared behind the frosted glass. Tall, Hooded.

The handle turned.

Draconia raised her wand, her hand trembling violently. The door slid open fully, revealing a cloaked figure filling the entrance.

Its robes did not hang like ordinary cloth. They drifted around a body which seemed not entirely present, edges moving without wind. One grey hand emerged from beneath the sleeve, skin stretched across long fingers and darkened near the nails.

No face was visible inside the hood. Only a deep black, almost a void. Harry stared into it. The cold entered his lungs. Every happy sound vanished from the train.

He knew they had existed. Students had laughed minutes before. Cards exploded. Hermione argued about timetables. Draconia's shoes turned purple. The memories remained, but the feelings attached to them seemed to drain. Harry tried to raise his wand, but his arm would not move.

Beside him, Hermione stopped breathing. Her fingers remained locked around his sleeve. Terror crossed her face, but her eyes looked beyond the compartment now, fixed on another room only she could see. Draconia's wandlight failed.

Darkness swallowed her. Draconia made a broken sound. The Dementor drew in a long, rattling breath. Harry's knees weakened as he heard a woman scream. The sound came from far away, then slowly drew nearer. It felt like something he had heard before.

Harry gripped the edge of the seat. The scream returned, louder now. Carrying fear, grief and his name.

"Mum?"

The compartment disappeared. Lily's hands were shaking. Not as a picture seen from across a room. They belonged to him now: longer fingers, a wedding ring pressing against swollen skin, nails catching upon the tiny buttons of a blue sleepsuit.

Baby Harry kicked in her lap. Harry was holding himself; the feeling was surreal. The impossibility tore through him, but the memory had no space for questions. Rain struck the nursery window.

The garden gate clicked below. Lily knew it shouldn't.

Fear occupied her body before the first ward failed. It tightened across her shoulders, sharpened her hearing and made the room painfully clear.

James's jumper caught around its neck, Harry's small hand curled in her hair.

Harry felt his mother love him. The knowledge hurt more than the cold. A ward opened downstairs. Lily's breath stopped; a detection ward suddenly went off.

"James."

Her voice barely formed. He looked up from the sitting-room floor. Harry saw him through her eyes. Untidy hair, Glasses catching firelight. One hand resting upon a wooden block while baby Harry's unfinished tower stood between them. James's face changed. The front door opened.

"Lily, take Harry and go!"

She moved; Harry felt her run.

The baby struck against her chest, startled into crying. Stairs rose beneath her feet. Each one took too long. James remained below without his wand.

His wand was upstairs. The knowledge screamed through Lily. She reached the nursery. Behind her, wood splintered. A spell struck the hall. James shouted.

Lily closed the bedroom door and dragged the chair beneath the handle, already knowing furniture could not stop Voldemort.

Harry felt her thoughts fracture. Go out the window- too small. There were apparation wards, and this room was far too small. No wand, no time.

She placed the baby inside the cot. He began to cry harder.

"Please."

Lily turned towards the dresser where James's wand rested. Downstairs, a body hit the floor.

Harry felt his father die through the woman who loved him. It was not only the sound. Something inside Lily tore from its foundation. Her mind reached for James and found absence where a living person had been seconds earlier.

No final words, no goodbye. He had been laughing over a ruined tower. Now he was dead. Grief attempted to take Lily to the floor. Harry felt her refuse. The baby was crying, but remained alive. She reached the wand when the thin white door exploded.

Lily raised James's wand. Light filled the room—red, white, then nothing as Voldemort deflected each curse. Magic tore plaster from the walls. A wooden star shattered above the cot. The wand flew from her hand.

Voldemort entered through smoke. Harry saw him from Lily's height. The tall, Pale form. It was inhuman- nearly snake-like, with dark, glowing red eyes fixed upon the child that sat in the cot.

The scar beneath Harry's fringe burned. Lily moved in front of the cot; her body knew there was no escape.

"Stand aside."

The voice hissed, seemingly echoing through the space itself. Lily spread her arms.

"No."

Harry felt the word leave her chest. Voldemort's sickeningly bored gaze passed over her.

"You need not die."

Behind Lily, the baby called out, a sound that almost resembled a word.

She nearly turned. Every part of her wanted one last look. She did not; no, she could not give Voldemort even that opening.

"Please," Lily said. "Not Harry."

The name broke inside her mouth.

"Stand aside."

"You can have me."

"I already do."

"Please."

No dignity remained to protect. Lily begged because pride meant nothing beside her son's life.

"He's a baby. He doesn't know anything. He can't hurt you."

Voldemort lifted his wand. Lily's terror became absolute. She thought of one step across the sitting-room rug. Porridge across James's glasses. Harry asleep with his hand around his father's finger. Years she would never see. The first School robes, birthdays, a voice changing with age. The face her baby might grow into, Friends he had not met, every future gathered behind her, contained inside a cot. Lily planted her feet.

"No."

Harry tried to move her. He tried to turn, to reach into the cot and take himself, to shout that the curse would rebound, that the baby would live and she did not have to die. The memory allowed no change. He was inside her last second. He felt the choice pulling him, with no ability to control it.

Green light filled Lily's vision. Pain arrived everywhere. It felt like burning and a forcible separation. Like skin being ripped apart. The body fell g away from the will still fixed upon the child behind it.

Her final thought was not fear. Her mouth formed his name.

"Harry—"

Harry hit the compartment floor. His shoulder struck wood. His glasses twisted beneath him.

"Mum!"

The word tore from his throat. The surrounding train compartment returned in fragments. Dim yellow light, rain beyond the frosted window. Hermione's arms around his shoulders. Draconia kneeling beside him, one hand locked around his.

Harry could not breathe. The green light remained inside his eyes. Lily's eyes looked at him as the light faded, impossibly large and disappearing too quickly. He tried to hold on.

It was already becoming a memory.

"No."

Hermione tightened her grip.

"You're here."

Harry looked at her without understanding.

"Harry, look at me."

Her face swam into focus. Her familiar brown eyes, Wild hair and pale, fear-stricken face. She was alive. He turned. Draconia's face had gone colourless. Her fingers crushed his hand. Her other fist rested against her mouth, and her wand lay upon the floor beyond reach.

Alive. Harry dragged air into his lungs, but it stopped halfway. The harder he tried, the less useful breathing became.

"She—"

His voice failed. Professor Lupin stood in the doorway.

Silver light spread from his wand. It did not form an animal; it filled the corridor like a shield made of moonlight. The Dementor had retreated beyond it. Harry saw only the edge of its black robes before it drifted away.

Lupin closed the compartment door as the light faded. He knelt beside Harry. His face looked older than it had that morning.

"Can you hear me?"

Harry nodded too quickly.

"What is your name?"

The question seemed ridiculous.

"Harry."

"Where are you?"

He looked around. Hermione held him. Draconia gripped his hand. Crookshanks crouched beneath the seat, fur raised as if he still felt threatened.

"Train."

"Good."

Lupin reached into his case. He broke a large bar of chocolate into pieces and held one towards Harry.

"Eat."

"I'm not hungry."

"I know."

The gentleness of the answer broke through something. Harry took the chocolate. His fingers barely worked. Draconia released his hand long enough to help remove the wrapper but immediately caught his wrist again.

Harry placed the chocolate upon his tongue. Its sweetness suddenly filled him with warmth. Lupin gave chocolate to Hermione and Draconia. Hermione accepted automatically. Draconia stared at it until Lupin repeated the instruction.

"Both of you."

Harry looked to Draconia, then followed her lead and took another small nibble. Colour returned gradually to the compartment. The frost began melting. Water travelled down the window in narrow lines. The lamp strengthened overhead.

Harry's breathing slowed. Hermione's arms remained around him. He became aware of the position and shifted. She released him at once, then seemed to regret it. Draconia did not release his wrist. Harry looked down at her hand. Her fingers only opened when she realised she hadn't released him.

"I was checking your pulse."

"For several minutes?"

"It was irregular."

Lupin's mouth twitched. Harry sat against the seat.

"What was that?"

"A Dementor," Lupin said.

"Why did it come inside?"

"The Ministry is searching the train."

"For Black?"

"Yes."

"Did they think we hid him in the luggage?"

Lupin looked towards the corridor.

"I intend to ask."

Anger sharpened the tired lines of his face. It was quiet anger, held with enough control to make it more convincing.

Hermione pulled her knees close.

"I couldn't move."

Her voice sounded small. Harry looked at her. She stared at the chocolate in her hand.

"I was back in the hospital wing," she said. "I knew I was there, but I couldn't open my eyes. I could hear people walking past the curtains."

Harry's stomach tightened.

"You were unconscious when you were Petrified."

"That's what Madam Pomfrey said."

Hermione rubbed one thumb against the wrapper.

"But I heard them."

Draconia looked towards the dark window. Harry waited.

"What did you hear?"

"Nothing."

Her answer came too quickly. Hermione did not challenge it. Draconia's hands had begun trembling again. Harry saw a faint line of ink beneath one fingernail. She pressed the hand into her robes.

"Tom?" Harry asked quietly.

Draconia closed her eyes.

"Yes."

The single word changed the room. No one asked what he said. Lupin looked between them. His expression contained no curiosity—only understanding.

"Dementors draw upon experiences of fear, despair and helplessness," he said. "They do not distinguish between memories you can describe and those your mind has buried."

Harry touched his scar.

"I saw my mother die."

Lupin stopped.

The rain continued against the glass. Hermione turned fully towards Harry.

"You heard her?"

"No."

Harry searched for words. He could still feel Lily's wedding ring around a finger that no longer existed.

"I was her."

The sentence sounded impossible. Draconia's gaze sharpened.

"What do you mean?"

"I felt what she felt. I was holding myself. I heard my father downstairs."

Harry's voice began shaking.

"He didn't have his wand. He tried to stop Voldemort anyway. Then Mum ran upstairs, and I—I knew she loved me."

The final words nearly failed. Hermione reached towards him, her hand stopping before contact. Harry closed the distance himself, wrapping his hand around her outstretched fingers.

The decision was small. Hermione's breath caught.

"She stood in front of me," Harry continued. "He told her to move. She could have lived."

Lupin looked towards the floor.

His face had lost every trace of colour.

"You knew," Harry said.

Lupin's eyes lifted.

"What?"

"You knew my parents."

He concluded his reaction rather than evidence. Lupin remained still. Draconia watched him with sudden intensity. Hermione looked between them.

Harry continued, "On the platform, you looked at me like—"

"Harry."

Lupin's voice was gentle, almost too gentle.

"Yes," he said. "I knew them."

The train shuddered beneath them. Movement returned slowly. Wheels caught the rails and began turning. The rhythm started uncertainly, then steadied. Lupin looked out of the window.

"Your mother was one of the kindest people I knew."

Harry's throat tightened.

"And my father?"

A smile touched Lupin's mouth.

It carried grief.

"Your father was one of the loudest."

Draconia glanced at Harry.

"That explains several things."

Harry nearly laughed. The sound emerged rough but real. Lupin looked at Draconia.

"So I thought."

"How well did you know them?" Harry asked.

Lupin folded the remaining chocolate.

"Well enough to miss them."

It was not a complete answer. Harry recognised a half-truth easily from two years with the headmaster, Dumbledore. Before he could ask again, Lupin stood.

"I need to speak with the driver and ensure no student requires Madam Pomfrey."

"You're leaving?" Hermione asked.

"Briefly."

He looked at Harry.

"Finish the chocolate."

"Yes, Professor."

Lupin moved into the corridor. At the door, he paused.

"They don't affect everyone the same way, Harry. You didn't do anything wrong."

Harry looked away. He had not used that word. Lupin heard it anyway, tilting his head, and swiftly closed the door and headed toward the front.

For several minutes, the trio remained on the floor. The train gathered speed beneath them, and the familiar clicking of the wheels on the rails quickened. No one suggested returning to the seats. The Hogwarts Express's atmosphere did not recover.

Before the stop, the corridors had been full of laughter, gossip and arguments. Afterwards, compartment doors remained closed. Voices lowered. Students sat close together and pretended they were cold only because of the weather.

Professor Lupin returned twenty minutes later.

He confirmed that no one had been seriously injured. Three students fainted. One attempted to climb through a window while panicking and required treatment for cuts. The Dementors found no trace of Sirius Black.

"They'll search the grounds," he said.

"What happens if they find him?" Harry asked.

"They will attempt to return him to Azkaban."

"Attempt?"

Lupin's expression closed.

"Black has already accomplished what no other prisoner has."

Draconia examined him.

"You do not sound surprised."

Lupin looked towards her.

"I am surprised."

"That is not the same as believing it impossible."

"No."

Draconia waited, but nothing came. The conversation had clearly ended. Harry thought of Lucius.

Sirius Black had no trial. Lupin knew Harry's parents. The Ministry searched a train full of children because Black might be moving towards Hogwarts.

Everyone possessed different pieces. No one offered enough to form the picture. Rain followed them north.

The sweet trolley passed again, but few students bought anything. Professor Lupin purchased another chocolate bar and divided it without asking.

Harry ate. When he finished, Draconia pushed one of Narcissa's sandwiches across the seat. He looked at it.

"Your mother packed that for you."

"She packed it for both."

"I'm not hungry."

"Chocolate is not lunch."

"We had lunch."

"That was hours ago."

Harry glanced towards Hermione. She was staring determinedly at her book.

"She told you to do this."

"She did not."

"You're both terrible at pretending."

Hermione turned the page without reading. Draconia left the sandwich between them. The train rocked gently as it went over another switch. His body felt hollow after the Dementor, every muscle exhausted. He picked up the sandwich, and Draconia opened her book. Hermione's shoulders lowered.

The rain became heavier near dusk.

It hammered upon the carriage roof and turned the world outside into moving darkness. The compartment lamps reflected their faces in the windows, making it impossible to see beyond the glass.

Draconia had not turned a page in nearly half an hour. Harry closed his eyes again. Lily stood in front of the cot immediately. He opened them.

Hermione lowered her book.

"What did you see before the train stopped?"

Harry knew what she meant.

"I told you."

"Not all of it."

Draconia looked up. Harry stared at his reflection.

The face in the window was his. For several minutes inside the Dementor's cold, he had possessed Lily's body, memories and love. Now only fragments remained. He could recall the feeling but not contain it.

"I saw the morning," he said. "Before Voldemort came."

"Yes. Except it wasn't the same."

Harry rubbed his palms against his trousers.

"I knew where Dad left his wand. I knew Sirius charmed the nursery curtains. I knew my mother hated those curtains."

Lupin's book stopped moving.

Harry saw.

He continued.

"I knew I took my first step that day."

Lupin closed his eyes.

"Yes."

"James."

"Yes."

"Yes."

Draconia's posture sharpened.

Hermione lowered her book completely.

"I met Harry this morning."

"Yes."

"Draco," Harry said.

She looked at him.

"Leave it."

Harry stood too quickly. Dizziness tilted the compartment. His hand caught the seat. Draconia moved between Harry and the corridor, delaying Hermione with a question about Crookshanks until his balance returned.

"You're welcome."

"First years!"

Hagrid's voice rose above the crowd.

"First years over here! Mind the edge!"

"Harry!"

Harry's ribs compressed.

"You too."

Hagrid set him down.

He looked at Hermione.

Hagrid hurried away.

Harry stopped.

A line of black vehicles waited beyond the station gates.

No animals stood between their shafts.

The carriages appeared to move by themselves.

He had seen them every year without questioning it.

Hermione looked ahead. "They're enchanted."

"No."

Harry turned.

"What?"

"Can you see something?"

Draconia's face had become pale again.

"No."

The answer came after too long. Hermione opened a carriage door; Crookshanks entered first with a quick leap and a seemingly happy purr.

Lights burned within hundreds of windows, gold against black stone. Towers rose into cloud. Water streamed from battlements, and the lake below reflected broken sections of the castle whenever lightning moved behind the hills.

Harry's chest tightened at the sight. The carriages entered the gates. Cold brushed the back of Harry's neck. He turned back and saw two tall figures standing beyond the ironwork. Dementors.

Their black robes moved beneath the rain. Hoods concealed their faces. They did not approach, but Harry felt their presence through the carriage walls, a pressure against every warm memory.

Draconia's hand found the seat's edge. Hermione moved closer to Harry. He fixed his eyes upon the castle doors. The cold diminished once they crossed the courtyard.

Students hurried up the stone steps, cloaks streaming water. Professor McGonagall waited inside the Entrance Hall, directing them towards the Great Hall with brisk efficiency.

Her gaze found Harry, then Hermione and finally Draconia. She appeared to count them. Professor Lupin stood near the wall speaking with Madam Pomfrey. The nurse touched his elbow and guided him towards a side corridor despite his quiet objection.

Harry watched him disappear.

"Mr Potter."

Professor McGonagall had approached.

"Professor."

"I understand the train was searched."

Hermione answered. "A Dementor entered our compartment."

McGonagall's mouth compressed.

"Were any of you injured?"

"No," Draconia said.

Harry said nothing. McGonagall's eyes settled upon him.

"Mr Potter?"

"I fainted."

The words felt ridiculous spoken in the Entrance Hall. Several students passed close enough to hear. Harry lowered his voice.

"I'm fine now."

Professor McGonagall did not challenge the phrase. Her gaze moved briefly to Hermione, who gave the smallest shake of her head.

"Report to Madam Pomfrey after the feast if you experience further dizziness, weakness or confusion."

"Yes, Professor."

McGonagall turned to Hermione.

"A word after dinner."

Hermione's hand moved towards her bag.

"Yes, Professor."

Draconia noticed; Harry did too. Before either could ask, the doors to the Great Hall opened. Warmth, light and hundreds of voices rushed through them.

The Great Hall had never looked more alive.

Thousands of candles floated beneath the enchanted ceiling. Tonight, it reflected the storm: dark clouds moving across an ink-black sky, lit occasionally from within by distant lightning. Rain did not fall inside, though the illusion made several first-years glance upward nervously.

The four House tables stretched beneath gold light.

Students filled the benches. Wet cloaks steamed. Plates, goblets and silver cutlery shone upon the tables while the smell of bread and woodsmoke replaced the cold left by the train.

Harry stopped inside the doorway. The Gryffindor table waited to his left. Slytherin waited across the hall. For two years, the Sorting had divided the trio every evening. The separation still felt wrong. Draconia glanced towards her House.

Pansy waved from halfway down the table. Daphne Greengrass sat beside her, already talking to someone Harry couldn't see.

"I'll find you after the feast," Draconia said.

"The library tomorrow," Hermione replied.

"Before breakfast?"

Harry stared. "Why?"

"To compare timetables."

"We know the classes."

"We do not know the final room assignments."

"The castle will still contain them after breakfast."

Hermione ignored him.

Draconia looked towards Harry. "Seven thirty."

Harry closed his eyes. "Fine."

"Do not vanish overnight," she added.

Hermione frowned. "That's a terrible way to say goodnight."

"He knows what I mean."

Harry did.

"Try not to curse any furniture," he replied.

Draconia's expression sharpened.

"That is also terrible," Hermione said.

"She knows what I mean."

A smile touched Draconia's mouth as she crossed towards Slytherin. Harry and Hermione joined Gryffindor.

Ron had already begun describing Crookshanks's attack to Dean and Seamus. His version included three separate lunges, a severed artery, and Harry performing emergency surgery in the corridor.

"You used one drop of potion," Hermione said as she sat.

Ron looked offended. "It could've been infected."

"It had existed for four seconds."

Scabbers remained inside his pocket. Only the rat's eyes and nose were visible. Across the aisle, Crookshanks shifted within his basket. Scabbers disappeared immediately.

Harry sat between Hermione and an empty place.

The Sorting began.

Professor McGonagall placed the Hat upon each first-year. Names were called. Students joined tables beneath cheers. Harry watched with less attention than usual.

His gaze kept moving towards the staff table. Professor Lupin had returned.

He sat beside Professor Flitwick, wearing clean robes which remained worn at the elbows. His face looked slightly healthier after whatever Madam Pomfrey provided, though shadows remained beneath his eyes.

Professor Snape occupied the other side of the table. He was staring at Lupin with open dislike.

The intensity surpassed Snape's ordinary contempt for colleagues. His black eyes followed every movement Lupin made, and his mouth tightened whenever Professor Flitwick included the new teacher in conversation.

Lupin appeared aware.

He did not look back.

"Snape hates him," Harry murmured.

Hermione followed his gaze. "Professor Snape dislikes most people."

"This is specific."

"How can dislike be specific?"

"Practice."

Across the hall, Draconia had noticed too. She met Harry's eyes and deliberately glanced toward Snape. When the final student was sorted, Dumbledore rose.

The hall quietened. He welcomed the first years, introduced Professor Lupin and announced Hagrid's appointment to Care of Magical Creatures. Gryffindor cheered so loudly that Hagrid's face vanished behind his beard.

Harry smiled.

Then Dumbledore's expression became grave.

"You will all have observed the additional guardians positioned around the school."

Unease moved through the tables.

"They are Dementors of Azkaban," Dumbledore continued. "They remain here at the request of the Ministry while Sirius Black is at large."

Harry looked towards the doors. The cold at the gates returned in memory.

"Dementors are neither forgiving nor capable of distinguishing youthful curiosity from hostile intent. You are not to approach them. You are not to leave the school boundaries without permission. Attempts to test these restrictions will be treated seriously."

Dumbledore's gaze moved over the hall until it seemed to rest on Harry.

"I must also make one point entirely clear," Dumbledore said.

His voice lowered. The candles seemed to grow still.

"No Dementor will cross the castle threshold while I am Headmaster."

The Headmaster's eyes softly twinkled as he moved away from the podium. The Certainty eased something for Harry.

The tables filled with roast meat, vegetables, bread and steaming dishes of potatoes. Conversation returned immediately, louder after being restrained. Hermione served Harry before he reached for anything. She noticed and stopped.

"Sorry."

Harry looked at the food upon his plate—roast chicken, potatoes and carrots—a normal portion.

"It's fine."

"No. I wasn't thinking."

"You usually aren't around potatoes."

She looked at him; Harry managed a small smile. Across the hall, Draconia was eating very little. Their eyes met again. He glanced towards her plate as she looked at his. Both frowned. Neither ate more.

After the feast, Professor McGonagall intercepted Hermione near the doors.

"Miss Granger."

Hermione stopped, and Harry, following her, stopped beside her.

McGonagall looked towards him. "The prefects are leading students to Gryffindor Tower."

"I know the way."

"That has not prevented you from reaching forbidden areas in the past."

Harry opened his mouth. McGonagall lifted one eyebrow. He looked towards Hermione.

"I'll wait near the stairs."

Hermione nodded. Draconia approached from the Slytherin table.

"Why are we waiting?"

"Private academic arrangements," Harry said.

Hermione's eyes narrowed. McGonagall led her into a side room and swiftly closed the door. Draconia crossed her arms.

"She has not explained."

"No."

"You did not ask."

"I considered waiting."

Draconia looked impressed despite herself.

"How unpleasant."

"Terrible."

Students streamed past them. Pansy called Draconia's name from the dungeon stairs. Draconia ignored her. Harry leaned against the stone wall. The journey's exhaustion had settled into his bones. Candles blurred slightly at the edges of his vision. Whenever he blinked too long, green light filled the darkness.

Draconia watched him.

"Do not sleep standing."

"I'm not."

"Your eyes closed."

"Blinking."

"For seven seconds."

"You timed it?"

"I estimate accurately."

Harry straightened.

"How are your hands?"

Her expression changed.

"What?"

"After the Dementor."

Draconia looked down.

The ink beneath her fingernail had vanished.

"Still got them."

"That answer sounds familiar."

"They are fine."

"You heard Tom."

She glanced towards the passing students.

"Not here."

Harry nodded.

Draconia examined him.

"You saw your mother."

"Yes."

"Not here?"

He understood.

"Not here."

A silent agreement settled between them. The side-room door opened. Hermione emerged carrying a small wooden box.

It fit inside one palm. No markings appeared on the lid, and a fine chain hung between her fingers before she hurriedly pushed it into her bag. Professor McGonagall stood behind her.

"Remember every condition we discussed."

"I will."

"This privilege may be withdrawn."

"I understand."

Professor McGonagall looked at Harry and Draconia. Her mouth tightened.

"Miss Granger is not permitted to discuss this arrangement."

Draconia lifted her chin. "We did not ask."

"You were about to."

Draconia appeared offended by the accuracy. McGonagall gave a curt nod, then turned and rounded the next corridor. Harry looked at Hermione's bag.

"So."

"No."

"What's in the box?"

"I'm not permitted to discuss it."

"Is it dangerous?" Draconia asked.

"No."

"Illegal?"

"No."

"Alive?" Harry added.

"No."

"Then it's already better than Hagrid's present."

Hermione adjusted the bag strap.

"We should go."

Draconia's eyes remained on the hidden box. Waiting, Harry remembered, was something she intended to practise. Her jaw tightened.

"Goodnight," she said.

Hermione blinked. "You aren't going to ask again?"

"No."

"Why?"

Draconia looked towards Harry.

"You told us we could wait until we were ready to talk."

Harry recognised his own lesson being used against him. It felt irritating but also fair. Hermione's expression softened.

"Thank you."

Draconia turned towards the dungeons.

"Seven thirty."

Harry groaned. She did not look back.

Gryffindor Tower had not changed.

The Fat Lady demanded the password with familiar self-importance. The common room opened around Harry in shades of red and gold: broad chairs gathered near the fire, old rugs, crowded bookshelves and windows darkened by rain.

Students filled every corner.

First-years stood together near the entrance, uncertain where to place themselves. Older Gryffindors exchanged summer stories. Fred and George Weasley had already begun selling sweets which changed the eater's voice, resulting in Percy shouting at them in a high soprano.

The room should have felt completely safe. Harry stood inside the portrait hole and listened to the fire. It cracked as wood split inside the grate. His body reacted slightly. No one noticed except Hermione. She stopped beside him.

"You all right?"

The question felt hollow, as if she already knew the answer. Harry looked around. No Dementor could enter the castle; Dumbledore had promised.

"Yeah."

Hermione accepted the answer because it was not automatic. They climbed the stairs. The third-year boys' dormitory contained five familiar beds beneath maroon curtains. Harry's trunk had already arrived. Hedwig waited near the window, and someone had placed the food basket upon his bedside table.

Ron entered behind him. Scabbers remained inside his pocket.

"You keeping that cat upstairs?" he asked Hermione.

"The girls' dormitory is in another tower."

"Good."

Hermione's expression sharpened. "Crookshanks is not dangerous."

"He tried to eat Scabbers."

"Scabbers escaped."

"That doesn't make it better."

Harry sat upon his bed.

"Could we postpone the animal trial until tomorrow?"

Ron looked at him properly. His attention went to the scar, then away.

"Dean says you fainted."

Harry's stomach tightened. Hermione answered before he could.

"Dean wasn't in our compartment."

"Someone heard."

"Several people fainted," she said. "The Dementors affected the entire train."

Ron shifted.

"I was only asking."

Harry looked at Hermione. She stood between him and the question without physically moving.

"I'm tired," he said.

Ron accepted the dismissal. The others entered. Curtains opened and closed. Trunks were unpacked. Neville searched for pyjamas folded inside his cauldron. Harry changed and climbed beneath the blankets. Hermione remained near the stairs.

She could not enter the boys' dormitory beyond the final step; the old enchantment would turn the staircase into a slide if she tried. She stood below, only her face visible through the open doorway.

"Harry?"

"Yeah?"

"If you can't sleep, come downstairs."

He looked at the other boys. Seamus was attempting to charm a poster straight. Dean threw a sock at him.

"I'll sleep."

"That isn't what I said."

Harry understood and nodded. Hermione disappeared down the stairs. Lights went out one by one.

Rain continued against the windows. Harry lay with his wand beneath one hand. The day had been too full to fit inside him: King's Cross, the train, Dementor, Lily, Lupin, Sirius Black and the cold at Hogwarts' gates.

He closed his eyes, his mind flashing to the same memory on the train. His mother holding him, then screaming and then the explosion.

Harry looked back out to the room and leaned back. The soft snores of the boys around him were somewhat comforting. Harry listened until he could tell whose strange breath or snore was who and let his guard slowly relax.

The fire stove grumbled as its fire re-ignited itself. Fresh steam forced its way up the chimney and into the many pipes that expanded up and into the walls. Harry gripped his wand, the sound reminding him too much of the chamber. Deciding sleep wasn't going to greet him tonight, he shifted. Taking one of his pillows, he leaned it against the wall and lay his head back. Tomorrow was another day.

The dungeons were colder than normal for the time of year. Water moved beyond the thick windows. Long strands of weed bent in the current, and the pale underside of a fish passed briefly across the glass before disappearing into darkness.

Draconia sat upon her bed.

The curtains had been tied to the posts with silver cord. All four remained open. Daphne's breathing had settled into an even rhythm. Millicent turned, her bed springs complaining beneath the mattress.

Draconia looked at the clock. One seventeen. She opened the notebook upon her knees. Every page contained her own handwriting. She had recorded dates, locations, and events since she returned from the Chamber.

1 September.

Departed the hotel at 8:42.

Reached King's Cross at 10:20.

Boarded the train with Harry and Hermione.

Dementor entered at approximately 4:13.

Lost awareness for an unknown duration. Likely under three minutes.

Harry collapsed. He reported experiencing his mother's final memory. Hermione reported the hospital wing. I heard Tom Riddle.

The name remained upon the page. It did not vanish. Draconia watched for twenty seconds. The ink dried and no response formed beneath it. She released the breath held inside her chest. A sudden click echoed through the room as the dormitory door opened. Draconia's wand appeared before she turned. Pansy stood in the entrance carrying a glass of water.

She froze.

"Draco?"

Draconia lowered the wand.

"What?"

"You nearly hexed me."

"You entered too quietly."

"I walked in?"

"I came in. I didn't shout."

Pansy looked towards the open curtains.

"Why are you awake?"

"I was writing."

"At one in the morning?"

"Yes."

"What?"

"Nothing relevant."

Pansy's gaze moved to the notebook. Draconia closed it. Something suspicious entered Pansy's face. Draconia's stomach turned. Last year, she had closed books whenever anyone approached. She had kept Tom's pages out of view. She had called secrecy privacy until the distinction nearly killed her.

She reopened the notebook as subtly as she could.

"Travel notes."

Pansy looked disappointed.

"Why?"

"Because memory is unreliable."

"That's weird."

"Yes."

Draconia held her gaze.

Pansy shifted.

"Was Potter really attacked by a Dementor?"

"Everyone in our compartment was."

"But did he faint?"

Draconia's fingers tightened around the quill.

"Who told you?"

"Everyone knows."

"Then everyone ought to find something else to talk about."

"Is it true?"

"Goodnight, Pansy."

Pansy looked towards the notebook again. Draconia reached for her wand. Pansy turned and climbed into her bed, closing her curtains. Draconia remained still until the metallic sliding of the curtain rings stopped scraping across the bar, signalling she was somewhat alone again.

Everyone knows.

Harry would hate that. She wrote the time of Pansy's entrance, then crossed it out. The notebook was meant to prove ownership of her memory. It was becoming another thing she obeyed. Draconia placed it inside the bedside drawer.

Hermione waited until Lavender and Parvati slept.

The girls' dormitory had become quiet an hour earlier. Still, quiet was not the same as safety. Lavender had already sat up twice to ask whether Hermione intended to keep reading. Parvati possessed an inconvenient talent for waking whenever someone moved near the door.

Hermione drew her curtains. She lit her wand beneath the blankets and removed Professor McGonagall's box.

The wood was dark, polished and warm from being carried near her body. A narrow brass clasp secured the lid. Hermione opened it. A long gold chain rested inside. At its centre hung a tiny hourglass; the sand within it glittered silver beneath her wandlight. Instructions had been folded underneath.

The device is authorised solely for attendance of approved subjects.

Every rotation returns the bearer one hour.

Their earlier self must not observe the bearer.

The bearer must not disclose possession of the device.

Alterations outside the established temporal sequence are strictly prohibited.

At the bottom, Professor McGonagall had added in her own handwriting:

Miss Granger, this is not a challenge to discover how much one student can endure.

Hermione touched the chain. She had selected too many classes; everyone said so.

Harry treated the impossibility as a joke. Draconia treated it as a strategic flaw. Her parents asked whether she might enjoy school more if she took fewer classes.

None understood. Hermione had spent weeks motionless in the hospital wing while something hunted her friends. She remembered fragments. Voices beyond curtains. Footsteps. Madam Pomfrey saying Harry's name. The dreadful knowledge that events continued while Hermione could contribute nothing.

She would never be unprepared again.

Ancient Runes might reveal forgotten enchantments. Arithmancy could expose patterns hidden inside magic. Muggle Studies mattered because both worlds shaped Harry's life. Divination was imprecise, but dismissing knowledge without examination was irresponsible. Care of Magical Creatures had already become relevant several times.

Every subject might matter. Every hour might contain the fact that saved someone. Hermione lifted the chain over her head. The hourglass settled beneath her nightdress, cold against the centre of her chest.

Classes began in two days. And Hermione already felt like she didn't possess enough time.
