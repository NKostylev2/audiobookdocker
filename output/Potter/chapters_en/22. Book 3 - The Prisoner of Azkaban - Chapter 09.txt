9

Harry learned to lie with food. Toast was easiest. It could be broken into pieces and rearranged across a plate until the crumbs implied an appetite. Sausages required more effort, but Neville could usually be persuaded to take one without asking why Harry had offered. Potatoes could be hidden beneath folded napkins. Porridge was nearly impossible, though stirring it long enough left a convincing ring around the bowl. Pumpkin juice helped with everything. If Harry drank two glasses at breakfast, Hermione watched the level fall and sometimes forgot to examine the plate. Draconia was harder to deceive.

She did not sit near him during meals, but distance only encouraged her attention. From the Slytherin table, she watched his hands. Harry began lifting his fork whenever her gaze crossed the Hall. He learned how long he needed to chew before looking occupied and which parts of a meal could be transferred to Neville without obvious movement. He did not decide to stop eating. That distinction mattered to him.

He still became hungry. He felt the emptiness in the morning, sharp and ordinary, but it often disappeared after the first mouthful. Food changed inside his mouth. Bread became dry. Meat became difficult to swallow. The effort seemed unreasonable for a body which would only demand the same thing several hours later. Sometimes Harry intended to finish. Then someone mentioned Sirius Black. Or a plate struck the table with the same metallic crack as the spoon Draconia had dropped when the diary's voice returned to her. Or the enchanted ceiling darkened, and Harry remembered hundreds of Dementors lifting their hidden faces beneath the Quidditch pitch.

His appetite would close. Harry became very good at making the moment invisible. Sleep was more difficult. No arrangement of blankets could deceive the night. When Harry closed his eyes, his mind returned to Godric's Hollow. It used pieces the Dementors had given him and constructed the rest from fear. He saw Sirius at the garden gate. Sometimes Sirius wore the face from the wedding photograph. Sometimes he was the black dog. He opened the door for Voldemort, stepped aside and laughed while green light filled the house.

Other nights began in the Chamber. Stone stretched beneath Harry's feet. The basilisk's body coiled around the pillars, though Harry knew he had killed it. Hermione stood behind him, pale and rigid. Draconia lay beside the diary. When Harry approached, Draconia opened her eyes. They were red.

"You took too long," she said in Tom Riddle's voice.

Then the Chamber became his bedroom at Privet Drive. Bars covered the window. Vernon stood outside the locked door while Hermione and Draconia called Harry's name from somewhere he could not reach. He usually woke before the scream. Afterwards, he opened the Marauder's Map. Moonlight crossed the dormitory. The curtains concealed the other boys, and the room settled around their sleeping breaths. Harry sat with his wand illuminated beneath the blankets and unfolded the castle across his knees. Hundreds of names moved through the night.

Filch patrolled the second floor with Mrs Norris. Professor Sinistra crossed the Astronomy Tower. Snape remained in his office long after midnight, his dot moving between desk and storeroom. Hermione's name sometimes appeared in the Gryffindor common room. Draconia's remained beside her bed beneath the lake. Remus Lupin's name vanished once a month. Harry noticed. He did not investigate. That would have required acknowledging that he still cared where Lupin went.

Instead, Harry searched for Sirius Black. The name never appeared. By morning, he had slept two or three hours and knew the exact location of everyone except the person he wanted to find. He washed, dressed and went to breakfast. Then he lied with food.

The plan to make Harry enjoy Saturday arrived upon green parchment. Draconia placed it across his Defence textbook during lunch. Harry looked down. Three columns divided the page into times, locations and approved activities. He read the title.

"Mandatory recreation?"

Hermione sat opposite him at the end of the Gryffindor table. "The word mandatory was Draco's."

"The word recreation was Granger's."

"You've made a kidnapping schedule."

"It is not kidnapping if you accompany us willingly," Hermione said.

"And if I don't?"

Draconia examined the paper.

"The schedule becomes inaccurate."

"That is an elegant way to avoid the question."

Harry read the first entry.

Eight thirty: breakfast.

He looked at Hermione.

"We're currently eating lunch."

"That is for tomorrow."

"You've scheduled breakfast?"

"You have missed it four times this week."

Harry's fingers stopped upon the page.

"I had an early practice."

"Twice," Draconia said.

"Wood likes preparation."

"Wood does not practise on Thursdays."

"He thinks about it intensely."

Hermione folded her arms. Harry continued down the list.

Nine fifteen: collect outdoor clothing.

"Nine fifteen?"

"You take too long to find your gloves," Hermione said.

"They are usually in my pocket."

"You still search your trunk first."

Harry glanced towards Draconia.

"You told her that?"

"You do it every time."

"I feel studied."

"You are being studied," Hermione replied. "You are the subject of a highly important experiment concerning whether stubbornness can be cured by exposure to fresh air."

"Results seem poor."

"Early stages."

The next entry read:

Ten o'clock: Hagrid's hut.

Harry looked up.

"What does Hagrid want?"

"To see us," Draconia said.

"He sent a note?"

"No."

"So you told him we were coming."

"Yes."

"Kidnapping."

"Social organisation," Hermione corrected.

Harry read the rest. Lunch with Hagrid. A walk beside the lake. Two hours without homework. Return before dark. The plan did not mention Sirius, meals Harry had missed or the fact that Hermione had begun checking the Marauder's Map before believing anything he said about his location. That absence was the point. They were trying an ordinary approach. Harry knew it.

He also knew refusing would confirm every worry they had attempted not to write down.

"What happens if it rains?" he asked.

Hermione pulled a second parchment from her bag.

"Alternative indoor schedule."

Harry stared at it.

Draconia looked impressed. "She didn't tell me about that."

"You were becoming obstructive."

"I questioned whether Gobstones qualified as restorative."

"Compared with what?"

"Not playing Gobstones."

Harry handed back both schedules.

"Fine."

Hermione blinked.

"Fine?"

"I'll go."

Draconia narrowed her eyes.

"That was easy."

"I can make it more difficult if you're disappointed."

"Why are you agreeing?"

"Perhaps mandatory recreation has cured me already."

Neither girl smiled. Harry reached for his pumpkin juice. Draconia looked at his untouched plate. She said nothing. That was what they had agreed.

Harry hated how grateful he felt.

The following morning was cold enough to harden the mud. Frost silvered the grounds. It clung to the bare branches of the trees and covered the reeds at the edge of the lake, breaking whenever the wind moved through them. Hogwarts rose behind the trio beneath a pale March sky, its windows bright with morning fire. Harry wore the scarlet scarf Hermione had given him. Draconia noticed.

She did not mention it. Hagrid stood outside his hut repairing a wooden enclosure. Fang lay beside the door, chin upon his paws, while six Nifflers escaped through the gap Hagrid was attempting to close. One reached Draconia before anyone noticed. It climbed her cloak. Draconia looked down.

The Niffler hung from the silver fastening at her waist and attempted to pull it free with both paws.

"Remove it."

Harry stopped beside her. "He likes you."

"This sentence has never preceded anything pleasant."

The Niffler abandoned the fastening and found the bracelet beneath her glove. Draconia seized it around the middle. It curled protectively over the jewellery.

"Hagrid."

The half-giant looked over.

"Ah. He's friendly."

"He is robbing me."

"Only because he likes the bracelet."

"I like the bracelet."

"Yeh've got that in common, then."

Harry began laughing. The sound surprised all of them. Draconia glared at him, though the effect was weakened by the Niffler climbing onto her shoulder and attempting to enter her hair. Hermione approached carefully.

"Don't pull him. His claws are caught."

"I am aware."

"Hold still."

"I am standing perfectly still."

"You're threatening him with your face."

"That is my ordinary face."

Harry reached for the Niffler. It saw his glasses. Its paws extended.

"No."

Harry stepped backwards. The creature launched itself from Draconia's shoulder, caught Harry's jumper and began climbing. Hermione laughed. Draconia folded her arms.

"He likes you."

"You appear to have recovered quickly."

Harry caught the Niffler before it reached his glasses. It struggled, then discovered the brass detector Lucius had given Harry in his pocket and attempted to swallow it. Hagrid rescued the instrument. By the time every Niffler had been returned to the enclosure, Hermione's hair contained a twig, Draconia's bracelet had vanished twice and Harry's scarf bore three muddy paw prints. Hagrid invited them inside.

The hut was almost painfully warm. A fire roared beneath a black iron kettle, filling the single room with smoke and the smell of burning pine. Copper pans hung from the rafters. Fang occupied most of the rug. Several baskets along the wall moved occasionally, though Hagrid did not explain what they contained. The table had been prepared for lunch. Hermione examined the bread, cheese, boiled eggs and plate of rock cakes.

"You told him," Harry said.

"Told him what?" Hagrid asked too quickly.

Harry looked at Hermione. She took off her coat.

"We said we might stay for lunch."

"You said I wasn't eating."

"I did not."

"Hermione."

"I may have suggested food which cannot resist being consumed."

Harry looked at the rock cakes.

"They appear capable of defending themselves."

Hagrid laughed. He poured tea into four enormous mugs. Harry sat between the girls. The chair creaked beneath him, one leg shorter than the others. Wind pressed against the windows while the fire made the room feel separate from the frozen grounds. For half an hour, nobody discussed anything important. Hagrid told them that one of the Nifflers had stolen Professor Sprout's wedding ring and buried it inside a bag of fertiliser. Hermione corrected his explanation of their natural habitat. Draconia attempted to soften a rock cake in her tea, only for the tea to become trapped inside it.

Harry ate bread without noticing. He finished an egg. Draconia saw. Her gaze dropped before he could catch it. The ordinary conversation thinned when Hagrid mentioned the Dementors.

"Been comin' closer ter the forest," he said. "Dumbledore's furious. Says if one crosses the grounds again, he'll send the lot back ter Azkaban himself."

Harry's hand stopped near the cheese. Hermione looked at Hagrid.

"Why are they coming closer?"

"Still reckon Sirius is hereabouts."

The name changed the room. Hagrid realised too late. He looked at Harry.

"Shouldn' have said that."

"Everyone else does."

"I mean—after what yeh heard."

Harry stared into his tea. Steam moved across the surface.

"Hagrid," he said, "did Sirius ever hold me?"

Hagrid's face fell. Hermione and Draconia became very still.

"In the photograph album," Harry continued. "There's one where he's holding me."

Hagrid lowered himself into the remaining chair.

"Yeah," he said. "He held yeh."

"Was it only once?"

"No."

The answer hurt. Harry waited. Hagrid rubbed both hands over his beard.

"Sirius was always carryin' yeh about. First time James let him, Sirius held yeh like yeh were made o' glass. Lily told him babies weren't broomsticks and he could bend his arms."

A brief smile crossed Hagrid's face.

"It frightened him?"

"Terrified him. Wouldn't admit it. Came back the next day with books."

"Sirius read books?" Hermione asked.

"He bought books," Hagrid corrected. "Dunno if he read 'em."

Harry pictured the man from the photograph purchasing instructions on how to hold him.

"What happened to them?"

"The books?"

"To him."

Hagrid's smile disappeared.

"I don' know."

"He didn't wake up evil one morning."

"No."

"So when?"

Hagrid stared towards the fire.

"War changes people."

"That's what adults say when they don't know."

"Happens ter be true."

"Not enough."

Hagrid's large hands closed around the mug.

"I keep thinkin' about that night," he admitted. "Sirius sittin' among what was left o' the house. He had blood on him. Wouldn't look away from James."

Harry's chest tightened.

"He gave you the motorbike."

"Yeah."

"Why?"

"Said he wouldn't need it."

"Because he planned to kill Pettigrew?"

"That's what I thought after."

"But not then."

Hagrid looked at Harry.

"No."

"What did you think then?"

"That he'd lost his brother."

Silence filled the hut. Harry looked towards the window. A black shape moved beyond the frost-clouded glass. He stood so quickly that the chair fell. Fang barked.

"What?" Draconia asked.

Harry crossed the hut and wiped one sleeve over the glass. The grounds outside were empty. Near the forest, dark trees shifted in the wind.

"I saw something."

"What?" Hermione joined him.

"A dog."

Hagrid came closer.

"Probably one o' the village strays."

"It was black."

"Dogs come in black."

"It was the same one."

Draconia understood.

"The dog from Privet Drive?"

"And the Quidditch pitch."

"You saw it at the pitch?" Hermione asked.

Harry closed his eyes. The question revealed the secret before he could decide whether to tell it.

"Yes."

"You said there was nothing there."

"I didn't know what it was."

"You still don't."

"I know."

Draconia moved towards the door. Harry caught her arm.

"What are you doing?"

"Checking."

"It's gone."

"So it will remain gone while I check."

She opened the door. Cold air swept through the hut. Crookshanks sat outside. The cat held a piece of bread between his teeth. Hermione frowned.

"That came from the table."

Crookshanks turned. He carried the bread towards the forest. Draconia followed him onto the step. The cat stopped beside the Niffler enclosure, looked back once and disappeared through the grass.

"He's feeding it," Harry said.

Hermione shook her head.

"Crookshanks eats anything he steals."

"He didn't eat that."

"Hagrid," Draconia said, "has he been taking food?"

The half-giant looked uncomfortable.

"Couple bits."

"How long?"

"Few weeks."

Hermione stared towards the trees.

"Why would a cat feed a dog?"

"Maybe he likes it," Hagrid said.

Harry thought of Crookshanks sitting beside the animal beneath the Quidditch stands. Sirius had entered Hogwarts without being found. The black dog appeared wherever Harry went. His fingers found his wand. Draconia noticed.

"Harry."

"If that dog belongs to him—"

"We do not know that."

"It's watching me."

"Then we tell a professor."

"No."

Hermione turned. "Why not?"

"Because the last time you told one, they took my broom for a month."

"And proved it was safe."

"This isn't a broom."

"No," Draconia said. "It may bite."

Harry looked towards the forest. The dog did not return. The ordinary day ended there. They walked back to the castle in a tight group, wands inside their sleeves and the mandatory recreation schedule forgotten upon Hagrid's table.

Harry missed Charms on Monday.

He did not intend to. After breakfast, the castle became painfully loud. Students filled the corridors. A suit of armour dropped its spear near the marble staircase, and the metallic crash sent Harry back into the Chamber before he could stop it. He kept walking. By the third floor, his hands had begun trembling. Flitwick's classroom waited to the left.

Harry turned right. He found an unused broom cupboard beside the trophy room, shut the door and sat upon an overturned bucket until his breathing returned. The room smelled of polish and old straw. Shelves pressed close on either side, leaving barely enough space for his knees. No window admitted light. The darkness should have bothered him. Instead, it felt contained.

No one expected anything inside a cupboard. Harry rested his head against the wall. When he opened his eyes, forty minutes had passed. The corridor outside was empty. Charms had ended.

Harry went to Potions. Hermione intercepted him near the dungeon staircase.

"Where were you?"

"Walking."

"You missed Charms."

"I know."

"Professor Flitwick asked me."

"What did you say?"

"That I didn't know."

"So you told the truth. This is a good day for us."

Hermione did not react.

"Were you ill?"

"No."

"Then why—"

"I lost track of time."

Her eyes moved towards his face. Harry smiled.

"I don't have your equipment."

"That isn't funny."

"It was a little funny."

Draconia arrived from the lower corridor.

"What happened?"

"Harry missed Charms," Hermione said.

Draconia looked at him. Harry held up one hand.

"I know what we agreed. You are both currently doing it."

"Doing what?" Draconia asked.

"Making the face."

"That was not included in the agreement."

"It was implied."

Hermione's mouth tightened.

"We have Potions."

"Thanks for telling me," Harry said. "I thought the dungeons meant Astronomy."

He walked into the classroom. Their concern followed. Snape noticed Harry during the first twenty minutes. The class was brewing a Strengthening Solution. Heat filled the dungeon, carrying the metallic smell of salamander blood and crushed snake fang. Harry worked beside Neville, cutting ginger roots into narrow strips. His hands felt slow.

The knife slipped. The blade crossed his palm. Blood ran across the cutting board. Neville gasped. Harry wrapped his fingers around the wound.

Snape reached their table. His gaze settled upon the blood.

"Potter," he said softly, "if you intend to contaminate your potion with bodily fluid, at least consult the instructions first."

Several Slytherins laughed. Harry picked up his wand.

"I thought originality was encouraged."

"Not from you."

Snape pulled Harry's hand closer before he could object. The cut was shallow but long, crossing the base of his thumb. The professor touched his wand to it. Skin closed.

"Thank you," Harry said.

Snape's eyes lifted. Harry had surprised him.

"Pay attention," he replied.

He moved away. Five minutes later, a goblet appeared beside Harry's cauldron. The liquid inside was pale blue. Harry looked towards Snape. The professor stood at the opposite side of the room criticising Seamus's solution.

Harry smelled the goblet. Mint. Honey. A bitter ingredient beneath both.

"Don't drink unidentified potions," Neville whispered.

"Good advice in this classroom."

Harry pushed it aside. Snape returned before the end of the lesson. The goblet remained full.

"Drink."

"What is it?"

"A nutritional restorative."

"I'm not ill."

"No. You are inattentive, underfed and testing the distinction between exhaustion and stupidity."

The room had quieted. Harry looked towards Hermione. She stared into her cauldron. Draconia stood several tables away, motionless.

"I ate breakfast," Harry said.

"Two glasses of juice do not constitute breakfast."

Harry felt every eye in the dungeon turn towards him. Anger rose hot beneath his skin.

"You've been watching too?"

Snape's face changed.

"This is not concern, Potter. It is quality control. An unconscious student is disruptive."

"Then I'll try to collapse quietly."

"Drink."

Harry lifted the goblet. The potion tasted worse than it smelled. Warmth spread through his stomach. The room sharpened around him, clearing the faint grey at the edge of his vision. He hated that it helped. Snape collected the empty goblet.

"For the next week, you will report to breakfast before eight."

"Is this detention?"

"It can become one."

"You'll supervise me eating?"

"I would rather supervise Flobberworms mating."

"Is that a yes?"

"Eight o'clock."

Snape moved away. Harry stared into his cauldron. Draconia's expression was carefully blank. Hermione looked near tears. Harry wanted to leave.

He finished the potion instead. Lupin waited outside the dungeon. He leaned beside a stone arch wearing patched brown robes, a stack of parchment beneath one arm. Students passed him on their way to lunch. Harry stopped when he saw him.

"I have another lesson."

"At lunchtime?"

"I'm very committed."

Lupin looked at Hermione and Draconia.

"Could I speak with Harry?"

Hermione's mouth opened. Draconia caught her sleeve.

"We'll be in the Great Hall," she said.

They left. Harry waited until the corridor cleared.

"Snape told you."

"Professor Snape sent a Patronus halfway through your lesson."

"Of course he did."

"He said you cut your hand."

"It's fixed."

"And missed Charms."

"Also fixed. I'll read the chapter."

Lupin shifted the parchment beneath his arm.

"Where were you?"

"Nowhere."

"Harry."

"A cupboard."

Lupin's expression remained still.

"It was quiet."

"Why did you need quiet?"

"The corridors were loud."

"Is that all?"

"Yes."

The lie felt childish. Lupin did not expose it.

"I have kept Thursday afternoons free," he said.

"I know."

"The lessons may resume whenever you decide."

"I can produce a shield."

"Yes."

"I don't need more."

"A shield is not the only reason I'm asking."

Harry laughed faintly.

"Now you're worried."

"I have been worried."

"You hid it well."

Lupin absorbed the blow. Harry hated him slightly for that too.

"I should have told you about Sirius," Lupin said.

"We've done this."

"No. You spoke. I listened. That is not the same as repairing what I damaged."

"I don't need it repaired."

"Harry—"

"People keep repairing me. It's exhausting."

Lupin's eyes dropped to the place Harry's hand had been cut.

"You are not an object."

"Tell everyone else."

"I am telling you."

Harry moved towards the staircase. Lupin spoke before he reached it.

"Your mother used to leave crowded rooms without explanation."

Harry stopped.

"She did not like being watched when she was upset," Lupin continued. "James followed her once. She hexed his shoes together and left him at the bottom of a staircase."

Despite himself, Harry pictured it.

"Why are you telling me?"

"Because similarity can explain an instinct without making it destiny."

Harry turned. Lupin's face held no accusation.

"You are permitted to need quiet," he said. "You are also permitted to tell people why."

"What if I don't know?"

"Then say that."

Harry looked towards the stairs.

"Did she come back?"

"Lily?"

"Yes."

"Always."

The answer lodged beneath his ribs. Harry left before Lupin could see what it did.

The Quidditch match against Ravenclaw took place beneath a clean blue sky. Spring had not yet reached the grounds, but the snow had begun retreating from the pitch. It remained in dirty ridges beneath the stands and glittered along the tops of the goal hoops. Students filled every bench, scarves bright against heavy winter coats. Wood had not slept.

Harry knew because the captain had arrived at breakfast carrying three diagrams and tried to explain Ravenclaw's formation while Harry ate under Snape's observation.

"Chang favours the left," Wood said, drawing arrows through Harry's porridge with the end of a spoon. "She'll try to keep the sun behind her."

Snape sat at the staff table and watched Harry lift a piece of toast. Harry bit it. Snape looked away. Harry placed the remaining half inside his sleeve. Wood continued.

"You have the faster broom."

"I had noticed."

"Do not become overconfident."

"You told me yesterday the Firebolt made losing mathematically impossible."

"Mathematics does not account for arrogance."

"Hermione will be devastated."

Across the Hall, Hermione turned. Draconia, sitting at the Slytherin table, followed Harry's glance and saw Snape. She looked at the hidden toast inside his sleeve. Harry pulled his cuff lower. Her eyes narrowed.

The match began at eleven. Harry rose into cold sunlight. The Firebolt felt perfect beneath him. Ravenclaw played cleanly and quickly. Their Chasers used the width of the pitch, drawing Gryffindor's defence out before cutting through the centre. Cho Chang circled high above the action on a Comet Two-Sixty, dark hair streaming behind her. Harry followed.

The crowd became colour below. Wind moved beneath his robes. His pulse steadied. For forty minutes, flying did what sleep could not. It narrowed his thoughts until nothing remained except the sky, the opposing Seeker and every flicker of gold between them. Ravenclaw scored. Gryffindor answered.

A Bludger crossed Harry's path. He dropped beneath it, turned and saw the Snitch near the western stands. Cho saw it too. They dived together. The Firebolt accelerated. Harry drew ahead.

The Snitch darted beneath the lowest row of seats. A black shape moved beyond the stand. The dog. Harry's concentration broke. He looked towards it.

The animal stood near the edge of the forest, watching him. Cho passed on Harry's right. The Snitch vanished. The Ravenclaw crowd shouted. Harry pulled up before striking the wooden supports.

"What happened?" Cho called.

"Nothing."

She regarded him doubtfully and climbed. Harry searched the ground. The dog had disappeared. Cold entered the air. Not the ordinary cold of March.

Dementors moved beyond the gates. Three of them glided along the boundary, distant but visible from Harry's height. Their cloaks dragged through the frost. His hands tightened upon the broom. The pitch faded at the edges. A scream waited inside him.

Not yet. Harry drew his wand. Below, Wood shouted something indecipherable. Cho turned towards the gates.

"You all right?"

Harry heard Lupin's voice.

Build it from the knowledge that fear has not yet taken everything.

The memory came. Pages turning in the library. Draconia fighting a dictionary. Hermione laughing while parchment flew around them. Harry raised his wand.

"Expecto Patronum!"

Silver light burst forwards. It did not form the animal. The shield spread through the air, bright enough to draw every face in the stands upwards. It curved around Harry and forced the approaching cold away. The Dementors remained beyond the boundary. They had not moved towards him.

The spell lasted anyway. Harry breathed. A flash of gold crossed the light. The Snitch. He shoved his wand away and dived.

Cho followed half a second later. The Firebolt gave Harry the difference. His fingers closed around the struggling golden wings twenty feet above the pitch. The whistle sounded. Noise struck him from every direction.

Gryffindor banners rose. Students shouted his name. Harry lifted the Snitch, sunlight breaking through its wings. For an instant, triumph reached him. Then the silver shield vanished. Everything beneath it felt empty. Harry landed among the team.

Wood seized him around the shoulders. Fred and George shouted into opposite ears. Alicia kissed his cheek. Students began pouring from the stands. Harry smiled when someone looked at him. He knew the correct expression. Hermione and Draconia reached the pitch together.

"You cast it," Hermione said.

Her face was bright with pride.

"Part of it."

"It crossed half the pitch."

"It didn't have a shape."

Draconia looked towards the Dementors beyond the gates.

"They stopped."

"They weren't coming closer."

"You did not know that."

Harry opened his mouth. The ground moved beneath him. Draconia caught his elbow.

"What happened?"

"Nothing."

Hermione touched his face.

"You're freezing."

"It's March."

"Harry."

"I'm fine."

He pulled away. The movement turned the world grey. Harry bent forward and vomited beside the Firebolt. Celebration continued around them. The nearest students stopped cheering.

Wood's hand froze upon Harry's shoulder. Hermione knelt.

"What did you eat this morning?"

Harry wiped his mouth.

"Breakfast."

"What?"

"Food."

"What food?"

Draconia looked at his sleeve. She reached inside it and removed the half piece of toast. Harry closed his eyes.

"That was for later."

Neither girl spoke. The silence was worse.

"I won the match," he said.

Hermione's expression broke. Draconia folded the toast inside her handkerchief.

"Yes," she said. "You did."

Harry straightened. He smiled at the team. The correct expression returned. This time, neither girl believed it.

They stopped trying to make him eat where he could see them.

Hermione left food inside his bag. Draconia bribed the kitchen elves into adding strengthening powder to Harry's tea until he detected the taste and threatened to report both her and the elves for conspiracy. Snape enforced breakfast for one week. Harry attended each morning, ate beneath the professor's unpleasant supervision and stopped arriving the day after the arrangement ended. The girls tried conversation.

Hermione asked about the Patronus. Harry explained the incantation and nothing else. Draconia invited him to watch Slytherin practise. He agreed, sat in the stands for ten minutes and disappeared before she landed. They tried silence. Harry filled it with jokes. They tried anger.

He apologised until they became guilty for raising their voices. Every attempt closed another door. Harry did not believe he was becoming worse. Worse implied movement. He felt suspended.

Days occurred around him. Bells rang. Lessons began and ended. People spoke, and Harry answered. He flew the Firebolt. He searched the map. He opened the photograph album and hated Sirius until hatred became another task requiring energy he no longer possessed. The part of Harry which noticed danger remained awake. Everything else began sleeping while he stood.

Near the end of March, Harry lost twenty minutes during dinner. He sat at the Gryffindor table beside Neville. One moment, the plates held roast chicken. The next, dessert had appeared and Hermione was saying his name.

He did not remember the food vanishing.

"What?" he asked.

Hermione studied him.

"I asked whether you wanted treacle tart."

"Yes."

"You don't like treacle tart tonight?"

"I said yes."

"You haven't moved."

Harry reached for the dish. His hand stopped halfway. The Great Hall sounded wrong. Hundreds of conversations pressed together beneath the ceiling. Cutlery struck plates. A first year laughed near the doors. The noises separated and sharpened until Harry heard each one individually. A fork fell.

Metal hit stone. The Chamber returned. Harry stood. The bench overturned behind him. People turned.

"Harry?" Neville said.

He moved towards the doors. Hermione followed. Draconia rose from the Slytherin table. Harry left the Hall before either reached him. The corridor beyond was empty.

He walked quickly. The noises followed through the doors. Metal. Stone. Laughter becoming screams. Harry reached the staircase and began climbing. He did not know where he intended to go. Away was enough. Second floor.

Third.

His chest tightened. He took out the Marauder's Map, but his fingers could not manage the folds. The parchment slipped to the floor. Harry left it. He turned into the north corridor. A suit of armour blocked the passage ahead. Harry took the narrow door beside it and entered an old service stairwell.

The door swung closed. Its lock clicked. Darkness sealed around him. Harry stopped.

No.

He reached for the handle. It would not turn.

"Open."

The door remained shut. Harry drew his wand.

"Alohomora."
