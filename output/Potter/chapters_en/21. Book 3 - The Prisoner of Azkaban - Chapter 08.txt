8

At eleven minutes past midnight, Christmas Day had already lasted twenty-seven hours for Hermione Granger. She sat alone in an unused classroom on the fourth floor with seven books open around her, a half-empty ink bottle beside her elbow and the cold weight of the Time-Turner resting against her chest.

Beyond the windows, snow continued falling over Hogwarts. The grounds had disappeared beneath it, leaving the castle suspended inside a white darkness. No footsteps passed in the corridor. The portraits had gone to sleep hours ago, and the suits of armour patrolling after Sirius Black's intrusion had been charmed to avoid this floor while Professor Flitwick inspected their tendency to challenge shadows. Hermione dipped her quill into the ink.

Symptoms of broom-based curse interference may include delayed response, altered balance, false acceleration, rider fixation—

She crossed out the final phrase. The entire paragraph came from a book published fifty-three years earlier and concerned racing brooms whose owners had deliberately altered the steering charms. It proved nothing about Harry's Firebolt. The next book proved less. The one after that contained an illustration of a Bulgarian witch being thrown into the Black Sea by an enchanted broom. Hermione had looked at the picture four times. Each time, the witch's hands lost their grip at exactly the same angle Harry's had before he fell. Hermione shut the book.

The resulting noise crossed the classroom too sharply. She waited. Nothing answered. At eight minutes past eleven on the first passage through the evening, Hermione had lain awake in the girls' dormitory listening to the snow brush against the windows. Lavender and Parvati had gone home for Christmas, leaving the room almost empty. Crookshanks slept across Hermione's feet. Downstairs, the common-room fire had burned low while Harry remained locked inside the boys' dormitory. At eleven minutes past eleven, Hermione had drawn the curtains around her bed, taken the hourglass from beneath her nightdress and turned it three times.

The world had folded. Noise stretched backwards. Falling snow climbed towards the clouds. The clock beside her bed spun from eleven to eight, carrying Hermione with it. Another Hermione existed in the castle now. Her younger self sat downstairs at the common-room table, trying to read while pretending not to watch the boys' staircase. In twenty minutes, she would give up, carry her books to bed and lie awake with Crookshanks across her feet. Three hours later, she would turn the Time-Turner and disappear into the past.

Hermione had spent those borrowed hours inside the unused classroom, building an argument no one had asked her to make. She told herself the research was necessary. Harry believed she had acted without evidence. If she found the right precedent—a famous assassination attempt, a disguised curse, an account of a broom sent anonymously during the war—he might understand why she had gone to Professor McGonagall. He might stop looking at her as if the Firebolt had been taken by Hermione's hands rather than the professor's. The evidence refused to appear. A line of candle wax reached the desk.

Hermione checked the clock. Twelve thirteen. She had slept four hours the previous night. The day before that, five. Christmas dinner had contained enough food to feed her properly, but she had barely tasted it. Harry had not attended. Draconia remained at the Slytherin table long after everyone else left, staring at the empty place where Harry should have sat. Hermione had spent twenty-seven hours awake and remained no closer to undoing what she had done. The Time-Turner could return her to the argument.

It could not make her choose differently. She gathered the books. Her hands shook when she lifted the largest volume. She moved too quickly, caught the ink bottle with her sleeve and sent it rolling across the desk. Hermione snatched it before it reached the edge. Dark ink spilled over her fingers.

For one terrible instant, the stain looked red. Mrs Norris hanging beneath the torch. Letters running down the corridor wall. Harry standing below them. Hermione blinked.

The ink was black. Only black. She cleaned her hands with a whispered spell, extinguished the candles and opened the classroom door. The corridor remained empty. Hermione checked both directions before stepping outside. The rules governing the Time-Turner were severe. She could not be seen by her earlier self. She could not alter an event she had already experienced. She could not allow anyone to understand that two versions of Hermione Granger occupied the castle at once.

She knew the rules. Knowing them did not quiet the strange pressure inside her skull whenever a floorboard creaked. At half past nine in the earlier version of the evening, Hermione had left the common room to retrieve a book she had forgotten in the library. That meant the seventh-floor corridor was unsafe for another seventeen minutes. She would need to wait behind the tapestry near the staircase until her younger self returned. Hermione climbed.

Her body felt heavier with each floor. The hours did not replace one another. They accumulated. Each turn of the hourglass added time to her life without adding strength to endure it. She reached the seventh floor and slipped behind the tapestry. Dust filled her nose. Hermione pressed one hand over her mouth before she could sneeze. The hidden space was narrow, occupied by the wooden framework of the tapestry and a stone ledge thick with dirt. Through a split in the fabric, she could see the corridor.

A suit of armour marched past. Its steps faded. Hermione checked her watch. Fourteen minutes. She leaned against the wall.

Footsteps approached from the direction of Gryffindor Tower. Hermione's pulse quickened. She watched through the split, expecting to see herself carrying the forgotten book. Harry appeared instead. He walked slowly beneath the torches, dressed in pyjama trousers, school shoes and the blue jumper Hermione's mother had knitted. His Invisibility Cloak was folded over one arm. The photograph album rested beneath the other.

Hermione almost called his name. The sound reached her throat before the Time-Turner pressed coldly against her skin. She could not allow him to see her. Her younger self was inside Gryffindor Tower. Harry would return there in less than an hour and find Hermione exactly where she was supposed to be. Hermione remained behind the tapestry. Harry stopped several feet away.

He looked towards the nearest window, though snow had covered most of the glass. His face was tired and empty. Not angry. Anger would have been easier. Anger moved towards a target. This looked like the stillness Hermione had seen in him after the letters stopped during the summer. Harry shifted the album. A photograph slipped from between its pages and fluttered to the floor. Hermione could see only the back.

Harry bent to retrieve it. His jumper rose slightly, revealing the sharp line of his spine beneath his nightshirt. He had not eaten Christmas dinner. Hermione had counted every untouched dish. The realisation came with the familiar impulse to fix it. She could step from behind the tapestry. She could take him to the kitchens. She could apologise, explain, insist, remain beside him until the emptiness in his face changed. She could do none of those things.

This hour did not belong to her. Harry tucked the photograph into the album.

"Brilliant Christmas," he muttered.

His voice broke against the final word. He continued down the corridor. Hermione watched until he disappeared around the corner. Then she pressed her forehead against the cold stone and wished she possessed less time in which to notice everything she could not repair.

The following morning, Harry did not come to breakfast.

Hermione knew before entering the Great Hall. She had lived through enough versions of his absence to recognise its shape. It existed in the empty place on the Gryffindor bench, in Neville's glance towards the doors and in the single plate Hermione had placed beside her before remembering that she had no reason to reserve it. Snow-bright light filled the Hall. Only a few dozen students remained for Christmas, leaving long gaps along the tables. Candles floated lower than usual, and the enchanted ceiling displayed a pale winter sky. Draconia sat alone at the Slytherin table.

Hermione carried her tea across the Hall and took the seat opposite her. Draconia looked at the cup, then at Hermione.

"You have a table."

"It's empty."

"So is most of this one."

"That makes it available."

Draconia returned to the letter in front of her. Narcissa's handwriting covered several pages. A green ribbon lay beside the envelope, and three unopened parcels occupied the bench. Hermione poured herself more tea from the Slytherin pot. Draconia's eyes narrowed.

"What?"

"That is not yours."

"It's tea."

"It is Slytherin tea."

"Does it contain snakes?"

"It might."

Hermione drank. The tea tasted exactly the same. She added milk. Draconia watched this with open disapproval.

"Where is Harry?" she asked.

"In Gryffindor Tower."

"You checked?"

Hermione thought of the corridor she had watched during a stolen hour.

"Yes."

"When?"

"Last night."

"That was not the question."

Hermione looked down at the tea. Draconia folded Narcissa's letter.

"You used it."

The chain beneath Hermione's robes seemed to grow heavier.

"I don't know what you mean."

"That answer has never convinced anyone."

"I used it for study."

"It is Christmas."

"Knowledge continues existing during holidays."

"How many turns?"

Hermione reached for the toast. Draconia caught the plate and pulled it out of reach.

"That is childish."

"How many?"

"Three."

Draconia stared at her.

"It was only three hours."

"Only."

"I was careful."

"Clearly. You look excellent."

Hermione's eyes burned. She had cleaned the ink from her hands twice, but dark stains remained around her fingernails. Her hair had been tied back without brushing it. One cuff of her jumper was inside out. She reached for the toast again. Draconia moved the plate closer this time. Hermione took a piece.

"What were you studying?" Draconia asked.

"Broom curses."

The answer silenced her. Hermione spread marmalade across the toast.

"I thought if I found a similar case, Harry might understand."

"Understand what?"

"Why I reported it."

"He understands why."

"No. He thinks I wanted to control him."

"You did control him."

Hermione put down the knife.

"I prevented him from flying an object which could be cursed."

"You decided what would happen before speaking to him."

"Because we both know what Harry would have decided."

"That does not make his choice irrelevant."

"If the broom had thrown him from the Astronomy Tower, would you still be defending his right to choose?"

Draconia's face went still.

"That is unfair."

"It is the question."

"No. It is the worst possible outcome disguised as the only one."

Hermione leaned forwards.

"We watched him fall."

"I remember."

"Then how can you—"

"I remember."

The words cut across the table. Draconia's right hand had closed around her left wrist. Her thumb pressed against the place where her pulse beat beneath the skin. Hermione stopped. Draconia lowered her hands into her lap.

"You believe being afraid makes you correct," she said.

"I believe being afraid can make someone cautious."

"Until the caution belongs to someone else."

"What would you have done?"

"I would have told him we wanted it checked."

"He would have refused."

"Then I would have explained."

"He would still have refused."

"Probably."

"And then?"

Draconia looked towards the Gryffindor table.

"I don't know."

Hermione laughed, though nothing about the conversation was funny.

"Exactly."

"No," Draconia said. "Not knowing what I would do is not the same as approving what you did."

"I chose his life over his trust."

"You say that as though you were offered only one."

"It may have been."

"And if it was not?"

Hermione looked at Harry's empty place. Draconia's voice softened.

"He used the passage because he knew we would prevent him from going."

"That does not make the passage our fault."

"No. But if we become people he must deceive in order to breathe, he will become better at deceiving us."

The sentence struck with uncomfortable precision. Hermione remembered Harry passing the tapestry, thin and silent, while she remained trapped inside an hour that should not have existed.

"I saw him last night," she said.

Draconia's attention sharpened.

"Where?"

"On the seventh floor. He had the photograph album."

"Did you speak to him?"

"I couldn't."

Understanding crossed Draconia's face.

"Your other self."

Hermione nodded.

"He looked—"

She stopped. Saying it aloud felt like a betrayal. Harry had not known she was there. He had been alone, or believed himself alone, and Hermione had already taken enough choices from him. Draconia waited.

"He looked tired," Hermione finished.

"That is not what you were going to say."

"No."

"What were you going to say?"

Hermione lifted her tea. The cup trembled against the saucer. Draconia reached across the table and steadied it. Neither commented. Draconia had no device capable of creating additional hours.

Instead, she divided the hours she possessed into smaller and smaller pieces until every part could be controlled. She woke each morning before the other Slytherin girls and named five facts before leaving her bed. Her name was Draconia Narcissa Malfoy. She was thirteen years old. She was inside the Slytherin dormitory beneath the Black Lake.

The diary had been destroyed. Harry and Hermione were alive. Only then did she draw back the curtains. The ritual had begun during the summer. At first, she had performed it only after nightmares. Then came mornings when the room felt unfamiliar for several seconds, and afternoons when she found herself staring at a blank page without remembering why she had opened it. By September, the five facts had become necessary.

She did not write them in a notebook. Draconia no longer trusted bound pages. Instead, she kept loose squares of parchment inside a locked silver box. Each morning received its own sheet. She recorded the date, the previous evening's final memory and the first thought which had belonged unmistakably to her. Most sheets ended with the same sentence.

Nothing is missing.

She never wrote what would happen if that changed. On the night after Christmas, Draconia woke with her wand pointed at Pansy Parkinson's bed. Green light moved across the ceiling. Something large passed beyond the dormitory windows, its shadow bending through the lake. Curtains stirred in the current of magic which followed Draconia's movement. Her heart beat hard enough to hurt.

Pansy continued sleeping. Draconia lowered the wand. Her hand did not feel like hers. The fingers were too cold. The grip was wrong. For half a second, she remembered another hand moving through hers, guiding a quill across paper while a boy's patient voice explained that secrets were proof of trust.

You may tell me.

Draconia dropped the wand. It struck the stone floor. Millicent stirred behind her curtains.

"Who's there?"

"No one," Draconia answered.

Her voice sounded correct. That helped. Millicent muttered and went back to sleep. Draconia sat upon the edge of the bed. Five facts.

Her name was Draconia Narcissa Malfoy. She was thirteen. The dormitory lay beneath the lake. The diary had been destroyed. Harry and Hermione were alive.

The final sentence caught. Harry had not come to breakfast. He had locked his dormitory door. He had left the Great Hall after giving McGonagall the Firebolt, and Draconia had not seen him since. Alive was not the same as safe. Her breathing quickened. Draconia reached for the silver mirror upon her bedside table.

She stopped before saying his name. He had asked for space. Care could become control. She knew that now. Knowing did not quiet the image of Harry falling.

Draconia opened the silver box and removed a fresh sheet of parchment. She dipped her quill and recorded the time.

Two seventeen in the morning.

She paused. The ink remained upon the page.

I woke with my wand in my hand.

It did not vanish. No answer formed beneath it. Draconia watched until the words dried. Then she turned the parchment over and wrote them again. Lessons resumed beneath a sky the colour of old iron.

The Christmas decorations disappeared overnight. Holly vanished from the staircases, the armour stopped singing and the Great Hall returned to its ordinary arrangement. Students filled the castle again, carrying stories of warm homes, family arguments and presents which became more expensive each time they were described. Harry listened when politeness required it. He spoke less. The change was gradual enough to deny. He still attended lessons. He still answered Snape with sufficient wit to lose Gryffindor points. He practised with the Quidditch team using one of the school brooms and complained about it with enough creativity to reassure Wood that his personality had survived.

But Hermione began noticing absences. Harry no longer waited outside Arithmancy. He stopped meeting Draconia and Hermione in the library before breakfast. At lunch he arrived late, drank pumpkin juice and left with most of his plate untouched. His Thursday afternoons remained empty. Lupin continued placing assignments upon his desk without mentioning the cancelled Patronus lessons. Harry completed them perfectly. That worried Hermione most.

Harry's work had always contained evidence that he would rather be doing almost anything else. Now each essay arrived on time, written neatly and without complaint. He finished early because he spent nights awake. Hermione knew this because the Marauder's Map showed his name crossing the castle after curfew. She knew because she checked. The map lay open across her knees one Tuesday morning while she sat inside an empty classroom waiting for herself to finish Ancient Runes. The strangeness of that sentence no longer troubled her as much as it should have.

Her earlier self attended Runes on the second floor. The Hermione sitting in the classroom had already completed that lesson, used the Time-Turner and attended Arithmancy in the hour which followed it. She now possessed forty-three minutes before she could safely rejoin the corridor without being seen. She should have used them to eat breakfast. Instead, she watched Harry's dot. It moved from Gryffindor Tower towards the library. Draconia's dot approached from the dungeons.

The two names met outside the doors. Hermione waited for them to enter. They did not. Harry Potter remained still. Draconia Malfoy remained beside him.

Then Harry's name turned and moved towards the western staircase. Draconia did not follow. Hermione traced the space between their dots. She could not hear what had been said. The map revealed location without context, movement without motive. It made knowledge feel complete while concealing everything which mattered. She folded the parchment.

The classroom clock struck nine. At the same instant, a second bell rang in the corridor. Hermione flinched. The sounds overlapped, one slightly ahead of the other. For several seconds, she did not know which hour she occupied.

Her hand closed around the Time-Turner. The classroom returned slowly. Desk. Window. Map. Clock. Nine in the morning.

Tuesday.

She had already attended Runes. She had not yet attended Charms. No—Charms had been yesterday. Hermione pulled her timetable from her sleeve.

Tuesday.

Ancient Runes, Arithmancy, Care of Magical Creatures, Transfiguration. Her breathing steadied. She checked her watch against the classroom clock. The watch was seven minutes slow. Or the clock was fast.

Hermione packed the map and left before either could answer. Professor McGonagall summoned her after Transfiguration. The classroom emptied around them. Desks returned to their places, quills vanished into bags and students poured into the corridor discussing the assigned essay. Harry left with Neville. He did not look back. Draconia paused outside the door. Hermione shook her head.

Go.

Draconia remained for another second before following the crowd. McGonagall closed the classroom door.

"Sit down, Miss Granger."

Hermione sat at the first desk. Professor McGonagall carried a stack of essays towards her own table. The afternoon light showed more silver in her hair than Hermione remembered from September.

"You have submitted three assignments this week," McGonagall said.

"Yes, Professor."

"One was due next month."

"I had time."

McGonagall placed the essays down.

"That is precisely what concerns me."

Hermione's hand moved towards her collar. She stopped it.

"I am keeping up with the schedule."

"You are exceeding the schedule."

"Is that wrong?"

"It can be."

Hermione did not understand the answer. McGonagall opened the top drawer of her desk and removed a narrow ledger. Columns of dates covered the page, accompanied by numbers written in dark blue ink. Hermione recognised them. Time-Turner rotations. Her stomach dropped.

"The Ministry device records its use," McGonagall said. "A necessary safeguard."

"You never told me."

"You were instructed to restrict its use to your approved timetable. Had you followed that direction, the method of recording it would be irrelevant."

Hermione looked at the ledger. Three rotations on Christmas night had been circled.

"I needed to study."

"On Christmas Day."

"Yes."

"Between eight and eleven at night."

"The library was closed, so I used my own books."

McGonagall's mouth thinned. "That is not the objection."

Hermione straightened.

"Nobody saw me. I didn't change anything. I followed all the rules about that."

"You added three unnecessary hours to a day in which you had already refused to sleep."

Hermione's certainty faltered.

"How do you know?"

"Because I possess eyes."

McGonagall moved around the desk and sat opposite her.

"The Time-Turner creates additional time, Miss Granger. It does not create additional endurance."

"I am not tired."

"You arrived at Transfiguration with ink upon your cheek, answered a question I had not asked and attempted to transfigure your matchbox after it had already become a needle."

Hermione looked down. The needle rested beside her parchment, silver and perfect.

"I corrected it."

"That is not the point."

"I can manage."

"So you continue telling everyone."

The sentence sounded too much like Harry. Hermione looked towards the classroom windows. Snow had begun again, fine white flecks disappearing against the glass.

"The course load is demanding," McGonagall said. "It was always going to be. You may choose to discontinue one subject."

"No."

"You have not heard the available options."

"I don't want to discontinue anything."

"Why?"

"Because I can do them."

"Ability is not obligation."

"If I stop, I've failed."

McGonagall removed her spectacles.

"Failed whom?"

The question entered a place Hermione had kept hidden beneath timetables and completed essays. She thought of Harry falling. Draconia lying in the Chamber. Her parents standing outside the Leaky Cauldron with returned letters and no way to reach the boy they had invited into their home.

"If I can know more," Hermione said, "then I should."

"Why?"

"Because next time I might know what to do."

McGonagall's face softened. Hermione hated it.

"Knowledge does not guarantee prevention," the professor said.

"It helps."

"Yes."

"Then why should I choose not to have it?"

"Because you are a child, Miss Granger."

"I won't always be."

"No. But exhausting the child does not produce a more capable adult."

Hermione blinked hard. McGonagall slid the ledger towards her.

"You will return to authorised use immediately. If I see another unexplained rotation, we will reconsider whether you should possess this device."

Hermione's hand closed around the edge of the desk.

"You'll take it?"

"If necessary."

"I need it."

"You need to sleep."

"That is not the same thing."

"No," McGonagall said. "It is more important."

Hermione left the classroom with the Time-Turner hidden beneath her robes and the disturbing sense that one of them had misunderstood everything. She could not decide which. Draconia waited near the staircase. She stood beside a window overlooking the courtyard, a Potions textbook tucked beneath one arm. Students moved around her in two directions.

"What did she want?" Draconia asked.

"Nothing."

"Your face suggests otherwise."

"My face is tired."

The answer slipped out before Hermione could prevent it. Draconia's gaze sharpened.

"McGonagall knows," Hermione said quietly.

"About the additional turns?"

"Yes."

"What happened?"

"She warned me."

"Only warned you?"

Hermione looked at her. Draconia appeared relieved. The feeling irritated her.

"You wanted her to take it."

"I wanted her to stop you from harming yourself."

"I am not harming myself."

"You are hiding in unused classrooms because two versions of you cannot occupy the same corridor."

"That is how the device works."

"That does not make it sensible."

"Neither does waking with your wand aimed at another person's bed."

Draconia went still. Hermione wished the sentence unsaid immediately. She had noticed the stiffness in Draconia's wrist at breakfast. The shadows beneath her eyes. The square of loose parchment which had fallen from her bag when she reached for a quill. She had not been told. She had only assembled the evidence.

Draconia's voice became quiet.

"How do you know that?"

"I didn't."

"You guessed."

"Yes."

"And decided to use it."

"I'm sorry."

"No, you are not. You are frightened, so you believe the cruelty should be excused."

Hermione felt the words strike their earlier conversation. Students continued past. Draconia stepped away from the window.

"Do not investigate me, Granger."

"I wasn't."

"You are always investigating."

"I notice things."

"So does Harry. He does not turn everyone he loves into a problem to solve."

The unfairness took Hermione's breath.

"No," she said. "He only lies, disappears and nearly dies alone."

Draconia's expression sharpened.

"At least I do something."

"You reported a broom."

"I protected him."

"You keep saying that. Perhaps if you repeat it enough, he will begin speaking to you again."

Hermione's eyes burned. Draconia saw. The anger left her face, but pride kept both of them in place.

"Fine," Hermione said.

She adjusted the books in her arms.

"What does that mean?"

"It means I have Arithmancy."

"You attended Arithmancy this morning."

"I have work."

Draconia looked towards the classroom door behind them. Hermione walked away. She did not turn the Time-Turner. She wanted to. That frightened her more than McGonagall's ledger had.

Draconia remained beside the window after Hermione disappeared. Her reflection stared back from the glass. Pale hair. Grey eyes. Controlled posture. Her face. She touched the tip of her wand beneath her sleeve.

The urge to test herself came immediately.

Name.

Age.

Place.

The diary was gone. Harry and Hermione were alive. Draconia stopped before completing the ritual. Hermione's accusation followed her into the dungeons.

At least I do something.

Draconia did things. She checked every drink before touching it. She examined parchment for enchantments. She wrote her days in fragments and locked them inside a silver box. She watched Harry's hands whenever he became too quiet. She watched Hermione touch the hourglass and pretended not to count. None of it changed the Chamber. None of it guaranteed she would recognise the next voice which entered her mind. The Potions classroom smelled of crushed roots and bitter steam. Cauldrons waited upon the tables, their black surfaces reflecting the green light from the high dungeon windows.

Professor Snape stood at the front.

"Today," he said, "you will brew an Antidote to Uncommon Poisons. The instructions upon the board are incomplete."

Groans moved through the room. Snape's gaze passed over the Gryffindors and sharpened with familiar contempt.

"You are expected to identify the missing stages by testing your mixture. Those incapable of distinguishing an antidote from decorative sludge may begin composing their apologies to Madam Pomfrey."

Harry sat beside Neville. Hermione took the table behind them. Draconia worked with Theodore Nott near the Slytherin side. Harry did not look at either girl. The lesson began.

Draconia measured powdered moonstone into the cauldron. Steam rose in pale ribbons. Theodore prepared the bezoar solution and spoke only when necessary. The antidote changed from blue to silver.

Correct.

Draconia stirred clockwise. The surface darkened. She stopped. A line of black moved through the potion.

Ink.

Her breath caught. The line vanished.

"Malfoy?" Theodore asked.

Draconia cast a detection charm. The potion remained silver. She cast it again. Then a third time.

"What are you doing?" Theodore asked.

"Checking for contamination."

"I didn't put anything in it."

"I did not say you had."

"You looked at me."

"I possess eyes."

Across the room, a cauldron emitted a purple cloud. Seamus coughed. Snape vanished the mixture before it reached the ceiling and removed fifteen points from Gryffindor for allowing a medicinal potion to develop weather. Draconia tested her antidote again.

Clear.

She added the bezoar. The liquid turned gold.

Correct.

A voice whispered at the edge of her hearing.

You see? You know exactly what to do.

Draconia dropped the spoon. Metal struck stone. Harry turned. Their eyes met across the classroom. He had heard nothing.

The concern in his face made the voice disappear. Draconia retrieved the spoon.

"I am fine," she said before anyone asked.

Harry's expression closed. He turned back to Neville. The words tasted wrong. Snape reached their table ten minutes later. He examined the golden antidote.

"Acceptable."

Theodore relaxed. Snape's gaze moved to the cluster of used testing strips beside Draconia's hand.

"How many times did you check the mixture?"

"Four."

"Seven."

Draconia looked up.

"I discarded three strips."

"I am capable of counting rubbish."

Theodore became intensely interested in cleaning his knife.

"The antidote is stable," Snape said.

"Yes, Professor."

"Then why are you still testing it?"

"To ensure—"

"That the previous six results had not conspired to mislead you?"

Several Slytherins nearby smiled. Snape's voice lowered.

"Remain after class."

Draconia's stomach tightened. When the room emptied, Harry left last among the Gryffindors. He paused beside the doorway. Snape noticed.

"Unless you have developed an unexpected passion for antidotes, Potter, leave."

Harry looked at Draconia. She wanted him to stay.

"Go," she said.

He went. The door closed. Snape returned to his desk. He placed Draconia's seven testing strips in a line across the black wood.

"Your father wrote to me."

Draconia's embarrassment became anger.

"Of course he did."

"He did not describe the rituals."

Her anger stopped. Snape sat.

"What did he say?"

"That you are sleeping poorly. That you refuse Dreamless Sleep. That your mother found three mirrors covered after you left the Manor."

Draconia looked towards the dungeon windows.

"Mother should not search my room."

"An argument you may make to her."

"I am not possessed."

"No."

"I know where I am."

"Do you?"

Her eyes returned to him. Snape's face contained none of the public mockery he used in class.

"I do," she said.

"Then tell me."

"My name is Draconia Narcissa Malfoy. I am thirteen years old. I am in the Potions classroom beneath Hogwarts. The diary is destroyed. Harry and Hermione are alive."

The five facts left her without pause. Snape stared. Draconia realised too late that he had not meant the question literally.

"How often?" he asked.

She said nothing.

"How often do you recite that?"

"Only when necessary."

"How often is it necessary?"

Draconia looked at the testing strips.

"Every morning."

Snape leaned back. The lamps threw hollows beneath his eyes.

"You keep trying to prove that you remember."

"I want to be sure."

"You were sure the first time. Then you checked again."

"It proves I remember."

"It proves you remember those five sentences."

Draconia's hands closed in her lap.

"What would you prefer?"

"That you cease confusing vigilance with recovery."

"I am not confused."

"You checked a correctly brewed potion seven times because its surface briefly darkened."

"There might have been contamination."

"There was not."

"I know that now."

"You knew after the first test."

Draconia looked away. Snape gathered the strips.

"When the Dark Lord entered a mind," he said, "he did not always leave it in the same condition."

"Your family does."

Draconia's mouth tightened.

"My family is not a competent society."

Hermione made a small, startled sound. It became laughter. The laughter broke halfway through, and Hermione covered her face.

"I don't know which version of today I told Harry I'd meet him," she whispered.

Draconia's amusement disappeared. Hermione lowered her hands.

"What if I forget something important because I've lived too much around it? What if one day I think I warned you, but I only warned a version who no longer exists?"

"All the versions exist."

"That isn't better."

"No," Draconia admitted.

Hermione stared at the Time-Turner.

"If I have the hours and still fail you, then what does that make me?"

Draconia thought of the silver box beneath her bed. Five facts. Seven testing strips. A wand in her hand and a voice which had been dead for months.

"Thirteen," she said.

Hermione looked at her.

"Frightened," Draconia continued. "Exhausted. Irritatingly convinced that the universe will collapse if you stop organising it."

"That is not an answer."

"It is several."

Hermione's fingers loosened around the hourglass. Draconia waited.

"I won't tell McGonagall tonight," she said.

Hermione searched her face.

"But?"

"Tomorrow, we make a schedule which contains sleep."

"I have a schedule."

"Then we make one based upon the behaviour of a human being."

"And if this happens again?"

"We go to McGonagall together."

Hermione looked down.

"You'll report me."

"No. I will tell you that I think we need to speak to her. You will decide whether to walk beside me or be dragged."

"That still sounds like control."

"It is a threat. Those are different."

Hermione's mouth moved faintly.

"Why are you helping me?"

Draconia looked towards the classroom door.

"Because you came to the Chamber."

"I was Petrified."

"You came afterwards."

Hermione remembered entering the hospital wing after Mandrake Restorative had returned movement to her body. Harry lay asleep beneath white sheets. Draconia occupied the bed beside him, awake and silent, staring at hands which no longer trusted themselves. Hermione had climbed between both beds and refused to leave.

"That was different," she said.

"Everything is different when it happens to you."

Hermione leaned her head against the wall. After several seconds, her shoulder rested against Draconia's. Neither moved away. They remained on the floor until ten thirty. When the minute arrived, Hermione felt the air shift.

Somewhere elsewhere in the castle, her earlier self turned the hourglass and vanished into the past. The day closed around one Hermione again. Draconia helped her stand. Hermione swayed. Draconia caught her.

"I can walk."

"I know."

"You can let go."

"I know."

She did not release Hermione until the corridor stopped tilting beneath her feet. Ronald Weasley declared Crookshanks a murderer two weeks later. He entered the Great Hall before dinner carrying a torn bedsheet. Three dark spots marked the fabric. Orange hairs clung near one edge. Ron dropped it in front of Hermione.

"Happy now?"

Conversation quieted along the Gryffindor table. Hermione looked from the sheet to him.

"What happened?"

"Scabbers is gone."

Harry stopped eating.

"When?" he asked.

"This afternoon. I left him in the dormitory. Came back, found this."

Ron pointed at the orange hairs.

"Your cat got in."

Crookshanks sat beneath Hermione's bench. He washed one paw without interest. Hermione lifted the sheet. The stains had dried brown at the edges.

"That might not be blood."

"It smells like blood."

"Why did you smell it?"

Ron's ears reddened. "That isn't the point."

"Did you find a body?"

"No, because that thing ate him!"

Crookshanks looked up at the accusation.

"You don't know that," Hermione said.

"There's blood and cat hair on my bed."

"Crookshanks sheds everywhere."

"Exactly!"

Draconia crossed from the Slytherin table before Snape could notice. She examined the sheet over Hermione's shoulder.

"The fabric is cut."

Ron glared at her. "Claws."

"No. The tear begins cleanly."

Harry stood.

"Where did you find it?"

"On my bed."

"Was the window open?"

"No."

"The door?"

"Dean came in before me."

"Anyone could have entered."

Ron stared at him.

"You're defending the cat?"

"I'm asking."

"No, you lot are making it complicated because you can't admit Hermione's pet finally killed something."

Hermione's face tightened.

"He did not."

"You don't know that."

"Neither do you."

"I found the evidence!"

Draconia lifted the sheet towards the candles.

"The blood is in drops."

"So?"

"A cat killing a rat would produce more."

Several students stopped eating. Harry looked at the hairs.

"Where's Crookshanks been all afternoon?"

"With me," Hermione said.

The answer came too quickly. Draconia turned. Hermione continued.

"Most of it."

"How much?" Harry asked.

"I was in the library."

"That was not the question."

Hermione's hand moved towards the Time-Turner. She stopped. Ron laughed bitterly.

"Of course none of you care. It's only my rat."

Harry looked at him. Scabbers had been old, ill-tempered and mostly interested in food. Ron was not Harry's friend, but grief did not require friendship to be real.

"I'm sorry he's gone," Harry said.

Ron's anger faltered. Harry continued.

"But Crookshanks didn't necessarily kill him."

"Whose side are you on?"

"The side where we find out what happened."

Draconia looked towards the entrance doors.

"Scabbers has escaped into the walls before."

"He came back," Ron said.

"Then he might again."

Ron gathered the sheet.

"If I see that cat near our dormitory, I'm hexing it."

Hermione rose.

"If you touch him—"

"Hermione," Harry said.

She turned.

"He thinks Crookshanks killed Scabbers."

"He hasn't proved it."

"No. But you haven't proved he didn't."

Betrayal entered her face. Harry recognised it. He had worn the same expression on Christmas morning.

"I'm not saying Ron's right."

"You're saying I'm wrong."

"I'm saying we don't know."

The words sounded like Draconia, Lucius and Lupin discussing Sirius. Hermione heard that too. She sat. Ron walked away. Crookshanks climbed onto her lap.

Hermione placed both arms around him. Draconia remained standing beside the table.

"Where was he this afternoon?" she asked quietly.

Hermione's fingers disappeared into orange fur.

"With me until three."

"And after?"

"I don't know."

Harry studied the cat. A narrow scratch marked the top of Crookshanks's head. It was too straight to have been made by a rat's teeth.

"Did he go outside?"

"The doors were closed."

"He knows passages," Draconia said.

Crookshanks began to purr. Harry thought of the black dog beside the Quidditch stands. He said nothing.

That night, he opened the Marauder's Map and searched Gryffindor Tower. Ronald Weasley slept inside the boys' dormitory.

Dean Thomas, Seamus Finnigan and Neville Longbottom occupied their beds. Crookshanks sat beside Hermione in the common room. No dot appeared for Scabbers. Harry had not expected one. The map did not name animals. He searched the walls anyway.

Near the edge of the parchment, outside the grounds, a dot moved once and disappeared before Harry could read the name.

By March, the trio had perfected the appearance of recovery. Harry ate when they were watching. Hermione used the Time-Turner only according to the schedule Draconia had helped construct. Draconia tested each potion twice.

They met in the library, completed assignments and exchanged insults with enough familiarity to convince anyone listening that nothing important had changed. The Firebolt flew perfectly. Scabbers did not return. Sirius Black did not enter the castle again. Professor Lupin taught every Defence lesson but no longer asked Harry to remain afterwards.

Everything appeared stable. The stability required constant effort. Hermione slept six hours and called it improvement. She sometimes woke without knowing whether the lesson she remembered had occurred that morning or in a borrowed hour the day before. When that happened, she checked the notes inside her sleeve and told no one. Draconia reduced the five facts to four, then added the fifth again after waking with ink upon her fingers. The ink came from a Potions assignment she remembered completing. That did not stop her from burning the parchment. Harry carried the map everywhere.

He searched for Sirius at night and looked at the wedding photograph when he believed the curtains hid him. His face thinned. His jokes became sharper and less frequent. He spoke to Lupin only in class. Each of them noticed the others. None knew how to begin. One evening, they occupied their usual library table while rain moved softly against the windows. Harry sat on one side with the Marauder's Map concealed beneath his Defence essay.

Hermione sat opposite him, the Time-Turner hidden beneath her collar. Draconia occupied the end of the table with a loose square of parchment inside her Potions book. Three secrets within arm's reach. Harry's stomach growled. Hermione's eyes lifted.

Draconia's hand paused. Neither spoke. Harry turned a page. Hermione returned to her calculations. Draconia completed the date at the top of her parchment.

The map between them showed every person moving through Hogwarts. Tiny names crossed corridors, slept in towers, worked in offices and hurried towards places they were expected to be. It revealed exactly where everyone was. It could not show when Hermione had last slept. It could not show why Draconia feared her own hands. It could not show what Harry saw whenever he closed his eyes.

At midnight, the three names remained together in the library. On the map, that looked close enough.
