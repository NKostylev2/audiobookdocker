7

Three days before Halloween, Gryffindor Tower began to smell of scorched sugar. The source was a collection of experimental sweets the Weasley twins had acquired from Hogsmeade and were attempting to improve beside the common-room fire. Their first batch made Neville's ears whistle whenever he breathed. The second caused Lee Jordan to announce his thoughts in the voice of Professor Snape. By the third, the carpet had acquired several smoking holes and Percy had threatened to report everyone involved. Harry sat near the window with the remains of his Nimbus across his knees.

Professor Flitwick had attempted a Mending Charm. Madam Hooch had tried three more specialised spells intended for damaged broomwood. Even Wood, who regarded the destruction of a good broom with the solemnity usually reserved for funerals, had spent an evening binding the broken handle with copper wire and muttering encouragement over it. Nothing had worked. The broom remained in pieces. Its magic had not survived the Whomping Willow. Harry fitted two sections of the handle together, watching the grain align along the break. The wood remembered its shape well enough to mock him with it. The instant he released the pieces, they separated again.

Behind him, Fred bit into an orange sweet. His hair turned blue.

George examined the result. "Better."

"My tongue's gone numb."

"Less ideal."

"Can I still speak?"

"Unfortunately."

Hermione looked up from the three books surrounding her. "You should stop eating unidentified substances."

"They're identified," George said. "Those are orange."

"That is a colour."

"And a fruit."

"You don't know that they contain oranges."

Fred opened his mouth and extended a bright green tongue. Hermione closed her book.

"I'm going to the library."

"You said that twenty minutes ago," Harry observed.

"I was waiting for the rain to ease."

Harry glanced at the window. Rain struck the glass so heavily that the grounds had disappeared. Water crawled beneath the old frame and collected upon the sill, where a folded towel had already soaked through.

"It's inside," he said.

"The corridor to the library is damp."

"The corridor is made of stone."

"Wet stone is slippery."

"So stay."

Hermione's hand moved towards the chain concealed beneath her robes. She stopped before touching it. The gesture had become more frequent over the last week. Harry would see her checking the hourglass at meals, beneath classroom desks and whenever a clock struck unexpectedly. She had begun carrying her timetable folded inside her sleeve, though she knew every lesson upon it without looking. Draconia had noticed too. Neither had said anything.

They were attempting not to count. It was going badly.

Hermione gathered her books. "I have to finish an essay."

"You finished it yesterday," Harry said.

"That was the first draft."

"How many drafts does one essay need?"

"As many as necessary."

"Professor Vector only requested one."

Hermione stared at him as if he had deliberately misunderstood the purpose of education.

"You could come," she said.

Harry looked down at the broom.

"I'm busy."

"With what?"

"Confirming that it's still broken."

Hermione's expression softened. Harry wished it had not.

"You could take it to Professor McGonagall again."

"She's already seen it."

"Perhaps another spell—"

"Nothing can repair it."

He had not meant to sound angry. The words emerged flat instead, which was worse. Anger invited argument. Flatness made Hermione careful. She moved closer and sat upon the arm of the chair.

"We could ask Lucius," she said. "The Malfoys know more about broom enchantments than most families."

"Draconia already wrote to him."

"What did he say?"

"That it was beyond repair."

Hermione looked at the splintered wood.

"I'm sorry."

"It's a broom."

They both knew he had used the same sentence in the hospital wing. Hermione did not challenge it. She touched the scarlet scarf around Harry's neck instead. The golden Snitches woven through it had gathered near his shoulder, trembling whenever the rain struck particularly hard.

"You're wearing it indoors."

"It was cold."

"The common room is warm."

"Then it's decorative."

"That is the first sensible thing you've said about it."

Harry glanced up. A smile hovered at the edge of Hermione's mouth. He reached towards her stack of books and slid the bottom one free before she could stop him.

Numerical Harmonies and the Elasticity of Time.

Hermione snatched it back.

"That isn't for an essay."

"It might be."

"In Care of Magical Creatures?"

"Arithmancy."

"You said the essay was for Hagrid."

Hermione held the book against her chest.

"I have more than one essay."

"Convenient."

"Harry."

"You're meant to use that thing for getting to lessons, not creating enough time to rewrite everything three times."

"I know what I'm meant to use it for."

Her answer arrived too quickly. The chain beneath her collar flashed as she stood. Harry watched her. The room continued around them. Fred's hair shifted from blue to purple. Percy shouted from the stairs. Crookshanks rolled onto his back in front of the fire, paws curled and orange fur glowing in the flames.

"We said we'd tell each other," Harry said.

"When care becomes control."

"And when we're hiding things."

"We said you would tell us when you were frightened."

"That wasn't meant to apply only to me."

Hermione's fingers tightened around the books.

"I am managing my timetable."

"I didn't say you weren't."

"You didn't need to."

The words hung between them. Harry recognised the defensive edge because it belonged to him. Hermione appeared to recognise it too. Her shoulders lowered.

"I'll return before dinner," she said.

"That isn't what I asked."

"I know."

She left before he could try again. Harry watched the portrait hole close behind her. The chair opposite him creaked. Crookshanks had abandoned the fire and jumped onto it. The cat stared at the broken broom, yellow eyes narrowed in apparent judgement.

"You can't fix it either," Harry said.

Crookshanks yawned. Outside, the rain continued to erase the world. Halloween entered the castle by candlelight. Pumpkins appeared in the entrance hall first, stacked along the marble staircase with faces carved into their orange skin. Some grinned. Others scowled at passing students. One near the dungeon corridor had been enchanted to complain whenever anyone stepped too close and spent most of the morning insulting first years.

By evening, black candles floated beneath the enchanted ceiling of the Great Hall. Bats swept among them in restless circles, their wings casting quick shadows over the tables. Storm clouds crowded the false sky overhead, though the actual rain had stopped beyond the windows. The feast began with a burst of music from a skeleton orchestra positioned beside the staff table. Harry sat between Neville and Hermione. Across the Hall, Draconia occupied her usual place among the Slytherins, her pale hair bright against the black and green decorations. A folded napkin bird landed beside Harry's plate. He opened it beneath the table.

The pumpkin behind Professor Snape resembles Lockhart.

Harry examined the display. The largest pumpkin had an enormous smile carved across half its face. A bewitched curl of orange vine had fallen over its forehead. He wrote beneath Draconia's sentence.

It needs more teeth.

Hermione leaned against his shoulder to read.

"He would be delighted by that comparison."

"That is what makes it insulting," Harry said.

She took the quill.

Do not let him hear you. He will commission portraits.

The bird flew back across the Hall. Draconia read their addition and looked towards the pumpkin. Pansy Parkinson attempted to see the note over her shoulder. Draconia folded it immediately and placed it inside her sleeve. At the staff table, Snape noticed. His gaze moved from Draconia to Harry and narrowed. Harry raised his goblet politely.

Snape deducted no points, though his expression suggested he had considered inventing a reason. The plates filled. Golden roast potatoes rolled around dishes of beef and gravy. Pies opened their crusts to release tiny trails of fragrant steam. Bowls of glazed carrots marched into neat rows whenever serving spoons approached them, and a dish of green peas attempted to escape Neville by rolling beneath his pumpkin juice. For a while, Harry allowed the noise of the feast to carry him.

He listened to Seamus describe an Irish spirit which supposedly stole socks and returned them knitted into tea cosies. Hermione insisted that this sounded more like an excuse invented by one of Seamus's cousins. Neville accidentally ate a Fanged Toffee and spent ten minutes trying to speak around a pair of rapidly lengthening teeth. Harry laughed until his ribs hurt. Nothing terrible happened. That had become a thought he noticed whenever happiness lasted longer than expected. He disliked the thought. It waited at the edge of good moments and measured them, warning him not to become comfortable.

Tonight, Harry ignored it. He sent a dish of treacle tart towards Hermione. She cut a slice, then placed half of it upon his plate without asking.

"You've already served me," Harry said.

"You ate half your dinner."

"I ate all the potatoes."

"Potatoes are not a complete meal."

"They've never complained."

"That doesn't mean—"

A sugar bat struck Hermione in the forehead. She stopped. Harry looked towards the Slytherin table. Draconia was examining her fingernails. Another bat circled Hermione's head.

Harry caught it from the air and bit off one wing. Hermione stared across the Hall. Draconia finally looked up.

"That," Hermione said, "was childish."

Draconia lifted her goblet. Hermione drew her wand beneath the table. The curl of vine upon the Lockhart pumpkin extended suddenly, wrapped itself around Draconia's shoulders and placed a crown of orange leaves upon her head. Pansy shrieked. Several Slytherins ducked.

Draconia's eyes widened. Harry covered his mouth with both hands. Professor Snape turned. Hermione had already returned her wand to her sleeve and was cutting her tart with perfect innocence. Snape's gaze passed over the Gryffindor table.

Harry attempted not to choke. The vine released Draconia, leaving three leaves caught in her hair. She removed them one at a time. A slow smile appeared upon her face. Harry recognised the promise of retaliation.

For the rest of the feast, the three of them were thirteen years old. They argued through enchanted birds, stole food from one another across House tables and competed to see who could make the Lockhart pumpkin resemble the former professor most convincingly without being caught. By dessert, it had acquired golden eyebrows, a set of polished teeth and a cluster of smaller pumpkins arranged like admiring students. Even McGonagall's mouth tightened suspiciously when she saw it. When the final dishes vanished, Dumbledore rose. The candles brightened above him.

"A most enjoyable evening," he said, looking directly at the decorated pumpkin. "Though I fear one of our guests has suffered a crisis of identity."

Laughter rolled through the Hall. Harry looked across at Draconia. She was already looking back. The warning thought returned. Nothing terrible happened.

Not yet. The Gryffindors discovered the Fat Lady shortly before ten. Harry reached the seventh-floor corridor behind Percy and several second years. The crowd ahead had stopped moving. Students pressed towards the front, whispering questions no one could answer.

"What's happened?" Hermione asked.

Percy pushed between two older students. Then he froze. The portrait hung in ribbons. Canvas drooped from the frame in long, jagged strips. The painted landscape behind the Fat Lady's chair had been slashed open until the wall showed through. Golden wood lay splintered across the floor. The Fat Lady herself was gone.

Harry stared at the ruined frame. The corridor grew quiet around him.

"Where is she?" Neville whispered.

Nobody answered.

Percy drew his wand. "Everyone stay where you are."

This instruction produced immediate movement. Younger students backed towards the staircase. Older ones crowded closer. A first-year began to cry when a loose section of canvas shifted in the draught. Hermione's hand closed around Harry's sleeve. He looked at her. She was staring at the cuts.

"They're too deep," she said.

"What?"

"That wasn't done with a quill or a piece of broken wood."

Harry saw it then. The edges of the canvas had been torn with force. Several slashes continued into the frame itself, gouging the wood. A blade. Footsteps struck the stones behind them. Professor McGonagall swept around the corner, tartan dressing gown visible beneath her cloak. Dumbledore followed with Snape and Flitwick close behind. The crowd separated.

McGonagall looked once at the portrait. All colour left her face.

"Mr Weasley," she said, "take the students to the Great Hall. No one is to enter a corridor alone. Professor Flitwick, alert the other Heads of House."

Snape raised his wand. "The attacker may still be nearby."

A silver shape burst from its tip and flew down the corridor. Harry had only a glimpse before it vanished around the corner: a narrow, pale creature with wings drawn close to its body. A Patronus. Dumbledore stepped towards the empty frame.

"Sir Cadogan," he called.

The knight in the neighbouring portrait jolted awake. He drew his sword so quickly that he struck himself upon the helmet.

"Invaders!" he cried. "Villainy! Inform me of the direction, and I shall render it significantly less occupied!"

"The Fat Lady," Dumbledore said. "Did you see where she went?"

"No, good sir! But I heard a scream capable of unmanning an army."

One of the painted monks farther along the corridor leaned from his frame.

"She ran towards the Arithmancy passage."

"Search every portrait," Dumbledore instructed. "Find her. Ask what she saw."

Painted figures began hurrying through one another's frames. Percy attempted to move the Gryffindors. Harry remained where he was. His scar did not hurt. The air had not grown cold. No voice moved inside the walls. That did not make the violence in front of him less real.

Someone had reached the entrance to Gryffindor Tower. Someone carrying a knife.

"Potter."

Snape stood beside him. Harry looked up.

"Move."

"Who did this?"

"That question will not become easier to answer because you are obstructing the corridor."

"Was it Sirius Black?"

Snape's face became unreadable.

"Great Hall," he said.

Hermione tugged Harry's sleeve. They followed the crowd downstairs. Every floor added more students. Ravenclaws emerged in dressing gowns. Hufflepuffs arrived from below, asking whether the rumours were true. Slytherins crossed the entrance hall beneath Professor Flitwick's silver Patronus, confusion giving way to fear as the story spread.

Azkaban.

Knife.

Gryffindor Tower. Harry searched the crowd. Draconia appeared near the foot of the marble staircase beside Pansy and Theodore Nott. Snape had reached them first. He spoke to the Slytherins in a low voice, directing the prefects to keep everyone together. Draconia saw Harry over Snape's shoulder. Her composure broke.

She left the group.

"Miss Malfoy," Snape called.

She continued across the entrance hall. Harry met her halfway.

"Are you hurt?"

"No."

"Was he inside?"

"I don't know."

"Did you see him?"

"No."

Draconia caught his wrist, fingers pressed to his pulse. She appeared unaware of the movement until Harry looked down. Her grip loosened but did not disappear.

"The Fat Lady's portrait was destroyed," Hermione said. "She's missing."

"With a knife?" Draconia asked.

"We think so."

Snape reached them.

"Miss Malfoy."

"I am returning."

"You are abandoning your House in the middle of a security emergency."

"I crossed one room."

"You will cross it again."

Draconia's jaw tightened. Harry expected her to argue. Instead, she turned her hand and pressed something small into his palm. The silver mirror caught the candlelight between them.

"You kept yours?" she asked.

"It's in my trunk."

"That is not useful."

"We're all going to the Great Hall."

"Get it when you can."

"Draco," Harry said quietly. "I'm not alone."

Her eyes moved towards the hundreds of students crowding the hall.

"That has not prevented much."

Snape's expression altered at the words. Only slightly.

"Miss Malfoy," he said, quieter now.

She returned to the Slytherins. The Great Hall had been stripped of its Halloween decorations by the time the students entered. Tables moved themselves against the walls. Hundreds of purple sleeping bags appeared across the floor in ordered rows, leaving narrow paths between them. No one intended to sleep. Rumours travelled faster than the prefects could silence them. Sirius Black had entered through the kitchens. Sirius Black had used Polyjuice Potion. Sirius Black had murdered three house-elves. Sirius Black was hiding beneath one of the sleeping bags. Harry sat with Hermione and Neville near the centre of the Hall.

Draconia settled several rows away among the Slytherins. Pansy whispered continuously beside her until Draconia said something which made her stop. Teachers moved through the doors at regular intervals. Each arrival silenced the room briefly. No one brought answers. Near midnight, the Fat Lady was found hiding inside a landscape on the second floor. Professor McGonagall entered with Dumbledore. The Headmaster raised one hand.

Conversation died.

"The Fat Lady has been located," he said. "She is shaken but unharmed."

Relief moved uneasily across the room.

"She has identified the person who damaged her portrait as Sirius Black."

The name settled over the students like another ceiling. Harry heard a first-year begin crying several rows away.

"He demanded entry to Gryffindor Tower," Dumbledore continued. "When she refused to admit him without the password, he attacked the portrait. We have searched the castle and grounds. He has not been found."

Harry felt Hermione turn towards him. He kept his eyes upon Dumbledore.

"All students will remain here until morning. Professors and ghosts will continue the search. You are safe in this Hall."

Safe.

The word had become too flexible to mean anything. The doors closed behind the professors. Gradually, the candle flames dimmed. Harry lay inside his sleeping bag staring at the enchanted ceiling. Clouds moved across the stars. They were not real clouds, yet they made him feel watched from above. Hermione shifted beside him.

"Harry?"

"I'm awake."

"I know."

"How?"

"You haven't moved."

"That usually means someone's asleep."

"Not you."

He turned his head. Her face was pale in the low light. Crookshanks sat upon her sleeping bag, staring towards the doors.

"Why would Black attack the portrait?" Hermione whispered.

"He wanted to get inside."

"Yes, but if he came for you, he would have prepared a better way."

"Maybe he thought the Fat Lady would be frightened."

"He didn't know the password."

"Most murderers are probably poor planners."

Hermione did not smile. Across the Hall, Draconia sat upright among the sleeping Slytherins. Her mirror rested in her hand. Harry lifted the one she had given him and whispered her name. The glass brightened.

Draconia's face appeared.

"You should be sleeping," Harry said.

"So should you."

"I asked first."

"That is not how mirrors work."

Hermione leaned closer, her hair entering the corner of the reflection.

"Dumbledore said the castle was searched."

Draconia looked towards the Hall doors.

"Father says Black escaped Azkaban without a wand. Entering Hogwarts should be more difficult."

"That is encouraging," Harry replied.

"It was not intended to be."

"Do you think he's still inside?"

Draconia hesitated.

"I think we should assume anyone capable of leaving Azkaban may also be capable of hiding from a search."

Hermione rubbed her forehead. "There are portraits everywhere. Ghosts. House-elves."

"And passages no one has mapped," Draconia said.

Harry looked at her in the mirror.

"How do you know?"

"My House has been hiding contraband for a thousand years."

"That's the first reassuring thing you've said."

"It should not reassure you."

"Too late."

Draconia's expression softened. A shadow passed behind her. Snape moved between the sleeping students, robes whispering across the floor. His gaze dropped to the mirror. Draconia placed it beneath her blanket. The reflection went dark.

Harry held the blank glass. Crookshanks rose suddenly. The cat's flattened face turned towards one of the narrow side doors behind the staff table. His tail swelled. A low growl vibrated through his chest. Hermione followed his gaze.

"What is it?"

Crookshanks sprang from the sleeping bag and ran. He crossed the Hall between rows of students, ignored Hermione's hissed command and stopped in front of the door. His claws scraped against the wood. Harry sat up. Something moved beyond it. A soft sound. Too low for footsteps.

Then silence. Hermione caught Crookshanks before he could pull at the bottom of the door. The cat twisted in her arms, staring over her shoulder.

"What did he hear?" Harry asked.

"Probably a mouse."

Crookshanks did not behave like a cat interested in a mouse. He behaved like a creature which had recognised someone. Harry looked towards the closed door until exhaustion dragged the ceiling out of focus. When sleep finally came, it brought no rest. A knife cut through canvas.

A woman screamed. A black dog waited behind the portrait hole with human eyes. The castle changed after Halloween. Portrait subjects began travelling in groups. Suits of armour patrolled the corridors in pairs, producing an endless clatter at night. Peeves was given permission to search rooms and abused the privilege so thoroughly that three teachers threatened to resign before Dumbledore revoked it. Sir Cadogan replaced the Fat Lady at the entrance to Gryffindor Tower.

He changed the password twice a day, challenged students to duels when they forgot it and once refused to open the portrait until Neville correctly identified seven parts of a battle-axe. Security guards appeared at every entrance to the grounds. The Dementors remained beyond the gates, but there were more of them now. Their silhouettes moved along the boundary at dusk, black shapes against the frost. Harry began sleeping with his wand beneath his pillow. He also began waking whenever the dormitory door opened.

Neither habit helped. Sirius Black was not found. He became an absence the castle could not stop searching for. Every draught might have entered through a secret passage. Every dog barking near Hagrid's hut brought Harry to the window. Every unfamiliar shadow made his hand close around his wand. Professor Lupin continued teaching. He did not mention the attack until Harry's next private lesson.

They stood inside the fifth-floor classroom with the empty black cloak resting upon a chair. Harry had produced a silver shield twice, though the beginnings of the animal had not returned.

"You're distracted," Lupin said.

"Someone with a knife tried to enter my dormitory."

"A reasonable distraction."

Harry lowered his wand.

"Did you search for him?"

"Yes."

"Where?"

"The grounds. Several unused rooms. Passages Professor Dumbledore believed he might know."

"Might know?"

Lupin set the chocolate aside. Harry saw the same hesitation which had entered his face when Sirius was mentioned before.

"You knew he knew passages into Hogwarts," Harry said.

"Sirius was a student here."

"So were thousands of people."

"He had a talent for reaching places he was not meant to enter."

"With my father?"

Lupin's expression closed. The answer was there.

Harry stepped nearer. "You knew he could get inside."

"I informed Professor Dumbledore of every entrance I believed Sirius might attempt."

"Before or after he reached the Fat Lady?"

"Before."

"And you didn't think to warn me?"

"What would you have done with the information?"

"Paid attention."

"You already do."

"Then I might have paid attention to the correct things."

Lupin moved towards the windows. Frost softened the corners of the glass, turning the grounds beyond into a blurred white landscape.

"I did not believe he could enter," he said. "The passages I knew had been sealed or placed under observation."

"You were wrong."

"Yes."

Harry waited. Lupin did not defend himself. The admission took some of the force from Harry's anger and left the fear exposed beneath it.

"Why did he go to Gryffindor Tower?"

"I don't know."

"He came for me."

"That is what the Ministry believes."

"What do you believe?"

Lupin looked tired again.

"I believe Sirius entered Hogwarts for a reason," he said. "I do not yet know what it was."

Harry's grip tightened upon his wand.

"You keep saying that."

"Because certainty would be dishonest."

"It would also be useful."

"Yes."

Lupin approached the empty cloak.

"We can end the lesson."

Harry raised his wand.

"No."

"You are angry."

"I know."

"The charm may not respond."

"Then it'll behave like most things in this school."

Lupin watched him carefully. Harry summoned the library memory. Pages turned. Draconia snorted. Hermione fled with parchment streaming behind her. The happiness felt thin beneath the anger.

"Expecto Patronum."

Silver mist emerged. It reached the centre of the room and collapsed. Harry tried again. The mist barely formed. His frustration rose.

"Again."

"Harry—"

"Expecto Patronum!"

Light burst from the wand, sharp and uncontrolled. It struck the empty cloak and set one edge alight. Lupin extinguished it. Harry stared at the smoking fabric.

"That wasn't a Patronus."

"No."

"It moved the cloak."

"So would Incendio."

"Then perhaps I've mastered two spells."

Lupin did not smile. Harry put away his wand. The lesson ended early. The next Hogsmeade weekend was announced for the first Saturday in December. Snow had begun falling two days earlier. It gathered along windowsills, softened the rooftops and turned the grounds into an expanse of white broken only by paths trampled between the castle, greenhouses and Hagrid's hut.

Harry did not ask McGonagall about his permission form again. He no longer needed her answer. Hermione and Draconia attempted to include him in their planning without appearing to do so. Their discussions of Hogsmeade occurred at meals where Harry could hear them. They complained that the village would be cold, crowded and probably disappointing. Hermione suggested staying behind to study for the end-of-term assessments. Draconia pointed out that they could purchase everything they required by owl order. Harry refused to help them pretend.

"You're going," he said on Friday afternoon.

The three of them occupied a table near the rear of the library. Hermione's books formed a wall around her. Draconia was writing a letter to Narcissa in small, precise lines. Harry had been reading the same chapter of The Rise and Fall of Defensive Enchantments for nearly half an hour. Hermione did not look up.

"I haven't decided."

"Yes, you have."

"There is work to finish."

"You finished it."

"I have additional work."

"You always have additional work. That doesn't count."

Draconia sanded the final line of her letter.

"The weather is poor," she said.

"You own a coat worth more than my broom."

"That does not make snow pleasant."

"It makes you look expensive while complaining about it."

Her mouth twitched.

Harry closed his book. "Go to Hogsmeade."

Hermione set down her quill.

"And what will you do?"

"Enjoy several hours without supervision."

"We do not supervise you."

Harry looked at her. Draconia folded her letter.

"Constantly," Harry said.

"We agreed to tell one another when we were doing that."

"I am telling you."

Hermione glanced towards Draconia. Harry saw the argument pass silently between them. He knew their expressions too well now: Hermione's narrowed eyes meant he is doing this so we do not feel guilty. Draconia's slight tilt of the head replied he knows we know. Harry disliked being discussed without words.

"I have plans," he said.

"What plans?" Hermione asked.

"Private ones."

"That is suspicious."

"It becomes suspicious only because you asked."

"Will you remain inside the castle?"

Harry hesitated. Only briefly.

"Yes."

Draconia noticed. Her gaze settled upon him with uncomfortable precision. Harry returned to his book. After several seconds, she resumed folding her letter. Neither girl mentioned the lie.

That was almost worse. On Saturday morning, Harry watched the Hogsmeade party leave from an alcove above the entrance hall. Hermione and Draconia walked together near the back of the group. Their argument carried faintly towards him.

"You said the grey scarf," Hermione complained.

"I said the green one was less offensive."

"You said grey made me look tired."

"You are tired."

"That was unnecessary."

"It was accurate."

Harry smiled despite himself. They passed beneath the gates. The Dementors turned as the students approached, faceless hoods following the movement. Professor McGonagall walked beside the group, wand visible in her hand. Only when the final student had entered the road to Hogsmeade did the creatures look away. Harry remained in the alcove.

"You planning to glare the gates open?"

Fred Weasley leaned against the wall behind him. George stood at his shoulder. Harry had not heard either arrive.

"I'm admiring the architecture."

"From behind a tapestry?" George asked.

"It's a very private admiration."

Fred looked through the narrow window towards the departing students.

"Still no permission?"

"No."

"Tragic."

"Heartbreaking," George agreed.

"I'm coping."

"That's clear," Fred said. "You have the haunted dignity of a prisoner staring through iron bars."

Harry looked at him.

Fred winced. "Poor choice of image."

"A bit."

George reached beneath his jumper.

"We've been discussing your condition."

"Please don't call it that."

"And we have decided," Fred continued, "that a person with your record of responsible behaviour deserves assistance."

"That sentence has several problems."

George produced a square of old parchment. It was folded many times and stained at the corners. No writing marked its surface. Harry examined it.

"Brilliant. Paper."

"Not paper," Fred said.

"Ancient, priceless paper," George added.

"Completely irreplaceable."

"Which is why we're giving it away."

Harry looked from one twin to the other.

"What does it do?"

Fred drew his wand and tapped the parchment.

"I solemnly swear that I am up to no good."

Ink spread beneath the wand tip. Lines emerged first, thin and black, branching across the parchment. Walls arranged themselves around corridors. Staircases appeared, then rooms, towers, hidden doors and narrow passages Harry had never known existed. Words wrote themselves across the top.

Messrs Moony, Wormtail, Padfoot and Prongs are delighted to present the Marauder's Map.

Harry unfolded another section. The castle continued. Tiny dots moved through it, each labelled with a name. Professor Flitwick crossed the Charms corridor. Filch prowled near the kitchens. Neville Longbottom stood outside Greenhouse Three, apparently unaware that the rest of his class had already gone inside. Harry stared.

"It shows everyone."

"Everyone on the grounds," George said.

"And several people beneath them," Fred added.

"How did you get this?"

"Filch confiscated it during our first year."

"We retrieved it through lawful means."

"You stole it."

"Our interpretation contains more nuance."

Harry found his own dot inside the alcove, flanked by Fred and George Weasley. Hundreds of names moved through the castle. None belonged to Sirius Black.

"If he's here," Harry said, "would this show him?"

The twins' amusement faded.

"We think so," Fred replied.

"You think?"

"We've never tested it on an escaped convict."

"The opportunity rarely arises," George said.

Harry traced a narrow line leading from the third floor to the edge of Hogsmeade.

"What is that?"

"A passage."

"Still usable," Fred said. "Mostly."

"What does mostly mean?"

"You'll need to bend."

"And avoid sneezing."

Harry followed the line backwards. It ended at the statue of a one-eyed witch near the Defence corridor. His pulse quickened.

"You're giving me this so I can go to Hogsmeade."

"We're giving you this because we've memorised it," George said.

"What you do with information is your own moral burden."

Fred tapped the parchment again.

"When you're done, say 'Mischief managed.' Otherwise anyone can read it."

The map cleared. Blank parchment rested between Harry's hands. He thought of Hermione and Draconia beyond the gates. He thought of the promise he had made in the library.

Will you remain inside the castle?

Yes.

Fred seemed to read the conflict upon his face.

"You don't have to use it today."

"Or at all," George added.

"But if Black found one of the passages—"

"We've checked them," Fred said. "The known ones, anyway. No suspicious names."

"Sirius Black's name?"

"No."

Harry slipped the map beneath his robes.

"Thanks."

The twins began descending towards the kitchens.

"Harry?" George called.

He turned.

"If you are going out, take the cloak."

"How do you know about the cloak?"

Fred grinned.

"You become an oddly shaped draught after curfew."

They disappeared around the corner. Harry stood alone with the map against his chest. He told himself he would only inspect the passage. Five minutes later, he returned to Gryffindor Tower for the Invisibility Cloak. The one-eyed witch stood in a deserted third-floor corridor.

Harry checked the map twice before approaching. Filch occupied the entrance hall. Snape moved through the dungeons. Lupin's dot remained inside his office, completely still. Harry unfolded the Cloak, slipped it over his shoulders and tapped the statue.

"Dissendium."

A hump upon the witch's stone back opened. Cold, stale air breathed from the darkness. Harry lit his wand and climbed inside. The entrance sealed behind him. The passage descended steeply. Stone gave way to packed earth, damp enough to darken the knees of his trousers whenever he slipped. Roots pushed through the ceiling in twisted bundles. The air smelled of soil, dust and water which had not seen daylight in years.

Harry kept one hand against the wall. The map floated ahead beneath his wandlight. His own dot moved through the narrow line marked beyond the castle grounds. He should turn back. The thought followed him for nearly half a mile. Every step carried him farther from the safety he had promised to remain inside. If Hermione discovered the map, she would recite every possible consequence without taking breath. Draconia would say far less and somehow make the silence more condemning.

Harry continued. He was tired of gates closing because adults had failed him. Vernon had torn the form apart. McGonagall had followed the rule. Dumbledore had allowed Dementors to stand between Harry and the village, then expected him to accept that their presence existed for his protection. The passage was his choice. Even if the choice was stupid, it belonged to him.

The tunnel began climbing. Harry extinguished his wand near the top and pushed against a wooden trapdoor. It opened into darkness filled with sweetness. He emerged inside Honeydukes' cellar. Boxes towered around him. Bottles clinked upon shelves. The air carried chocolate, peppermint and the sharp chemical scent of experimental sweets. Pipes crossed the low ceiling, rattling whenever someone walked above. Harry closed the trapdoor and pulled the Cloak over his head.

A staircase led upward. He waited until the map showed the storeroom empty, then slipped through the door. Honeydukes was crowded. Students filled every aisle. Warmth fogged the windows. Shelves overflowed with jars, boxes and enchanted displays: peppermint creams shivered beneath glass, liquorice wands conducted one another from a jar, and a pyramid of chocolate frogs attempted to escape each time a customer lifted the lid. Harry moved carefully.

The Cloak hid him, not the space he occupied. Twice, students walked directly into him. A Hufflepuff apologised to the empty air. A Ravenclaw accused her friend of shoving her. Harry grinned. For the first ten minutes, secrecy felt like freedom. He sampled a sugar mouse from an open tray, watched a tower of sherbet lemons collapse after Neville sneezed near it and followed the crowd into the High Street. Snow fell lightly over Hogsmeade.

It softened every roof and gathered upon shop signs. Smoke curled from chimneys into a pale winter sky. Students carried steaming drinks between gloved hands. Music emerged from the Three Broomsticks whenever the door opened. Harry stood in the centre of it unseen. The village was smaller than he had imagined and better for it. No hidden monster waited beneath its streets. No staircase moved beneath his feet. It was only a collection of crooked buildings filled with warmth, noise and people who belonged there. He walked towards Zonko's. Hermione and Draconia emerged from Scrivenshaft's before he reached it.

Harry stopped. Hermione carried three books and a paper bag. Draconia held a pair of gloves she had apparently purchased and was attempting to remove a silver label from one cuff.

"You bought green again," Hermione said.

"They suit me."

"All your gloves are green."

"That is why they suit me."

"You considered the black ones."

"I corrected the impulse."

Hermione shifted the books against her chest. "The shopkeeper said these are charmed against ink."

"The shopkeeper was selling them."

"He demonstrated."

"He poured ink onto a sample he controlled."

"You distrust everyone."

"I trusted you to choose your own scarf. Observe the result."

Hermione wore the grey scarf Draconia had criticised. She looked down at it.

"You said grey made me look tired."

"You are wearing it vindictively."

"Yes."

"That does improve it."

Harry almost spoke. The word reached the back of his teeth before he remembered the Cloak. He could remove it. He could surprise them and endure the argument. The three of them could enter the Three Broomsticks, and Harry could pretend he had never promised to remain in the castle. Then Hermione glanced over her shoulder. For one irrational second, Harry thought she had seen him.

Her gaze passed through the place he stood.

"Do you think he's all right?" she asked.

Draconia looked towards Hogwarts. The castle rose beyond the village, distant towers fading into snowfall.

"It has been one hour and twelve minutes."

"You counted."

"I glanced at the clock."

"Four times."

"You counted that."

Hermione drew the silver mirror from her pocket. Draconia stopped her.

"He asked for several hours without supervision."

"This is not supervision."

"He can call us."

"What if he doesn't?"

"Then we allow him not to."

Hermione looked unhappy. Draconia did too. She was simply better at shaping the feeling into stillness.

"We cannot keep making his choices for him," she said.

Harry felt the map beneath his robes. Guilt entered the adventure and spoiled it. Hermione put away the mirror.

"Fine. We'll call after lunch."

"Agreed."

They continued towards Honeydukes. Harry remained in the snow after they had gone. He had wanted them to trust him. They had. He had used the opportunity to deceive them.

"That seems about right," he muttered.

A passing third year turned towards the disembodied voice. Harry walked quickly towards the Three Broomsticks. The pub was full enough to conceal him. Harry slipped through the door behind a group of Ravenclaws. Heat struck his face. Butterbeer, smoke and damp wool filled the air. Every table had been claimed, and conversations gathered beneath the ceiling in a warm, confused roar. He found a narrow space beside the bar.

Madam Rosmerta moved between customers with three mugs in each hand. Her dark hair was pinned above her neck, and she had charmed a quill to record orders in a ledger without assistance. Harry intended to stay only long enough to warm his hands. The door opened again. Professor McGonagall entered. Harry pressed himself against the wall.

She was accompanied by Professor Flitwick, Hagrid and Cornelius Fudge. Two Ministry officials remained outside, snow collecting upon the shoulders of their cloaks. Madam Rosmerta's smile disappeared.

"Minister."

"Rosmerta." Fudge removed his green bowler hat and shook snow from it. "Awful weather."

"It was perfectly pleasant before Azkaban surrounded the village."

Several nearby conversations faltered. Fudge glanced around.

"A private table, perhaps."

Rosmerta led them towards a booth screened by a tall Christmas tree. Harry waited until the surrounding students returned to their conversations, then followed. He crouched beneath an empty table nearby. The Cloak settled around him. His knees pressed against the floorboards, and the edge of someone's boot remained inches from his hand. Rosmerta placed drinks before the adults.

"Any sign of him?" she asked.

"No," Fudge replied. "But the Dementors remain confident he has not left the area."

"Do Dementors become confident?"

"They have methods."

"They've frozen half my pipes."

McGonagall removed her gloves. "Their presence is no guarantee of safety. Black passed them once."

"Yes, yes," Fudge said. "Which is why we are reviewing every entrance to Hogwarts."

Hagrid's chair creaked.

"He wouldn't have got near the Fat Lady if those things were any use."

"They guard the boundary, Hagrid," Flitwick said. "Black entered by some means we have not identified."

"Or he was already inside," Rosmerta said.

Harry's stomach tightened. Fudge took a long drink.

"Impossible."

"So was escaping Azkaban," McGonagall replied.

Silence followed. Harry shifted his weight. The movement made the floorboard complain. No one seemed to notice.

Rosmerta lowered her voice. "Is it true he is after Potter?"

Harry stopped breathing. Fudge answered.

"The evidence suggests it."

"But why?" Rosmerta asked. "What could Sirius Black want with a thirteen-year-old boy?"

Hagrid made a low, angry sound.

McGonagall looked towards the crowd. "This is not a conversation for a public room."

"No one can hear us over this lot," Rosmerta said. "And everyone knows he came to Gryffindor Tower."

"They do not know the history," Flitwick murmured.

"What history?"

The fire cracked. Harry felt the room narrow around the table. Fudge sighed with the importance of a man about to reveal information he enjoyed possessing.

"Black was more than James Potter's friend."

Hagrid's mug struck the table.

"That's enough."

Fudge ignored him.

"He was the boy's godfather."

The words did not make sense. Harry waited for them to rearrange themselves. They did not. Beneath the Cloak, his fingers went numb.

"His godfather?" Rosmerta repeated.

"James and Lily appointed him shortly after the child was born," Fudge said. "They trusted Black above everyone."

McGonagall's voice became very cold. "Which is why what followed was so unforgivable."

Harry could hear his own heartbeat.

Godfather.

The word belonged to birthday cards, spare bedrooms and adults who promised to take care of children if their parents could not. It belonged to a person who should have known Harry existed before Hagrid arrived on his eleventh birthday. Sirius Black had possessed that place. Then he had given Voldemort the address. Rosmerta sat opposite them.

"I thought the Potters were hidden."

"They were," Flitwick said. "The Fidelius Charm concealed their home."

"And Black broke it?"

"He was their Secret Keeper," McGonagall said. Each word sounded cut from stone. "The location could not be found unless he revealed it. James insisted upon using him."

Hagrid's breathing grew louder.

"I lent him the motorbike," he said.

Harry pressed one hand against the floor.

"What?" Rosmerta asked.

"That night. When I went ter get Harry." Hagrid's voice cracked across the boy's name. "Sirius was there. House was gone. James an' Lily—"

He stopped. McGonagall placed one hand upon his arm.

"He gave me the bike," Hagrid continued. "Said he didn't need it. I thought he was grieving. Thought he'd lost everythin'."

"He had," Fudge said grimly. "His master had fallen, and his betrayal was exposed."

Hagrid's chair scraped backwards.

"Don't call You-Know-Who his master near me."

Fudge raised both hands.

"Of course."

Rosmerta's face had lost its colour. "And then Black killed Pettigrew."

"Twelve Muggles," Fudge said. "Peter Pettigrew confronted him in the street. Accused Black of betraying the Potters. Witnesses heard the accusation. Black drew his wand."

"All they found of Peter was a finger," Flitwick said sadly.

Harry remembered the photograph in the Daily Prophet: a laughing young man with watery eyes and a pointed nose. Peter Pettigrew. Awarded the Order of Merlin after his death.

"He laughed," Fudge said. "Black stood in the middle of the street surrounded by bodies and laughed until the Magical Law Enforcement Patrol took him away."

"Was there a trial?" Rosmerta asked.

Fudge hesitated. Harry remembered Lucius walking beside him through Diagon Alley.

There are gaps in the official account.

"Not a conventional one," Fudge admitted. "The evidence was overwhelming. Witnesses, the Potters' deaths, Pettigrew's accusation—Crouch was sending suspected Death Eaters directly to Azkaban at the time."

McGonagall's mouth tightened.

"A dangerous practice, even during war."

"Are you suggesting Black was innocent?"

"No," she said. "I am suggesting that certainty should not excuse the abandonment of law."

Fudge leaned forwards.

"Sirius Black entered Hogwarts with a knife."

"I am aware."

"He served You-Know-Who, destroyed thirteen lives in one street and has spent twelve years among Dementors without expressing a trace of remorse."

"You cannot know what he expressed," Flitwick said. "No one visited him."

The boot beside Harry's hand shifted. He pulled his fingers back. The conversation continued above him, but the words began losing shape.

Godfather.

Secret Keeper. James trusted him above everyone. Harry saw his mother carrying him up the stairs. He felt the panic inside her body. He heard Voldemort enter a house which should have been impossible to find. Not impossible.

Invited.

A chair moved. Harry flinched hard enough to strike his head beneath the table. The voices stopped.

"What was that?" Rosmerta asked.

Harry clamped both hands over his mouth. Hagrid leaned down. His enormous face appeared beneath the edge of the table. Harry remained motionless. The Cloak covered him, though Hagrid's eyes paused upon the shallow marks Harry's knees had left in the dust.

"Mouse," Hagrid said.

He sat again. Harry crawled away. He did not wait to hear more. Snow had thickened outside. Harry moved through the village without seeing it. Students passed close enough to brush the Cloak. Laughter followed him between shops. The church bell struck two and sent birds rising from the roof.

Sirius Black had been meant to protect him. The thought hurt more than the betrayal of his parents. Harry did not understand why. Perhaps because James and Lily had existed mostly in photographs and stories. Their love reached him through other people's memories, bright but distant. A godfather belonged to the life after them. Sirius should have been the person who remained. He had chosen Voldemort instead.

Harry reached Honeydukes and descended through the cellar without checking the map. The tunnel felt longer on the return. Darkness closed around his wandlight. Roots caught his robes, and twice he slipped upon the damp earth. Anger travelled with him. It gave every old wound a name. Ten years in a cupboard because Sirius Black had destroyed the people who should have raised him. Birthdays forgotten. Meals withheld. Vernon's hand closing around his shoulder. Petunia speaking about Lily with hatred sharpened by fear.

Harry had blamed Voldemort for all of it. Now the blame widened. By the time he climbed through the witch's hump, his hands were filthy and shaking. He opened the map. Hermione and Draconia stood in the entrance hall.

Their dots were motionless.

Waiting.

Harry closed his eyes.

"Brilliant."

He pulled the Cloak around himself and began walking. He could avoid them. The map showed three staircases leading around the entrance hall. He could reach Gryffindor Tower unseen, hide the Cloak and pretend he had spent the day in the library. A promise broken did not improve when buried beneath a second lie. Harry descended the main staircase. Hermione saw his footprints first.

Wet marks appeared across the marble floor, crossing the dry hall without an owner. She drew her wand. Draconia followed her gaze.

"Harry," she said.

He stopped. Students moved through the doors behind them, shaking snow from their coats. Harry removed the Cloak. Hermione's face changed. Snow had melted through his hair. Mud streaked his robes from the tunnel. A Honeydukes label clung to one sleeve.

"You left the castle," she said.

"Yes."

"You told us you wouldn't."

"I know."

"How?"

Harry took out the folded map. Draconia looked at it.

"What is that?"

"A map."

"I had gathered that."

"It shows secret passages."

Hermione stared at him. "You used an unknown passage while Sirius Black is somewhere near Hogwarts."

"I checked it first."

"With what?"

"The map."

"You used the unknown map to confirm that the unknown passage was safe."

"When you say it like that, it sounds irresponsible."

"It was irresponsible!"

Several students turned towards them.

Harry lowered his voice. "Can we not do this here?"

"Where would you prefer?" Hermione demanded. "The hospital wing? We could save time."

Draconia touched her arm.

"Not here."

Hermione pulled away.

"You knew he was lying yesterday."

Draconia's expression hardened. "So did you."

"And you said nothing."

"We agreed to stop controlling him."

"Trusting him was clearly a mistake."

The words landed harder than Hermione intended. Harry folded the map.

"Good to know."

Her anger vanished. "Harry, I didn't mean—"

"Yes, you did."

"I meant trusting you about this."

"Much better."

He moved towards the staircase. Draconia caught his sleeve.

"What happened in the village?"

Harry looked at her hand. She released him.

"You are angry," she said. "Not only with us."

Harry laughed once.

"No."

"Who did you see?"

"No one."

"What did you hear?" Hermione asked.

The question stopped him. He looked between them. Students passed on either side. Snow melted into shining pools across the entrance hall. Beyond the open doors, Dementors moved near the gates.

"Sirius Black was my godfather," Harry said.

Neither girl responded.

"He was my parents' Secret Keeper. He was the only person who could tell Voldemort where they were, and he did."

Hermione's lips parted. Draconia went completely still.

"Who told you?" she asked.

"Fudge. McGonagall. Hagrid. I heard them in the Three Broomsticks."

Hermione glanced towards the crowded hall.

"Let's go somewhere else."

This time, Harry followed. They entered an empty classroom near the Great Hall. Draconia locked the door and drew the curtains across the windows. Hermione lit the lamps. Harry remained standing.

"He was supposed to take care of me," he said.

Hermione set down her shopping bags.

"Harry—"

"He gave Voldemort the address. Then he killed the man who confronted him and twelve other people."

"According to Fudge," Draconia said.

Harry turned towards her. She did not retreat.

"Father said there wasn't a trial," she continued. "Nobody had to prove any of it."

"He was the Secret Keeper."

"That is what they believe."

"McGonagall said it."

"Professor McGonagall can be wrong."

"You weren't there."

"No. I was following the rules you broke."

Hermione shot her a warning look. Draconia drew breath. The anger in her face shifted.

"I should not have said that."

"But you did."

"Yes."

Harry pushed one hand through his wet hair.

"Why are you both trying to defend him?"

"We aren't," Hermione said. "We are trying to understand what you heard."

"I heard enough."

"You heard part of a private conversation from beneath a table."

"Do you want me to go back and ask for references?"

"No."

"Maybe Fudge has diagrams."

"Harry."

"He betrayed them."

Hermione took a step closer. "Probably."

"Probably?"

"It sounds as though he did it. I know that. But they didn't ask him, Harry."

"Why does there always have to be more for you?"

Her face tightened.

"Because there usually is."

"Some people are exactly what they look like."

"You taught me they aren't."

The answer silenced him. Hermione's voice softened.

"You were accused after Mrs Norris was attacked. Everyone saw you standing beneath the writing. They believed the obvious explanation."

"That was different."

"It was different because we knew you."

"No one knew Sirius better than my parents, and look what happened."

Hermione stopped. The sentence frightened Harry after he heard it aloud. Trust had not saved James and Lily. It had given Voldemort the door. Draconia seemed to hear the thought beneath his words.

"You are not your parents," she said.

"You don't know that."

"And we are not Sirius Black."

"I didn't say you were."

"You thought it."

"No."

"For half a second," she said. "You did."

Harry looked away. He wanted to deny it. The lie would not survive her attention. Hermione sank into a chair.

"What else did they say?"

Harry told them. He repeated what he remembered of the Fidelius Charm, Pettigrew's confrontation and the street full of bodies. He told them Sirius had laughed. He told them there had been no trial. When he finished, the room felt colder. Hermione rubbed both hands over her face.

"We need to research the charm."

Harry stared at her.

"That is your response?"

"I want to know what actually happened."

"My parents are dead, and you want a library book."

"I cannot change what happened."

"You think I don't know that?"

"I'm trying to help."

"Then don't turn it into homework."

Hermione recoiled. Draconia moved between them.

"That is enough."

"You don't decide that," Harry said.

"No. But I can recognise when you are looking for someone to hurt because Black is not here."

Harry's anger struck the truth and flared brighter. He reached the door.

"Where are you going?" Hermione asked.

"To find Lupin."

"Why?"

"Because he knew."

Lupin opened his office door upon the second knock. He wore an old cardigan over his shirt and held a stack of essays against one hip. Surprise crossed his face when he saw Harry. Then he noticed Hermione and Draconia farther down the corridor.

"What happened?"

Harry entered without being invited. Lupin closed the door. The office fire had burned low. Curtains covered one window, and books lay open across the desk. A photograph rested beside the inkpot, facedown. Harry stopped in the centre of the room.

"Sirius Black was my godfather."

The essays slipped slightly in Lupin's arms. He placed them upon the desk.

"Who told you?"

"Does it matter?"

"Yes."

"I heard McGonagall and Fudge in Hogsmeade."

"You went to Hogsmeade?"

Harry laughed.

"Is that the important part?"

"No."

"It's everyone else's favourite part."

Lupin lowered himself into the chair behind the desk. He suddenly looked much older than he had during their last lesson.

"You knew," Harry said.

"Yes."

"You knew every time I asked about him."

"Yes."

"And you didn't tell me."

"No."

Lupin did not disguise the answer. It made Harry angrier.

"You said you would be honest."

"I should have told you."

"That isn't an explanation."

"No."

"Then explain."

The fire settled beneath ash. Lupin looked towards the facedown photograph.

"When your parents chose Sirius as your godfather, none of us considered anyone else," he said. "He loved James more than he loved himself. He loved you before you were old enough to recognise him."

"He gave them to Voldemort."

"That is what I believed."

"What you believed?"

"What I still have every reason to believe."

"You never sound like it."

"Because the person I remember and the crimes attributed to him do not fit neatly together."

Harry's hands curled.

"Maybe you didn't know him."

"Perhaps not."

"Or maybe you knew and ignored it."

Pain crossed Lupin's face. Harry saw it. He continued anyway.

"My parents trusted him. You trusted him. Everyone trusted him, and he got them killed."

"Yes."

"Why didn't you warn them?"

"I did not know there was anything to warn them about."

"Someone should have."

Lupin's eyes closed briefly.

"I have spent twelve years agreeing with you."

The anger lost its direction again. Harry hated Lupin for allowing that.

"You said he was my father's closest friend," he said. "You let me ask whether Sirius wanted to kill me. You let me tell you I heard my mother die. And the entire time, you left out the one thing that mattered."

"I was afraid."

Harry stared at him.

"Of what?"

"Of telling you that the man who betrayed your parents was once trusted with your life. Of giving you a connection to him before I knew whether you wanted it. Of admitting how completely all of us failed to see what he was."

"So you decided for me."

"Yes."

The answer entered the same raw place Hermione and Draconia's fear had occupied.

"You sound like the Dursleys."

Lupin flinched. Harry regretted it. Not enough to withdraw it.

"They tell me half the truth too," he continued. "Then they say the rest wouldn't have helped."

"I was wrong."

"You keep saying that after."

"After what?"

"After the Dementors. After Black gets into the castle. After I find out what you didn't tell me."

Lupin stood.

"I cannot change the order in which you learned these things."

"No. You can only decide which ones I'm allowed to know."

"That was not my right."

"Then why did you do it?"

"Because being an adult does not prevent cowardice."

The words stopped Harry. Lupin faced him without defence.

"I lost James and Lily in one night," he said. "Peter was murdered the following day. Sirius killed him. By the end of that week, every person I had loved most was either dead or responsible for their deaths."

Harry's anger remained, but another feeling entered it unwillingly. Lupin continued.

"I told myself I had no place in your life. I had no home to offer you, no money and no certainty that my condition would permit me to care for a child safely. Dumbledore said you would be protected with your mother's relatives. I believed him because believing him allowed me to leave."

Harry looked at the patched sleeve of Lupin's cardigan.

"You knew I went to the Dursleys?"

"Yes."

"You never checked?"

"No."

The single word held more shame than any apology. Harry's throat tightened.

"You all left me there."

Lupin's face broke. Harry could not remain to see it. He opened the door. Hermione and Draconia stood outside. Neither tried to stop him.

Harry did not speak to Lupin for twelve days. He attended Defence Against the Dark Arts, completed every assignment and answered questions when called upon. He did not remain after lessons. Thursday came and passed without private training. Lupin did not send for him. That almost made the silence worse. Harry carried the Marauder's Map inside his robes.

At night, he opened it beneath the blankets and searched the castle. Hundreds of names moved through corridors, dormitories and hidden rooms. He learned the routes of patrolling professors. He watched Hermione's dot appear in two classrooms within an hour that should have permitted only one. He found Draconia awake in the Slytherin dormitory long after midnight, her dot motionless beside her bed. He searched for Sirius Black. The name never appeared. Harry told Hermione and Draconia about the map.

He did so two days after Hogsmeade, partly because keeping it secret had begun to feel unbearable and partly because Hermione threatened to search his trunk. She spent an hour examining the parchment.

"This magic is extraordinary," she admitted.

"I know."

"It is also an appalling invasion of privacy."

"You can give it back while saying that."

Hermione kept reading. Draconia studied the inscription across the top.

"Moony, Wormtail, Padfoot and Prongs," she said. "Those cannot be real names."

"Probably not."

"Then the creators concealed their identities."

"That is generally the purpose of nicknames."

"Why these?"

Harry shrugged. Draconia traced the final name.

"Prongs," she repeated.

Something about it tugged at Harry's memory. Silver branches rising from his Patronus.

Antlers.

The connection felt too small to hold. He dismissed it. Draconia wanted to tell a professor about the map. Hermione wanted to understand how it worked first. Harry wanted to keep it.

They compromised by doing exactly what Harry wanted while both girls complained. The map became part of their routine. They used it to avoid Filch, locate empty classrooms and confirm that Sirius had not entered the castle. Hermione refused to use it for academic advantage until Draconia pointed out that avoiding Peeves preserved study time. After that, she checked his location before visiting the library. Harry searched for Sirius every night. He also began studying the photograph album Hagrid had given him during his first year.

The pages held a childhood he had never lived long enough to remember. James lifting baby Harry above his head. Lily asleep on a sofa with one arm curled around him. His parents standing beneath a garden arch at their wedding, young and alive and unaware that the future had already begun hunting them. Harry had looked at those pictures hundreds of times. Now he noticed the man beside James. Sirius stood taller than the others. Dark hair fell around a handsome, laughing face. One arm rested across James's shoulders while Lily reached over to straighten his tie. He looked at home between them.

On the next page, Sirius held baby Harry. The photograph moved. Baby Harry caught a fistful of his hair. Sirius pretended to collapse in agony. James laughed behind them while Lily retrieved the strands from Harry's fingers. The image repeated. Sirius accepted the child again.

Baby Harry reached for him without fear. Harry sat alone inside the curtains of his bed and watched the man betray him over and over. Winter deepened. Snow buried the paths and reached the lower windows of Hagrid's hut. The lake froze at its edges. Draconia reported that the green light inside the Slytherin dormitories had faded to grey, while large shapes continued moving beneath the ice beyond the glass. The Christmas holidays approached.

Most students intended to return home. Hermione's parents wrote three times asking whether she would join them. Narcissa sent Draconia a letter long enough to require two owls. Harry received invitations from both families. Mrs Granger promised there would be no studying on Christmas Day, a claim Hermione described as unrealistic. Narcissa wrote that the Manor's western rooms had been secured, the elves had been instructed not to interfere with Harry's correspondence and Lucius possessed several government officials he would gladly inconvenience if Hogwarts refused to release him. Harry declined both invitations.

Sirius Black had entered the castle once. The Dementors guarded every route away from it. Travelling felt more dangerous than staying, and the idea of choosing one family over the other made Harry feel ungrateful towards both. That was what he told everyone. The truth was less reasonable. He did not want to enter another home and discover how easily he might belong there. Belonging had begun to feel like a position from which one could be removed.

Hermione announced she would stay at Hogwarts. Draconia did the same.

"You don't have to," Harry told them.

"We know," Hermione said.

"I always spend Christmas at the Manor," Draconia added. "A change may improve my character."

"Your mother will blame me."

"Mother sent three additional jumpers for you. I think she has accepted your involvement."

"I have enough jumpers."

Draconia and Hermione exchanged a look.

"What?" Harry asked.

"Nothing," they said together.

Lucius arrived at the castle two days before the end of term. He met Dumbledore behind closed doors, spoke with Snape and spent an hour in Professor McGonagall's office discussing the Dementors. By dinner, rumours claimed he had threatened to sue the Ministry, purchase Azkaban and personally relocate every Dementor to Cornelius Fudge's garden. Draconia denied the final two claims.

"The first is possible," she admitted.

Lucius found Harry in the entrance hall. He wore black travelling robes beneath a fur-lined cloak. Snow had melted across his shoulders, and his silver-topped cane struck the floor with controlled displeasure. Harry stopped.

"Am I in trouble?"

"Why would that be your first assumption?"

"Experience."

Lucius's mouth tightened. Draconia stood beside her father. Narcissa had not come, though the number of parcels stacked near the doors suggested she had sent half the Manor in her place. Lucius looked Harry over.

"You have lost weight."

"Hello to you too."

"Are you eating?"

"Yes."

Hermione opened her mouth. Harry glared at her. She closed it. Lucius noticed the exchange.

"I see."

"That means nothing," Harry said.

"It rarely does."

Lucius handed him a narrow envelope. Inside lay a legal document bearing several Ministry seals.

"What is this?"

"A temporary guardianship petition."

Harry stared at him.

"It has not been submitted," Lucius said. "Your aunt's blood connection complicates the matter, and Dumbledore refuses to discuss the protections upon your residence with sufficient clarity. I will not act without your consent."

Harry read the first line twice. Lucius Abraxas Malfoy and Narcissa Malfoy petitioned for—

"You want guardianship?"

"Narcissa wants you fed. I want the legal right to prosecute anyone who prevents it."

Draconia sighed. "He rehearsed a kinder version."

"It was imprecise."

Harry folded the parchment carefully.

"I don't know."

"I did not ask for an immediate answer."

"Most adults do."

"Most adults lack patience."

Draconia looked at him. Lucius ignored her.

"There is another matter," he said. "My inquiries concerning Black have produced very little."

Harry's hand tightened around the envelope.

"You knew he was my godfather."

Lucius went still.

"Did you?"

"No."

Harry searched his face. Lucius's surprise appeared genuine.

"Dumbledore omitted that detail," he said.

"So did Lupin."

"Remus Lupin knew Black?"

"They were friends."

The change in Lucius was immediate and small. His gaze sharpened. His fingers settled more firmly around the head of his cane.

"Interesting."

"What?"

"Old relationships are rarely irrelevant."

"He thinks Sirius did it."

"Does he?"

"He says he does."

Lucius looked towards the corridor leading to the staff offices.

"That is not the same thing."

Harry heard Draconia's earlier words in the entrance hall.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Then perhaps everyone has observed the same problem."

"He was the Secret Keeper."

"So the surviving witnesses believe."

"He killed Pettigrew and twelve Muggles."

"So the Ministry recorded without trial."

"You sound like Hermione."

"I will attempt to recover."

Harry nearly smiled. Lucius lowered his voice.

"I am not telling you Black is innocent. I am telling you the Ministry considered him guilty before anyone asked him a question. Those are different claims."

"He entered my tower with a knife."

"Yes."

"Why would an innocent man do that?"

"I do not know."

Harry shoved the envelope into his pocket.

"Everyone has become very fond of that answer."

Lucius did not stop him when he walked away. Christmas morning came bright and silent. Snow filled the windows of Gryffindor Tower with white light. The common-room fire had burned low during the night, leaving the air cool enough for Harry's breath to show when he pushed aside his curtains. Presents covered the foot of his bed. More presents than he had ever received.

Narcissa's parcels were wrapped in silver paper and tied with green ribbon. The Grangers used red and gold. Hagrid's package had been wrapped in brown paper which bore several suspicious grease marks, while Hermione had covered hers in a Muggle newspaper and added six separate instructions warning Harry not to tear the object inside. Harry sat cross-legged among them. For several minutes, he did not open anything. Christmas at Privet Drive had meant Dudley's presents arranged beneath a tree while Harry received a pair of old socks or a coat which no longer fitted his cousin. The inequality had once hurt. Later, it became expected.

This felt more dangerous. Every parcel contained proof that someone had thought of him while he was not present. Harry began with Hagrid's. It contained treacle fudge dense enough to serve as building material and a hand-carved wooden stag with one antler slightly shorter than the other. A note beneath it read:

Reminded me of the silver thing yeh said yeh nearly made. Don't worry, Harry. Takes some wizards years.

Harry turned the stag in his hands. Hermione's present was a dark blue jumper Mrs Granger had knitted, accompanied by a book on defensive magic. Several pages had been marked with slips of parchment. On the first she had written:

The Patronus chapter is outdated, but the grounding exercises are useful. I checked.

Draconia sent dragon-hide gloves, a silver case for his glasses and a tin of expensive chocolate with a note attached.

The gloves are not green. I expect gratitude.

Narcissa's parcels contained clothes, sweets and a scarf darker than Hermione's but no less warm. Lucius had sent a small brass instrument designed to detect tracking charms, hidden Portkeys and several categories of curse. Harry examined the crowded bed. A final parcel rested beneath the others. It had no card. No familiar handwriting marked the brown paper. The package was long, narrow and too light for its size.

Harry's pulse quickened. He pulled away the wrapping. Polished wood caught the winter light. Harry stopped breathing. The broom lay across his bed like a captured piece of flame.

Its handle had been carved from dark ash, smooth and perfectly balanced. The tail twigs swept backwards in a precise curve, every one aligned. Fine gold letters shone near the top.

FIREBOLT.

Harry touched it with one finger. The broom vibrated beneath his hand. Power travelled through the wood, quiet and immediate. Even at rest, it felt faster than the Nimbus had ever been. His laughter escaped before he could stop it. Dean and Seamus pulled back their curtains.

"What is that?" Dean asked.

Harry lifted the broom. Seamus swore. Within thirty seconds, every boy in the dormitory had gathered around the bed. Questions struck Harry from all directions. Who sent it? How fast did it go?

Was it true each twig had been chosen by hand? Could they touch it? Harry did not know the answer to the first question. He barely heard the others. For the first time since the Whomping Willow, he imagined the sky without falling.

Harry carried the Firebolt to breakfast. He had wrapped it again, though one end of the polished handle remained visible beneath his arm. Students turned as he crossed the entrance hall. By the time he reached the Great Hall, rumours had already passed him and improved the broom into a prototype capable of travelling through time. Hermione and Draconia waited near the Christmas tree beside the staff table. Draconia saw the exposed handle first. Her eyes widened.

"What is that?"

Harry pulled away the paper. The Firebolt emerged beneath hundreds of floating candles. Hermione's mouth fell open. Draconia moved closer with the reverence of someone approaching a sacred object.

"Where did you get it?"

"It was with my presents."

"Who sent it?"

Harry's excitement faltered.

"There wasn't a note."

Hermione looked from the broom to him.

"No note?"

"Maybe it fell off."

"You checked the wrapping?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

"I didn't eat it, Hermione."

Her expression had begun changing. Wonder receded beneath concentration. Harry recognised the process and immediately disliked it. Draconia ran two fingers above the handle without touching.

"This is a Firebolt."

"I had noticed."

"There are fewer than twenty in Britain."

"You know exactly how many, don't you?"

"Seventeen privately owned. Professional teams possess additional models under contract."

Hermione looked around the Hall.

"Who would send you one?"

"Narcissa?"

Draconia shook her head.

"Mother would include a note. Father would include the receipt so you understood the cost."

"The Grangers?" Harry suggested.

"My parents think fifty pounds is too much for a bicycle," Hermione said. "They did not buy you the fastest broom in Europe."

"Hagrid?"

They both stared at him.

"Fine."

Hermione drew her wand. Harry moved the broom away.

"What are you doing?"

"Checking it."

"Lucius sent me a device for that."

"Use it."

Harry set the Firebolt upon the nearest table and retrieved the brass detector. The needle spun when he passed it over the handle, then settled in the green section marked Clear.

"There."

"That checks common curses," Hermione said.

"And tracking spells. And Portkeys."

"Not every form of malicious enchantment."

Draconia's excitement had dimmed.

"She is right."

Harry looked at her.

"You've wanted to touch it for three minutes."

"I still want to touch it."

"Then touch it."

"That does not make it safe."

"It came through the school post."

"So did a cursed necklace two years ago," Hermione said.

"That was not for me."

"This could be."

Harry picked up the broom.

"You think Sirius Black sent it."

Neither answered quickly enough.

"Brilliant."

"We don't know," Hermione said.

"Exactly."

"That is why it needs to be examined."

"It has been examined."

"By a pocket detector."

"A very expensive pocket detector," Draconia said. "Though insufficient for enchantments woven into the broom's core."

Harry stared at her. She lifted her hands.

"I am not saying it is cursed."

"You're only explaining how it could kill me."

"Yes."

"Helpful."

Hermione stepped closer.

"Harry, no one knows who sent this."

"Maybe someone doesn't want credit."

"Who?"

"I don't know."

"Name one person who could afford it."

"Lucius."

"He didn't send it," Draconia said.

"Narcissa."

"She did not."

"Dumbledore."

Hermione looked towards the staff table. The Headmaster was absent.

"Why would Professor Dumbledore anonymously send you a broom?" she asked.

"He anonymously gave me an Invisibility Cloak."

Draconia turned.

"He did what?"

Harry realised he had never explained the first-year Christmas gift in detail.

"That isn't important."

"It is exceptionally important," Draconia said.

"Later."

Hermione reached for the Firebolt. Harry pulled it back.

"Don't."

"I want Professor McGonagall to inspect it."

"No."

"She can return it after—"

"No."

The word came louder than he intended. Several students looked towards them.

Hermione's face hardened. "You cannot fly it until we know it is safe."

"You don't decide that."

"We agreed to say when care became control," Draconia said quietly.

"It became control five minutes ago."

Hermione looked at her.

"You agree it needs examining."

"I agree it may need examining."

"That is the same thing."

"No. The words differ."

"Only because you are trying to avoid upsetting him."

"I am trying to include him in a decision concerning his property."

Hermione's exhaustion sharpened her voice.

"And when he refuses?"

Draconia's gaze cooled.

"Then we continue the conversation."

"Until he flies it?"

"Until we have agreed upon a reasonable method of inspection."

"You mean until he agrees with you."

"I mean without going behind his back."

The answer stopped Hermione. Harry looked between them.

"You already told McGonagall."

Hermione said nothing. The excitement inside him vanished.

"When?"

"Before you came down."

"You hadn't seen the broom."

"Dean told a portrait. The portrait told Sir Cadogan. He shouted it through half the seventh floor."

"And you went straight to McGonagall."

"I came to find you first."

"After reporting it."

"I told her an anonymous broom had arrived."

Harry laughed without humour.

"Of course you did."

"Harry, think."

"I am thinking."

"No, you're excited."

"They can occur together."

"You lost your broom. Someone knew exactly what you wanted and sent an object worth hundreds of Galleons without a name."

"So I should assume it's a murder attempt."

"We should at least have it checked."

"Because every good thing must be one?"

Hermione flinched. Harry knew the sentence was unfair. He did not stop.

"You couldn't wait ten minutes before turning it into evidence."

"I am trying to keep you alive."

"And I am trying to have one present without everyone deciding it contains death."

The doors behind the staff table opened. Professor McGonagall entered. She wore dark green robes and an expression which suggested Christmas had not weakened her relationship with rules. Her eyes found the Firebolt. Harry gripped it more tightly.

"Mr Potter."

"No."

McGonagall stopped.

"I beg your pardon?"

"I know why you're here."

"Then this conversation may be shorter than expected."

She approached the table. Hermione stood very still. Draconia looked furious, though Harry could no longer tell at whom. McGonagall examined the broom without touching it.

"Miss Granger has informed me that this arrived without identification."

"Yes."

"It will need to be tested for curses."

"Lucius's detector said it was clear."

"Mr Malfoy's instrument is intended to identify common travel enchantments. A broom of this quality requires a more thorough examination."

"How long?"

"I cannot say."

"A day?"

"Possibly several weeks."

Harry felt the handle beneath his palm. Several weeks of teachers pulling it apart, testing every twig and reducing the first thing which had made him want to fly again into another problem.

"No."

McGonagall's eyebrows rose.

"It was not a request."

"It belongs to me."

"And while you are a student at this school, I am responsible for preventing dangerous objects from reaching you."

"You let Dementors reach me."

Silence spread across the nearest tables. McGonagall's face changed. The sentence had struck. Harry wished it had not needed to.

"That failure," she said carefully, "does not excuse another."

She held out her hand. Harry did not move. Draconia stepped beside him.

"Professor, could the inspection be performed without dismantling the broom?"

"If possible."

"And could my father's curse specialist observe?"

McGonagall considered.

"If Mr Malfoy provides an appropriately qualified person, Professor Flitwick may permit it."

"That might get it back sooner," Draconia said to Harry.

He looked at her.

"You agree with them."

"I agree that we do not know who sent it."

"So Hermione reports me, McGonagall takes it and you arrange the procedure."

"I am trying to return it sooner."

"No one asked you to."

Hurt flashed in Draconia's eyes.

Hermione stepped forwards. "Don't speak to her like that."

Harry turned.

"She's defending you."

"She is trying to help you."

"Both of you are always trying to help."

"What is that supposed to mean?"

"It means somehow I'm the only person here who doesn't get a say in my own life."

"That is not true," Hermione said.

"You went to McGonagall before speaking to me."

"Because I knew you would refuse."

Harry stared at her. The certainty in her answer hurt more than the report itself.

"Then why pretend it was a conversation?"

Hermione's anger disappeared. McGonagall held out her hand again.

"Mr Potter."

Harry looked down at the broom. The gold letters shone beneath his fingers. For one irrational second, he considered mounting it inside the Great Hall and flying through the doors. The Firebolt seemed to invite the thought. Power waited within the handle, eager and effortless. Then Harry imagined it bucking beneath him as Quirrell's curse had done. He saw the ground rising through rain. He hated Hermione for placing the image there.

He gave McGonagall the broom. She accepted it carefully.

"I will return it as soon as we are certain it is safe."

Harry said nothing. McGonagall carried the Firebolt towards the staff door. The broom disappeared with her. Brown wrapping remained across the table. Hermione drew breath.

"Harry—"

"Don't."

"I'm sorry you're angry."

"That isn't an apology."

Her face went pale.

"No," she said. "It isn't. Because I would do it again."

Harry nodded.

"That's what I thought."

He turned towards the doors. Draconia followed.

"Harry."

He stopped without looking back.

"I would not have reported it without telling you," she said. "But I would have asked Professor Flitwick to examine it."

"Congratulations."

"That is not fair."

"I don't care."

"Yes, you do."

Harry faced her. Draconia stood between him and Hermione, torn so visibly that the sight should have softened him. It did not.

"Go help her write a procedure," he said.

He left them beneath the Christmas candles. The Gryffindor common room was empty. Harry climbed the stairs, closed the dormitory door and pulled the curtains around his bed. The presents remained where he had left them. Green ribbon trailed across the blankets. Hagrid's wooden stag stood beside the lamp, one uneven antler turned towards the photograph album. Harry took out the Marauder's Map.

No unfamiliar names moved through the castle. Professor McGonagall crossed the second floor carrying the Firebolt. Hermione Granger remained in the Great Hall. Draconia Malfoy stood outside the doors. Remus Lupin occupied his office. Sirius Black was nowhere. Harry cleared the map and pushed it aside.

The photograph album opened beneath his hands. James and Lily smiled from their wedding beneath a sky filled with summer light. Sirius stood between them, young enough to believe friendship could survive anything. The picture changed. James pulled Sirius closer. Lily laughed. Sirius reached towards the camera and dragged someone else into view—a smaller man with fair hair and tired eyes.

Lupin.

Peter Pettigrew appeared at the edge of the frame a second later, clapping James upon the shoulder. Four friends. Within three years, two would be dead, one believed murdered and another imprisoned for betraying them all. Harry watched the scene repeat. James trusted Sirius.

Lily trusted Sirius. Lupin had trusted him too. Baby Harry had reached for him. Beneath the picture, Hagrid's handwriting identified the occasion.

Your mum and dad's wedding. Sirius Black beside your dad—best man and your godfather. Peter on the right. Professor Lupin pretending he didn't want ter be in the picture.

Harry stared at the words. They had been there for two years. He had never known enough to understand them. Someone knocked upon the dormitory door. Harry did not answer.

The handle turned, but he had locked it.

"Harry," Hermione called.

He closed the album.

"I know you're in there."

The Marauder's Map lay beside him, blank and silent. Hermione waited outside. After a while, he heard her footsteps retreat. Harry opened the album again. In the photograph, Sirius threw his head back and laughed.

He looked nothing like a murderer. He looked happy. Harry touched the edge of the picture.

"Why?" he whispered.

The man in the photograph offered no answer. He only smiled beside the people who had trusted him, trapped forever in the last years before he destroyed them.
