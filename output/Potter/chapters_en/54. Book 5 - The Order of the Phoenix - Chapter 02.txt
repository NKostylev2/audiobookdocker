2

Harry had been awake long enough to hear the kitchen kettle being filled twice. He lay watching the pale strip of light beneath his half-open door, listening to the floorboards settle after each journey across the room below. Whoever was downstairs was making no attempt to hide it.

At last he sat up, pulled on the grey shirt lying over his chair and picked up his trainers by their laces. He carried them down barefoot rather than wake the girls by hunting for his socks. The third step creaked beneath him, and he paused, waiting for the familiar demand to explain where he thought he was going.

Only a pipe answered from behind the bathroom wall. Harry continued to the kitchen, where the open door let out the smell of tea and the low murmur of someone turning a page.

Remus occupied the counter beside the kettle, one hand curled around a mug. A book rested against the bread bin. He had reached the same page Harry had seen him reading before bed, though he turned it when Harry entered. Sirius slept at the table.

His head lay upon one folded arm. Reports surrounded him in a rough circle, marked in black ink and occasionally singed where anger had overtaken accuracy. One hand remained closed around a small silver instrument linked to the medical warning charm in Harry's room. Harry had agreed to let Pomfrey's overnight checks continue while the curse residue settled. The charm could signal a change that needed attention; it could not listen to him or tell anyone what he was dreaming. The needle inside it pointed steadily towards green. Harry stopped beside the table.

"How long?"

Remus looked at Sirius.

"Twenty minutes."

"Before that."

"He slept for nearly two hours yesterday afternoon."

"That isn't what I meant."

"I know."

The kettle began to murmur. Remus removed it before it whistled and filled a second mug without asking. Harry sat opposite Sirius. The silver instrument caught the faint light. Its needle quivered within the green band, following changes in the curse residue rather than anything Sirius could control. Harry watched his godfather's fingers tighten around it in his sleep and wondered how long he had been waiting for it to turn. Harry set his trainers beneath the chair. Sirius did not stir.

"He checks it during the day too," Remus said.

"Why?"

Remus glanced at Sirius's closed hand. "He can look at it when he can't see you."

Harry had expected something about the wards or a warning Pomfrey had given them. The answer left him looking at Sirius instead, at the hollows beneath his closed eyes and the grey beginning to show at one temple.

Harry looked towards the windows. Darkness reflected the kitchen back at him: crooked shelves, drying herbs, maps hidden beneath tea towels and Sirius asleep over proof he no longer needed to watch.

"I'm upstairs."

"He knows you're upstairs, Harry. He still spent twelve years unable to find out whether you were all right. And after what happened in June—" Remus stopped and lowered his voice. "Knowing takes a little time to catch up."

Harry's eyes returned to Remus. He said it without drama. Twelve years of Sirius's life had been reduced to an explanation for why one hand would not open around an instrument.

"Should I wake him?"

"Would it help?"

"I don't know."

"Let him have a few minutes more. I'll wake him before his neck seizes up."

Remus placed tea in front of Harry. It contained milk and enough sugar to make Sirius's opinion visible from another room. Harry wrapped both hands around the mug without drinking.

"Did you sleep?" Remus asked.

"Some."

"How much is some?"

"Hermione has forbidden that answer."

"Sensible."

"You all keep banning words. Eventually I'll be unable to speak."

"Draconia may regard that as a successful safety measure."

Harry smiled. Remus let the smile fade without trying to recover it. He turned towards the bread bin, giving Harry a moment to drink his tea while the kitchen window slowly lost its reflection to the light outside.

Remus opened the bread bin. Inside, Dobby had arranged rolls, fruit and several parcels labelled for people who forgot that breakfast existed before eight. Harry accepted one and discovered cheese, tomato and too much mustard.

"Sirius made these," he said.

"Dobby is still offended."

"He should be."

Harry ate anyway. By half past seven, the rest of Hawthorn had begun to wake. Harry heard the first door open upstairs while he was finishing the sandwich, followed by footsteps, running water and Lucius asking whether anyone intended to vacate the bathroom this morning.

Hermione opened hers and crossed the landing carrying three books. Draconia emerged from the bathroom wearing a dark dressing gown and stopped when she discovered Lucius waiting outside with a comb, a clean shirt and the expression of a man betrayed by occupied facilities. Narcissa called everyone to breakfast. Dobby and Winky disagreed over porridge. The peacocks announced themselves from the garden. By then Sirius had woken, hidden the monitoring instrument beneath a newspaper and claimed to have been reviewing reports. Harry did not expose him.

But the red marks left across Sirius's palm were still there when he reached for the teapot, and Harry found he could not ignore those as easily as the newspaper. Harry caught his wrist and gently opened the fingers still marked by the instrument's ridged handle.

"Green," Harry said quietly.

Sirius's eyes flicked towards Remus. Remus continued buttering toast.

"It could change," Sirius said.

"Then the alarms change with it. You don't have to hold the needle there yourself."

Around them, Narcissa asked Winky for another cup and Hermione moved her papers away from the butter dish. Harry could feel the conversation continuing without anyone insisting that he and Sirius finish theirs in front of the table. Sirius looked at Harry's hand upon his wrist.

"The alarm sounded twice last night."

"Once because Draconia dropped a book and startled me."

Across the table, Draconia lifted her head. "I did not drop it."

"The floor did?"

"The bedside table is poorly constructed."

Sirius's mouth moved. Harry released him.

"Try sleeping tonight," he said. "It would be embarrassing if I became the responsible one."

"Impossible."

"Reassuring."

Sirius reached for the teapot. The monitoring instrument remained beneath the paper.

The rest of Tuesday and all of Wednesday passed in visits, letters and arguments about what the Ministry could be made to hear. Harry learned where Hawthorn kept the clean mugs, which patch of the garden stayed in shade after lunch and how difficult it was to finish an essay with three adults discussing security on the other side of the kitchen door. Cedric came on Wednesday evening with his parents. After supper, the two boys sat outside until Narcissa called them in, neither mentioning the following afternoon until Amos appeared with the hearing papers. By Thursday morning, there was no avoiding them. The inquiry was due to begin at two, and preparation had spread into every room downstairs.

Hermione transformed the sitting room into a records office. Copies of Cedric's original statement covered the long table, each marked by coloured tabs identifying physical evidence, independent witnesses and the points the Ministry had distorted. Three issues of the Daily Prophet lay beside them with misleading sentences underlined in red. A French newspaper had printed Cedric's account without alteration. Viktor Krum had sent a signed account from Bulgaria, and Fleur had provided hers in English and French so no translation could be blamed for its contents. Neither had been in the graveyard. Their statements concerned the maze, the champions' return and what they had witnessed at Hogwarts afterwards; Hermione had marked those limits as carefully as the passages supporting Cedric.

At the opposite end of the table, Draconia sat facing Lucius, having pushed the spare parchment aside. Rather than organise another copy of the statement, she asked the questions she expected the Ministry to use. Lucius answered them with his hands flat upon the table, repeating himself whenever she caught him making an answer sound more comfortable than it was.

"Why did you answer the summons?"

"Because refusal would have exposed our family before I possessed a means to protect it."

"So you acted from fear."

"Yes."

"Fear for yourself?"

Lucius paused.

"Partly."

Draconia did not rescue him from the answer.

"When did you decide to betray the gathering?"

"Before I arrived."

"Can you prove it?"

"Narcissa was beside me when the summons came. Dumbledore lifted the wards so I could go, knowing I meant to bring Potter back if I found him. Their accounts establish what I said before I left. The rest depends on what I actually did."

"But you still knelt."

"Yes."

"You watched Harry being tortured."

Lucius looked down at his hands. The scratching of Hermione's quill stopped at the far end of the table, and Draconia waited long enough that Harry could hear a chair being moved in the kitchen. Lucius's hands lay flat upon the table. The scar near his throat had darkened.

"Yes."

Draconia's next question did not arrive. Harry stood in the doorway with Sirius and Remus. He had entered quietly enough that neither Malfoy noticed. Hermione did. Her gaze moved from Harry to Draconia and then towards the prepared chair beside her. Harry crossed the room. He did not take it. He sat beside Draconia upon the edge of the table.

"You moved when you could," Harry said to Lucius.

Lucius looked at him.

"The committee will ask why I did not move sooner."

"They should."

Draconia's head turned sharply. Harry continued before she could defend either of them.

"But they need to ask what you did afterwards too. You got my wand back. You gave us a way out. They can't just leave that part out."

Remus's attention sharpened from across the room. Lucius sat back.

"You intend to attend the hearing."

"That was the plan."

"The plan placed you in the public gallery."

"They'll notice me there too."

"You are not scheduled to testify."

Harry looked at the prepared statements.

"Plans have gone wrong around me before."

Draconia shut her eyes. Hermione pressed two fingers against her mouth. Sirius laughed from the doorway. Lucius did not.

"That comparison is not comforting."

"It wasn't meant to be."

Narcissa entered carrying formal robes across both arms. She took in the papers, the expressions and Harry upon the table.

"We leave in ninety minutes," she said. "If anyone begins another argument which cannot be completed within forty-five, they may continue it while dressing."

Everyone moved.

Harry discovered the problem with his formal robes when he tried to reach for the hairbrush. The cuffs pulled back above his wrists and the shoulders tightened, leaving him staring at the sleeves as though they had been replaced while he slept.

They had fitted at the beginning of June. By Thursday, the sleeves ended too high above his wrists and the fabric pulled across his shoulders whenever he raised his arms. The changes were not large. Harry might not have noticed if Hermione had not stared from the doorway while Draconia adjusted the collar.

"What?" he asked.

Hermione looked away. "Nothing."

"That was clearly something."

"You have grown."

Harry examined the sleeve.

"A little."

"Apparently," Draconia said.

She stood close enough for her breath to move the fine hair near his temple. Her fingers worked beneath his chin, replacing the plain clasp with a narrow silver one Narcissa had found among the clothes rescued from the Manor. Draconia's concentration remained upon the fastening. Colour had begun to rise near her ears. Harry watched it.

"You could use magic," he said.

"I am aware."

"This seems slower."

"Stop moving."

"I'm standing still."

"Your mouth is moving."

"Usually a separate problem."

Her fingers stopped beneath his jaw. Harry smiled. Draconia looked up. The silver clasp remained unfinished between them. Hermione cleared her throat and crossed to Harry's sleeve.

"This requires lengthening."

"Does it?"

"Unless exposed wrists are part of your political strategy."

"It would confuse them."

"Everything confuses the Ministry," Draconia said, recovering the clasp. "We need a higher standard."

Hermione drew her wand. Warmth travelled over Harry's arms while the fabric extended. Her hand followed the seam to check it had settled correctly. Draconia finished the collar. For several seconds, both girls remained within reach, Hermione's fingers around his wrist and Draconia's beneath his chin. Harry became aware of the mirror opposite them.

In the mirror, Harry could see the three of them gathered so closely that Hermione's curls brushed Draconia's sleeve. Draconia glanced from the clasp to his mouth and back again. Hermione noticed, then looked at Harry, and he could no longer pretend that standing still was the only difficult part of being fitted for robes. Neither expression resembled simple friendship. Harry's confidence deserted him with impressive efficiency.

"Is this where I ask whether I look presentable?"

Hermione released his wrist.

"No."

Draconia stepped back. "You have missed the opportunity."

"Tragic."

"Deeply."

The door opened. Sirius entered wearing black formal robes over a white shirt, his hair tied neatly enough to reveal Narcissa's involvement. He looked from Harry to Hermione to Draconia.

"Have I interrupted something?"

"Competence," Draconia said.

"Then I wouldn't recognise it."

She smiled despite herself. Sirius's gaze moved over Harry's robes and settled upon the silver clasp.

"You look good."

The words were uncomplicated. Harry found them more difficult than teasing.

"Thanks."

Sirius came farther into the room and closed the door behind him. Hermione gathered her notes. Draconia reached for the folder containing Lucius's statement.

"Give us a minute," Sirius said.

The girls exchanged a glance. Hermione left first. Draconia followed after touching Harry's sleeve once, an adjustment the fabric did not require. Sirius waited until the door shut.

"You can stay here."

Harry looked at him.

"I know."

"You can come to the Ministry and leave before the hearing. You can sit in the gallery without speaking. You can speak and stop halfway. You can change your mind while a question is being asked."

"Hermione wrote that on page one."

"Then she has excellent judgement."

"She wrote it because you dictated."

"Still excellent."

Harry moved towards the window. The ash tree filled the glass. Beyond it, Bill and Kingsley were inspecting the Ministry Portkeys in the garden. Moody argued with both from a safe distance. Cedric stood near the gate beside his parents, leaning upon a cane while Amos straightened a collar which did not need help.

"Do you want me to stay?" Harry asked.

Sirius joined him.

"I want you somewhere no one can look at you like evidence."

"That place doesn't exist."

"I know."

Harry watched Cedric move from the gate towards the cottage. His limp remained visible, though smaller than it had been at King's Cross. Every step carried him towards a room designed to question whether he understood what had happened to his own body.

"He shouldn't do it alone."

"He won't."

"I mean someone should be there who was in the graveyard."

Sirius looked at Harry's reflection in the glass.

"Lucius was there."

"Not where Cedric was."

Harry remembered a second heartbeat hidden beneath his. The awareness had faded since the grass tether died, but sometimes sleep returned it. He would wake feeling another pulse beneath his ribs and lie still until his body accepted that solitude did not mean Cedric had stopped breathing.

"I don't want them to make him prove it without me," he said.

Sirius's hand settled upon his shoulder.

"Then we go."

Sirius did not tell him he was brave or say that Cedric was lucky to have him. Harry was grateful for that. He wanted to go, but he did not want the decision made harder to change by hearing how proud someone was of it. Harry leaned briefly into the hand.

"You understand," Sirius added, "that if anyone in that building calls you unstable, I will become extremely stable while placing them through a wall."

"Is stability important to the technique?"

"Essential."

"I'll warn Remus."

"Traitor."

Harry smiled. The smile remained when they left the room.

Cedric had escaped the preparations long enough to claim the garden bench beneath the ash tree. Through the open kitchen window came Amos's voice asking whether they needed another copy of his son's statement; Cedric closed his eyes briefly at the sound. His cane rested beside him. Summer light filtered through the leaves and moved across his face, revealing the scar near his hairline whenever the wind shifted. Harry found him there fifteen minutes before departure.

"Hiding?"

Cedric moved his coat from the other end of the bench.

"Strategically waiting."

"That's what I call it too."

Harry sat. The cottage blocked most of the garden noise. Peacocks moved along the far wall. Protective runes had been carved into the old stones beneath them, visible only when the birds stepped over each line. Beyond the wards, fields rolled towards a line of trees blurred by heat. Cedric turned the cane between his hands.

"You look different."

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Better robes."

"Draconia chose the clasp. Hermione lengthened the sleeves."

"That explains why you look terrified."

Harry glanced at him. Cedric grinned.

"You are enjoying this too much."

"I spent a year competing against you. I deserve something."

"You got half the prize money."

"This is better."

Harry looked towards the upstairs windows. A pale shape moved behind one. Brown hair appeared at another.

"Nothing is happening."

"Of course not."

"They said—"

"Harry."

"Fine."

Cedric continued grinning for several seconds. Then his hand tightened around the cane.

"I don't want to repeat it."

Cedric had still been smiling a moment earlier. Now he studied the end of the cane, turning it against the grass while his shoulders rose with a breath he seemed reluctant to finish. Harry did not ask what it meant.

"You shouldn't have to."

"I know. But they're still going to ask."

The branch shadows moved across Cedric's formal robes. Yellow thread at the cuff formed a small badger, charmed to turn its head whenever his pulse rose. It had begun looking around.

"Your statement is already in the record," Harry said. "They could read it."

"They have. Today's hearing is meant to decide whether I knew what I saw."

"You did."

"I know."

"Then why are you worried?"

Cedric glanced towards him. Harry heard the question after it left his mouth. He laughed once.

"Right. Stupid question."

"Spectacularly."

"I have a talent."

The badger at Cedric's cuff stopped moving.

"When I close my eyes," Cedric said, "I see it through gaps in the roots. Never the whole graveyard. Pieces. Nott's ring. The cauldron fire. Your hand against the stone. Sometimes I remember pain in places where they hadn't touched me. I know it came through the spell, but knowing doesn't stop me feeling it again."

Harry looked at the grass beneath their shoes.

"The Ministry healer asked about that."

"Seven times."

"They'll use it."

"Yes."

Harry searched for reassurance and found only dishonest versions. Cedric did not need to hear that the confusion meant nothing. Voldemort had placed false deaths inside Harry's mind so convincingly that he still sometimes saw them over living faces. Cedric had seen the attack from the ground and felt some of Harry's pain through the spell; that was different from seeing inside his head. The inquiry would have to distinguish those things. Pretending either boy remembered every detail perfectly would only make the Ministry's argument easier.

"Tell them," Harry said.

Cedric frowned.

"All of it. Tell them what you aren't certain about. Then tell them what you are."

"You think admitting uncertainty will help?"

"If there are gaps, let them hear about them from you. Don't let her act as though she's caught you hiding them."

Cedric studied him.

"Who taught you politics?"

"The Dursleys."

Harry had meant the answer as a joke. Cedric did not laugh, and for a moment Harry saw Petunia correcting his account of a broken plate before Vernon had even entered the kitchen. He had learned early that one uncertain detail could be used to dismiss everything else.

"Tell them which bits you're sure of," Harry said. "You don't have to pretend you remember everything. They'll find one mistake and spend the rest of the afternoon on it. Don't give them that."

Cedric looked towards the cottage.

"Lucius said nearly the same thing."

"Don't tell him."

"Never."

They sat in silence. The word graveyard did not enter it. Neither needed the reminder.

"Did the shroud hurt you?" Harry asked.

Cedric's grip loosened upon the cane.

"Not while it was active. Afterwards, yes. My magic still tries to follow your pulse when I panic." He touched the centre of his chest. "Healers say it will fade."

"Mine listens for yours when I wake."

Cedric's head turned. Harry kept looking at the garden.

"I thought that might happen," Cedric said.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"You had enough people studying your sleep."

"That is not an answer."

"It is the same answer you would give."

Harry disliked this enough to know Cedric was right.

"We're both idiots," he said.

"Joint champions."

"Same thing, apparently."

Cedric laughed. Harry held out one hand.

"If it becomes too much, stop."

Cedric looked at the hand.

"You too."

"I'm not scheduled to speak."

"And how long do you think that'll last?"

Harry stared at him. Cedric's expression remained innocent.

"You heard that conversation inside?"

"It seemed like a good moment."

"I was hoping everyone had forgotten it."

Cedric took his hand. Their palms met. No silver roots grew between them. No heartbeat moved from one body into another. Harry felt only the warmth of Cedric's hand and the slight pressure of his fingers. He had spent too many nights wondering whether the old spell still needed something from him. It was a relief to sit beside Cedric and know that this contact belonged entirely to them.

"Together?" Cedric asked.

Harry thought of the centre of the maze. Two hands upon the Cup.

"Together."

They travelled by Portkey. The Ministry supplied a tarnished brass umbrella stand which smelled of wet wool despite the clear weather. Moody tested it with six spells, a Sneakoscope and one drop of his own blood before permitting anyone near it. Bill altered the landing sequence so the group would arrive inside Amelia's secured office rather than the public Atrium.

"That defeats the point," Amos said.

"The point is reaching the hearing alive," Moody replied.

"The point is being seen arriving. They want Cedric to look hidden."

"Kingsley's just reported fourteen press photographers at the Ministry entrance and two suspect objects. And Black makes three people I want searched before we leave."

"Which three?"

Moody's magical eye rotated towards Sirius.

"A photographer near the fountain and a wizard selling remembrance ribbons. Kingsley is having both checked. The third is Black."

Sirius looked down at his coat. "This is protective equipment."

"That is a knife."

"A protective knife."

Remus removed it. Harry held the umbrella stand beside Hermione and Draconia. Cedric stood opposite him with his parents. Lucius and Narcissa took the remaining space. Sirius placed one hand around Harry's upper arm. Remus joined them at the stand, checking that everyone's fingers had found a clear patch of brass. Bill and Moody would follow on the separately authorised Portkey once they had confirmed the garden wards were settled. The hook caught behind Harry's navel. Hawthorn vanished.

Amelia's office appeared sideways. Harry struck the carpet shoulder-first. Draconia landed across his legs. Hermione collided with both of them while retaining hold of every page in her folder. Cedric arrived upright, balanced upon his cane with the irritating competence of someone who had kept his knees bent and listened to Bill's warning about the landing.

"Show-off," Harry said from the floor.

Cedric offered a hand.

"Practice."

Lucius had landed upon his feet. He pretended Narcissa's grip beneath his elbow had nothing to do with it. Sirius pulled Harry upright and inspected him before releasing his arm. The office door opened.

Amelia Bones entered wearing formal plum robes and her monocle. Two Aurors accompanied her. Kingsley closed the door behind them and sealed it with a sweep of his wand.

"The location changed," Amelia said.

Hermione straightened. "To somewhere smaller?"

"Larger. Amos's foreign press invitations attracted diplomatic observers. Fudge attempted to cancel rather than permit them inside Committee Chamber Seven. Madame Maxime sent a formal notice reminding him that two foreign schools possess standing under the Tournament charter."

Cedric smiled faintly.

"Where are we going?"

"Wizengamot Hearing Chamber Two."

Sirius's hand tightened upon Harry's shoulder. Sirius's grip made Harry look around. Lucius had stopped adjusting his cuff, and Remus's smile was gone; whatever the chamber meant to them, it had not been part of the journey they had prepared him for.

Lucius looked towards Amelia. "A criminal chamber."

"A public chamber," she corrected. "It seats three hundred and possesses an independent transcription ward. Fudge cannot close the gallery without a vote."

"He agreed to this?" Remus asked.

"No."

Amelia adjusted her monocle.

"He lost the vote."

Amos straightened at that. Even Lucius looked less guarded, though Sirius's hand remained upon Harry's shoulder. A room large enough for witnesses and reporters was what they had wanted; Harry wished the prospect did not also leave his mouth so dry. Harry's scar tightened. A chamber underground. Rows of faces. A chair in the centre. Questions concerning whether memory belonged to him. The office walls seemed to move closer. Draconia's fingers found the inside of his wrist. Hermione stepped against his other shoulder. Neither announced what they were doing. Harry touched the key beneath his robes. Sirius noticed.

"Still your choice," he said.

Harry looked at Cedric. The charmed badger at his cuff had begun moving again.

"Larger room," Harry said.

Cedric met his eyes.

"Larger room."

Amelia led them into the Ministry.

The first thing Harry noticed beyond Amelia's office was how clean the Ministry corridors were. After the crowded cottage, with its abandoned mugs and parchment balanced beside breakfast plates, the place looked as though nobody was permitted to leave evidence of having lived through a difficult morning.

Floors reflected the people walking across them. Brass signs shone beside every lift. The tiled corridors smelled of wax, old paper and the faint metallic smoke left by defensive spells. Even the rubbish bins had been emptied, as though disorder might suggest the institution responsible for containing it had lost control. People stopped working when Harry passed.

Quills paused above forms. Conversations collapsed. A witch carrying six interdepartmental memos walked into a pillar because she watched Cedric instead of the corridor. Several employees nodded towards Amelia and then looked quickly away from Lucius.

Nobody said Voldemort, but Harry could see the question passing between the employees as they recognised him and Cedric. They had read enough to know why the group was here. Whether they believed it was harder to tell.

Amelia kept the group together. Kingsley walked at the front. Moody occupied the rear and announced every concealed wand he detected until a clerk abandoned one inside a potted fern. Sirius remained beside Harry. Hermione and Draconia followed so closely that their sleeves repeatedly brushed his. They reached the lifts. One was already waiting.

The golden grate stood open. Inside, a small woman in rose-coloured robes held a stack of cream folders against her chest. She possessed soft cheeks, carefully curled brown hair and the fixed smile of someone who regarded friendliness as an administrative procedure. A narrow black band circled her throat. Upon it hung a silver Ministry seal. Her eyes passed over the group. They stopped upon Harry.

"Mr Potter," she said.

Her voice was higher than he expected and much less warm than the smile arranged around it. Harry waited.

"Dolores Umbridge," Amelia said. "Senior Undersecretary to the Minister and appointed counsel for the Ministry position."

Umbridge's smile widened by a measured amount.

"We have heard so much about you."

"That explains the newspapers."

Hermione's elbow found his ribs. Umbridge looked towards her.

"And Miss Granger."

Hermione became completely still. The Undersecretary turned to Draconia next.

"Miss Malfoy. This must be a very difficult day for your family."

Draconia's face settled into the expression she used when strangers attempted familiarity.

"It will be easier if the questions are fair."

Lucius's mouth moved at one corner. Umbridge stepped deeper into the lift.

"Of course. I hope today's proceedings provide clarity."

"For everyone, I hope," Harry said.

Sirius made a sound suspiciously like a cough. The lift grate closed. They descended. Paper aeroplanes entered through slots near the ceiling, circled the crowded space and left again when they found no room to land. A calm woman's voice announced departments at each level. The farther underground they travelled, the colder the metal beneath Harry's hand became. Umbridge stood opposite him. She did not stare. She glanced in intervals, returning to the folders whenever Harry met her eyes. The top one bore his name. Not Potter, Harry.

HARRY JAMES POTTER: CAPACITY AND INFLUENCE.

He read it twice to make certain the words did not change.

"Interesting title," he said.

Umbridge placed one gloved hand over the label.

"Confidential documents should not concern you, Mr Potter."

"My name usually concerns me."

"The Ministry has a responsibility to consider the wellbeing of every minor involved."

"Is that why you printed my medical condition?"

The lift became very quiet. Umbridge's smile remained.

"No private medical record has been released by my office."

"The Prophet knew which curses affected my memory."

"The public already knows You-Know-Who was alleged to have used invasive magic."

"Voldemort," Harry said.

Her fingers tightened upon the folder. The lift stopped.

"Level Ten," announced the voice. "Wizengamot hearing chambers, evidence archives and secure holding rooms."

The grate opened. Cold air entered. Umbridge stepped out first.

"The chamber will determine which allegations the evidence supports," she said. "I suggest allowing it to do so before reaching conclusions."

Harry followed.

"I reached mine in person."

Sirius placed one hand between Harry's shoulders. The gesture might have been warning. It felt like approval.

Wizengamot Hearing Chamber Two had been built to make individuals feel temporary. Dark stone curved upwards in tiered rows. Three hundred wooden seats faced a circular floor sunk below the gallery. Lamps floated along the walls without visible chains, their blue-white light leaving no shadow deep enough to hide inside. An enchanted quill the height of Harry's arm waited above the central recording desk. Its point hovered over a roll of parchment disappearing through a slot in the floor. Seven chairs occupied the raised bench. Amelia's stood in the centre.

Five other panel members filled the chairs beside her: an elderly witch with bronze spectacles, a narrow wizard Harry recognised from the Tournament committee, a representative from St Mungo's and two officials whose names Hermione whispered before anyone asked. Dumbledore occupied the final place at the far left, not as Headmaster but as Chief Warlock. His robes were dark blue. He met Harry's eyes once and inclined his head. The public gallery was already full.

Reporters occupied the front rows. Quick-Quotes Quills trembled above notebooks. Madame Maxime sat beneath a reinforced section of bench beside two officials from the French Ministry. A Durmstrang representative waited across the aisle. Professors McGonagall, Flitwick and Sprout had travelled from Hogwarts. Arthur Weasley sat near the rear with Bill and Kingsley. Xenophilius Lovegood wore yellow robes and held a printing plate upon his lap. He waved when Harry noticed him. Near the doors, Rita Skeeter smiled without moving her eyes.

"Who invited her?" Harry whispered.

Hermione followed his gaze.

"The gallery is public."

"That wasn't the question."

"Probably herself."

Rita's quill wrote something. Harry resisted the urge to wave. The witness table stood in the centre of the floor. Not a chained chair, but a narrow desk and two seats beneath every watching face. A silver line encircled it, designed to identify concealed enchantments and prevent Apparition. Cedric looked at the ring. His badger cuff turned its head rapidly. Harry moved closer. Cedric's shoulders lowered. Sirius noticed.

He did not object when Harry chose the seat directly behind the witness area rather than the protected family enclosure Amelia's clerk indicated. Hermione sat upon his right. Draconia took his left. Sirius occupied the aisle. Remus sat behind them with Narcissa. Lucius remained standing until his name was called. The chamber filled around him. Whispers followed the pale head through every row.

Death Eater.

Traitor.

Malfoy.

Draconia heard them. Her posture became flawless. Harry placed his hand upon the bench between them, palm upwards. She looked down. Draconia looked at his open palm, then at the reporters below. The word traumatised entered the chamber in Harry's own voice. It felt different from seeing it in the Prophet. Still unpleasant. His. The recording quill wrote it without comment. Umbridge took one step closer.

"You are an exceptionally articulate young man."

Harry waited for the compliment to reveal its cost.

"Your guardian and friends have clearly helped you develop language for a distressing experience."

There it was. If he struggled, he was unreliable. If he spoke well, he had been rehearsed.

"Mostly they ask me to eat," Harry said.

Laughter returned. Umbridge's quill pressed hard enough to tear its page.

"Let us discuss the alleged torture."

Sirius moved. Harry's stomach closed.

"No."

Umbridge blinked.

"I have not asked a question."

"I'm not going through it again. You've got the healers' reports."

The chamber waited. Amelia leaned forward.

"The witness has declined that subject. Move on."

Umbridge looked towards the panel.

"The nature of the magic may affect his perceptions."

"Three healer reports describe the magic. Mr Potter is not required to reproduce pain for this chamber."

Harry breathed. The refusal felt more exposed than answering would have. Voldemort had asked questions and punished silence. Every part of Harry's body expected consequence.

None came. A clerk placed a glass of water within reach and withdrew without asking Harry to look at him. Harry curled his fingers around it. The water was cold enough to feel through the glass; when he lifted it, the rim clicked against his teeth. He took a small drink and set it down carefully. Sirius slowly sat. Pride and grief occupied his face together. Umbridge changed direction.

"You describe an attempt to murder Mr Diggory," Umbridge said. "Yet he is alive."

"Yes."

"Neither of you witnessed a death in the graveyard."

"Not that I saw."

"Then the account concerns injuries and threats, rather than a murder committed by the restored individual."

Harry looked towards Cedric. His friend sat very still beside his mother.

"Crouch cast the Killing Curse at him twice. He survived because I put something between them. Later, Voldemort ordered Lucius to kill him while he was unconscious. Lucius refused."

"The order is corroborated by Mr Malfoy."

"I heard it too."

"You were severely injured."

"Yes. That doesn't make Cedric surviving evidence that nobody tried to kill him."

Amos's hand closed upon the edge of the bench. The recording quill moved above them, preserving the distinction. Umbridge waited for the murmuring to stop.

"You claim knowledge of the attacker's intentions."

"He gave orders in front of me."

"Yet you also claim to have resisted his attempts to enter your mind."

"Not all of them."

"Did you enter his?"

The question had not appeared in any preparation. Harry's scar burned. A room without windows. A snake near the fire. The sense of watching through a height and angle that did not seem to belong to him. He had glimpsed those things in dreams, before and after the graveyard, without knowing whether they were memories, inventions or something coming through the scar. A rush of anger that had sometimes outlasted waking, with no argument at Hawthorn to explain it.

Harry had been warned that the scar might still carry a connection. That was different from knowing which dreams came through it, or whether any of them showed Voldemort as he really was. He had reported that the nightmares continued, but not how often they seemed to begin from somewhere outside his own memories.

"I don't know," Harry said.

Umbridge became very still.

"Explain."

"No."

"Mr Potter—"

"I don't know what happened. I'm not guessing because you asked in public."

Amelia's hammer touched the bench.

"Move on."

Umbridge's private quill wrote three lines. Harry knew he had given her something. Refusal protected the details, not their existence. He would tell Sirius and Remus about those details as soon as they could speak privately. Saying he had slept badly had left too much out. The realisation arrived without time to examine it. Umbridge returned to the Ministry table.

"One final question."

Harry waited.

"What outcome do you want from this inquiry?"

Simple answers lined up. Belief. Revenge. An admission that Fudge was frightened. Aurors sent against Voldemort. The Prophet forced to print what happened. Enough protection that nobody else would wake with rope around their chest. Harry looked at the enormous quill.

"I want you to stop wasting time deciding whether the person trying to kill us is politically convenient."

No one laughed. Umbridge lowered her folder.

"The Ministry exists to establish facts before acting upon them."

"Then establish them. You have Cedric, Lucius, Crouch's exposed impersonation, the Cup, three independent identifications of Pettigrew, my blood and every spell the healers found. If that isn't enough to investigate, this was never about evidence."

Umbridge's smile returned.

"You believe the Ministry is afraid."

"I believe the Ministry knows exactly what believing me would require it to do."

The recording quill reached the end of a page. It drew another from the floor. Umbridge sat.

"No further questions."

Harry remained inside the circle. Amelia examined the panel.

"Questions from the bench?"

The St Mungo's representative asked whether Harry remained under care. He answered yes. The narrow wizard asked whether he had communicated with Voldemort since the graveyard. Harry said not knowingly and explained that dreams made certainty difficult. The elderly witch with bronze spectacles asked whether he wanted protective custody.

"I have a guardian," Harry said.

"The Ministry may provide additional—"

"I have a guardian."

Sirius looked down. His hands closed upon the rail. Dumbledore asked the final question.

"Harry, when you are uncertain whether an experience is memory, dream or intrusion, whom do you tell?"

Harry understood the question he was not asking.

Had he told anyone how those dreams felt?

"Sirius," he said. "Hermione. Draconia. Usually Remus. Sometimes all of them if they refuse to leave."

"And concerning the uncertainty you identified today?"

Harry met his eyes.

"I will."

Dumbledore inclined his head.

"Thank you."

Dumbledore did not tell him that the omission was harmless. He simply held Harry's gaze for another moment, long enough for Harry to understand that the conversation could wait until they had somewhere private to finish it. Harry left the witness circle. The silver line dimmed behind him. Sirius reached him at the aisle and put both hands upon his face.

The gesture startled Harry more than an embrace would have. Sirius examined his eyes, his scar and the colour beneath his skin in front of the entire chamber.

"Still here?" Harry asked.

Sirius's thumbs rested near his temples.

"Still here."

Harry covered one hand with his own. Hermione moved into the space beside them. Draconia followed. Cedric stood near the next bench. The chamber continued around the small cluster because public proceedings rarely paused for private survival. Harry sat between the girls again. This time, neither pretended the contact was accidental.

The panel adjourned at half past six. By then, the chamber lamps had dimmed from white to evening blue. The gallery had thinned only slightly. Reporters sent completed pages away by enchanted paper bird and immediately began new ones. Nobody wanted to leave before discovering which version of the day would become official. Amelia instructed the witnesses to wait in the adjoining consultation room.

It contained twelve chairs, one narrow window near the ceiling and a table bearing water nobody trusted. Cedric sat between his parents. Lucius lowered himself into the nearest chair with enough care to reveal how much the walk from the chamber had cost. Narcissa knelt to examine his injured leg. He objected quietly until she looked at him. The objection ended. Harry stood near the door.

Adrenaline still moved through him without finding an exit. His fingertips tingled. Sounds arrived too distinctly: water pouring into a glass, Amos's shoe against stone, the rustle of Hermione rearranging notes already arranged. Sirius did not ask him to sit. He stood close enough that Harry could choose contact without having it imposed.

"I need to tell you something," Harry said.

"I know."

"You don't know what it is."

"Dumbledore's question wasn't subtle."

Across the room, Dumbledore spoke quietly with Amelia's clerk. Remus had already noticed. His attention rested upon Harry while he pretended to examine the hearing programme. Hermione closed her folder. Draconia left Lucius's side and crossed the room. Nobody demanded privacy from the others. Harry lowered his voice anyway.

"The dreams aren't always the graveyard," Harry said. "There's a room I don't recognise. A fire and a snake. Sometimes I seem to be looking from somewhere that isn't me." He pressed two fingers against his scar. "I know we talked about the connection at Hogwarts. I didn't know whether these were part of it or just more nightmares."

Sirius's face hardened. Not at Harry.

"How often?"

"More than once. And sometimes I wake up angry, but I don't know what I'm angry about. At Privet Drive, one dream felt as though something had noticed me looking."

Hermione went pale.

"You didn't tell us."

"I didn't know what I was telling."

"You could have said that."

Harry looked away. The consultation room became the Hogwarts library, Hermione on the opposite side of a table insisting that not understanding danger did not make it less worth reporting. Four years and a war had changed the size of the consequences without changing the argument.

"I should have," he said.

Hermione's anger faltered because he had not made her fight for the admission. Draconia stood before him.

"Were we in those dreams?"

"No."

"Hawthorn?"

"Not in those ones. I dream about Hawthorn too, but that feels different."

"Anything which may indicate where he is?"

"A room without windows. A fire. The snake."

She absorbed each fact.

"We write it down when we return."

"Draco—"

"Not for the Ministry. For us."

Harry stopped objecting. Sirius looked towards Dumbledore.

"We knew the connection might continue."

It was not a question. Dumbledore finished speaking with the clerk and approached.

"Yes," Dumbledore said. "Bill distinguished it from the blood curse at Hogwarts, and Harry and I spoke about it afterwards. We still do not know how it behaves. These details may help us recognise a pattern, but we must not treat a dream as a reliable account of where Tom is."

"Then we need more than telling him to report anything unusual," Sirius said. "He's been having nightmares for weeks. How was he meant to separate one from another?"

The room sharpened. Dumbledore did not retreat behind gentleness.

"Yes."

Harry looked at him. He remembered the conversation in Dumbledore's office, the promise that uncertainty would no longer be used as a reason to leave him uninformed. They had discussed keeping Voldemort out. They had not worked out what Harry should do when he woke unable to tell whether that had failed. Sirius did not soften.

"What do we do now?"

"Begin with what Harry can recognise, without entering his mind or requiring him to relive the attack." Dumbledore's gaze moved to Harry. "I should have helped you make a practical plan sooner. Knowing a connection may exist is little use if we leave you to decide alone what it means."

"Yes," Harry said.

The answer entered the room without deference. Dumbledore inclined his head.

"We will correct it."

"We?" Sirius asked.

"Harry, first. You as his guardian. Remus, if Harry wishes. A healer experienced in mental magic. Severus, if—and only if—the conditions can be made safe and Harry agrees."

Harry thought of Snape inside his memories. The possibility immediately produced resistance.

"Not yet."

"Then not yet," Dumbledore said.

Remus joined them.

"We don't have to start by entering your mind," he told Harry. "Before we attempt Occlumency, we'll work on recognising what's happening. A way to steady yourself when a memory catches you. We can write the dreams down while they're fresh, too, if you find that helps."

The word carried less threat in his voice.

"You'll teach me?"

"If you want me to."

Harry looked at Sirius. Sirius still watched Dumbledore, anger held in reserve rather than dismissed.

"With the door open," Harry said.

Remus understood.

"With the door open."

The consultation-room door opened before anyone could continue. Dolores Umbridge stood outside. Her pink robes appeared brighter against the dark corridor.

"The panel will return shortly," she said. "Chief Warlock, the Minister requests a word."

Dumbledore looked towards Harry.

"Later," Harry said.

"Later," Dumbledore agreed.

Umbridge waited while he entered the corridor. Her eyes travelled through the room and settled upon Hermione's folder.

"You take excellent notes, Miss Granger."

Hermione placed one hand over them.

"Thank you."

"It is encouraging to see a student so committed to correct information. Hogwarts will require that quality during the coming year."

"Why?"

Umbridge's smile returned.

"The Minister is concerned that certain disagreements among staff have affected student welfare. Curriculum, communication and appropriate political neutrality are under review."

Professor McGonagall moved from the far wall.

"Hogwarts has never required Ministry supervision to establish its curriculum."

"Requirements change when circumstances do."

Draconia's repaired bracelet pressed against Harry's sleeve. The threat did not resemble a curse. That made it no smaller.

"Are you planning to teach?" Harry asked.

Umbridge looked pleased that he had spoken.

"I go where the Minister considers me useful."

"Hogwarts has stairs that move. They may have standards."

Sirius turned away. Remus appeared to choke upon nothing. Umbridge's face remained pleasant through visible effort.

"School provides valuable structure for young people who have experienced instability. I hope you will benefit from it."

"Four years so far."

"Then we may expect improvement."

She followed Dumbledore into the corridor. The door closed.

"I dislike her," Harry said.

"A restrained conclusion," Draconia replied.

Hermione opened her folder to a clean page. At the top, she wrote:

Ministry educational intervention.

Below it:

Dolores Umbridge.

On Hermione's page, the name was only another line of neat ink. Harry could hear Umbridge's voice when he looked at it, the patient sweetness with which she had asked him to doubt himself. He was glad Hermione had written down what she had said about Hogwarts.

The panel returned seven minutes later. Everyone resumed their places. Harry sat beside Sirius this time. Hermione and Draconia remained in the row ahead, close enough that either could turn around without attracting attention. Cedric sat with Amos and Mrs Diggory on the opposite side of the aisle. Lucius accepted Narcissa's arm while descending the steps and ignored anyone who noticed. Amelia waited for the chamber to settle.

"The panel has reached four findings."

The enormous quill lowered.

"First: the evidence establishes a premeditated attack upon the Triwizard Tournament and the abduction of Harry Potter and Cedric Diggory. The evidence identifying Barty Crouch Junior, Theodore Nott Senior and Peter Pettigrew as participants is sufficient to require their apprehension and formal examination. This inquiry makes no criminal conviction in their absence."

No surprise.

"Second: the witness accounts, examined alongside the physical and medical evidence, establish sufficient grounds to investigate a ritual intended to construct or restore a human body. The accounts identify bone taken from the grave of Tom Riddle Senior, the hand severed by Peter Pettigrew and blood taken from Harry Potter as its components."

Murmurs crossed the chamber. The ritual and the body emerging from it had entered the official finding, with the limits of the evidence recorded beside them.

"Third: credible evidence supports the identification of the restored individual as Tom Marvolo Riddle, also known as Lord Voldemort. The panel orders a full investigation into that identification, the preservation of all connected evidence and immediate review of individuals previously marked or associated with his organisation."

Harry stopped breathing. Not acceptance. Not denial. Credible evidence. Investigation. Words smaller than the truth and larger than anything Fudge wanted written. The gallery erupted. Amelia struck the hammer twice.

"Fourth: the panel recognises Harry Potter, Cedric Diggory, Lucius Malfoy, their households and the protected associates named within the sealed appendix as protected witnesses. No Ministry department may release private medical, residential or guardianship information without review by the Office of Magical Law Enforcement."

Umbridge stood.

"The Minister's office objects to the third and fourth findings."

"The objections are recorded," Amelia said.

"The panel exceeds its authority by implying confirmation where identity remains disputed."

"The finding says credible evidence and orders investigation."

"The public will interpret it otherwise."

Amelia removed her monocle.

"The public is entitled to interpret the evidence you attempted to prevent them hearing."

Noise rose again. Umbridge gathered her folders. The private one bearing Harry's name disappeared beneath the others. Fudge had lost the vote. He had not lost the Ministry. Harry understood the distinction as soon as hope attempted to simplify it. The chamber doors opened. Reporters surged towards the exits. The first account of the hearing would be written before Harry reached the Atrium.

They tried to leave through a secured corridor. Someone had leaked the route. The first flash struck Harry as the door opened. White light filled his vision. Sirius pulled him back while Kingsley raised a shield across the passage. Questions hit the transparent barrier from the other side.

"Mr Diggory, do you consider the finding vindication?"

"Mr Malfoy, will you accept prosecution for your prior activities?"

"Harry, are you receiving psychiatric treatment?"

"Mr Black, have you coached your godson's evidence?"

"Miss Malfoy, did your father's relationship with Harry Potter influence his betrayal?"

Draconia flinched. Harry turned towards the voice. Rita Skeeter stood three rows back, green quill moving above her notebook. The question had not been hers. A young wizard wearing the badge of the Prophet pressed against the shield near Kingsley.

"Ask me," Harry said.

Sirius caught his arm.

"You don't owe them anything."

"I know."

Harry looked at him.

"I want to."

Sirius searched his face. Then he nodded to Kingsley. The shield opened only wide enough for Harry, Cedric and Lucius to stand behind it without entering the crowd. Hermione and Draconia remained immediately behind Harry. Narcissa stood beside her husband. Sirius's hand rested at the centre of Harry's back. Questions multiplied. Harry raised one hand. Unexpectedly, the reporters quieted. He had not known they would.

"The transcript is public," he said. "Print it."

Quills moved.

"Do you believe the panel confirmed You-Know-Who's return?" asked a witch from the Prophet.

"Voldemort," Harry said. "The panel found credible evidence and ordered an investigation. If the Ministry had done that in June, the hearing wouldn't have been necessary."

"Does the failure to confirm his identity disappoint you?"

"I wanted them to investigate in June. I still want them to do it now."

The corridor went silent. Sirius's fingers tightened against Harry's back.

"Harry," another reporter called, "the hearing established that you suffer continuing false perceptions. Why should the public trust your account?"

"They shouldn't trust it without evidence. They should read the evidence."

"Do you believe every person who disagrees with you is cowardly?"

"No. Some haven't read anything."

Cedric lowered his head to hide a smile. The reporter flushed.

"Mr Diggory," called a French witch, "do you stand by the complete account published last month?"

"Every word. Including the parts where I acknowledged uncertainty."

"Mr Malfoy, why should anyone believe a former Death Eater?"

Lucius leaned upon his cane.

"They should examine the witness accounts of my departure from Hogwarts, the spells upon my wand and the memories I have surrendered. I do not expect my word alone to satisfy them."

Rita's quill scratched furiously. The young Prophet reporter pushed closer.

"Miss Malfoy, is it true your father acted because of your attachment to Harry Potter?"

Harry felt Draconia become motionless behind him.

"Her father acted because two people were about to be murdered," he said.

"But was your relationship—"

"Not evidence."

The answer came from Hermione. She stepped beside Draconia.

"The hearing just spent four hours explaining the difference. You may request the transcript if you missed it."

The reporter looked between the girls.

"Miss Granger, your own relationship with Harry has also attracted—"

"Still not evidence," Harry said.

Rita Skeeter smiled. Her quill wrote faster. Draconia moved forward. Harry expected anger. Her voice emerged cold and precise.

"My father answered for himself in there. Harry and I are friends. You can report what happened in the graveyard without turning that into a story about us."

Rita laughed. The Prophet wizard retreated. Xenophilius Lovegood raised his printing plate above the crowd.

"The Quibbler will reproduce the transcript in full. We have acquired a larger typeface for the Minister's benefit."

Harry recognised Luna inside the sentence.

"Thank you."

"My daughter says hello. She also says you still possess her glass star."

"I do."

"Good. They dislike drawers."

Nobody asked what they meant. Hermione reached around Harry and handed Xenophilius one of her prepared index sheets.

"This lists the supporting reports entered today."

"Excellent. Would you like a job?"

"She is fifteen," Draconia said.

"We offer flexible hours."

Kingsley closed the shield before Hermione could answer. They continued towards the Atrium. The press followed at a controlled distance, still shouting questions. Ministry employees emerged from offices to watch the procession. Some looked afraid. Some looked angry. One elderly clerk near the lifts placed two fingers against her heart when Cedric passed. A young Auror nodded to Lucius and then pretended he had not.

Harry noticed those small gestures as they passed. Nobody stopped work to declare the Ministry had been wrong, and the clerk lowered her hand before anyone could ask what she meant by it. Still, a few people had chosen to acknowledge them, and after the morning's averted eyes Harry found himself remembering each face. The Atrium opened ahead.

Green flames filled the fireplaces. Gold statues shone above the fountain. Hundreds of witches and wizards crossed the polished floor beneath enchanted notices announcing that the Ministry remained committed to public safety. At the foot of the fountain, Cornelius Fudge waited. He had not attended the hearing.

He wore formal robes and the expression of a man prepared to claim responsibility for any conclusion he failed to prevent. Two assistants stood behind him. Umbridge occupied his right side. The crowd slowed. Fudge looked at Harry.

"A difficult day," he said.

Harry considered the answer.

"You could have come."

Fudge's face tightened.

"The Minister cannot attend every preliminary proceeding."

"This one was about Voldemort returning. What was the other meeting?"

Several Ministry employees stopped walking. Fudge glanced towards the reporters.

"The hearing did not establish that conclusion."

"It established enough to investigate."

"Which the Ministry will do responsibly, without spreading panic or permitting interested parties to exploit tragedy."

Sirius stepped beside Harry.

"Interested parties. You mean witnesses."

"I mean those who benefit from instability."

Lucius approached on Harry's other side.

"Your definition has changed since I stopped financing it."

Fudge reddened. Umbridge intervened.

"The Minister will issue a complete statement this evening. Public discussion in the Atrium obstructs Ministry function."

Harry looked around. Every face had turned towards them.

"Then we'll read the statement," he said. "We have practice finding what it leaves out."

Sirius guided him away from the fountain before Fudge found a reply. Kingsley met them at the edge of the crowd and took them through a staff passage to Amelia's office, where their return Portkey waited. Behind them, cameras flashed around the golden fountain.

The Portkey returned them to Hawthorn shortly after eight. In Amelia's office, Harry had waited while the last witnesses collected their papers and the Aurors checked the route home. He had said little, saving his attention for the brass stand and the promise of the garden at the other end. Harry landed upon his feet. Cedric did too. They looked at one another.

"Practice," Harry said.

"Show-off," Cedric replied.

Then Harry's knees weakened. Sirius caught one arm. Cedric caught the other. Together, they lowered him onto the garden bench beneath the ash tree before anyone could call it collapse.

"I'm fine," Harry said.

Hermione appeared in front of him.

"Banned word."

"Upright."

Draconia looked at his position upon the bench.

"Inaccurate."

"Returned."

Sirius sat beside him.

"Acceptable."

Narcissa began directing everyone towards food. Winky had prepared a late supper. Dobby had prepared another in case the hearing lasted longer and a third in case the Ministry attempted starvation as policy. Moody inspected the boundary before entering. Lucius allowed Remus to take his cane for precisely long enough to cross the path. Harry remained beneath the tree. Cedric lowered himself onto the grass opposite him. His formal robes had creased at the knees. The badger at his cuff appeared asleep.

"Larger room," Cedric said.

Harry looked towards the cottage. Voices moved through the open kitchen windows. Hermione and Draconia argued over which reporter required correction first. Amos explained the panel finding loudly enough for Winky to applaud. Sirius sat close enough that their shoulders touched.

"They heard us," Harry said.

"Some did."

"Fudge didn't."

"He heard. That was the problem."

Harry considered this. The hearing had not produced the satisfying change stories promised after evidence entered a room. No collective gasp. No Minister transformed by fact. Fear survived contradiction by changing vocabulary.

But the name existed in the record, along with the evidence Fudge had wanted separated from it. Their families had been granted protection, and the panel had ordered an investigation. Harry did not know how much those words would change by morning. He knew they were more than the Ministry had been willing to give them when they entered the building.

"You were good," Harry told Cedric.

"So were you."

"I made a joke about his face."

"Important evidence."

"Umbridge disagreed."

"She looked as though she disliked most of today."

Cedric rested both hands behind him in the grass.

"When she asked whether the tether changed my memories, I thought I was going to be sick."

Harry looked at him.

"You didn't seem—"

"Neither did you."

Fair.

"I kept checking the cuff," Harry admitted.

Cedric glanced down at the sleeping badger.

"Mum's idea."

"Good idea."

"Draconia made fun of it."

"Better idea."

Cedric laughed. Mrs Diggory called him from the kitchen. He pushed himself upright with the cane.

"I'm claiming half the credit for that insult," he said.

"The one about Voldemort's face?"

"I was there. I laughed. Friends ought to share these things."

Harry held out his hand. Cedric took it.

"See you Saturday," he said.

"Why Saturday?"

"The party's on Monday. Mum wants to talk about presents before you find a way to refuse them."

"I object now."

"Too early. Doesn't count."

Cedric entered the cottage. Harry watched him go. Sirius remained beside him.

"You stopped," he said.

"Cedric?"

"In the hearing. When she asked about the torture."

Harry's body remembered the silence after no.

"Amelia said I could."

"People have told you that before."

Sirius looked towards the branches above them.

"You believed it this time."

Harry leaned back against the bench.

"I thought Umbridge would punish me."

"I know."

"Part of me still thinks she will."

"She'll try."

Harry turned. Sirius's expression was serious.

"She may try to make trouble," he said. "If she does, we'll deal with it. You were still allowed to say no today."

Harry absorbed the words.

"That sounds like Remus."

"I steal his best material."

"Cedric stole mine earlier."

"War destroys morality."

Harry rested his head briefly upon Sirius's shoulder.

"Were you proud?"

The question escaped before he could make it casual. Sirius went still. Then his cheek rested against Harry's hair.

"Painfully."

Harry closed his eyes.

"Good."

They sat beneath the ash tree until Narcissa threatened to deliver supper to them personally.

The silver clasp refused to open. Harry stood in his bedroom before the mirror, pulling at the fastening beneath his jaw while the formal robes attempted to choke him. The mechanism turned, slid and returned to its original position with pure-blood confidence.

"Need help?" Hermione asked from the doorway.

"No."

The clasp tightened.

"Convincing."

Draconia appeared behind her. She took in Harry's hands, the collar and the colour rising above it.

"You are attempting to open it backwards."

"It has a direction?"

"Most mechanisms do."

"Doors are friendlier."

Draconia crossed the room.

"Hands down."

Harry obeyed. Her fingers touched the base of his throat. The clasp released immediately.

"It dislikes you," he said.

"It respects competence."

"Same difference."

She removed it and placed it upon the desk. Harry loosened the collar. Air returned. Hermione sat upon the edge of his desk beside the unfinished animal charms. She had removed her formal outer robe but still wore the blue dress beneath it. Ink marked the side of her hand. A crease crossed her forehead where concentration had remained too long.

"You should have told us about those dreams," she said.

She had been holding it back since the consultation room.

"I know."

"That does not make me less angry."

"I know that too."

Draconia leaned against the wardrobe.

"Agreeing with us won't get you out of this."

"I thought you'd appreciate the time saved."

Hermione looked at him. Harry allowed the humour to go.

"I'm sorry."

Her expression softened without losing concern.

"Were you afraid we'd think the visions were real?"

"I was afraid saying it would make them more real. And that Dumbledore would try to keep all of you away if Voldemort could see through me."

Draconia's face changed.

"He cannot make that decision alone."

"He won't," Hermione said. "Not now."

"You don't know that."

"No. But Sirius is your guardian, and Amelia put protections into the finding. They can't simply decide where to send you. We'd know, Harry. All of us would."

Harry looked at the folder beneath her hand.

"You really do make everything sound like administration."

"I'll take the paperwork if it keeps you here."

"Terrifying."

"Useful," Draconia corrected.

Harry sat upon the bed. The girls joined him, Hermione on one side and Draconia on the other. The pattern had become natural enough to frighten him in a different way. Two people within reach. Two people Voldemort already knew how to use inside his mind. Love multiplying vulnerability and protection at the same time.

"When Umbridge asked how I know what's real," he said, "I didn't have an answer until I looked at you."

Hermione's hand found his.

"You had the answer."

"You reminded me."

Draconia rested her head briefly against his shoulder.

"You can look at us whenever you need to."

"I thought it was correcting essays."

"That too," Hermione said.

Harry looked down at her hand inside his.

"You were brilliant today."

She blinked.

"I hardly spoke."

"You made the reporter regret his career."

"He asked an irrelevant question."

"Terrifying," Harry repeated.

Hermione smiled.

"You were good too."

"I insulted several officials."

"Not that part."

"That was my favourite."

Draconia lifted her head.

"Unfortunately, confidence suits you."

Harry turned towards her. She appeared to realise the sentence had escaped only after it reached him.

"Unfortunately?"

"You were already difficult."

"And now?"

"Unbearable."

Harry leaned closer by less than an inch.

"You stared."

Draconia's eyes narrowed. Colour rose beneath her skin.

"I was monitoring your condition."

"From my mouth?"

Hermione made a startled sound. Draconia forgot how to answer. For one glorious second, Harry understood the appeal of confidence. Then Hermione laughed. Not politely. She folded forward, still holding Harry's hand, curls falling across her face. Draconia stared at both of them in betrayal. Harry began laughing too.

"You are both intolerable," she said.

"You said it suited me."

"I withdraw the observation."

"Entered into the record," Hermione managed.

Draconia seized a pillow. Harry's laughter ended when it struck his face. He caught the second. Harry dragged the caught pillow against his chest and looked at the two girls advancing on him. For the first time that evening, neither had a folder open or a warning ready. He shifted carefully away from the bedrail, grinning as Hermione tried to rescue her papers before Draconia found another weapon.

Hermione used another pillow to defend the folders. Draconia climbed onto the bed to improve her angle. Harry called Expelliarmus out of habit and was promptly hit with another pillow for expecting the word to do anything without his wand. A third pillow split along one seam, releasing feathers into the air. The door opened. Lucius stood outside. One white feather settled upon his hair. Nobody moved. He reached up, removed it and examined the room.

Draconia still knelt upon the mattress with a pillow raised above Harry's head. Hermione held another across her chest. Harry had lost his glasses somewhere near the foot of the bed. Lucius looked at his daughter.

"Your mother wishes to know whether you are coming to supper."

"Eventually."

"I will inform her that the hearing produced instability."

He closed the door. Silence lasted two seconds. Harry found his glasses. The laughter returned harder than before. They heard Lucius call from the stairs that supper might require another warming charm. Draconia threw the last pillow at Harry as he tried to insist that this was her fault.
