14

Every biscuit on Dolores Umbridge's tray had a smiling face. Harry noticed them before the forms, and wondered whether she had chosen the icing herself. They lay in a neat circle upon a silver tray, each pink mouth stretched beneath two dots of chocolate, as though somebody had decided that being questioned in this office ought to be a pleasant experience. One face had slipped sideways while the icing dried. Harry chose that biscuit when the tray reached him.

Outside, the castle was settling into Monday evening. He had spent most of the day thinking about seven o'clock, first during lessons and then while pretending to finish an essay. Now the eighth of January had brought him here, and he would have liked the clock above Umbridge's mantelpiece to stop sounding so pleased with itself. The tray stood between two Ministry forms, a pink teapot and a narrow wooden box. Nobody had explained the box. Harry set his biscuit upon the saucer and looked instead at its brass fastening, which had been polished more recently than the wood around it. There was a faint tick from inside, easily lost beneath the clock.

"What's in the box?" he asked.

"An example of a standard screening instrument," Umbridge said. "It need not concern you."

Shah looked directly at her. "No instrument is to be used without an explanation and Harry's agreement. Keep it closed."

Umbridge gave a small, impatient nod. Harry watched her move it beside the teapot before looking towards Sirius.

He moved his hand away from the tea. The room was already too warm; a fire burned behind an ornamental screen, and the smell of sweet potpourri caught at the back of his throat. Every framed kitten upon the walls seemed to have found something fascinating about his chair. Sirius occupied the chair beside him. The chair was too small. Umbridge had either selected it deliberately or furnished her office according to the proportions of people she preferred to question. Sirius's knees rose above the seat. His black robes gathered around the carved legs. He wore the expression of a man imagining several ways to improve the room with fire.

Professor McGonagall stood near the door. Healer Anjali Shah sat on Harry's other side with a blank notebook closed upon her lap. Sirius had first consulted her after the graveyard, and the August panel had confirmed her role in Harry's independent care. She had seen him after the train attack and had visited Hawthorn to discuss what the household needed to share, and what Harry could keep private. He knew her well enough to remember that she waited for an answer instead of treating a pause as one. She wore dark blue robes without a St Mungo's badge and had declined Umbridge's offer of tea. Before they entered, she had asked Harry what he wanted her to do if he became uncomfortable. He had said, rather awkwardly, that he wanted someone to notice before discomfort became an argument about his manners. She had taken that seriously.

Now she set her notebook upon her lap and waited. Umbridge glanced at the unopened cover twice. Harry found himself liking it very much.

"This is not a disciplinary meeting," Umbridge said.

McGonagall looked towards the locked door.

"Then the lock is unnecessary."

Umbridge smiled. "Student privacy."

"Open it," Sirius said.

"Mr Black—"

"Open it or the meeting ends before your first question."

The smile remained. Umbridge touched her wand to the latch. The door opened by three inches. Noise from the corridor entered: distant footsteps, a portrait complaining about draughts, somebody calling that the library would still be there after dinner. Harry could see a narrow strip of flagstone beyond McGonagall's shoulder. He looked at it until the tightness beneath his collar eased. Shah followed his glance, then met his eyes briefly. Her pen remained inside the notebook. He was relieved that the moment could happen without becoming another entry on a form.

"This review," Umbridge continued, "is intended to support Mr Potter's return to appropriate academic participation following a series of disruptive perceptual episodes."

"I warned people that Arthur Weasley was being attacked," Harry said.

"You reported an image which coincided with an incident."

"At the correct door. On the correct floor. With the correct injuries."

"Coincidences may feel significant to a distressed mind."

Sirius reached for one smiling biscuit. He broke it cleanly in half. Umbridge's eyes followed the movement.

"Is that a diagnostic technique?" Harry asked.

Healer Shah's mouth moved at one corner.

"No," she said. "Nor is anything Commissioner Umbridge has said so far."

The smile vanished.

"The Ministry has authorised student stability reviews beneath Directive Eleven."

"The Ministry may review school safety," Shah replied. "It may not diagnose illness, compel therapeutic disclosure or reinterpret a corroborated warning as evidence of illness simply because its source remains uncertain."

"No diagnosis has been proposed."

"Then use no diagnostic language."

Harry looked at Sirius. Sirius mouthed, I like her. Harry looked at the forms. The first page divided symptoms into neat boxes. Hallucination. Paranoia. Aggression. Fixation. Authority resistance. The final category had been underlined twice.

"Do I fill those in?"

Umbridge placed one hand over the page.

"We will proceed conversationally."

"Then why have you already decided which boxes I might belong in?"

"Mr Potter, I would ask you to take this seriously."

"You could ask him about the boxes," Shah said. "He has asked you twice."

Umbridge took a breath.

"Harry, do you believe people in positions of authority intend to harm you?"

"Some of them have."

"The question concerns belief."

"So does the answer."

"Can you identify an example?"

Harry looked at the framed Educational Decrees lining the wall.

"You used a Blood Quill on me in this office. That seems a reasonable example."

McGonagall's gaze moved from Harry to the form. "Record the answer he actually gave you." Umbridge dipped her quill into ink.

"Subject responds to reasonable inquiry with deflection and hostility."

Healer Shah opened her notebook at last.

"Commissioner interprets disagreement as symptom before establishing clinical relevance."

Umbridge's quill stopped. Sirius ate one half of the broken biscuit. Harry decided he liked Healer Shah as well. Sirius asked him quietly whether he wanted to leave. Harry shook his head. For the moment he wanted the answers on the record, provided Shah remained beside him. The questions continued.

"Have you heard voices which other people could not hear?" Umbridge asked.

Harry glanced at Sirius. "In second year. There was a basilisk moving through the pipes."

"You believe that was the source?"

"We found it. It was quite large."

Her quill moved again. "And do you believe animals communicate specifically with you?"

"I can understand snakes. You know that already." He shifted the saucer away from his sleeve. "Hedwig makes herself understood too, mostly when I've done something she dislikes."

"The question concerns speech."

"Then write down snakes. You can leave Hedwig out of it. She'd only object."

Did he experience an exaggerated sense of personal responsibility for events beyond his control? Harry hesitated. That one belonged to a real conversation. Umbridge watched him find the difference.

"Sometimes," he said.

Sirius turned towards him but did not interrupt. Shah's attention changed. Not sharper. More present.

"Can you give an example?" Umbridge asked.

"No."

"Your refusal may indicate—"

"He can discuss that with me privately," Shah said. "It isn't a condition of staying at school."

Harry released a breath. The room separated itself into questions he could answer and questions Umbridge merely wanted to possess. He became better at recognising them. Did he become angry when authority figures challenged his account?

"Depends whether they're challenging it with evidence."

Did he encourage other students to share his beliefs?

"I tell them what I saw. They can ask questions. I don't get to decide what they believe."

Did he practise unauthorised defensive magic?

"That sounds disciplinary."

"It concerns risk-taking behaviour."

"Then ask whether I take risks."

Umbridge's quill scratched across the form.

"Do you?"

"Yes."

The simple answer unsettled her.

"Do you consider yourself unusually capable of surviving them?"

The graveyard entered quietly. Harry saw wet stone. His own blood across marble. Cedric unmoving behind the headstone while the binding weakened one curse at a time. He pressed one foot more firmly against the rug. The graveyard had been cold; this room was hot enough to make his collar itch. Sirius's knee touched his, and the biscuit lay untouched upon its saucer. Beyond the open door, two students were arguing about a book. Harry listened until he could make out separate words.

He was sitting in a chair at Hogwarts. He could stand and leave it. Shah had said so before they came in.

"No," he said.

Umbridge leant forward.

"Yet you repeatedly place yourself at the centre of dangerous events."

"I haven't exactly been choosing the invitations."

"The Tournament selected you after you had already survived several school incidents. The train was attacked while you travelled upon it. Arthur Weasley was attacked while on duty in a restricted Ministry corridor, and you reported knowledge you should not have possessed. At what point do you consider the possibility that your presence creates the danger you then claim to resist?"

The question found every place Harry had worked to cover. Sirius put down the biscuit.

"Enough."

"The pattern is relevant."

"You're blaming him for people attacking him. We're finished here."

"Mr Potter may answer for himself."

Harry's hands had gone cold. He pressed them against his knees beneath the desk. The train. Arthur. Cedric. Hermione petrified. Draconia inside the Chamber. People hurt in rooms Harry entered. People harmed because someone wanted him afraid.

"Harry," Shah said.

Her voice did not ask him to answer Umbridge. It asked him to return.

"Whose choice caused the train attack?"

Harry looked at her.

"The Death Eaters'."

"Whose choice sent the snake after Arthur?"

"Voldemort's, I think. Whoever sent it." Harry swallowed. "Not mine."

Umbridge's mouth tightened at the name.

"Whose choice brought assistance?"

"Mine."

"You helped bring assistance," Shah said. "Somebody else arranged the attack. Being the person they want to hurt does not make their choices yours."

Harry looked down at his hands. He had heard versions of that before, often from people who loved him enough to say them more than once. It was harder to remember when Umbridge could arrange the whole year into a question and place it before him as though the answer were obvious.

"I know," he said, although he was still trying to feel it.

"You don't need to settle it in this room."

Harry breathed. Sirius's hand settled against the back of his neck.

"This review is concluded," Shah said.

Umbridge looked towards the forms.

"We have not completed the perceptual assessment."

"You have established no clinical reason to continue."

"Directive Eleven requires—"

"Directive Eleven does not override consent."

Umbridge touched the wooden box.

"One objective response will settle the matter."

Shah stood.

"Do not open that."

Umbridge flipped the fastening before Shah could reach across the desk. Silver light unfolded from the box. McGonagall stepped forward, wand raised; Sirius was already getting out of his chair. Something long and dark rose through it, growing until its head hung above the desk. Harry saw the movement before he could decide what sort of magic produced it. The pink walls seemed to fall away. He knew he had been looking at an office; he could no longer make the knowledge hold still. Its scales filled Harry's sight, green-black and wet. The projected mouth opened. Harry knew the fangs before he could see them clearly, and memory supplied the blood. He felt stone beneath a body which had no feet and Arthur's pulse breaking against his tongue.

Hunger entered. Harry's chair struck the floor behind him. His wand appeared in his hand.

"Harry."

Sirius's voice came from too far away. The snake moved. Harry cast.

"Protego!"

The shield burst between him and the desk. Shah snapped the lid shut while McGonagall forced Umbridge's wand arm away from it. The last projected light struck Harry's shield, scattering the snake into silver fragments. There had been no second spell from Harry's wand. He held it pointed at the same place, struggling to understand why his chair was behind him and why his mouth tasted of something he had not eaten. Harry stood behind the shield, breathing through teeth which felt too sharp.

His scar burnt. Mine, he thought. Hands. Feet. Wand. Heart. The borrowed hunger recoiled. Sirius reached him from the side rather than the front.

"May I touch you?"

Harry nodded. Sirius's hand closed around his upper arm. The contact gave his body a boundary. Healer Shah kept her wand across the closed box. The lid sealed beneath blue light. McGonagall stood between Umbridge and the desk, her own wand still raised.

"What was that?" McGonagall demanded.

Umbridge had backed against her desk.

"A standard projection stimulus. His violent response—"

"Was a Shield Charm," Harry said.

His voice shook. He kept speaking.

"I raised a shield. I didn't attack you."

The distinction mattered. Umbridge stared at the bright curve still standing between them. Neville had used the same spell beneath a falling candlestick. Harry remembered his friend explaining that there had been no time to ask permission. There had been no time here either. His wrist shook as he held the shield, but nobody on its other side was hurt.

"You were told to stop," Shah said. "Instead, you opened an instrument you had not explained and used a snake image after his account of the basilisk and the known circumstances of Arthur Weasley's injuries. You had no consent to expose him to it. He protected himself. You will not record that as an attack."

Her voice carried through the open door. Harry saw McGonagall look towards the corridor, then back at Umbridge, as though making certain there would be no later argument about who had heard.

"The Ministry employs this instrument in witness screening."

"The Ministry is not treating Harry."

Footsteps ran in the corridor. Draconia's warning had reached her in the library. She had called to Hermione and Ron, and they had fetched Flitwick from the nearby classroom before going to Umbridge's office. Draconia reached the open door first. Hermione came behind her with Ron and Professor Flitwick. Draconia's grey stone burnt red against her throat. She took in the fallen chair, Harry's raised wand and the remains of silver smoke above Umbridge's desk. Her face emptied.

"What did you do?"

Umbridge straightened.

"Miss Malfoy, this is a private—"

"What did you do to him?"

Harry lowered the shield. Draconia crossed the room. She did not draw her wand. The restraint was more visible than movement would have been. Hermione stopped beside Harry, leaving a little space between them.

"Harry, can you tell me where we are?"

"Her office." He swallowed. "Still, unfortunately."

Hermione's mouth trembled before she made it steady. "And who's beside you?"

"Sirius. You. Draco." He looked towards the door. "Ron looks furious."

"Accurate," Ron said. "Do you want your bag? I brought it."

Harry managed half a breath that might have become a laugh on another evening. Draconia took his free hand when he opened it towards her. The grey stone at her throat remained red, warning of the interference which had brought her running; it could not tell her who had caused it or whether Harry was ready to be touched. She watched his face while the colour slowly faded.

"I'm here," Harry said.

"I can see that."

Her voice broke upon the last word. Sirius turned towards Umbridge.

"The review is over. The box and the forms are to be preserved. I want an independent account before anything is altered."

"You have no authority to seize Ministry property."

"I have authority to prevent a second attempt to use it on my godson. Minerva?"

McGonagall stepped between Umbridge and the box.

"The instrument will remain sealed until the Board and safeguarding panel inspect it."

"You report to the Ministry."

"I witnessed what happened. I will notify the governors and Professor Dumbledore before anyone uses that instrument again."

Flitwick entered and cast three containment charms around the desk. Umbridge looked from professor to healer, from guardian to students. Shah asked Harry whether he could walk. He nodded, then discovered that nodding had been easier than moving. Sirius waited while he found his balance, one hand beneath his elbow. Ron pulled the fallen chair out of their way without looking at Umbridge. At the door, Harry could smell cold stone again. Someone had abandoned a stack of books against the opposite wall. He watched Hermione pick them up and realised she must have dropped them when she ran. Draconia kept his hand all the way into the corridor.

Harry did not return to dinner. He sat in an unused classroom while Healer Shah checked his pupils, pulse and response to a few simple questions. McGonagall had lit the fire before bringing him in. The desks smelled faintly of polish and the chalkboard still carried a half-erased diagram from somebody's afternoon lesson. Harry followed one of its crooked lines while the warmth returned to his fingers. Sirius remained by the window. Hermione occupied the desk beside Harry. Draconia stood until McGonagall brought a chair and told her that vigilance was not improved by damaged knees.

Ron waited outside with Neville and half the Gryffindor fifth-years. News travelled through Hogwarts without enchanted mirrors.

"The instrument projected a snake," Shah said. "You raised a shield and stopped when you recognised the room. Nothing in that suggests you intended to hurt anybody."

Harry rubbed his thumb against a mark on the desk. "It didn't feel like recognising the room. It felt like remembering pieces and hoping they belonged together."

"That took effort. I saw it."

"I cast before I knew where I was."

"You cast a shield before you knew where you were. That is significant."

Harry looked at his wand.

"I wanted to hurt it."

"A desire is not an action."

Arthur had said the same in different words. Draconia's fingers tightened upon the chair.

"Did the connection open?"

Shah looked towards the brass detector upon the table. Snape had brought it from his office, explained the examination before conducting it and left to report his findings to Dumbledore. For once, he had made no comment about Harry's decision to raise his wand.

"The detector was not in the office when she opened the box," Shah said. "Professor Snape examined you afterwards and recorded no pattern of continuing external pressure. We have your account of the scar pain and Draconia's warning, but neither tells us whether another mind received anything."

"So we can't be sure nobody saw," Hermione said.

"We can't. His memories were stirred too. The detector reading afterwards cannot reconstruct the moment of the projection, and a reaction alone cannot tell us whether somebody entered those memories."

Hermione looked towards Harry, then put her questions aside. She took a cup from the tray McGonagall had brought and set it where he could reach it. He was grateful that she did not ask him to remember the whole office again. Sirius turned from the window.

"I can take you home tonight. Poppy can check you before we leave."

Harry had expected it. Part of him wanted Hawthorn so badly that the old impulse rose in defence.

"If I leave after one meeting, Umbridge will say I couldn't manage school."

"I do not care what she says."

"I do."

"I know it matters to you."

"Not more than being safe. But it matters. She wants the review to become proof I shouldn't be here. If I leave because she frightened me, she gets to use both things."

Sirius's jaw tightened.

"Staying to deny her proof still lets her decide."

Harry looked down. That was true. The classroom held the difficult space between escape and endurance. At Privet Drive, staying had never been a choice. In the graveyard, leaving without Cedric would have been possible only by becoming someone Harry could not live with. At Hogwarts, both directions had reasons which belonged to him and reasons which belonged to other people.

"What do you want?" Sirius asked.

Not what would prove strength. Not what would embarrass Umbridge. Harry looked towards the door. Ron was talking quietly to Neville beyond it. They had lessons tomorrow, essays to finish, beds in a room he knew. Hawthorn had its kitchen and Sirius's chair and the lamp he liked to leave low. He wanted both places, which made answering harder than simply choosing the one Umbridge would dislike.

"I want to sleep here tonight," he said at last. "With Poppy, if that's all right. I want you to stay until the corridors close. And I don't think I can do lessons tomorrow. Could I decide again at lunch?"

Sirius studied him.

"All three."

Relief arrived before Harry could hide it.

"All three," he agreed.

Healer Shah wrote the arrangement into her report, then let Harry read the few lines before she closed it. McGonagall said she would speak to his teachers. Sirius pulled the remaining chair beside his and sat down with a small complaint about its legs.

"Better than the last one?" Harry asked.

"The last one was built for someone a foot shorter than me. This one has only lost a leg."

Draconia laughed softly. Harry kept the cup between his hands while Sirius settled beside him. Nobody began another assessment.

Remus arrived before breakfast with two paper bags and a Ministry visitor badge pinned upside down. Harry noticed the badge first. It read NON-HUMAN CONSULTANT — SUPERVISED ACCESS in letters large enough to remain visible from the opposite end of the hospital wing. Remus wore it over an old brown coat.

"Who is supervising you?" Harry asked.

Madam Pomfrey emerged from her office carrying a tray of potions.

"I am. Anyone who has a difficulty with that can bring it to my office after I've finished seeing my patients."

Remus indicated her with one paper bag.

"Poppy."

"He has been well behaved so far," she said.

Harry sat higher against the pillows. The hospital wing looked blue in the winter morning. Frost silvered the tall windows. Empty beds disappeared into shadow on either side, and the fire near Madam Pomfrey's office had burnt low enough to show red between black coals. Harry had slept in short stretches, waking at footsteps or the click of the kettle in Pomfrey's office. Sirius had left after they agreed that Harry would call if he needed him. Neville, permitted to wait until breakfast after McGonagall spoke with Pomfrey, had fallen asleep in a chair beside the open ward entrance. Harry had heard him snore twice and found the sound unexpectedly comforting.

The chair now stood empty.

"Where did Neville go?"

"Breakfast," Remus said. "He left a guard."

A toad raised its head from the bedside table. Trevor sat inside the empty spare tumbler beside Harry's water jug, looking pleased with a posting which offered no possibility of damp grass.

"Excellent," Harry said. "Has he been briefed?"

"Very thoroughly. He has agreed not to give interviews."

Harry held out a finger. Trevor regarded it without enthusiasm. Harry withdrew the finger before he could be mistaken for breakfast, and Remus tucked the fallen glove safely onto the chair arm. Remus placed the bags upon Harry's blanket. One contained warm rolls, cheese and an apple. The other contained a folded jumper which belonged to Harry and a letter sealed with the trio's new mark.

"From Hawthorn?"

"Sirius wrote the first four pages. Narcissa reduced it to two sentences."

Harry opened the seal.

EAT. DECIDE AGAIN AT LUNCH.

Beneath it, Sirius had added:

I OBJECT TO EDITORIAL TYRANNY.

Harry smiled. Remus pulled the nearest chair beside the bed.

"How is your mind?"

"Still there."

"A promising start."

Harry tore one roll in half.

"Did he see it?"

Remus did not need to ask who.

"Snape recorded no continuing external pressure afterwards. He could not measure what happened before he arrived. We don't know whether the stone's warning and your scar pain meant anything passed through the connection."

Harry picked up the roll. "I wish people could stop saying they don't know."

"So do I. Saying something else wouldn't help you."

The bread was still warm inside the paper. Harry managed a bite while Remus waited, giving him time to chew before expecting the next question.

"If Voldemort felt it, he knows the snake still works."

"It frightened you. That is not secret intelligence."

"He might know Umbridge can reach me."

"He might. But you still came back to the room, and we have witnesses to what she did. If he learned anything, it wasn't that you were left there alone."

Harry looked at him. Remus's face held the fatigue which never entirely left near the full moon. It also held certainty.

"You raised a shield," he said. "You were frightened enough to lose track of the room, and you still found the spell we practised."

"I didn't think about it."

"We spent quite a long time making sure you wouldn't have to think about every part. I remember a considerable number of broken cushions."

Harry looked at him. Remus had taken off his gloves; one lay upon his knee and the other had slipped between the chair and the bed. He seemed prepared to spend the morning discussing cushions if that was what Harry could manage.

"I remember you saying they were expendable."

"I was attempting to sound wealthy."

Harry picked at the edge of the bag.

"I wanted to curse it."

"And I wanted to curse Umbridge after hearing what she did. Sirius wanted to feed her the wooden box. Minerva proposed three administrative consequences which frightened him more."

"Did you?"

"Did I what?"

"Curse her."

"No."

"Then apparently desire and choice are different."

Remus looked offended by the returned lesson.

"You could let me finish a lesson occasionally."

"You'd only give me another one."

"Experience has made you suspicious."

"You taught that too."

Harry finished the roll. Madam Pomfrey placed a potion beside him and inspected the remaining food before returning to her office.

"The badge is horrible," Harry said.

Remus glanced down.

"It got me through the door."

"That doesn't make it less horrible."

"No."

"Why wear it upside down?"

Remus's fingers touched the pin.

"So anyone who wishes to classify me must make an effort to read it."

Harry laughed. The sound disturbed Trevor, who climbed from the glass and left wet footprints across the bedside table.

"McGonagall sent another message," Remus said. "The candlestick has been removed from the entrance hall."

Harry stopped laughing.

"Why?"

"Unauthorised political display."

"It was a bent candlestick."

"Apparently remembering what happened now counts as political."

Harry looked towards the pale windows. Eleven minutes. The time between a rule and the first moment survival made disobedience necessary.

"Where is it?"

"Confiscated by the Commissioner's office. Neville asked before breakfast."

"He went to Umbridge?"

"He stood outside her office until she answered."

Harry thought of Neville in the entrance hall, holding the bent brass after saving a younger student. A year ago, he might have apologised for the damage. Now he had asked an authority to return evidence that her rule deserved breaking.

"What did she say?"

"That symbols encouraging unauthorised defensive behaviour create risk."

Harry looked at the sealed letter from home.

"She's frightened of a candlestick."

"She doesn't like people agreeing about what happened. It makes it harder to change the story afterwards."

Remus leant back.

"What are you going to do?"

The question did not mean what should be done? It asked what Harry chose.

"Talk to Neville first. It's his." Harry folded Sirius's note and put it beside the jug. "And I promised I'd decide about staying after lunch. You could still be here for that, if you haven't got somewhere else to go."

"I can be here."

Remus said it without consulting his watch. Harry looked down at the paper bag, relieved enough that he had to spend a moment adjusting it.

"Nothing alone," he added.

"A very good beginning. Now, would you like me to rescue my glove before Trevor mistakes it for a second accommodation?"

At lunch Harry counted eleven small brass pins upon school robes. He noticed the first while walking past the Hufflepuff table: two bent lines attached beside a name badge. Then he saw another on a Ravenclaw's scarf.

None resembled a candlestick closely enough to confiscate without explanation. Some were straight lines bent near the top. Others showed two parallel marks, the Muggle number eleven rendered in copper, tin or folded parchment. A first-year Ravenclaw wore two quills side by side behind one ear. Fred and George sold nothing; they distributed wire and denied understanding what students did with it.

Neville wore the original plaque. He had found it upon the empty display case after Umbridge took the candlestick. ELEVEN MINUTES rested against his chest on a piece of string. Harry returned to the Great Hall for lunch after Pomfrey agreed that walking downstairs would do him more good than another morning staring at the windows. Remus accompanied him as far as the entrance, then waited to speak to McGonagall. At the threshold, Harry found himself slowing. He had missed dinner, breakfast and every opportunity to discover what people thought of the previous evening.

Nobody rose to make an announcement. Conversation continued around the tables, but he began noticing small things inside it. Students looked at him, then at Neville, then continued speaking loudly enough to make silence impossible. Hannah Abbott had attached two brass lines to her Hufflepuff badge. Anthony Goldstein wore the number beneath his prefect pin. Three Slytherins at Draconia's end of the table had marked eleven upon their cuffs in green thread.

Umbridge sat at the staff table. No decree floated above her. Harry reached Gryffindor's bench. Ron moved his bag off the bench. Hermione passed Harry the serving dish and asked whether he wanted the seat facing the doors; he nodded and sat there. Across the Hall, Draconia had a letter open beside her plate. She looked up when he reached the table, held his gaze for a moment, then gave him the small smile she used when she was asking a question without wanting everyone to hear it.

Her grey stone stayed grey. Harry touched the green charm beneath his robes. Then he ate. Neville arrived carrying his lunch and sat opposite Harry, the plaque knocking gently against the edge of the table. He asked whether Harry had seen Trevor.

"Guarding the jug," Harry said.

"Good. He likes a job."

Ron passed Neville the potatoes. Harry stayed through the meal and the argument about whether Trevor ought to receive a prefect badge. By the time they stood, he had decided to remain at school for another night. By dinner, Ron had counted forty-three pins, although Ginny maintained that he had included one prefect's ordinary badge twice.

The D.A. met on Thursday, the eleventh of January. The hidden room had altered. Practice platforms remained along the walls, but the centre now contained six doors standing without rooms around them. Each had a different lock. Tables held mirrors, blank parchment, sealed boxes and pairs of ordinary coins. A large clock occupied the far wall. Its single hand pointed to eleven. Neville came in carrying the bent candlestick wrapped in an old towel.

Ron stopped inspecting the locks. "You got it back?"

"McGonagall did. She said it belongs to the school, and the governors want everything connected with the shield ban kept where they can examine it." Neville set the bundle on the table and folded the towel away. "Professor Flitwick checked it first. I'm meant to take it back to his office when they ask."

Ginny touched the crooked base. "Did Umbridge hand it over?"

"To McGonagall. I wasn't there."

"Probably fortunate," Ron said.

The room supplied a case beneath the clock. Neville lifted the candlestick into it himself, checking that the damaged base would stand, and placed the plaque below. The glass caught the reflection of his sleeve before he stepped away.

Harry watched his face. "Better?"

"Yes. I thought she might throw it out."

"McGonagall would've made her fetch it from the bin," Ginny said.

Neville smiled. They left the case where everyone entering could see it. The D.A. began. Cedric took the first half of the meeting. He held no wand.

"We need to practise something before the spells," he said. "Being asked a question when you're frightened, or when three people ask it at once. You can know the answer perfectly well and still forget what you're entitled to keep to yourself."

Susan glanced towards the doors. Harry could see her recognising a situation without wanting to offer it to the room.

Zacharias raised his hand. "Are you teaching us to lie?"

"I'm teaching you to ask what the question is for," Cedric said. "You can answer honestly without answering everything. Hermione has the school rules, Draco has written some rather unpleasant questions, and I'll help you sort out what you actually remember."

"And Harry?" Zacharias asked.

"Harry has agreed to provide distractions."

Everyone looked at him.

"Apparently I'm particularly qualified," Harry said. "I wasn't asked to explain why."

Ron made a noise which earned him the first practice jinx. The room divided them into groups. One student played an official. One carried information. A third created interruption: noise, time pressure, misleading questions or controlled jinxes. They began with questions alone. "Including the running?" Harry asked.

Draconia stepped upon his foot.

"Thank you," he amended.

The officer gave him a look which suggested that she had encountered fifteen-year-olds before. Ron collected the empty crate, Fred offered to carry it and George announced that lunch had become a matter of urgency. Before going inside the pub, Harry called Sirius. Hermione used her separate mirror to reach Narcissa, while Cedric stayed beside the boys and kept the receipt. "No. We wanted the pins displayed, not Warrington firing at you. George went for the officer."

Rosmerta wore an eleven pin upon her apron. Two off-duty Ministry clerks near the door wore them too. Draconia answered one of Blaise's comments without smiling. Harry found that reassuring until she laughed at the next one, at which point he became annoyed with himself for keeping count.

"It records pressure," he explained. "The veins darken where something presses, then fade after an hour. I thought it might help with copies that have been handled."

Hermione stared at the leaves.

"That's brilliant."

Neville coloured. "It's only a Sensitive Fern."

"It's thoughtful."

Ron drank half his butterbeer at once. Harry recognised the strategy. Ron seemed to have discovered the same difficulty. His bottle occupied him through Neville's explanation of the fern, and he took another long drink when Hermione asked how often the plant needed watering. Blaise looked at Harry.

"Potter, walk with me."

Harry nearly asked why. Draconia became suspicious.

"What are you doing?"

"Preventing murder through clarification."

"Whose?"

"Mine, eventually."

Blaise stood. Harry followed him because he wanted an explanation and had been trying not to ask for one all morning. He left the peeling bottle label beside his plate rather than carry evidence of the glare with him. They stopped near the back door.

"This is not a date," Blaise said.

Harry looked at him.

"I didn't ask."

"You've been glaring at me since breakfast. I thought you might appreciate knowing why I asked her."

"People keep criticising my face."

"It lacks discretion."

Blaise adjusted one cuff.

"My mother asked whether I'd invite her. Since Christmas, people have been withdrawing invitations from the Malfoys and asking other families to do the same. Draco has friends who are getting letters about whether they should sit beside her."

Harry stopped rubbing the sticky patch the label had left upon his thumb.

"You didn't know?" Blaise asked.

"She hasn't said."

"She doesn't particularly enjoy explaining that her father helping you has made her unpopular in certain drawing rooms. Mum thought being seen together would make a few of them reconsider. Draco agreed. I agreed. We also wanted lunch."

Harry looked towards the table. Draconia had turned in her seat to watch them.

"So you asked her for politics."

"She's my friend, Potter. I'd rather go for a walk with her than let somebody else's father decide I shouldn't. Mum agreeing made it easier. It didn't make me ask someone I dislike."

Harry's dislike became more difficult to maintain. He resented Blaise for this too.

"Then why did she look at me before answering?"

Blaise's eyebrows rose.

"You could ask her that yourself. I'm already doing rather a lot of your conversation."

"I haven't asked her anything yet."

"Yes. That appears to be the problem."

He returned to the table. Harry remained by the door. Draconia joined him a moment later.

"What did he say?"

"That he's in love with me."

Her eyes narrowed.

"Try again."

"He explained the politics."

"And?"

Harry looked through the steamed window at the street. Brass pins moved past upon coats and scarves.

"I was going to ask you."

Draconia became still.

"When?"

"The morning the notice appeared."

"Blaise asked at breakfast."

"I am aware. I spilt tea on myself."

"I saw."

"You said yes."

"It's February, Harry. We talked about this at Christmas."

Harry looked at her. The pub continued around them. Bottles touched tables. Someone laughed near the bar. Behind Draconia, Hermione was pretending not to watch while listening with her entire body.

"I didn't know I was supposed to ask," Harry said.

"You wanted to spend the day with me."

"Yes."

"That is generally when people ask."

"I kept thinking if I asked, it might sound as though I assumed—"

"That I wanted you to?"

"That it meant more than you wanted."

The anger left her face. What replaced it hurt more.

"I wanted you to ask," she said. "I didn't know how much clearer to be without arranging the whole thing myself."

Harry looked at the damp window. "That would've saved time."

"It would've made me cross."

"I was rather hoping to avoid that."

"You've found a complicated way of doing it."

Draconia tried not to smile.

"This is serious."

"I know."

He looked at her hand between them.

"I hated seeing you with him."

"That is not especially noble."

"It wasn't meant to be."

"Good."

Harry met her eyes.

"Can I walk with you for a bit? Before you meet the others."

She considered.

"I promised Daphne we'd meet at Honeydukes this afternoon. Blaise has other things to do too." She glanced towards the table. "One aunt has already seen us together and spent twenty minutes pretending she wasn't watching. I think we've done what his mother asked."

"Did it work?"

"She nearly injured herself attempting to look casual."

"Then could I walk you there, if Blaise doesn't mind?"

"You can ask him. I doubt he'll object."

Harry held out his hand. Draconia looked at it. Through the steamed glass, Harry could see students stopping outside the pub. He knew some of them would notice if they left together. A few had already noticed how long he had been standing close to her by the door. His palm felt damp; he resisted the urge to wipe it upon his trousers. Draconia looked at his offered hand, then up at him. She could make him wait when she wanted to. This time he suspected she was nervous too.

She placed her hand in his.

"You may ask me properly next time."

"How far in advance?"

"Before somebody else asks would be a good start."

"Very demanding."

"I have been patient."

Harry smiled. "I'll try to do more than stand there next time."

"You also spilt tea."

"I'd worked hard on that."

Draconia laughed, squeezing his hand once before they moved away from the door. They returned to the table holding hands. Blaise moved his chair without being asked, leaving the place beside Draconia for Harry. Hermione looked at their hands, then at Harry's face. Her smile took a moment to arrive, and he felt a small ache of gratitude when it did.

Ron inspected the two of them. "Was there a meeting?"

"We're arranging a walk," Harry said.

"Thrilling. Did you bring minutes?"

Draconia took a chip from Ron's plate. "Do you want us to?"

He moved the plate away. "Apparently not."

Blaise agreed to the change of plan and asked Draconia to bring him the peppermint humbugs he had forgotten to buy. They stayed until the food had gone, then Harry and Draconia walked the short way to Honeydukes. Her glove was warm inside his hand. They argued about which sweets would survive the return journey and bought a bag neither of them intended to share fairly. At the shop, Daphne called Draconia over. Harry let go only when she turned to say goodbye, still surprised that the afternoon could contain both an ordinary appointment and something he would remember long afterwards.

Harry and Ron walked to the castle together anyway. Draconia had returned to the friends she had promised to meet, and Hermione had accepted Neville's invitation for the entire day. Harry and Ron spent the last hour in Zonko's, where Ron found a joke quill he wanted to use on his brothers and Harry bought an entirely unnecessary packet of sweets because Draconia had taken custody of the first one.

She had said next time, however, and he was trying to allow himself to believe there would be one. Harry had called Sirius again before leaving the village and told him the group was heading back. He respected the girls' plans. He also spent much of the climb looking backwards. Ron did the same.

"Neville bought her a plant," he said.

"You noticed."

"It was on the table."

"So were chips. You noticed those too."

"I usually understand chips."

The path rose beneath them. Afternoon light had softened, turning the snow blue in the shadows. Students walked in groups ahead and behind, brass pins bright upon their coats. Ministry officers remained near the village gates, writing names into ledgers which would become evidence of something after they decided what. Ron kicked a piece of ice from the path.

"Do you like Hermione?"

Harry nearly slipped.

"What?"

"You heard me."

"Yes."

Ron's face changed.

Harry added, "She's my friend. Of course I like her."

Ron looked at him until Harry sighed.

"I used to think about what it might mean if she liked me another way. Then I got so worried about hurting her that I didn't ask what she wanted either."

"And now?"

Harry glanced towards the groups behind them. "I want to be with Draco. I still don't want Hermione to think she matters less. I haven't got particularly good at showing both things."

Ron considered that for several steps. "That sounds difficult."

"It's easier when people tell you what they're thinking. Unfortunately, that includes me."

"Do you?"

"I keep noticing who she's sitting with. It's becoming irritating."

They walked ten paces.

"We're terrible at this," Ron said.

"Draco says the problem is failure to ask questions."

"You asked me."

"It hasn't helped."

Ron laughed. Harry did too. Harry was still embarrassed by how much he had watched the other tables. Saying it aloud had made it easier to recognise that Hermione had done nothing wrong. Beside him, Ron stopped looking backwards long enough to offer another sweet.

"Neville is decent," Ron said reluctantly.

"Blaise too. Annoyingly."

"Would've been easier if they were awful."

"Much."

The castle waited above them. The gate came into view, with the inspection queue already curling along the wall. Ron reached into his pocket for the sweets and offered Harry one without looking at him. Harry took it.

"We could probably try asking questions before someone else does," he said.

"You first."

"I tried one today."

Ron looked backwards again, towards Hermione laughing at something Neville had said. "Then apparently I'm behind."

They joined the queue together, chewing sweets too sticky to allow any further dignified discussion.

The search station at Hogwarts confiscated every brass pin. Students placed them into grey Ministry boxes one by one. Nobody argued. The patrol had prepared for argument. It had not prepared for a line of children removing identical pieces of metal in complete silence while the reporter who had followed the incident in the village counted. She remained beyond the school boundary, her quill moving above a notebook balanced against the stone gatepost. McGonagall stood inside the gates overseeing the return; each grey box was labelled before it left the table.

When Neville reached the table, he surrendered the plaque after the officer pointed to it beneath his coat. The officer turned it over.

"What does this number refer to?"

Neville looked at the Quick-Quotes Quill beyond the gate.

"It's in the statement I gave in the village. You can ask the officer who took it."

"Answer the question."

"Am I required to?"

The officer looked tired.

"Move along."

Neville did. Harry reported the chase to McGonagall at the gates, as agreed. Pins filled the box at her feet. Hundreds of small pieces of brass lay tangled together, no longer shaped clearly enough to read. "Symbolic items intended to undermine school safety will not be returned," she announced. "Any replacement will result in detention."

Fred raised his hand.

"What if we replace them with different symbolic items?"

"Detention."

"What if the symbolism is accidental?"

"Detention."

George lifted one finger. "Could we have written guidance distinguishing an object from a symbolic item?"

"Detention."

"Excellent. We can compare notes."

"Commissioner, perhaps disciplinary decisions should wait until students are actually alleged to have violated a rule."

"Their intent is plain."

"Asking what a rule means is not a breach of it. You may speak to me about their conduct when there is conduct to discuss. And the plaque goes to Professor Flitwick, as the governors directed. It is not yours to destroy."

"They've let me keep this," he murmured.

Harry glanced down. Two bright threads remained caught in the cloth. "Very careless."

"Might have to leave it there. Mending's difficult."

They went upstairs while McGonagall continued asking what offence the twins had actually committed.

Hermione found him in the library on Sunday afternoon. She placed the Sensitive Fern beside his Defence essay. One leaf had darkened where Hermione's thumb had pressed it while she carried the pot from the common room. The mark was fading from black to deep green.

"Did you have a good time?" Harry asked.

Hermione sat opposite.

"Yes."

The answer still created a small ache. Harry allowed it to exist without making her manage it.

"Neville chose well."

"He did. We went to the herb stall and Scrivenshaft's. He'd remembered a book I mentioned before Christmas." She turned the pot so the marked leaf faced the light. "Then you and Ron ran through the middle of the village."

"That was Montague."

"You ran."

"Ron suggested it."

"Your defence weakens."

Harry looked at the fern.

"Was it a date?"

Hermione traced the fading print without touching it.

"I think so," she said. "Neither of us announced it, but he asked me to spend the day with him. I wanted to."

Harry nodded. The small ache remained, less sharp now that he could hear how pleased she was.

"He was nervous," she added. "So was I, actually. I kept explaining things he already knew."

"He probably liked hearing you."

Hermione looked up, surprised. "He said that."

"Draco would define it."

"Draco has been trying to get you to ask her out since Christmas. I wouldn't rely upon her patience with definitions."

"Sometimes. Mostly you've been looking at her as though she's put a particularly difficult question in front of you."

"I asked yesterday."

"For a walk to a sweet shop. A fine beginning."

"You're enjoying this."

"I'm allowed to enjoy some things, Harry."

He smiled, then watched her straighten a leaf which had caught beneath the edge of her sleeve. There had been evenings when he could not imagine talking to her about Draconia without making everything worse. This afternoon Hermione had brought her plant, asked about his essay and stayed to laugh at him. He did not assume that meant every difficult feeling had disappeared. He was glad she still wanted the chair opposite his.

"Ron thinks your hair looked positive," Harry said.

Hermione blinked. "What?"

"Different. He meant it positively. Apparently."

"Did he appoint you translator?"

"No. I thought you might prefer to know what he was trying to say."

Hermione smiled despite herself. Draconia arrived carrying three books and no visible brass pin. She placed one volume beside Hermione and another beside Harry. The third remained in her arms.

"You are both behind on Astronomy."

"Happy Valentine's Day to you too," Harry said.

"That was Wednesday. You're four days late, and this essay is due tomorrow. I suggest concentrating upon the part you can still improve."

She sat at the end of the table.

Hermione looked at her. "Did Blaise's aunt injure herself?"

"She walked into a chair while reporting our departure to Mrs Bulstrode."

"Then the outing succeeded."

"Entirely."

Draconia opened the third book. Hermione's gaze moved to Harry.

"And the walk to Honeydukes?"

"Acceptable," Draconia said.

Harry stared. "Acceptable?"

"You told me you weren't interested in the fudge, then took three pieces before we reached the door."

"I became interested."

"An encouraging development. You might eventually manage the same with invitations."

Hermione laughed. Draconia joined her. Harry opened the Astronomy book with great dignity. For ten minutes, the table felt like theirs again. Then Hermione placed a blank sheet of parchment between them.

"We need to replace the pins."

Draconia's attention sharpened.

"With another object?"

"With something people can read. Taking the pins hasn't made the questions go away."

She wrote a question at the top.

HOW LONG DID THE RULE LAST BEFORE SOMEONE NEEDED HELP?

Harry read it. The candlestick had become important because a falling object made the ban absurd. But the school contained hundreds of smaller moments no single witness knew: curfews delaying a frightened child, seized mirrors preventing a parent from being reached, inspections exposing medication, locked doors turning a safe corridor into a trap.

"Testimony," he said.

"Anonymous where necessary," Hermione replied. "Verified by two people when possible. Description first. Interpretation separately. Cedric's method."

Draconia took out a quill.

"Ask what happened when people did obey," she said. "There'll be children who followed the rule and got hurt anyway. We need to hear them too."

Hermione added the line. Harry looked between them. Hermione moved the parchment so Draconia could add her question. Neither waited for Harry to approve it. He watched their hands work around each other, one holding the page while the other wrote, and felt the familiar pleasure of making something together. The table held their essays, Hermione's new plant and a piece of wrapping paper from sweets Draconia had apparently decided to share after all.

Harry took a piece when she pushed the packet towards him. Her knee touched his beneath the table; Hermione asked him to pass the ink. He found he could enjoy the contact and listen to his friend without wondering whether one cancelled the other. He opened the bottle and placed it between their quills.

"Where do we start?"

By Thursday, the twenty-second of February, they had collected one hundred and three accounts. Writers passed them to agreed messengers or the adults helping the parents' association; nobody needed to know where the D.A. met to contribute. Hermione and Cedric brought the copies into the hidden room, keeping consent notes and identifying originals separately. Some were three sentences. Others filled pages. The room displayed them upon the walls with names concealed unless the writer had chosen otherwise. Each began with a time.

FOUR MINUTES AFTER THE OWL INSPECTION BEGAN, MY MOTHER'S LETTER ABOUT MY BROTHER'S ILLNESS WAS READ BY A STRANGER.

TWENTY-TWO MINUTES AFTER CURFEW, A FIRST-YEAR HID IN A BATHROOM BECAUSE SHE THOUGHT ASKING A PREFECT FOR HELP WOULD GET HER DETENTION.

SIX DAYS AFTER PRACTICAL DEFENCE WAS SUSPENDED, I STILL HAD NO APPROVED PLACE TO PRACTISE THE SHIELD THAT HAD FAILED ME DURING THE TRAIN ATTACK.

ELEVEN MINUTES AFTER SHIELD CHARMS WERE BANNED, NEVILLE LONGBOTTOM STOPPED A CANDLESTICK FROM STRIKING ME.

The Hufflepuff second-year had signed her name. Neville stood before the account for a long time. Harry joined him.

"I didn't know she remembered my surname."

"You saved her."

"It was a candlestick."

"Those can be vicious."

Neville smiled faintly.

"You know what I mean."

Harry did. Neville reached towards the page, then let his hand drop before touching it. Harry thought of how quickly the candlestick had fallen, and of the girl looking up afterwards. Neville had gone on talking about the rule. She had apparently gone on remembering the person who stood beside her when it broke.

"You could talk to her," Harry said.

"I have. About her homework."

"Then you're ahead of some of us."

Neville looked at him, understood, and laughed quietly. Cedric stood upon the central platform holding the collected pages.

"The Ministry can dispute Harry," he said. "It can call me grateful. It can accuse Lucius Malfoy of bargaining for immunity and Dumbledore of seeking power. It will have to explain these accounts one by one instead of answering all of us with the same claim about Harry."

"It will try," Susan said.

"Yes."

Cedric did not promise evidence made institutions honest.

"So we make the attempt visible."

Hermione had prepared copies for four outlets with help from the parents' association and the adults who had agreed to receive them. Each writer had seen the version of their account being used. Details which could identify somebody without permission had been removed, and the original pages would remain separate from the copies sent outside school. The publication would have four routes. One was wizarding. One belonged to a Muggle-born parents' association. One was an international student-safety circular Fleur contacted from France. The fourth was a single sheet which could be copied by any enchanted press.

Harry read the proposed heading.

ELEVEN MINUTES: WHAT HOGWARTS STUDENTS SAY SAFETY COSTS

The copies for Friday breakfast had an additional instruction at the bottom of the first page. They had agreed upon it after Ginny pointed out how easily a hundred pupils doing one thing could be called a disruption if everyone began at once without knowing why. The breakfast action would involve reading, sitting where they normally sat and leaving the people who did not wish to participate alone.

The instruction was printed in ordinary black ink.

TURN THE PAGE AT 8:11.

His name appeared nowhere upon the first page. At first, that hurt. The response embarrassed him. He had spent months hating the Ministry for placing him at the centre of every argument. Now a document mattered without him and some small, injured part mistook absence for rejection. He looked for his name again, then realised what he was doing. He had wanted other people to speak for themselves. Now that they were doing it, a small part of him was looking for reassurance that they still wanted him there.

Harry put down the page. Across the room, Ginny needed someone to hold a stack while she folded the next sheet. He went to help, relieved to discover a job which did not require him to become the reason for the whole report.

"Are you all right?" Ron asked.

Harry looked at the room. Hermione and Draconia reviewed accounts together. Neville spoke to the Hufflepuff girl near the wall. Pansy corrected a sentence which accidentally revealed its writer through family detail. Ginny and Luna folded printed sheets. Cedric prepared other people to answer questions. Nobody waited for Harry to save the meeting.

"Yes," he said.

It was true.

The report appeared Friday morning. Copies occupied every table in the Great Hall before the post arrived. Harry knew who had placed the copies on his own table; he had helped Ron unfold them before breakfast. Other volunteers had taken stacks to their Houses, each person setting out only what they had agreed to carry. Most students arrived to find a sheet beside their plate and a friend willing to explain it.

Umbridge entered through the staff doors, saw the heading and stopped. Students continued eating. She seized the nearest copy. A Hufflepuff took another from beneath her plate, where it had been waiting folded in half. A Slytherin unfolded one from inside his napkin. Several Ravenclaws had prepared a limited copying charm which supplied an extra sheet when the first was torn; when Umbridge cancelled one of those charms, the girl holding it simply opened a fresh copy from her book. Harry heard Ron make a small, delighted sound beside him.

"This publication is unauthorised," Umbridge announced.

The Hall quieted.

"Possession or distribution will result in immediate disciplinary action."

Neville looked at Harry. Harry did not stand. Neither did Cedric, Hermione or Draconia. At exactly eleven minutes past eight, students turned to the second page. Paper moved throughout the Hall in one soft, enormous sound. Umbridge stood beneath hundreds of candles with a report she could confiscate from any one pair of hands and not from all of them. Harry watched the realisation reach her.

She had spent the year trying to make him the centre because a centre could be isolated, assessed, discredited and removed. The D.A. had become a shape without one. Above the entrance doors, two candle flames bent in opposite directions. Eleven. Professor Flitwick studied his breakfast with suspicious concentration. Harry smiled. Ron turned the page slowly enough that Harry could hear the edge catch against his sleeve. Across the Hall, Draconia had her report open beside Daphne's. Neville leaned towards a younger boy who was struggling with one of the longer sentences and explained it quietly.

Remus called that evening. Harry answered from the unused classroom where they often made their evening calls. He and Hermione used their separate mirror pairs to contact two adults at Hawthorn. Hermione asked Narcissa to check who had answered Harry's call; Remus showed the sitting room, and Narcissa came from the study to confirm she had found him there. They asked fresh questions as well, making the second contact check rather than echo the first. The familiar voices and private phrases helped them begin; they did not treat anything Harry could remember as proof on its own. Draconia kept an eye upon the open doorway while they established who was answering. Remus's face appeared in Harry's glass from Hawthorn's sitting room.

He held a copy of the report.

"I am told you were not responsible for the candle flames."

"No evidence links Professor Flitwick to anything."

"I didn't mention Filius."

"Neither did I."

Remus smiled. Behind him, Sirius crossed the room carrying a letter. The envelope bore a French stamp.

"Another one?" Harry asked.

Sirius concealed it against his chest.

"We are discussing educational resistance."

"It looks educational."

"You've been encouraging him," Sirius told Remus.

"I had rather a lot of help."

Harry leant closer to the mirror.

"Is Marlene coming to Britain?"

Sirius left the visible portion of the room.

"Coward," Harry called after him.

"I hear you selected a very solemn jumper," Sirius replied from the hall.

"How does he know about that?"

Hermione became interested in her books. Draconia examined the ceiling.

"Betrayal," Harry said.

Remus's smile deepened.

"How was Hogsmeade?"

"I ran into a signpost, carried a crate through a chase and eventually managed a walk with Draco."

"In that order?"

"It was a busy day."

Remus's eyes moved briefly towards Draconia. "I'm glad there was a walk."

"Did my dad ever wait too long to ask my mum something?"

"Constantly."

"Sirius makes him sound confident."

"He could usually work out what to do on a broom. With your mother, he needed to hear what she wanted before he could do anything useful. He found that much more difficult."

Harry thought of the weeks between their conversation at Christmas and the Hogsmeade notice.

"What did he do?"

"Eventually? Asked. Before that, he became very skilled at appearing wherever your mother intended to be and claiming coincidence."

"That sounds familiar," Hermione said.

"It took nine days."

Harry glanced at Draconia.

"I can beat nine."

"A stirring ambition," she said.

Remus became serious.

"I have had years to discover how difficult simple things can be."

Sirius returned to the edge of the frame as Remus spoke. He set a hand upon the back of Remus's chair and looked at him for a moment before turning towards Harry. Remus leaned back beneath the hand, apparently without noticing he had done it. Harry wondered how many conversations they had begun and never quite finished, in all those years he knew mostly through photographs.

"All right," Harry said. "I'll ask."

Sirius leaned farther into view. "Is Ron still with you?"

"Upstairs," Harry said. "He'll send a search party if we're much longer."

"It's nine-thirty."

"An excellent time to leave me alone."

"You're writing back to Marlene."

"Goodnight, Harry."

The mirror darkened. Harry put it away, then looked at Draconia gathering the books from the table. He had promised to ask instead of spending another evening preparing a question which never reached her.

"Sunday?" he asked.

She stopped with one hand beneath her essay. "What about Sunday?"

Hermione looked up too. Harry could feel himself becoming warm under both pairs of eyes.

"Would you spend Sunday afternoon with me? We can walk by the lake if it isn't frozen solid. Somewhere inside if it is." He swallowed. "As a date. That's what I'm asking."

Draconia's expression softened. "Yes. Sunday afternoon."

Harry let out a breath. "I should have tried that earlier."

"You have asked now," she said. "We can work out the rest tomorrow."

Ron appeared at the door carrying the jumper Harry had left in the common room. "Have you all finished? Neville says if Trevor's here, you're to bring him back."

"We're finished," Hermione said, packing the remaining copies of the report. "Come on."

When they reached the place where the dungeon stairs separated from the route to Gryffindor, Draconia touched his hand before letting go. Harry watched her descend for a moment, then followed Ron and Hermione upstairs. He had asked the question. She had answered. Harry let himself think about Sunday as he climbed the stairs, and remembered to take the jumper when Ron pushed it into his hands.
