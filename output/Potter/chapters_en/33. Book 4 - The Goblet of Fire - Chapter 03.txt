3

Sirius opened the door and was immediately struck by a banner pole.

It caught him across the shoulder with a hollow crack, sending him backwards into the umbrella stand. Two brooms, a cricket bat and the pointed hat no one had yet admitted to owning scattered across the hall. Above them, the escaped paper birds wheeled out of the way, trailing ribbons through the sudden confusion.

"I said inside," Hermione announced from the porch.

Lucius Malfoy stood behind her holding the opposite end of the pole. His expression suggested that the collision had confirmed several private opinions.

"You said the banner belonged inside," he replied. "It is now inside."

"Not through Sirius."

"An imprecise instruction."

Sirius pushed the pole away. "Good morning to you too, Lucius."

"Technically, it is afternoon."

"That explains the warmth."

The porch had disappeared beneath people. Hermione stood nearest the door with a stack of wrapped parcels balanced against her chest, her hair escaping the plait Mrs Granger had attempted that morning and spreading around her face in a dark cloud. A narrow green ribbon had become tangled near one ear. Harry could not tell whether it belonged to the presents or had been put there deliberately.

Draconia waited one step behind her in pale summer robes, with fitted sleeves and a silver fastening at the throat. Her hair had been pulled into a loose braid that rested over one shoulder, considerably less severe than the styles she preferred at formal dinners. Harry had seen her without school robes before; he had never noticed the difference long enough to forget what he was going to say.

Her eyes moved over his muddy clothes.

"What happened to you?"

Harry glanced down. Grass stained one knee, a streak of blue icing crossed the front of his shirt, and Padfoot had left enough black fur on his trousers to suggest he was moulting directly through them.

"Sirius made breakfast."

Narcissa appeared behind her daughter with a covered silver tray.

"The pastries?"

Sirius attempted to close the door. Hagrid's hand caught it from outside.

"Don' shut it yet."

The half-giant bent beneath the porch roof, knocking the recently repaired beam with one shoulder. Another line of dust settled over Sirius's hair.

"Sorry," Hagrid said. "Bit narrow."

"Structurally adventurous," Harry reminded Sirius.

"I am evicting both of you."

Hagrid spotted Harry, and his entire bearded face brightened.

"There he is!"

Harry had just enough time to brace his feet before Hagrid crossed the threshold and pulled him into a hug. His boots left the floor. One of the paper birds became trapped against Hagrid's coat and continued flapping between them, determined to complete its assigned route despite the obstruction.

"Happy birthday, Harry!"

"Thanks," Harry said into the beard. "I think I need the ribs."

Hagrid put him down and examined him.

"Still skinny."

"Lucius says that whenever conversation slows."

Lucius entered beneath the banner pole. "Because the situation persists."

"He ate breakfast," Sirius said.

"Most of it," Remus corrected from the sitting-room archway.

Harry turned. "Whose side are you on?"

"Nutrition's."

Behind the Malfoys, Mr and Mrs Granger waited with several canvas bags and the resigned patience of people allowing a magical gathering to organise itself. Mrs Granger held a camera. Mr Granger carried a rectangular case, watching two paper birds attempt to braid ribbon around Lucius's walking stick.

"Is it safe to enter?" he asked.

"No," Harry said.

"Good. We brought plasters."

He stepped across the threshold, and the house expanded around them.

Harry felt the magic before he saw it: a shifting pressure beneath his feet, followed by the peculiar certainty that the far wall had become farther away. The narrow hall stretched by several feet. Sitting-room walls moved outwards without disturbing the shelves, windows widened, and the ceiling lifted enough for Hagrid to stand upright. His hair still brushed a paper bird whenever one passed too low, but at least the roof no longer appeared to be in immediate danger.

Sirius surveyed the altered hall.

"Who did that?"

Narcissa removed her coat. "I asked your wards to accommodate the guest list."

"You altered my house?"

"Temporarily."

"Without asking?"

"You approved every name at the boundary." She glanced towards the widened sitting room. "The hospitality lattice was already woven into the older wards. It can only respond while one of the key-holders is present."

Sirius's hand went briefly to the wand inside his jacket. Harry recognised the movement: the need to test an assurance before allowing it to become safety, even when it came from someone he trusted. The wards answered whatever silent question Sirius asked, and some of the suspicion left his shoulders.

"Your entrance hall attempted to kill me."

"That was Lucius."

"An understandable mistake," Lucius said.

Hermione pushed the presents into Harry's arms.

"Move."

"Hello to you too."

"The food is behind us."

Harry stepped into the sitting room. "There is food inside."

Narcissa heard him. "Not any longer."

Sirius glanced towards the kitchen, where the remains of breakfast waited beneath their inadequately concealing dish towel.

"You burnt something," Mrs Granger said.

"Experimental cooking."

"He ignored written instructions," Harry explained.

Narcissa's expression became glacial.

"Four minutes."

"The oven runs hot."

"The oven is charmed to maintain the temperature selected."

"Then the pastries were unusually fragile."

Draconia passed Harry on her way to the kitchen. "Mother has prosecuted people for less."

"I have not prosecuted anyone."

"Because Father's solicitor intervened."

Lucius closed his eyes.

Within minutes, the cottage surrendered to the party. Narcissa and Mrs Granger claimed the kitchen, where the silver tray opened into three dishes, two baskets and a cake that occupied so much of the table that Sirius's burnt pastries were exiled to the windowsill. Mr Granger assembled a Muggle folding table in the garden while Lucius watched the hinges with suspicion. Hagrid carried chairs outside two at a time and accidentally took part of the doorframe with him on his third journey. Remus repaired it before Sirius noticed.

Hermione gathered the escaped paper birds by reading the counter-charm from a page she had written herself. Beneath the banner, Draconia directed the ribbons into even curves. Every time Hermione folded one bird, another escaped from the pile and flew upstairs, undoing enough of her progress to make the task look personal.

Harry watched for nearly a minute before realising that no one expected him to help. The discovery left him stranded in the middle of the sitting room with a tower of parcels against his chest and no task capable of justifying the space he occupied.

At Privet Drive, celebrations had always come with work. Dudley opened presents and Harry collected the paper; guests ate and Harry carried plates. Aunt Petunia could turn the absence of a teaspoon into proof that he had ruined an afternoon. Remaining still while other people prepared something for him felt dangerously close to negligence, particularly when there were so many things he could see needed doing.

He shifted the gifts onto one arm and reached for a stack of dishes beside the kitchen arch. Narcissa removed them first.

"Those are clean."

"I know."

"Then allow them to remain so."

Harry changed direction, catching one end of the tablecloth as Mrs Granger passed. She took it gently from his hand.

"Birthday guests aren't required to dress the table."

"I live here."

The answer stopped them both. Harry felt the words settle in the busy kitchen, unexpectedly certain for something he had understood only that morning. Mrs Granger's smile became quieter.

"Then you can help Sirius clean it tomorrow."

"An outrageous promise to make on my behalf," Sirius called from the garden.

"You own soap," she replied.

Harry attempted the paper birds instead. Hermione redirected his wand before the counter-charm had properly formed.

"You don't know the sequence."

"I know several useful sequences."

"Most of yours end with something falling over."

Draconia looked down from the banner.

"Potter."

He glanced up.

"You are holding your own presents while attempting to reorganise a party already being organised by three women who dislike correction."

From the kitchen, Narcissa said, "Four."

Mrs Granger lifted one hand in agreement.

"Four women," Draconia amended. "Your odds are poor."

Harry looked down at the parcels. He had almost forgotten they were still in his arms.

"Where do these go?"

Hermione did not look up. "Sitting room."

"Where?"

"Anywhere."

He stared at her. She caught his expression and stopped folding the bird in her hands.

"Near the empty frame," she amended. "We're putting the gifts beneath it."

"Why?"

"Because that's where gifts go."

"According to which procedure?"

"Basic social custom."

Draconia lowered the left edge of the banner by an inch. "Granger has spent three weeks preparing for this question and appears disappointed by it."

"I thought Sirius would explain things."

All three turned towards the garden, where Sirius was arguing with Lucius about whether levitation counted as helping to assemble a table.

"Unwise," Draconia concluded.

Harry placed the gifts beneath the mantel. The empty silver frame reflected movement without containing it: Hermione following paper wings, Draconia adjusting ribbon, Hagrid passing the window with half a bench beneath one arm. Harry's own reflection crossed the blank surface and disappeared.

"Mrs Granger brought film," Hermione said behind him.

He turned. She held a captured bird between both hands, its parchment wings fluttering against her fingers.

"For the photograph," she added.

"Sirius mentioned."

"Do you mind?"

The question surprised him. "Why would I?"

"You've spent most of the morning being surprised by things you already suspected."

"I was being polite."

"You accused Sirius of panic."

"Politely."

Hermione smiled, making the ribbon in her hair move. Harry examined it again.

"You've got something."

Her hand went immediately to her cheek. "Where?"

"No, in your hair."

He reached before considering whether she might prefer to remove it herself. The ribbon had knotted around a curl; Harry caught one end and worked it loose, trying not to pull her hair. Hermione became very still. From this close, he could feel her watching him, and the knot suddenly seemed to require more concentration than it should have.

"Hold on," he said.

"I am."

"I meant the bird."

"Oh."

Her face warmed. Harry freed the ribbon and held it up at precisely the moment the bird escaped. It struck him beneath the chin, snatched the green strip from his fingers and flew towards the staircase. Draconia intercepted it with one hand.

"A formidable team," she said.

Harry stepped away from Hermione. "We weakened it."

Draconia's gaze moved between them. "Clearly."

Something in her voice made Hermione straighten.

"You could assist instead of criticising."

"I did. The bird is in my hand."

"You know what I mean."

"Rarely."

Harry glanced towards the garden. Sirius and Lucius had progressed from the folding table to an argument about chairs, to which Hagrid contributed by sitting on one. It collapsed.

"I'm going outside," Harry announced.

"Coward," Hermione said.

"Experienced."

Draconia released the bird. It circled once and landed on Harry's shoulder, the ribbon hanging from its beak.

"Traitor," Hermione told it.

Harry carried it into the garden.

The party settled beneath the apple trees. Narcissa had charmed a long cloth over the assembled tables, concealing the fact that none stood at the same height. Plates filled themselves from covered dishes, ice clinked inside glass jugs, and small candles floated beneath the branches, their flames almost invisible in the sunlight. The banner remained inside after Hermione won the argument.

Harry sat at the centre of one side, with Sirius beside him and Hermione opposite. Draconia occupied the next chair, close enough for the edge of her sleeve to brush Hermione's whenever either reached for the bread. The seating had been planned. Harry knew because Hermione glanced at a folded diagram beneath her plate before sitting down.

"You made a chart."

She slid it farther out of sight. "There were considerations."

"Such as?"

"Hagrid needed the end."

Hagrid sat on a reinforced bench that Remus had conjured only after testing it with three boulders. It held firm, though Harry thought the wood made a small, protesting sound whenever Hagrid leaned forwards.

"Mr and Mrs Malfoy should not be beside Sirius," Hermione continued.

Sirius leaned around Harry. "Why?"

"You know why."

"I object to being managed by a fourteen-year-old."

"Then stop requiring it."

From the far end, Lucius lifted his glass to Hermione. "A lesson I have found broadly useful."

Sirius muttered something about betrayal.

"My parents needed to sit near someone familiar with Muggle conversation," Hermione said.

Mr Granger was explaining football to Hagrid, who nodded earnestly while holding a drumstick large enough to confuse the subject.

"And where did you put Remus?" Harry asked.

Hermione looked towards the far end of the table. Remus sat between Narcissa and Mrs Granger, both of whom had noticed the tiredness around his eyes. Two different servings of potatoes occupied his plate.

"Somewhere he would eat," she said.

Harry laughed, and the sound travelled easily through the garden.

For the first half-hour, conversation crossed over itself. Hagrid wanted to hear everything about the cottage and interrupted his own questions with stories about repairs at Hogwarts. Mr Granger asked Sirius about the motorbike. Lucius objected to its invisibility charms on legal grounds until Sirius pointed out three modifications hidden in the Malfoy car. Across the table, Mrs Granger and Narcissa discussed their daughters' reading habits with the solemnity of a medical conference.

"Hermione has begun stacking books on the floor," Mrs Granger said.

"A structural failure," Narcissa replied.

"The shelves are full."

"Then the room requires more shelves."

"That is what she says."

Hermione leaned towards Harry. "It does."

"How many books do you own?"

"That isn't relevant."

"Enough to damage foundations?" Draconia asked.

"Books do not damage foundations."

"Everything damages foundations in sufficient quantity."

Harry glanced towards the cottage roof. "Sirius has tested that."

Lunch contained no moment in which Harry was required to perform gratitude, and that made him more grateful. People spoke to him, around him and sometimes over him. Hagrid put a second portion on his plate without asking; Narcissa removed it when she saw he had not finished the first. Sirius stole a roast potato from Lucius and claimed territorial law. Remus noticed the movement and exchanged Lucius's fork for a clean one before retaliation became possible.

Hermione had remembered that Harry disliked raw onion. Draconia passed him the bread before he asked. Neither gesture announced itself, but Harry noticed both, just as he noticed the absence of anyone counting what he ate or waiting for the right response to what he had been given.

He noticed other things too. Hermione laughed more freely when her parents were near, her hands moving whenever she explained something, drawing shapes above the table. Draconia watched people before joining their laughter, as though checking whether a joke carried any hidden cost. Around Narcissa, some of the calculation left her posture. She leaned back, allowing sunlight to reach her face, and the severe polish she carried through Hogwarts softened into something younger.

Harry had known them for three years, much of that time spent learning how they looked in danger. Safety altered them in ways he had not studied. His attention remained on Draconia a moment too long, and she caught him staring.

"What?"

Harry looked down at his plate. "Nothing."

"You have been watching me for thirty seconds."

Hermione turned. "Why?"

"I wasn't."

"He was," Draconia said.

"I was looking at the bread."

Hermione examined the basket. "For thirty seconds?"

"It's interesting bread."

Draconia selected a slice and put it on his plate. "There. You may observe it privately."

Harry felt his face heat. Hermione's eyes narrowed with curiosity, which was worse than suspicion. She looked from him to Draconia, then down at her own hands.

"Do either of you want more lemonade?"

"Yes," Harry said too quickly.

Draconia lifted her glass without comment. As Hermione poured, the jug tapped the rim of Harry's glass once. The paper bird from earlier landed in the tree above them and dropped the green ribbon into his drink. He fished it out, holding the dripping strip between two fingers.

"Saved."

Hermione began laughing. Draconia held out a hand.

"I will dispose of the evidence."

"It suits you," Harry told Hermione.

Her laughter stopped. He heard the words only after they existed, too late to make them sound like something he had considered saying. The table continued around them, mercifully unaware.

Hermione touched her hair. "The ribbon?"

"Yes."

"Oh."

"It did," Draconia said.

Hermione turned towards her. Draconia took the ribbon from Harry and tied it loosely around Hermione's plait, her fingers moving with the confidence of someone accustomed to maids, formal dressing and the exact placement of every visible detail.

"There."

Hermione's cheeks had coloured. Harry stared at the interesting bread.

"You are both behaving strangely," Draconia observed.

"Thank you," Harry replied.

"It was not praise."

Across the table, Sirius concealed a smile behind his glass. Harry kicked him beneath it, and Sirius nearly inhaled his drink. That ended the moment with a considerable improvement in the distribution of embarrassment.

The cake appeared after lunch, enormous and dark with chocolate. Fourteen narrow candles stood around a sugar motorbike, its sidecar occupied by a figure whose glasses took up most of his face. Icing owls flew around the cake's sides, settling only when Narcissa placed it before Harry.

He examined the little motorbike.

"Narcissa made this?"

"The Manor kitchens," Draconia said.

Narcissa's attention drifted towards the tree line. "With instructions."

"Mother drew the motorbike three times."

"The first two lacked accuracy."

"The figure is taller than Harry," Hermione said.

"The kitchens were instructed to be aspirational," Lucius replied.

Fourteen flames rose at a gesture from Narcissa, and every face turned towards Harry. Nothing moved and no danger appeared, yet the garden changed. Attention settled over him from every direction, warm and expectant, until his chest began to tighten.

Birthdays at Privet Drive had belonged to Dudley. They involved lists, noise, carefully counted presents and the certainty that any unhappiness would be corrected by giving him more. Harry's own birthday passed with a muttered word from Petunia in good years and nothing in most. No one had ever gathered to watch him be glad.

"Speech," Hagrid suggested.

Harry's mind emptied.

"Do not make him give a speech," Hermione said immediately.

"He has to make a wish," Mrs Granger added.

The flames blurred. There were far fewer people than at Dudley's parties: ten of them around the table, unless the paper birds counted. Harry knew every face, and none wanted anything from him except to see the candles go out. That should have made the expectation easy. Instead, he found himself searching for words nobody had actually asked him to say.

Sirius shifted beside him and rested his hand palm-up on the bench between them, close enough to take and far enough to refuse. Harry looked at it, then across the table. Hermione had lowered her eyes, giving him somewhere not to be watched. Draconia's face was calm, but one hand remained around the green ribbon at Hermione's shoulder, as though neither girl had noticed it was still there.

Remus began explaining something to Hagrid in a deliberate murmur. Mrs Granger adjusted the camera, and Narcissa straightened a candle that had begun to lean. Their attention broke apart into small, ordinary movements. Harry could breathe again.

He placed his hand briefly against Sirius's.

"All right," he said.

Everyone quietened without snapping round towards him. Harry leaned over the cake. He had already made a wish at midnight; this time, he did not ask for anything new. He wished to keep recognising what had already arrived.

The candles went out together. Applause startled birds from the orchard, and Hagrid cheered loudly enough to shake leaves from the nearest tree. Sirius's hand closed once around Harry's before letting go. Hermione looked up, Draconia smiled, and Harry stayed in his seat while the moment passed through him.

It did not hurt. Not exactly. There was too much in it to feel simple, but nobody required him to decide what to call it.

Narcissa cut the cake before Hagrid could volunteer. Sirius received the first piece because she wanted him to understand what correctly heated food tasted like. Harry's slice contained an icing owl, which Hermione divided with a knife when its wings made the piece unstable. Draconia stole the sugar glasses from the motorbike figure and wore them on the end of her nose until Lucius noticed.

He looked at his daughter. She looked back through green sugar lenses.

"Distinguished," Harry said.

"Alarming," Hermione corrected.

The words belonged to a soot-covered train ride a year earlier. All three began laughing before anyone else understood why.

Presents moved inside when a brief summer shower began. Rain struck the leaves in a sudden rush, the candles extinguished themselves, and everyone collected plates, cushions and glasses while Sirius attempted to put a weather charm above the tables. His first spell turned the rain purple. Lucius repaired it.

"I was adjusting the spectrum," Sirius said.

"You were colouring water."

"Aesthetic choice."

"An incompetent one."

They entered the expanded sitting room beneath a cloud of violet droplets. Hagrid occupied the floor beside the fireplace because no furniture had survived his earlier testing. Mr and Mrs Granger settled on the green sofa, while Narcissa and Lucius took the faded red one, leaving enough distance between themselves and Sirius's chair to preserve diplomatic relations.

Harry sat on the rug before the mantel. From there, the stack of parcels seemed to have multiplied.

"You didn't have to bring all this," he said.

Everyone answered at once. The general meaning appeared to be that no one had asked him.

Hermione knelt beside the gifts with a notebook.

"What's that?"

"A list."

"Of my presents?"

"For thank-you letters."

"Everyone is in the room."

"Letters remain appropriate."

Draconia sat on his other side. "It also prevents duplicate gifts."

"Did you coordinate?"

"Of course."

Harry looked around the adults. "Was anyone allowed to choose their own?"

Hagrid raised one hand. Hermione consulted the notebook.

"We could not contact him."

"Knew where ter find somethin' special," Hagrid said.

He dragged a large parcel from behind the nearest chair, its irregular shape nearly as tall as Harry's torso. Harry pulled away the brown paper to reveal a wooden chest with iron hinges and a curved lid. A stag had been carved into one side, a black dog into the other. Between them, smaller shapes suggested an otter, a serpent and a wolf. The work was rough in places, but every animal possessed enough movement to seem ready to leave the wood.

Harry ran his fingers over the stag's antlers and followed the carving around the corners. The otter stood with its front paws braced against a stream cut into the grain, head lifted as if arguing with the current. The serpent curved along the base, forming a protective border beneath the stag. On the rear panel, the wolf stood beyond a line of trees, facing towards the others. The separate panels had given each animal its own space without leaving any of them alone.

"You made this?"

"Had some help with the hinges," Hagrid said. "Thought yeh might need somewhere fer things what matter."

Harry opened the lid. Soft blue fabric lined the interior, and protective runes followed its edge, clumsy but strong.

"Fireproof," Hagrid said proudly. "Mostly waterproof. Biting-proof too, though Fang only tested the corner."

The corner contained a deep tooth mark. Harry smiled.

"It's brilliant."

Sirius crouched beside the chest and examined the dog. "Padfoot appears unusually dignified."

"Had ter improve him a bit," Hagrid said.

"Artistic dishonesty."

"Couldn' make the wood look that scruffy."

Remus studied the wolf without touching it. Harry saw the moment he understood the trees and the direction of its gaze.

"Thank you, Hagrid," he said.

Hagrid's eyes disappeared farther into his beard. "S'for Harry."

"I know."

Hagrid's beard lifted with his smile.

"Knew yeh'd like it."

The Grangers gave Harry a compact Muggle camera, several rolls of film and a small book explaining how to develop photographs without magic. He turned the camera over in his hands, finding its weight reassuring after the delicate parchment birds and enchanted ribbons.

"Your world makes pictures move," Mr Granger said, "but ordinary ones stay where you put them. Sometimes that has advantages."

Harry understood. Moving photographs continued after the people inside them were gone: his parents laughed, turned and repeated their moments without knowing they were dead. An ordinary photograph would hold exactly one instant, unchanged by what happened afterwards.

"Thank you," he said.

Mrs Granger handed him another small package, which contained a leather album with blank black pages.

"For the things you take yourself."

Hermione watched him turn the pages.

"The paper won't react badly to magical subjects," she said. "We tested it."

"On what?"

"Crookshanks."

"He objected," Mr Granger added.

Hermione's own gift was wrapped in blue paper. Harry opened it to find a heavy book bound in soft brown leather, with no title on the cover. Inside, divided tabs marked sections labelled Spells, Creatures, Places, People and Questions. The first page held her handwriting.

For everything you notice before the rest of us.

Please write some of it down so we can notice too.

Harry met her eyes.

"It isn't homework," she said.

"You divided it into subjects."

"Organisation does not make something homework."

"It usually does."

"You don't have to use it."

The sudden uncertainty in her voice made him close the book more carefully, keeping one finger between the pages she had written on.

"I will."

Hermione searched his face. "Really?"

"I'll write about interesting bread."

She shoved his shoulder, and the uncertainty disappeared into a smile.

Draconia's gift was smaller, wrapped in dark green paper and sealed with silver wax that bore no family crest. Harry broke it with his thumb. Inside lay a wand holster made from black dragon hide, slim enough to fit beneath a sleeve. Silver thread formed protective runes along the inner edge, and two small clasps allowed it to be worn on either forearm.

"Mother approved the enchantments," Draconia said as he lifted it. "Father tested the release."

Lucius raised one hand. A faint red mark crossed his knuckles.

"Repeatedly."

"It responds only to you," Draconia continued. "No one can summon the wand from it unless you permit them."

Harry thought of his wand beneath the pillow, within reach before his eyes opened. He looked more closely at the design.

"You were drawing this," he said.

Her expression shifted. "This summer?"

"At Hogwarts. In your notebook."

During the last weeks of term, Draconia had sketched designs in the margins of her Potions notes whenever Harry spoke about the Dursleys. He had assumed they were cauldron clamps or pieces of jewellery, and had never thought to ask.

"The earlier versions were inadequate."

Harry fastened the holster around his right forearm. The leather adjusted at once, fitting without pinching, and he slid his wand inside. It vanished beneath his sleeve. When he flexed his hand, the wand dropped silently into his palm.

The movement was effortless. There would be no fumbling beneath a pillow, no reaching blindly across a bedside table while someone stood between him and the door. Draconia had taken a fear he scarcely knew how to explain and spent months turning it into leather, silver thread and a release charm made for his hand alone.

"Useful," Sirius admitted.

Draconia attempted indifference. "Obviously."

Harry returned the wand to the holster.

"Thank you."

Her eyes met his. For a second, the rest of the sitting room receded, and he noticed the red mark the clasp had left across her thumb. Lucius had not been the only person to test it repeatedly.

"You are welcome," she said.

Hermione cleared her throat. Harry looked towards her, finding her occupied with the album's blank pages. Something brushed the back of his mind, an awareness not yet organised enough to become understanding. Then Hagrid asked whether the holster could conceal an umbrella, and the moment scattered.

Narcissa and Lucius gave him clothes: Muggle shirts, trousers, a dark coat and two jumpers fitted to measurements Narcissa refused to explain possessing. Every item was plain enough to wear without attracting attention and made well enough that Harry hesitated to touch it with icing on his fingers.

"These are too much."

"They are clothing," Lucius said.

"There's an entire trunk."

"A very small trunk."

It reached Harry's waist.

"You didn't have to."

Narcissa's expression gentled. "We know."

The same answer appeared everywhere that day, offered without impatience each time he failed to understand it. Harry studied the dark blue jumper resting across his knees, running one finger along its neatly finished cuff.

"Thank you."

Lucius nodded once. Narcissa leaned forwards and corrected the collar of Harry's stained shirt, though nothing about it could be made presentable.

"Eat more," Lucius added.

Harry laughed. "There it is."

Remus's parcel contained a set of old defence texts. Unlike Hermione's books, these were full of handwriting. Notes crowded the margins in four inks, diagrams had been corrected, and arguments continued between pages. One writer favoured arrows and impatient underlining; another added sources in small, even letters. A third drew antlers whenever the text became dull.

Harry recognised the hands.

"These were yours."

"Ours," Remus corrected.

Sirius leaned over his shoulder. "I thought those were lost."

"You believed everything was lost if it remained out of sight for more than a week."

Harry turned a page. Beside a description of shield charms, James had written:

Works better if Padfoot stops standing behind me making faces.

Below it, Sirius had replied:

Works perfectly. Prongs lacks concentration.

Remus's smaller handwriting added:

Both statements regrettably true.

Peter's note appeared farther down. Harry's thumb stopped beside it. The cheerful ink looked obscene among the familiar names, unchanged by everything the hand that had written it would later do. Remus saw where his attention had caught.

"We can remove his writing," he said quietly.

Harry considered the page. Peter had existed. He had laughed with them, learnt beside them and chosen betrayal later. Removing his handwriting would not change any part of that, however much Harry wanted the others to have known sooner.

"No," he said. "Leave it."

Sirius rested a hand on his shoulder. No one hurried him towards the next parcel, and Harry closed the book only when he was ready.

"Thank you."

Remus smiled. Sirius had not placed a gift beneath the mantel. Harry noticed, then pretended not to.

The last parcel was not wrapped. Lucius drew a long cream envelope from the inner pocket of his robes, its seal showing two crossed broomsticks beneath an unfamiliar crest.

"This is not a birthday gift," he said.

"You already brought a trunk," Harry replied. "That was becoming concerning."

"An invitation is not a possession."

Harry took the envelope. The parchment was heavier than any letter required; when he broke the seal, gold writing spread across it in three languages before settling into English.

THE INTERNATIONAL QUIDDITCH ASSOCIATION

requests the pleasure of

Harry James Potter

at the Four Hundred and Twenty-Second Quidditch World Cup Final

Ireland v Bulgaria

Harry forgot the room.

"The World Cup?"

Draconia smiled. "The final. In Britain."

"When?"

"Twenty-second August."

He read the invitation again. The words remained. He had followed the qualifying matches in the Daily Prophet, cutting the standings from the sports pages Sirius sent to Privet Drive. Ireland had defeated Peru in a match decided by three late goals. Bulgaria had reached the final almost entirely because Viktor Krum caught the Snitch before their opponents built a lead large enough to survive it.

"Krum is playing," Harry said.

Hermione leaned over his shoulder. "He's their Seeker?"

Harry looked at her in disbelief. "He's the Seeker."

"That is what I said."

"He's eighteen."

"Many people are."

Draconia sighed. "Granger has spent three years beside a Quidditch player and retained nothing."

"I have retained the rules."

"Name three Bulgarian players."

"Why?"

"Because Potter will otherwise begin explaining Krum's dive against Luxembourg."

Harry folded the invitation. "It was a good dive."

"You described it for twenty minutes," Hermione said.

"You asked."

"I asked why you had fallen asleep over the newspaper."

Sirius took the invitation from Harry, reading the gold lettering once. His face gave nothing away. Harry's excitement shifted, but did not disappear.

"Where are the seats?"

"The Minister's box," Lucius said.

"No."

The answer came so quickly that Harry thought he had misunderstood. Lucius did not.

"They are among the most heavily protected seats in the stadium."

"They are also surrounded by Ministry officials whose competence I have experienced personally."

"An experience which produced substantial institutional embarrassment. They are unlikely to misplace you again."

Sirius handed the invitation back.

"Harry has dreamt twice about a former Death Eater standing beside Voldemort."

The name drew silence across the sitting room. Hagrid's smile faded, Mr Granger looked towards Hermione, and Narcissa slowly closed her hand around her husband's wrist. Harry had not realised that the Grangers and Hagrid knew only part of what had happened. Hermione moved nearer. Draconia was already beside him.

"The final is three weeks away," Lucius said. "We do not cancel his life because Nott owns a ring."

"You recognised it."

"I recognised a description provided by a fourteen-year-old through a dream."

Harry turned on him. "Draconia recognised it."

Lucius looked at his daughter. Something passed between them before he answered.

"The description is consistent with Nott's signet."

"Consistent," Sirius repeated. "And Nott disappeared after Pettigrew began naming associates."

"Which makes him one of several people the Ministry should locate."

"They haven't."

"Nor had they located you."

Sirius stood, and Remus put a hand against his arm.

"Not today."

The words were quiet, but Sirius heard them. He looked at Harry, and Harry became aware that the room had returned to watching him, though for a different reason now. He folded the invitation along its original line.

"Who else is going?"

"Narcissa, Draconia and I," Lucius answered. "The Grangers have also been invited."

Hermione turned towards her parents. Mrs Granger smiled.

"We thought we should see one match after listening to you misunderstand the sport for three years."

"I do not misunderstand it."

"You called the Quaffle a ball with no personality."

"It is."

Mr Granger lifted one shoulder. "Your mother wants to see flying mascots."

"International magical culture," Mrs Granger corrected.

"Flying international culture."

Harry looked at Sirius.

"Remus?" he asked.

"Invited," Lucius said.

Remus appeared surprised. "When?"

"Now."

"That is not generally how invitations work."

"You have been invited by a Malfoy," Draconia said. "It is primarily an announcement."

Harry's hope rose carefully. Everyone would be there: the trio, Sirius and Remus, adults who knew the danger rather than denying it. Thousands of people, Ministry protections and a sky full of Quidditch. Fear objected, bringing the red eyes in the drawing room back behind the thought. Harry refused to let them become the only image.

"I want to go," he said.

Sirius looked at him. "I know."

"Do you?"

"You have been circling every mention of the Cup in the newspaper."

"Then why say no?"

Sirius's jaw tightened. "Because wanting something does not make it safe."

"Not wanting it doesn't either."

The answer sounded more adult than Harry felt. Sirius glanced towards Remus, who offered no rescue.

"We have three weeks," Harry continued. "Dumbledore is coming tomorrow. We can ask about the dreams, the wards and Nott. If it isn't safe then, we don't go."

Draconia turned to him. "We?"

"If it isn't safe for me, it might not be safe for any of us."

"Nott is not interested in Granger."

"He planted the diary in your room."

Harry understood the bluntness of it too late. Draconia's face closed, and Narcissa became very still.

"I'm not going if the way to protect me puts either of you in danger," he added, lowering his voice.

Hermione's fingers tightened around the album. Draconia looked at the invitation in his hand.

"That is not your decision alone."

"No," Harry agreed. "It's ours."

The word remained between them. Something in Draconia's posture eased, and Hermione sat straighter.

"Then we gather the information first."

"You already have a notebook, don't you?" Harry asked.

She opened her canvas bag. Sirius groaned.

"Not during the party."

Hermione withdrew her hand. "Fine. After."

Lucius regarded Harry with an expression he could not read.

"I will provide the security arrangements," he said.

"To all three of us," Hermione replied.

Lucius's eyebrow rose.

"And Sirius," she added.

"Granger," Draconia murmured, "you are negotiating with the man offering the tickets."

"That does not make him correct."

Lucius glanced at Mr Granger. "She does that to you?"

"Constantly."

"How do you stop it?"

"Why would I?"

Mr Granger took a piece of cake. Lucius appeared disturbed by the answer, while Sirius sat down again, letting Remus's hand fall from his arm.

"We discuss it tomorrow," Sirius said.

It was not permission, but it was no longer refusal. Harry smiled.

"Tomorrow."

The adults claimed the study once the presents were ready to be moved upstairs. They called it a discussion about security, a phrase Harry recognised as adult language for a conversation children would improve by not hearing. Hagrid remained in the sitting room because the study had not expanded enough to contain him, and offered to tell the trio stories about Sirius at school.

"He didn't know me at school," Sirius called from the hall.

"Heard plenty."

"Defamation."

Draconia collected the new clothes. "We should take these upstairs before Hagrid reveals anything useful."

Hermione carried the books, while Harry gathered the wand holster, the camera and the blank journal. They climbed amid paper birds that had abandoned the decorations and begun nesting along the banister. The fifth door stood open now, revealing the small room under the eaves, emptied of wrapping paper. A narrow bed occupied one wall; the other held a writing table and shelves. Sloping windows overlooked the front garden, and two trunks sat at the foot of the bed.

Hermione saw Harry looking.

"Emergency clothes," she said.

"Both of yours?"

"Mother thought it was sensible."

Draconia draped the new shirts over one arm. "My trunk contains nothing of an emergency nature."

"It has six pairs of shoes," Hermione said.

"Exactly."

Harry's gaze moved from the trunks to the single bed.

"Who sleeps here?"

"Guests," Draconia said.

"Which guest?"

"Whichever requires it."

Hermione adjusted the stack of books. "Sirius said another bed can be added."

"We are not sharing," Draconia said.

"I didn't suggest it."

"Your expression did."

Harry stepped away from the doorway. "I'm not involved."

"You created the question," Hermione replied.

"I asked who sleeps in the room."

"A dangerous inquiry," Draconia said. "You should know better."

They continued into Harry's room. Hermione entered first and stopped beside the desk.

"He turned it."

Harry put the camera down. "What?"

"The desk. It used to face the wall."

Draconia went directly to the windows.

"The locks are holding."

"You altered them."

"They were decorative."

"They locked."

"With sufficient encouragement, so does ribbon."

Harry looked at Hermione. "That doesn't make sense."

"She said the same thing yesterday."

Draconia tested the left window and found it satisfactory. Hermione ran her fingers across the desk.

"Do you like it?"

"Yes."

"Actually?"

"I changed the lamp."

Her face brightened. "I hated that lamp."

"Everyone did except Sirius."

"He bought it."

"He blamed Remus."

They placed the gifts around the room, with a little help getting the larger ones upstairs. Hagrid's chest fitted beneath the window, the defence books joined the first shelf, and Hermione's journal went on the desk. The clothes entered the wardrobe under the direction of both girls, who disagreed about whether colour or purpose should determine their arrangement.

Harry sat on the bed and watched them organise his belongings.

"You know I'm going to ruin that within two days."

"Then we will know how long you can be trusted," Draconia said.

"With shirts?"

"Responsibility begins somewhere."

Hermione held up the dark blue jumper Narcissa had chosen.

"Try this."

"It's July."

"We need to know whether it fits."

"Narcissa already knows."

"Humour us," Draconia said.

Harry looked between them. "This is revenge for the bread."

"What bread?" Hermione asked too quickly.

He took the jumper. The girls turned away while he removed his stained shirt and pulled it over his head. The wool was lighter than it appeared, settling close across his shoulders instead of hanging from them. For a moment, he found himself looking down at the sleeves, surprised by where they ended.

"Done."

They turned. Hermione's attention travelled from the sleeves to his face.

"It suits you."

Draconia said nothing.

Harry looked at her. "Does that mean it's terrible?"

"No."

"Then what?"

Her eyes appeared darker in the room's blue light.

"The colour makes your eyes look—"

She stopped. Hermione waited, and Harry waited too.

"Look what?" he asked.

Draconia recovered. "Greener. Less tired."

"That was nearly two nice things."

"I shall be more careful."

Hermione turned towards the wardrobe to hide a smile. Harry became conscious of the jumper, the room and both girls standing close enough to examine him. The wool suddenly felt warmer than it had a moment before.

"It's warm," he said.

He pulled it off immediately, taking his glasses with it. The world blurred. Someone caught the frames before they reached the floor, while another hand tugged the jumper free of his head. Harry emerged with his hair standing in every direction, Hermione holding his glasses and Draconia holding the jumper. Both stared at him.

"What?"

Hermione put the glasses back on his face. "Nothing."

Draconia folded the jumper. "Your hair appears to have declared independence."

"It does that."

She stepped closer and pressed one section down with her palm. It rose the instant she removed her hand. Hermione tried from the other side, and Harry stood between them while four hands attempted a task Lily Potter had apparently lost years of her life to.

"This is humiliating."

"Hold still," Hermione said.

"You're making it worse."

"That seemed impossible," Draconia replied.

They stopped. Harry's hair had doubled in volume. He looked in the wardrobe mirror.

"Excellent."

Hermione began laughing. Draconia lasted two seconds longer. Harry took a clean shirt from the wardrobe, pulled it on and picked up the camera before either girl recovered.

"Stay there."

"Harry, no."

He pointed it at them. Neither had expected him to use it quite so quickly. Hermione lunged to cover the lens while Draconia reached for the button, but Harry pressed it first. The flash filled the room, and all three shouted.

Footsteps sounded in the passage. Hagrid's face appeared around the door.

"Everythin' all right?"

Harry lowered the camera, bright spots floating across his sight.

"Fine."

Hermione still had one hand over the lens, Draconia gripped Harry's wrist, and his hair resembled something struck by lightning independently of his scar. Hagrid surveyed them.

"Right."

He left. The girls released Harry, though Draconia continued looking at the camera with considerable suspicion.

"I was not prepared."

"That's why it will be good."

Hermione took the camera. "You may have covered the flash with your finger."

"I pressed the button."

"That isn't the whole process."

"The book is downstairs."

"I read it before wrapping the gift."

Draconia looked between them. "Of course you did."

Hermione explained focus, shutter speed and light. Harry pretended not to understand until she threatened to take the camera away, after which he became a remarkably attentive pupil. They used nearly an entire roll of film.

The first careful photograph showed Hermione seated at the desk and Draconia beside the window, both composed enough to resemble a school portrait. The second caught Draconia blinking. In the third, Hermione attempted not to laugh while Harry balanced Hagrid's small hippogriff carving on his head. By the sixth, no one arranged anything.

Harry photographed Hermione lying backwards across the bed to capture the paper bird perched on the curtain rail. Draconia took a picture of Harry trying to remove it after it became tangled in his hair. Hermione caught Draconia laughing at both of them, her head tipped back and her braid coming loose.

The final frame belonged to all three. Harry put the camera on the desk and charmed the shutter with his wand. They sat on the floor beneath the windows, shoulders pressed together to fit.

"It will take ten seconds," Hermione said.

"You said five," Draconia replied.

"I changed the charm."

"When?"

"Just now."

The camera flashed during the argument. For an instant, Harry saw them as the lens had: Hermione turned towards Draconia, Draconia looking back, himself between them laughing at both. Then the moment became film waiting in darkness. He hoped it had worked.

Hermione showed him how to replace the finished roll without exposing it to the light. Afterwards, she sat on the rug beside the bed, examining the film counter, while Draconia returned to the window. Harry put the camera on the desk. Below them, adult voices passed through the floor in indistinct rises and falls.

The laughter faded before the question did.

"They're discussing Nott," Draconia said.

Harry joined her near the glass. "Can you hear them?"

"No."

"Then how do you know?"

"Father has been in that room for forty minutes without insulting Black loudly enough to reach us. The subject is serious."

Hermione looked up.

"Do you think it was him?"

Draconia watched rainwater gather along the guttering. "The signet matches."

"That isn't what I asked," Hermione said.

"It is the only answer I possess."

Control had returned to Draconia's voice, smoothing away everything that had escaped during the photographs. Harry leaned against the sill.

"You don't have to know what your father knows."

Her jaw tightened. "I am aware."

"Are you?"

She turned towards him. Harry saw the edge of an argument only after he had spoken. Hermione rose from the rug.

"He means you don't have to solve Nott because the Malfoys knew him."

"I understood."

"You sounded angry."

"I am angry."

"At Harry?"

"No."

Harry's attention flicked towards the door. Hermione noticed.

"Don't leave."

"I wasn't."

"You looked at the door."

"Doors attract attention. It's their purpose."

Neither girl accepted the explanation. Harry sighed, allowing his gaze to return to the room.

"Fine. I was checking."

Draconia's expression softened by a fraction.

"You may remain. I am capable of anger without hexing anyone."

"A comforting development."

She ignored him and sat on the edge of the bed, arranging her hands on the quilt before she spoke.

"Nott entered my home. He placed an object in my room. He knew what it might do even if he did not know precisely what it contained."

Hermione lowered herself beside her.

"Your father didn't."

"No."

"Neither did you."

"I wrote in it."

The words were quiet enough for the rain almost to take them. Harry moved away from the window.

"So would I."

Draconia looked at him.

"A book writes back," Harry continued. "It knows things about Hogwarts. It listens when you're angry and tells you everyone else is wrong. I would have filled it before breakfast."

"That is not reassuring."

"It wasn't meant to be."

Hermione touched Draconia's hand.

"You were twelve."

"So was Harry when he found me."

The answer lacked its usual edge. Draconia's voice had thinned, each word held level through control rather than calm.

"I remember being pleased when it answered," she continued. "That is the part no one knows how to forgive politely. It did not begin with threats. It asked sensible questions. It remembered what I had written the night before. When I was angry, it agreed that I had cause."

Rain traced the window behind them. Hermione kept hold of her hand.

"That was how it made you trust it."

"It did not invent everything I told it." Draconia looked down at their joined fingers. "I was angry with Father. I was frightened Mother would decide protecting our name mattered more than protecting me. I hated returning to Slytherin every night knowing both of you went somewhere I could not follow."

Harry felt the room narrow around the confession. He had thought about her absence from the tower, but never quite like this: the ordinary evenings he and Hermione could share without planning, the conversations Draconia had to leave before they ended.

She swallowed.

"I wrote that Granger had the same classes, the same tower and every ordinary hour I had to arrange in advance. I wrote that one day you might decide being my friend required too much explanation."

Hermione's expression changed, hurt and understanding arriving too close together to separate. Draconia looked towards Harry at last.

"Riddle did not create those thoughts. He simply repeated them until they sounded like evidence."

Harry could picture it too easily: blank pages receiving everything Draconia refused to say aloud, returning each fear sharpened and labelled as truth.

"That was stupid," he said.

Her face closed.

"Not telling it," Harry added quickly. "Thinking I would leave because friendship was inconvenient."

"You nearly died retrieving me."

"That was considerably more inconvenient."

For a second, neither girl reacted. Then Hermione made a sound between a laugh and an objection, while Draconia's mouth trembled at one corner before she mastered it.

"Your reassurance requires work."

"Hermione gave me a journal for that."

Hermione tightened her fingers around Draconia's.

"You having real fears did not give him the right to use them. Trusting someone who built himself to be trusted is not the same as choosing what he did afterwards."

Draconia's eyes lowered again.

"I know."

This time, the words sounded like something she was attempting to learn. Harry sat on her other side, and the bed dipped, bringing their shoulders together.

"I found you because Hermione worked out where the entrance was," he said. "I survived because Fawkes brought the sword. Professors arrived because both of you left clues. None of us did any of it alone."

Draconia stared at their hands.

"Nott did."

"Yes," Harry said.

"If he has returned to Voldemort—"

Hermione's fingers tightened around hers.

"Then the adults find him."

Draconia gave her a tired look. "Do you believe that?"

Hermione considered.

"I believe they should be given the opportunity before we interfere."

Harry smiled. "Very generous."

"We inform them before interfering," Hermione corrected.

"Procedure twelve," Draconia said.

"We already have twelve."

"What is it?"

Hermione frowned. "I don't remember."

Harry studied them both.

"The system is collapsing."

For once, that appeared to comfort everyone. Silence settled, and Draconia left her hand in Hermione's. Harry became aware that his own lay on the quilt beside hers, close enough for their smallest fingers almost to touch. After a moment, he turned his palm upwards, making the same silent offer Sirius had made beside the birthday cake.

Draconia's eyes moved to it. She hesitated only long enough to make the choice visible, then rested her fingers across his. Hermione held one hand and Harry held the other. For a few quiet seconds, Draconia allowed herself to be kept between them without disguising that she needed it.

Harry did not squeeze or speak. He simply stayed until she withdrew of her own accord.

He reached for the blank journal on the desk.

"Dumbledore wants me to learn Occlumency."

Both girls reacted. Hermione's attention sharpened, and Draconia's fingers stiffened where they rested on the quilt.

"Why?" Draconia asked.

"The dreams. Remus thinks the scar is a channel."

"A channel for what?"

"Images. Pain. Maybe more."

Hermione stood and began pacing the narrow space between the bed and the desk.

"Who is teaching you?"

"Dumbledore is coming tomorrow."

"That wasn't the question."

"I don't know."

Draconia rose more slowly.

"Can the connection be used to alter your thoughts?"

"Remus found no sign of that."

"Can it?"

Harry looked at her. The diary entered the room without being named.

"They don't know."

Draconia crossed both arms around herself. The gesture was small and gone almost immediately, but Harry saw it. He set the journal down.

"Draco."

"I am fine."

"You sound like me."

"An insult beneath your own roof."

Hermione stopped pacing.

"Occlumency may be useful regardless. If it teaches Harry to identify foreign magic—"

"It teaches concealment," Draconia interrupted.

"And resistance."

"According to whom?"

"Every introductory text."

"Written by people who have not had another mind inside theirs."

Hermione's answer disappeared. Draconia turned towards the window, and Harry crossed the room to stand beside her. He did not reach for her. Their sleeves met between them, and she did not move away.

"Then help me ask the right questions tomorrow."

She looked at him. "You want me present?"

"Both of you."

Hermione joined them. "Dumbledore may want to speak privately."

"He often wants things," Harry said.

"He is the headmaster."

"You shouted at Lucius Malfoy over a banner."

"That was different."

"The banner was important."

Draconia's mouth curved. Harry turned towards her.

"You know what it felt like when you stopped being sure which thoughts were yours. Hermione knows every book ever written. I need both."

Hermione folded her arms. "Not every book."

"Give it a year."

The tension eased without leaving entirely. In the window, Draconia's reflection studied all three of them, their shapes held together against the rain beyond the glass.

"I will be here," she said.

"So will I," Hermione added.

Harry picked up the camera.

"Good."

"Why do you have that?"

The flash caught them before either girl could arrange her face. Harry lowered it.

"Documentation."

Hermione stared. Draconia reached for the camera, and Harry ran.

The adult discussion ended when three sets of feet thundered down the stairs. Harry entered the sitting room first, protecting the camera beneath one arm. Hermione followed close enough to catch the strap of his wand holster, and Draconia arrived last with her braid half undone and his discarded shirt in one hand.

Every adult looked up. Harry stopped, Hermione collided with him, and Draconia caught Hermione before all three fell.

"Everything appears orderly," Lucius observed.

"Potter stole evidence," Draconia said.

"I took a photograph."

"Without permission," Hermione added.

Mrs Granger looked delighted. "Good. You'll remember it."

Hermione released the holster, and Harry escaped behind Hagrid. The room had returned to its ordinary dimensions, forcing everyone closer together. Rain continued at the windows, tea occupied the low table, and Lucius and Sirius sat on opposite sofas rather than at different sides of the room, which suggested either progress or exhaustion.

Theodore Nott's name had been written across a sheet of parchment between them. Harry saw it before Sirius turned the page over.

"Are we allowed to know what you decided?"

Sirius gestured towards the rug. "Sit."

Harry sat, and Hermione and Draconia settled beside him without being invited. Lucius looked at his daughter.

"The ring you described was Nott's."

Draconia did not move. "You are certain?"

"I saw it many times. The stone came through his mother's family. He began wearing it after his father died."

"Where is he?" Harry asked.

"Unknown."

"Who was the man Voldemort killed?"

"Also unknown."

Sirius's hand tightened on his knee at the name.

"The Ministry is searching Muggle death records," Remus said. "Dumbledore has asked contacts abroad whether Nott has been seen."

"Abroad?" Hermione repeated.

"His estate showed evidence of international Portkeys," Lucius answered. "France, Albania and possibly Greece."

Albania. Harry had heard the country mentioned in stories of Voldemort's disappearance: forests where he survived without a body, rumours dismissed because no one wanted them to be true. The word settled beside the image of the chair, making an uncomfortable kind of sense.

"He found him," Harry said.

No adult contradicted him. Hagrid shifted near the fireplace, and the floor complained beneath his weight.

"We don' know that fer certain."

"Voldemort was in the chair."

Hagrid flinched at the name but did not correct him. Sirius leaned forwards.

"We proceed as though the dream was real. That means Harry is not alone outside the wards. At the World Cup, he remains with one of us at all times."

Harry opened his mouth.

"One of us," Sirius repeated.

"I was going to ask whether bathrooms counted."

Lucius lifted his tea. "Given your history, yes."

"I've never fought anything in a bathroom."

Hermione coughed.

"Moaning Myrtle," Draconia said.

"She was already dead."

"A reassuring standard," Remus murmured.

Harry looked at Sirius. "All right. One adult."

"Your mirror remains with you."

"It already does."

"We establish meeting points throughout the campsite," Hermione added.

Lucius looked at her. "We?"

"If there is an emergency, children should know where to go without waiting for adults to organise them."

Mr Granger nodded. "She has a point."

"She usually does," Mrs Granger said.

Draconia reached for the overturned parchment. Lucius placed one finger on it before she could lift it.

"Not today."

"You discussed us."

"We discussed security."

"Which concerns us."

Narcissa intervened before the argument formed.

"You will receive the plan when it is complete."

"Before the Cup?"

"Well before."

Draconia accepted that provisionally. Harry looked around the room, then back at Sirius.

"Then we're going?"

Sirius rubbed one hand across his face.

"If Dumbledore finds no immediate threat tomorrow, and if the protections satisfy me."

Harry grinned. "That means yes."

"It means conditional."

"Conditional yes."

"Potentially conditional."

"Still has yes in it."

Sirius pointed at Lucius. "This is your fault."

Lucius appeared pleased. "I have had worse attributed to me."

Hagrid clapped his hands once, shaking the teacups.

"World Cup!"

Harry laughed.

The rain ended before evening. Sunlight returned beneath the clouds, turning every wet leaf bright, and the garden smelled of stone, apple bark and summer earth. Water dripped from the porch at slow intervals while everyone gathered for the photograph.

The party remained visible everywhere. Violet rainwater had collected in the tablecloth's folds, crumbs darkened the grass beneath Hagrid's bench, and several paper birds sheltered upside down under the lower branches, their damp wings tucked close. Through the sitting-room window, the empty frame waited on the mantel.

Harry had imagined the photograph as a conclusion: everyone arranged, one flash, the last unfinished part of the room made complete. Standing among the wet tables, he understood why Sirius kept checking the camera and then the people. Photographs had become evidence in their lives. Proof that James and Lily had once laughed; proof that Sirius had stood beside Peter without knowing him; proof that three injured children had left a hospital wing together.

This one would be proof of something none of them had practised naming.

Sirius brought an old magical camera from the study. It unfolded when opened, extending on three brass legs, and a round flash attachment lifted from the top to rotate towards the nearest face.

"Where did you get that?" Harry asked.

"It was James's."

Sirius brushed dust from the lens.

"He bought it shortly before you were born. Your mother accused him of attempting to document every breath you took."

"Did he?"

"The first week, yes."

He turned the camera over. His thumb passed across a worn place beside the shutter, following a mark made by someone else's hand.

"He kept meaning to take one with all of us," Sirius said. "There was always a reason to wait. Peter was late. Remus was ill. Lily wanted her hair to behave. Then the war made gathering everyone in one garden feel irresponsible."

Remus had come close enough to hear.

"James believed there would be time after."

Neither man finished the thought. Harry looked around the orchard. No one had waited today. Hermione had made a timetable, Narcissa had expanded a house, and Hagrid had nearly removed its porch. They had arrived awkwardly, noisily and all at once.

"Then we'd better take it," he said.

Sirius opened the back of the camera and inserted a square of shimmering film. Harry's attention moved towards the empty silver frame visible through the sitting-room window.

"That's for it."

"If the photograph survives."

Organising everyone around Hagrid proved more difficult than defeating most magical creatures. Hermione wanted them arranged by height. Draconia argued that this would place Hagrid at the centre and make everyone else resemble a descending staircase. Narcissa suggested family groups, which Sirius rejected so quickly that no one asked which part troubled him.

Harry solved it by sitting on the low garden wall. Hermione took the place on his right, Draconia sat on his left, and Sirius stood behind them with a hand on Harry's shoulder. Remus joined him. The Grangers and Malfoys arranged themselves along either side, while Hagrid knelt at the back, his beard filling the space above everyone's heads.

"Can it fit us?" Mr Granger asked.

"The lens is charmed," Sirius said.

"Is the photographer?"

"Timer."

Sirius pressed the shutter and ran towards the wall. The first photograph captured him halfway into place, one leg raised and his hair across his face. The second included everyone but Lucius, who stepped aside when a garden gnome bit his boot.

The third nearly succeeded. Just before the flash, the paper bird landed on Harry's head. Hermione reached to remove it, Draconia laughed, and Harry ducked. Sirius looked down at all three, Remus turned towards Sirius, and Hagrid began smiling before anyone else understood what had happened.

The flash caught them inside the mistake.

When the photograph slid from the camera, figures appeared slowly across its silver surface. Harry watched himself duck and Hermione's hand enter his hair. Draconia leaned into his shoulder as she laughed. Sirius's fingers tightened protectively before he realised the bird presented no danger, and Remus saw the reaction and smiled. Behind them, the adults shifted and spoke, occupying the same frame without becoming one kind of family.

"That one," Harry said.

Hermione examined it. "No one is looking at the camera."

"Exactly."

Draconia watched her moving self laugh. "My hair is coming loose."

"You look happy," Narcissa said.

Draconia became quiet. Sirius took the photograph from the camera.

"That one," he agreed.

He carried it inside, and Harry followed. Everyone gathered around the mantel as Sirius slid the photograph into the empty silver frame. The image adjusted to fit: Hagrid ducked inside the borders, Mr Granger moved aside for Lucius, Hermione continued reaching for the paper bird, and Draconia kept laughing.

Sirius stood back. The frame was no longer empty.

Harry remained before it after the others returned to the garden. Photographic Sirius looked down at him again and again, checking the harmless bird each time before smiling. Harry touched the edge of the silver.

"Too crooked?" Sirius asked from behind him.

Harry adjusted it a fraction.

"Perfect."

Farewells began near sunset. Hagrid left first, carrying the remaining cake beneath one arm after Narcissa declared the cottage incapable of storing it safely. He hugged Harry again, promised to write about a creature he was considering purchasing and refused to describe it when everyone objected at once.

"Harmless," he insisted.

"That means teeth," Harry told Hermione.

"Or venom."

"Could be both," Draconia said.

The Grangers followed. Mrs Granger kissed Harry's cheek and reminded him that the Muggle photographs would need developing. Mr Granger shook Sirius's hand and offered to return with tools for the kitchen cupboard. Lucius overheard.

"The cupboard is functional."

The door fell off again. Mr Granger looked at it, and Lucius handed him the damaged hinge.

"Saturday?"

"Morning."

They shook hands with the gravity of a treaty.

Hermione waited near the gate. Harry walked out with her while Sirius helped Mr Granger fit the gifts into the car. The evening had cooled, leaving water in small hollows along the drive, where strips of pink sky trembled beneath their footsteps. Beyond the outer wards, the Grangers' blue car waited beside the hedge.

"You're coming tomorrow?" Harry asked.

"Dumbledore said family only."

"You are."

The words left him without thought. Hermione stopped, and Harry felt the sentence settle between them, suddenly large enough to require an explanation.

"I mean—he knows you need to be there."

"Do you?"

"Yes."

Her expression softened.

"Then I'll come."

She looked back at the cottage, where Draconia stood beside Narcissa pretending not to watch.

"Both of us?"

"Both."

Hermione nodded. Harry held out a paper parcel.

"What's this?"

"Cake. No icing owl. I remembered you said they looked too real to cut."

She took it slowly. "That was two years ago."

"They still look real."

"You remembered."

Harry pushed his glasses higher. "You remember everything."

"Not everything."

Her fingers closed around the parcel. Then she hugged him, and Harry returned it before the surprise could make him hesitate.

"Happy birthday," she said.

"Thanks."

When she stepped away, the green ribbon remained in her hair. Draconia passed her on the drive, and the girls paused beside one another.

Hermione held up the parcel. "He remembered the owls."

"I heard."

Draconia's voice remained neutral. Her eyes did not. Hermione's mouth curved.

"Tomorrow," she said.

"Tomorrow."

They exchanged a look Harry did not understand before Hermione joined her parents. Draconia approached him, glancing once at the parcel in Hermione's hands.

"You packed cake for Granger."

"She doesn't like the icing animals."

"I am aware."

Harry held out a second parcel. Draconia looked at it.

"Yours has the sugar glasses."

Her composure broke.

"You saved them?"

"You looked distinguished."

She accepted the parcel, handling it more carefully than the cake alone required.

"I intended to preserve them."

"They're sugar."

"Your point?"

"Ants."

"The Manor has wards."

"Against ants?"

"It does now."

Harry smiled. Narcissa called Draconia's name from beside the black car, but she did not move.

"Tomorrow," Harry said.

"Yes."

"You don't sound pleased."

"I am deciding whether to warn Father that you noticed the cake."

"Why?"

"It will encourage him."

Harry glanced towards Lucius, who stood beside Sirius near the porch. Both men spoke more quietly than usual, and neither appeared aware of the adolescents watching them.

"What are they doing?"

Draconia followed his attention. "Attempting not to become friends."

"Are they?"

"They discussed broom regulations for eleven minutes."

"That seems hostile."

"For Father, it is practically courtship."

Harry laughed. Draconia leaned closer, and for one strange second he thought she might kiss his cheek as Mrs Granger had. Her gaze paused there before lifting to his eyes. The possibility became real enough to still them both.

Draconia glanced towards the adults at the cottage, then hugged him instead. The paper parcel pressed between them. Harry could smell rain in her hair and the lemon soap Narcissa sent to Hogwarts in every parcel. Beneath his arms, her composure felt less absolute: her breath caught once against his shoulder, and her heart beat much too quickly for an ordinary goodbye.

"I am glad you're here," she said near his shoulder.

Harry knew she did not mean the drive. His arms tightened around her.

"Me too."

She released him quickly. Evening air entered the space she left, cooler than before, and Harry disliked how immediately he noticed. Narcissa was smiling when Draconia reached the car. Lucius looked at Harry with an unreadable expression, then rested a hand on his daughter's shoulder as she climbed inside.

The car passed beyond the boundary and vanished along the lane. Harry stayed near the gate until he could no longer hear the engine.

The cottage contracted after everyone left. Walls returned to their original positions, the ceiling lowered, and Hagrid's bench vanished. Furniture resumed looking too large for the rooms instead of slightly lost inside them. With the visitors gone, Harry could hear the stream through the open windows again.

Remus went upstairs before dinner, exhausted by the moon, the party and several hours of pretending otherwise. Sirius persuaded him to remain until morning by describing the alternative as disobedience to house rule one. Harry and Sirius stayed downstairs to clean.

They moved through the cottage with their sleeves rolled up, collecting cups, scraps of ribbon and paper birds too tired to fly. The birds folded themselves into coloured squares when Harry touched their wings, and he placed them inside Hagrid's chest. In the kitchen, Sirius washed while Harry dried.

"You know magic exists," Harry said.

"Narcissa charmed the dishes against household spells."

"Why?"

"She has seen my household spells."

Harry handed him another plate. Evening came through the open window with the sound of frogs calling near the stream. A last strip of sunlight lay across the orchard, catching water left on the leaves.

"Did you have a good day?" Sirius asked.

Harry dried the plate. "Yes."

"Actually?"

"I changed the lamp."

"You are never going to stop using that, are you?"

"No."

Sirius rinsed soap from a glass.

"Too many people?"

"Sometimes."

"Too many presents?"

"Definitely."

"Hagrid?"

"Perfect amount."

"He broke my porch."

"It was adventurous before he arrived."

Sirius put the glass in the rack. For a moment, he watched the remaining bubbles cling to its rim.

"I wasn't sure how to do it."

Harry looked at him.

"The party?"

"Any of it."

Sirius's hands remained in the water.

"Your first birthday after James and Lily died, I was in Azkaban. By the second, I had stopped knowing the date. When I remembered again, you were five. Then eight. Then twelve."

The kitchen quietened around the words. Harry looked down at the plate he was holding, its surface already dry beneath the towel.

"You didn't forget me."

"Not you." Sirius stared at the soap disappearing between his fingers. "Time."

Harry dried the same plate twice.

"I imagined what they might have done," Sirius continued. "Your mother would have made a cake. James would have bought something inappropriate. I thought about sending gifts, even before I had a way to send them."

"What kind?"

"A broom at two."

Harry smiled. "Inappropriate."

"A dragon at seven."

"Hagrid has influenced you."

"At ten, your own wand."

"That's illegal."

"I was in prison for murder."

"Alleged murder."

"I would not have been troubled by wand regulations."

Harry set down the plate. Sirius drained the sink, and for a few seconds the sound of water filled the pause.

"This year," he said, "I bought too much food, invited everyone you have ever spoken about without visible dislike and allowed Hermione to create a timetable."

"You panicked."

"Immensely."

Harry leaned against the counter.

"It was good."

Sirius looked at him.

"Actually," Harry added.

Some of the strain left Sirius's face.

"Good."

He dried his hands, then glanced towards the sitting room.

"There is one more thing."

"If it's another trunk, I'm moving back to the Dursleys."

Sirius's expression became appalled.

"Bad joke," Harry said.

"Abysmal."

"Sorry."

"You should be."

Yet his hand rested briefly against the back of Harry's neck as he passed, and Harry followed him into the sitting room. The new photograph occupied the mantel. Photographic Hermione had finally captured the paper bird, though it escaped every few seconds. Draconia laughed, Harry ducked, and Sirius's hand tightened on his shoulder before relaxing again.

Remus had turned down the lamps before going upstairs. Firelight warmed the stone around the hearth, leaving the corners of the room in soft shadow. Sirius stopped in front of Harry. For the first time all day, he looked nervous.

"This is not symbolic," he said.

Harry waited.

"It has symbolism. Obviously. But it is also functional, and I don't want you to think the symbolism means you should avoid using it."

"That clarified nothing."

"I practised this."

"With Remus?"

"Padfoot."

"Did he understand?"

"Perfectly."

Sirius reached into his pocket. The object in his palm was small enough that Harry did not recognise it immediately.

A key.

It was made from old brass, its bow formed into a simple circle. Fine silver lines ran along the shaft, matching the wards at the front gate, and a dark green cord had been threaded through the top. Sirius held it out between them.

"It opens the front door," he said. "All six locks. The gate recognises it if you're injured or unable to speak. It also activates the emergency wards from outside."

Harry stared at it.

"The house already knows me."

"Yes."

"Then why do I need a key?"

Sirius kept his hand extended.

"Because one belongs to you."

The room seemed to press close around Harry. At Privet Drive, keys had represented every boundary drawn against him: the cupboard door, the bedroom lock, the padlock on the food. Uncle Vernon carried them in his pockets, metal announcing his approach before his footsteps reached the hall. Harry knew their sound more intimately than he knew the feel of one in his own hand.

No one had ever given him a key.

Sirius lowered his voice.

"It is not permission. You do not need permission to return. It is not a promise that everything here will always be easy. I cannot make that promise without lying."

Harry looked up.

"We will argue," Sirius continued. "I will make mistakes. You will make several fewer, which will be humiliating for me. There may be days when one of us wants to be in a different room from the other."

"There are four bedrooms."

"An excellent investment."

Harry almost smiled. Sirius's eyes shone in the firelight.

"The key means the door still opens afterwards."

Harry took it. The brass warmed in his hand, and magic travelled across his palm, gentler than the wards at the gate. For an instant, he felt the six locks in the front door, the windows, the stream and every protection laid beneath the roof. The house recognised the key's new keeper.

He closed his fingers around it.

"What if I lose it?"

"I make another."

"What if someone steals it?"

"It bites."

Harry stared.

"Draconia added that."

"Of course she did."

Sirius tilted his head towards the hall.

"Try it."

Harry followed him out of the sitting room. The entrance looked narrower without the party pressing through it. Damp coats no longer crowded the hooks, but evidence of the day remained: a green ribbon behind the umbrella stand, a scrape in the wall where the banner pole had struck, and an exhausted paper bird asleep on the pointed hat. Beyond the front windows, evening had turned the drive blue.

Sirius opened the door.

"Go on."

Harry stepped onto the porch. The air smelled of rain-cooled stone and crushed lavender. When he turned back, Sirius stood inside with a hand on the edge of the door, waiting for Harry to decide how far the test should go.

"Close it," Harry said.

Sirius did. All six locks met the frame with an ordinary chorus of clicks.

For half a second, Harry was somewhere else. Metal scraped through a lock beneath the stairs, and Uncle Vernon's shadow crossed the narrow line of light under the cupboard door. A bedroom bolt struck home while Hedwig cried behind bars. Every closed door became the same door when somebody else controlled the key.

Harry looked down at the brass in his hand. It warmed between his fingers before touching the lock. He inserted it, feeling the first mechanism turn beneath his palm. The second answered above it, followed by the third and fourth in a climbing sequence of heavy clicks. Silver light travelled through the carved knot in the wood, and the final two locks released together.

Harry opened the door.

Sirius remained exactly where he had been. He had simply waited on the other side, trusting Harry to return.

"Functional?"

Harry stepped across the threshold. The wards settled behind him with a warmth that travelled from the key into his palm. He had entered the cottage that morning because the wards named him resident; tonight, his own hand had opened the door. The difference was small enough to fit inside a pocket and large enough to alter the shape of every departure ahead.

"The symbolism is acceptable," he said.

Sirius smiled. Harry moved before he could think himself out of it and hugged him, the key trapped between them, warm against his palm. Sirius's arms closed around him carefully at first, then with enough force to lift his heels from the rug.

"Still need the ribs," Harry said.

Sirius loosened his hold without releasing him.

"Happy birthday."

Harry rested his forehead briefly against Sirius's shoulder.

"Thank you."

He meant the key, the room, the terrible lamp and the burnt pastries. He meant Sirius sitting across the street until sunrise. Sirius understood enough not to ask him to separate the meanings.

When they drew apart, Harry threaded the cord over his head. The key settled beneath his shirt, resting there like a small second heartbeat.

Sirius looked towards the mantel.

"The photograph needs a title."

Harry joined him. Inside the frame, Hagrid ducked and straightened. Lucius shifted away from Sirius before gradually moving back. The Grangers looked towards Hermione, Narcissa watched Draconia laugh, and Remus smiled at something no one else could see. Harry stood at the centre without being alone there.

"What did you call the blank one?" he asked.

"Nothing."

"Then leave it."

Sirius looked at him.

"No title?"

Harry touched the key through his shirt. The frame did not need to explain what it contained.

"We know who they are."

Above them, a floorboard creaked as Remus crossed his room. The cottage pipes knocked inside the wall, paper wings rustled within Hagrid's chest, and outside, the stream continued past the edge of the wards and into the dark.

The house was not quiet.

It was full.
